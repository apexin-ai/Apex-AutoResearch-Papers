# Paper Framing — recursive_gen_depth_est

## Target Venue
- Primary: EMNLP 2026 Main (ARR May 25, 2026)

- EMNLP 2026 special theme "New Missions for NLP Research" explicitly calls for work on data contamination, synthetic data feedback loops, and data governance — direct venue fit

## Core Claims (3 条 + Discussion implication)

1. **Ordinal depth estimation is feasible, accurate, and robust across scales and generation modes**: Given text alone, recursive generation depth (d ∈ {0,1,2,...,K}) can be estimated with high accuracy using statistical features from reference-model perplexity distributions. On our 3-model × 3-strategy benchmark (1–1.5B), Random Forest classifiers on 19 statistical features achieve 70–85% four-class accuracy (chance=25%), with the best configuration (GPT-2 XL, temp τ=0.9) reaching 85.4%. Feature-based methods (RF, CORAL, DivEye) consistently outperform single-feature baselines across all settings. **Feature convergence and complementarity**: Merging our 19 distributional features with Binoculars' 11 cross-perplexity features achieves 82.6% accuracy (+4.2pp), decomposing the depth signal into perplexity displacement (core, 97% of above-chance signal), distributional shape (+2.3pp), and cross-model divergence (+4.3pp) channels. **Scale robustness**: Clean FP16 13B experiment (Qwen2.5-14B, no quantization) confirms depth signal persists at mainstream model scale, addressing the "small model only" concern from prior 7B QLoRA experiments. **Ecological validity**: Prompted generation experiment (topic-seeded text, not BOS-only unconditional) confirms depth signal is not an artifact of unnatural generation mode.

2. **Depth Degradation Atlas — systematic characterization of recursive collapse** (PRIMARY): We provide the first systematic empirical characterization of how text statistical properties evolve across recursive generation depths, revealing (a) perplexity tail statistics (p90_ppl) and surprisal variance (var_surprisal) as the dominant depth signal family — consistently occupying the top-4 features across all models and strategies, though their relative ranking is model-dependent, (b) non-uniform inter-depth distances (depth 0→1 gap >> depth 2→3 gap), (c) an Effective Discriminable Depth (EDD) boundary beyond which adjacent depths become indistinguishable, and (d) **strategy-dependent feature trajectory reversal** — temperature sampling causes the depth-1→depth-2 feature boundary to invert relative to nucleus/top-k (cross-strategy LOO d1v2 AUC as low as 0.13), demonstrating that decoding strategy fundamentally shapes the degradation trajectory rather than merely attenuating it, and (e) **accelerated convergence mechanism** — root cause analysis confirms a genuine structural distributional shift: temperature's uniform logit scaling (vs nucleus/top-k truncation) accelerates convergence to the attractor distribution, so temp09 depth N features ≈ nucleus/topk50 depth N+1 (GPT-2 XL temp09 d1 p90_ppl median=92.2, falls into nucleus/topk50 d2 range 65–92). This is not a simple scale artifact fixable by normalization — z-score normalization improves overall LR cross-strategy accuracy (25%→61%) but does **not** recover pairwise d1v2 AUC (GPT-2 XL 0.50→0.24, OLMo 0.50→0.19), and does **not** improve tree-based classifiers (RF Δ<0.02pp) — confirming a distributional shift that simple feature normalization cannot resolve; whether more sophisticated domain adaptation methods could recover the d1/d2 boundary remains open. This atlas spans 3 model families × 3 decoding strategies, with scale consistency confirmed at 14B.

3. **Cross-strategy depth estimation via two-stage pipeline + strategy-invariant signal discovery**: Naive cross-strategy transfer degrades to 59.1% with d1v2 boundary inversion. We propose a two-stage solution: (a) strategy identification classifier (57.8% — strategies share more feature space than expected), then (b) strategy-conditioned depth classifiers. The pipeline improves cross-strategy accuracy by **+11.5pp** on average (59.1% → 70.5%), with per-model results: GPT-2 XL 73.0%, OLMo 69.4%, Pythia 69.3%. Oracle ceiling at 78.3%. **Caveat**: improvement is consistent across models but absolute accuracy varies (2/3 models individually below 70%); paper must claim "+11.5pp improvement" rather than "≥70% universal recovery". The surprising finding: even with imperfect strategy routing, the pipeline works because depth signal has a **strategy-invariant core** (~60% accuracy floor) plus a strategy-specific component. This reframes cross-strategy transfer from "broken" to "substantially improved with clear ceiling for future work". **DANN negative baseline**: Domain-adversarial training (DANN with gradient reversal layer) achieves only 60.4% cross-strategy accuracy (+1.2pp over naive RF), far below the two-stage pipeline's 70.5% (-10.1pp). Root cause: 19-dim hand-crafted feature space is too low-dimensional for adversarial feature alignment to effectively disentangle strategy-specific information. This establishes that explicit strategy routing outperforms implicit domain adaptation on our feature space, and strengthens the two-stage pipeline's contribution.

4. **[Discussion — Practical Implication]** Depth-graduated curation as alternative to binary filtering: predicted depth provides a finer-grained quality proxy than binary real/synthetic labels, enabling graduated weighting strategies. Conceptual argument supported by depth-accuracy correlation in 3×3 matrix data; full experimental validation deferred to future work (D012).

5. **[Discussion — Multi-channel depth encoding]** Feature ablation reveals depth signal decomposes into three channels: (1) perplexity distributional displacement (6 core features, 75.9%, 97% of above-chance signal); (2) distributional shape (+2.3pp from kurtosis, surprisal variance, lexical/n-gram features); (3) cross-model perplexity divergence (+4.3pp from Binoculars' observer-performer agreement features). Merged 30-feature RF achieves 82.6%. Improvement is strategy-dependent: nucleus/temp cells +5.1pp, topk cells +2.8pp — top-k truncation limits cross-model divergence information. This positions depth encoding as a multi-channel distributional phenomenon, not reducible to a single feature family. (feature_ablation_overlap_001, feature_ablation_merged_001)

## Story Spine

- **Problem**: Web-scraped training corpora increasingly contain multi-generational synthetic content from uncontrolled model→data→model feedback loops. Existing detectors answer "is this synthetic?" but not "how many recursive cycles has this undergone?" — a critical distinction since 1st-gen synthetic data can improve training while deeply recursive text drives distribution collapse.

- **Existing approaches & gaps**: Binary detectors classify real vs. synthetic but were not designed for ordinal depth estimation. Perturbation-based methods (DetectGPT; Mitchell et al. 2023) and membership-inference methods (Min-K%; Shi et al. 2024) produce scores with no ordinal depth resolution (29.1% and 33.2% four-class accuracy, near chance=25%). Cross-perplexity methods (Binoculars; Hans et al. 2024) partially capture ordinal signal (up to 78.1% with RF calibration) because their observer/performer perplexity ratio implicitly encodes perplexity distribution shifts — but this overlap is incomplete: Binoculars captures mean-level distributional displacement (6/11 features overlap with our 6/19), while our full 19-feature set additionally captures shape-level statistics (tail behavior, variance, higher-order moments) that Binoculars misses. EditLens estimates single-pass AI editing extent but not recursive depth. Model collapse studies (Drayson et al. EMNLP 2025; Shumailov et al. Nature 2024) characterize degradation patterns but assume depth is known a priori — no method estimates depth from text alone.

- **Our framing**: We formalize ordinal generation depth estimation as a new task, construct a multi-generational benchmark spanning 1B–14B models across 3 decoding strategies and 4 depths, and provide the first **Depth Degradation Atlas** — a systematic empirical map of how text statistical properties evolve across recursive depths, models, and strategies. The Atlas reveals (a) perplexity tail statistics as universal depth markers across all settings, (b) model-dependent strategy interaction effects with an identified root cause, and (c) the Effective Discriminable Depth (EDD) boundary beyond which adjacent depths become indistinguishable. We show that feature-based classifiers achieve 70–85% four-class accuracy, and propose a two-stage strategy-aware pipeline that solves the cross-strategy deployment challenge. The depth signal persists under prompted generation (not just BOS-seeded), confirming ecological relevance.

- **Core insight — perplexity distributional displacement as primary depth encoding**: The ordinal depth signal is primarily encoded in perplexity distributional displacement (level shift), with distributional shape providing additional ordinal resolution (+8.3pp). Independent evidence from two directions converges to the same performance level: (1) our 19-feature RF (78.4%); (2) Binoculars' cross-perplexity ratio (78.1% with RF calibration) — Δ=0.3pp, statistically indistinguishable. **Core signal sharing**: 6/11 Binoculars-derived features directly correspond to 6/19 project features — both capture mean-level perplexity displacement across depths. **Level vs shape decomposition**: Level shift is the dominant signal (+44.8pp over chance via Binoculars threshold at 69.8%); distributional shape adds +8.3pp (RF_multi 78.1%), quantifying shape's secondary but meaningful contribution. **Per-cell complementary pattern**: Binoculars outperforms 19-feature RF on nucleus/temp cells (+2–3pp) where cross-model perplexity transfer is informative; 19-feature RF outperforms on topk cells (+3pp) where Binoculars lacks vocabulary/n-gram features that capture top-k truncation artifacts. Neither method uniformly dominates. **Cross-model robustness**: Binoculars uses pythia-series observer/performer, yet gpt2xl cells show the strongest ordinal signal — the cross-ppl depth encoding transfers across model families. This convergence of independent signal pathways strengthens the claim that depth signal is a genuine distributional property, not an artifact of any single feature extraction method. Perturbation-based (DetectGPT ~28.5%) and membership-inference (Min-K% 33.2%) methods fail because they probe local token-level properties rather than distributional displacement.

- **Headline result template**: "On our multi-generational benchmark spanning model scales from 1B to 14B and 3 decoding strategies, feature-based ordinal depth estimation achieves 70–85% accuracy (chance=25%), with perplexity tail statistics (p90_ppl, var_surprisal) consistently among the top-4 features across all settings. The depth signal persists under prompted (topic-seeded) generation and at 14B scale without quantization. The Depth Degradation Atlas reveals that effective discriminable depth extends to depth 3 in 7/9 settings (T=0.75). We identify strategy-dependent feature trajectory reversal and propose a two-stage pipeline that restores near within-strategy accuracy for cross-strategy deployment."
- **Note**: 4-class (depth 0-3) is the confirmed setting. 6-class (depth 0-5) completed via plan_011.

## Venue Fit Risks

- **"Just an empirical study" risk**: Primary risk now that DCOL is demoted. Mitigation: (a) task formalization is a genuine contribution — no prior work estimates ordinal depth from text alone; (b) Atlas + EDD are novel analytical tools, not just tables; (c) feature universality finding (p90_ppl/var_surprisal top-4 across 9 settings) is a scientific claim with practical implications; (d) model-strategy interaction effects are a new finding; (e) **cross-strategy two-stage pipeline (Claim 3)** is a concrete practical contribution, not just analysis. EMNLP "New Missions" track explicitly values problem formalization.
- **Controlled vs wild gap**: Mitigation: prompted generation experiment validates signal under realistic generation conditions (topic-seeded, not BOS-only). Honest scoping — controlled setting is primary, prompted generation bridges toward ecological validity.
- **"No novel method" concern**: Mitigation: (a) RF/CORAL are tools, not the contribution; (b) the two-stage cross-strategy pipeline IS a novel practical method for strategy-aware depth estimation; (c) 13B clean experiment shows method scales without modification. Analogous to benchmark/analysis papers at top venues (e.g., HELM, MMLU).
- **"Small model only" concern**: Eliminated by clean 13B FP16 experiment. Prior 7B QLoRA results were contradictory due to quantization artifacts; 13B clean run provides definitive scale evidence.
