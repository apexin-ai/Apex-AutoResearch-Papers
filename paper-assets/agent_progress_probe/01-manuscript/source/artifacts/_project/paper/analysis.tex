\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{On circular evaluation.}
The structural macro-F1 of 0.9951 (\S\ref{sec:main_results}) is a predictable consequence of feature--label overlap: the 30 structural features (repetition rates, file operation counts, edit distances) are computed from the same signals that $\lambda_1$--$\lambda_3$ consume to produce silver labels.
A classifier trained on approximate LF rule inputs can reconstruct majority-vote outputs with near-perfect fidelity; this score upper-bounds LF reverse-engineering, not true detection accuracy.
The cross-agent GB result on OpenHands (0.967 macro-F1) is non-circular: models trained on SWE-Agent LF distributions transfer to an agent whose firing patterns diverge---$\lambda_1$ fires on 92.3\% of OH windows versus 65.6\% on SWE-Agent (\S\ref{sec:cmd_rep_ablation}).
The human-annotated gold set (\S\ref{sec:gold_set}) provides the definitive non-circular evaluation.

\paragraph{Why temporal label models fail in the 3-LF regime.}
With only three labeling functions, majority vote already achieves high consensus: TWV agrees with MV on 88.5\% of all windows, and modifies only 14.1\% of conf${=}1$ labels (\S\ref{sec:temporal_label_models}).
This leaves minimal room for temporal correction.
TWV achieves the highest conf${\geq}$2 F1 (0.9003, +0.57\,pp over MV), yet the conf${=}1$ flips degrade downstream probe performance from 0.8468 to 0.8211 ($-2.57$\,pp), indicating that flipped labels introduce more noise than they correct.
HMM overpredicts stagnation (29{,}176 vs.\ 25{,}633 progress windows), collapsing downstream F1 to 0.6564.
Lag-HMM, despite explicitly modeling per-LF temporal offsets, merely tracks MV ($-0.1$\,pp conf${\geq}$2 F1).
The bandwidth $\sigma$ has a narrow operating range: only $\sigma{=}2$ improves over MV; $\sigma{=}1$ drops to 0.8105 and $\sigma{\geq}5$ falls below 0.81.
We conjecture that temporal label models require substantially more LFs with higher inter-LF conflict rates; in the 3-LF low-conflict regime, majority vote is near-optimal.

\paragraph{Error analysis.}
Of 1{,}824 conf${\geq}$2 test windows, the probe misclassifies 131 (7.2\%).
False positives dominate: 85 errors (64.9\%) are windows where the agent executes diverse commands and file edits---appearing productive---but LF labels indicate stagnation.
The probe is highly confident on these errors (mean predicted probability 0.922; 73\% exceed 0.9), revealing a systematic blind spot rather than borderline uncertainty.
This pattern suggests semantically diverse stagnation that falls outside the coverage of three LFs.
LF conflict predicts errors: 39.7\% of misclassified windows have conflicting LF votes versus a 31\% base rate across all test data.
Errors also concentrate spatially: the top two trajectories account for 18.3\% of all errors, and four trajectories have 100\% per-trajectory error rates.
Resolved-task trajectories are nearly immune (1.3\% error rate vs.\ 7.4\% for unresolved), confirming that $\lambda_3$ correctly anchors labels on successful trajectories.

\paragraph{Outcome backpropagation as privileged distant supervision.}
A three-seed holdout experiment trains the probe without $\lambda_3$ on split-isolated data and evaluates against the full 3-LF conf${\geq}$2 test set.
Mean macro-F1 drops to $0.770{\pm}0.022$ from the baseline of 0.847---a $-7.7$\,pp deficit that quantifies $\lambda_3$'s unique contribution beyond $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$.
Removing $\lambda_3$ also reduces confident-label coverage by 33.5\% (from 15{,}401 to 10{,}243 conf${\geq}$2 windows), compounding signal loss with data quantity loss.
Where both 2-LF and 3-LF pipelines produce labels, agreement is 100\% (43{,}402 windows), confirming that $\lambda_3$ extends coverage without contradicting local LF signals.
The asymmetry between $\lambda_3$'s lagging temporal role (+6.7 steps, \S\ref{sec:lf_temporal}) and its high marginal contribution indicates that retrospective outcome information complements, rather than duplicates, the real-time signals of $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$.
