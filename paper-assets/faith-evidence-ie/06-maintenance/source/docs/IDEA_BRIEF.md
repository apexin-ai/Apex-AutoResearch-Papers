## Core Problem

LLM-based document-level IE systems have rapidly closed the extraction accuracy gap with encoder-based methods — DocKS-RAG achieves F1 63.59 on DocRED test (ICML 2025, LLM-enhanced hybrid prompt tuning with GNN + RAG), LMRC achieves F1 60.69 (LLaMA3.1-8B with relation pre-classifier), narrowing the gap to DREEAM's encoder SOTA of 67.53. However, no LLM-based DocRE method generates verifiable evidence to justify its extractions. Encoder-based evidence-aware methods (DREEAM Evi-F1 57.34, SAIS Evi-F1 55.67) use evidence annotations to guide internal attention but produce no interpretable evidence as output. LLM-based methods (DocKS-RAG F1 63.59, LMRC F1 60.69, EP-RSR, RelPrior F1 48.34, AutoRE F1 45.96) produce raw triples with zero provenance. This creates a critical interpretability gap: as LLM-based extraction approaches encoder-level accuracy, the absence of evidence provenance becomes the primary barrier to deployment in human-in-the-loop knowledge base population, biomedical IE requiring regulatory audit trails, and RAG pipelines needing traced provenance for compositional reasoning. The technical challenge is designing RL reward signals that train LLMs to generate faithful, discriminative evidence without sacrificing extraction accuracy — especially since naive NLI-based faithfulness rewards (as in RSAT) are gameable: models can cite any entity-mentioning sentence and receive positive entailment scores without selecting genuinely informative evidence.

## Opportunity / Why Now

Three converging trends create a timely opportunity: (1) GRPO with verifiable rewards is proven for IE (ProUIE achieves 71.17 F1 on RE tasks using stepwise structural rewards on Qwen3-4B; Extract-0 validates SFT+GRPO for document IE), (2) curriculum-based GRPO training significantly improves learning efficiency (RuCL achieves +7.83% avg improvement via rubric-stratified curriculum; VCRL shows variance-based difficulty scheduling yields +4.67% over baselines), and (3) standard IE benchmarks contain gold evidence annotations (DocRED evidence sentences) that all LLM-based methods completely ignore. The critical paradigm gap: encoder-based evidence-aware methods (DREEAM's KL-divergence attention guidance, SAIS's intermediate supervision, EIDER's inference-stage fusion) cannot transfer to generative LLMs; inference-time verification (AEVS's anchor-constrained provenance tracking) cannot improve extraction through training; and evidence-grounded RL methods demonstrate evidence-as-reward-signal viability but target free-form QA, not structured IE with schema constraints — EAPO achieves +3.9% over vanilla GRPO on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking (59.2% → 63.1%) using group-relative evidence rewards for long-context QA, CaRR uses citation-aware rubric rewards for deep search, and LongRLVR (ICLR 2026) proves that dense verifiable context rewards are essential for long-context grounding (boosting RULER-QA from 73.17 to 88.90 on a 14B model), formally showing that outcome-only rewards cause vanishing gradients for evidence selection. A key weakness of existing faithfulness rewards (RSAT's NLI-based R_faith) is that they only verify entailment of cited evidence — they cannot distinguish between truly informative evidence and superficially relevant distractors (entity-mentioning sentences that receive positive NLI scores despite not supporting the specific relation). In DocRE specifically, documents contain an average of 8 sentences (Yao et al., 2019), creating abundant distractors that share entities with correct extractions but do not actually evidence the relation. No existing work addresses this reward gameability problem for evidence-grounded IE.

CLATTER (Jun 2025) proposes decomposed entailment for hallucination detection but not as a reward. **The gap:** No work combines (a) contrastive evidence discrimination as a training-time reward signal with (b) curriculum-based progressive evidence difficulty for (c) LLM-based structured extraction on standard document-level IE benchmarks. The specific problem of reward gameability (entity-mentioning distractors receiving positive NLI scores) in evidence-grounded IE rewards is unaddressed. LongRLVR's theoretical analysis of vanishing gradients for evidence grounding further motivates dense, evidence-specific rewards (like CED) over sparse outcome-only signals.

## Proposed Approach

Fine-tune Qwen3-4B (matching ProUIE's backbone) with LoRA to jointly generate structured extractions AND supporting evidence spans in a unified output format — (head, relation, tail, [evidence_sentence_ids]) for DocRED. LoRA is essential: GitHub issue TRL #3456 documents OOM when running full-parameter Qwen3-4B GRPO on A100 80GB; LoRA with rank 64 + gradient checkpointing reduces trainable parameters to ~1.5% of total, making GRPO feasible on single A100. **Phase 1: SFT warmup** using gold evidence annotations from DocRED to teach joint extraction-plus-evidence format with non-thinking mode (enable_thinking=False) to avoid the documented Qwen3 GRPO speed regression. LoRA rank 64, learning rate 2e-5, max sequence length 2048. **Phase 2: GRPO with Evidence Curriculum** — three progressive stages inspired by RuCL's rubric stratification, each building on the previous: **Stage 2a (Extraction Foundation):** GRPO with extraction-only reward — stepwise structural F1 adapted from ProUIE's SFR. Builds reliable extraction capability before introducing evidence complexity. num_generations=4 (reduced from default 8 to fit A100 memory with LoRA GRPO). Transitions to Stage 2b when extraction F1 on held-out validation stabilizes (< 0.5% improvement over 2 consecutive checkpoints). **Stage 2b (Evidence Introduction):** Adds basic NLI faithfulness reward — DeBERTa-v3-base entailment of (cited_evidence, verbalized_fact), matching RSAT's proven approach. DeBERTa inference runs on CPU or a separate GPU to avoid competing for A100 VRAM with the policy model. Model learns to cite supporting evidence. Includes evidence parsimony penalty to discourage over-citation. Transitions to Stage 2c when faithfulness reward stabilizes. **Stage 2c (Contrastive Evidence Discrimination):** Replaces basic NLI with the full CED reward — the key technical contribution. CED addresses the reward gameability problem by requiring the model to select evidence that is MORE entailing than plausible document-internal alternatives. This is motivated by LongRLVR's formal analysis showing that outcome-only rewards (analogous to flat NLI) produce vanishing gradients for evidence grounding — CED provides a dense, discriminative gradient signal specifically targeting evidence quality. **CED Reward Mechanism:** For each generated (head, relation, tail, [evi_ids]): (1) Verbalize the extraction as a natural language claim c (e.g., 'Albert Einstein was born in Ulm'). (2) Compute positive entailment: p_pos = NLI_entail(concat(cited_evidence_sentences), c) using DeBERTa-v3-base. (3) Mine hard negatives from the source document: select sentences mentioning at least one of {head, tail} that are NOT in the cited evidence set; rank by entity overlap with the claim; take top-m (m=3) as hard negative set H. (4) Compute negative entailment: p_neg = max_{h ∈ H} NLI_entail(h, c). (5) CED reward R_CED = max(0, p_pos − p_neg) × I(p_pos > τ), where τ = 0.5 is a minimum faithfulness threshold. The margin (p_pos − p_neg) rewards discrimination: the model must select evidence that is substantially more entailing than the best distractor. The threshold I(p_pos > τ) ensures basic faithfulness. **Why CED is novel and necessary:** Unlike RSAT's flat entailment (which only checks p_pos), CED creates a discriminative signal against document-internal hard negatives. Unlike EAPO's group-relative scoring (which compares evidence quality across different rollouts), CED compares within the same document — directly addressing the distractor problem specific to document-level IE where multiple sentences share entities. Unlike LongRLVR's context rewards (which use gold evidence chunk IDs as verifiable signals), CED generates verifiable evidence rewards from NLI without requiring chunk-level gold labels during RL — using gold evidence only for SFT warmup and evaluation. Unlike CaRR's rubric decomposition (which decomposes questions), CED decomposes the evidence verification problem into positive vs. contrastive negative signals. **Composite GRPO reward in Stage 2c:** R = α·R_extraction(SFR) + β·R_CED + γ·R_parsimony + δ·R_schema, where R_schema is a lightweight dictionary lookup verifying relation ontology conformity (engineering complement, not a contribution). **Why evidence curriculum matters:** Starting with CED directly risks reward hacking — models might cite very specific (short) evidence to maximize the margin against distractors, sacrificing recall. The curriculum lets the model first learn what constitutes good evidence (Stage 2b), then learn to discriminate (Stage 2c). This is analogous to RuCL's insight that models should master foundational rubrics before tackling advanced ones. **F1 projection grounding and competitive positioning:** FREIGE does NOT aim to match DocKS-RAG's F1 63.59, which relies on GNN-based knowledge graph construction and sentence-level RAG infrastructure that FREIGE intentionally avoids. Instead, FREIGE's value proposition is providing evidence provenance as a new capability alongside competitive extraction accuracy. LMRC achieves test F1 60.69 with LLaMA3.1-8B using SFT + pre-classifier (no GRPO). FREIGE uses SFT + LoRA GRPO (no pre-classifier) on Qwen3-4B (smaller model). We target F1 53-60 on DocRED test: the SFT-only baseline should reach ~48-53 (Qwen3-4B with LoRA SFT, comparable to pure LLM methods without pre-classifiers); GRPO adds ~5-7 F1 points based on ProUIE's demonstrated GRPO gains on RE tasks. The publishability criterion is a dual threshold: (1) Evi-F1 significantly exceeding DREEAM's 57.34 (the encoder-based evidence SOTA), demonstrating that FREIGE produces higher-quality evidence than attention-guided encoder methods, AND (2) F1 > 53, demonstrating competitive extraction accuracy among generative LLM methods. Even at F1 ~55, FREIGE with Evi-F1 >> 57.34 would be the first LLM method on DocRED that jointly generates extractions and faithful evidence — a qualitatively new capability that no prior method at any F1 level provides. At F1 ~58-60, FREIGE would additionally approach LMRC's extraction accuracy while adding interpretable evidence that all prior LLM methods entirely lack. **Practical use-case grounding for evidence-generating extraction:** (1) Human-in-the-loop knowledge base population: IE practitioners report that qualitative validation is 'very tedious' because they must 'go back and see' extraction sources (Megagon Labs study, Li et al.); evidence-backed extractions eliminate this re-reading step. (2) Biomedical and legal IE: regulatory compliance requires audit trails linking extracted facts to source text; raw triples are insufficient for FDA submission support or legal discovery. (3) RAG pipeline provenance: downstream compositional reasoning over extracted KGs requires traced provenance to prevent hallucination cascading. **Failure mode:** If Qwen3-4B achieves F1 < 50 with CED, the paper pivots to an analysis contribution — examining whether evidence generation competes with extraction accuracy for model capacity, and whether curriculum staging mitigates this competition. This analysis would be the first empirical study of the extraction-evidence tradeoff in LLM-based DocRE. **Baselines:** (1) SFT-only (no GRPO), (2) GRPO with extraction-only reward, (3) GRPO with flat-NLI faithfulness reward (RSAT-style, no contrastive signal), (4) FREIGE with full CED reward. Compare extraction quality against DocKS-RAG (F1 63.59), LMRC (F1 60.69), and evidence quality against DREEAM (Evi-F1 57.34) and SAIS (Evi-F1 55.67). **Make-or-break experiment:** Controlled ablation of CED vs. flat-NLI reward: both should achieve similar basic faithfulness (p_pos), but CED should produce significantly higher evidence precision (fewer distractor citations) and Evi-F1. Report evidence distractor rate: fraction of cited evidence sentences that are entity-mentioning non-evidence. CED should reduce this substantially vs. flat-NLI. The dual publishability threshold: Evi-F1 > 57.34 (exceeding encoder-based evidence SOTA) AND F1 > 53 (competitive generative LLM extraction). **GPU memory strategy:** LoRA (rank 64) + gradient checkpointing on Qwen3-4B; num_generations=4 for GRPO rollouts; DeBERTa-v3-base for CED reward computed on CPU or separate GPU; vLLM for efficient rollout generation with gpu_memory_utilization=0.5. **Scoped to DocRED only** for the ARR sprint (Re-DocRED or WikiEvents deferred to camera-ready). Evaluate extraction accuracy (F1, Ign-F1), evidence quality (Evi-F1), and evidence discrimination (distractor citation rate). Model size ablation: Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-8B alongside primary Qwen3-4B.
