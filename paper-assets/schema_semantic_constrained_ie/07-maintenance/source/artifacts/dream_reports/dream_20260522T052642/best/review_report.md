## R1 Review: Methods and Experiments

### Soundness: 3/5

The violation taxonomy (DR, RV, ET, CF, and EE-specific EV/RoV/AC) is well-defined and covers the major modes of ontology non-compliance. The two-layer decomposition (grammar + semantic) is motivated by a clear decidability argument (Proposition 1), and the five-way ablation on SciERC is properly controlled. However, several methodological concerns reduce confidence:

(1) **Incomplete results presented as complete.** The 70B LoRA fine-tuned results in Section 5 (runtime paragraph) contain literal `XX.XX` placeholders -- these experiments are still running per the project status. The paper cannot be reviewed with placeholder data in the main text. This is a submission-readiness failure.

(2) **Predicted vs. actual data ambiguity.** The WikiEvents table (Table in Section 5.4) presents grammar-only rows for all three models, but only Mistral grammar-only was actually run as a registered experiment. The Qwen grammar-only (RoV=76.1%) and Llama grammar-only (RoV=77.0%) values are not clearly distinguished from actual experimental results, yet the experiment registry only contains `ee_mistral_wikievents_grammar_only_v3`. If these are predicted/interpolated, they must be marked.

(3) **Statistical testing is inconsistent.** Bootstrap CIs are reported for SciERC R-F1 ($p=0.58$) and DocRED R-F1 ($p<0.001$), but EE metric significance is only discussed qualitatively for entity F1 (floor-to-nonzero). The paper claims bootstrap 10,000 iterations in table captions but does not report CIs for RE cross-model results or the FT composability experiments. The EE FT composability paragraph acknowledges insufficient power ($n=20$) but still reports trigger F1 shifts without CIs.

(4) **DocRED 70B LoRA uses a 200-document subset** (per decision D082), but the 8B comparisons use the full 998-document set. This asymmetry is mentioned but not statistically justified -- no power analysis is provided to demonstrate that 200 documents suffice for the claimed comparisons.

(5) **Constraint tightness $\bar{\kappa}$ regression** has $n=4$ calibrated data points, $R^2=0.886$, $p=0.058$. The paper correctly notes this is descriptive, but it is presented as a meaningful correlation throughout the narrative (including the abstract's "characterized by constraint tightness"). Four data points cannot support a correlation claim.

### Excitement: 2.5/5

The core idea -- decomposing ontology constraints into a grammar layer (context-free) and a semantic layer (stateful masking) -- is sound engineering with clear theoretical grounding. The decidability analysis provides genuine insight into why grammar-constrained decoding alone is insufficient. However, the excitement is dampened by several factors: (a) the method introduces CF violations at rates of 10-21% in zero-shot and 14.9% even for Mistral under full S1, meaning the semantic layer creates a new violation category while eliminating others; (b) the strongest results (DR near-elimination under FT) exist in a regime where violations are already low (1-2%), raising questions about practical value beyond what post-hoc filtering achieves; (c) the zero-shot results, where constraint enforcement is most needed, show low absolute F1 (2-13% RE, 0-12% EE), making it hard to assess whether the method matters in practice; (d) post-hoc filtering achieves higher R-F1 in 5/6 zero-shot RE settings. The paper is a thorough empirical study with useful negative findings, but the mitigation story is incomplete.

### Reproducibility: 3.5/5

The paper provides detailed hyperparameters (LoRA config, learning rates, hardware specs), prompt templates (Appendix A), and seed variance analysis. The method description (grammar compilation via XGrammar, semantic layer with entity registry and continuation masking) is sufficiently detailed to reimplement. Code release is promised upon acceptance but not available. The greedy determinism verification (3 seeds, byte-identical outputs) is a strong reproducibility signal. However, the 70B experiments use AWQ INT4 quantization, which introduces a confound not present in the 8B experiments. WikiEvents EE FT uses only 20 test documents, making independent replication statistically unreliable.

### Strengths
- [S1] **Well-motivated architecture.** The decidability analysis (Proposition 1) cleanly separates context-free constraints (grammar) from prefix-decidable constraints (semantic), providing a principled reason for the two-layer design rather than an ad hoc engineering choice.
- [S2] **Comprehensive ablation.** The five-way ablation on SciERC (unconstrained/grammar/semantic/S1/S2) and four-way on DocRED, replicated across FT settings (Table in Appendix), systematically isolates each layer's contribution. The "latent violation surfacing" finding (grammar-only increases DR on DocRED from 18.46% to 26.17%) is a genuinely surprising and well-documented result.
- [S3] **Honest treatment of limitations.** The CF-DR trade-off ablation (Table in Appendix) directly demonstrates that eliminating CF re-routes violations to DR (+13.85pp), and the paper does not hide this fundamental tension. The dual-mechanism CF analysis (registry override vs. grammar-first fallback) provides actionable diagnostic information.
- [S4] **Cross-task generalization.** Extending from RE to EE on WikiEvents without method modification demonstrates that the framework generalizes beyond its original design scope, covering 245 constraints across 56 event types.
- [S5] **Practical latency overhead.** The 10-13% overhead under greedy and 1.54x under beam search ($k=4$) is modest and well-characterized, including scaling benchmarks up to 200 entity types and 1,000 relations.

### Weaknesses
- [W1] **Incomplete 70B LoRA results.** The paper contains `XX.XX` placeholders in Section 5 for 70B LoRA fine-tuned results (SciERC, DocRED 200-doc subset). These experiments are listed as "running" in the project status. A paper submitted with placeholder data in the main text is not ready for review. This undermines the "scale-independent composability" claim, which currently rests on zero-shot 70B results only.
- [W2] **CF violation is a serious method limitation inadequately addressed.** CF rises from 0-0.24% (unconstrained) to 6.56-12.02% (S1) for calibrated models in zero-shot RE, and to 14.9-21.1% in EE. The semantic layer introduces more type inconsistencies than it eliminates in some configurations. The 15-case error analysis (Section 5.9 equivalent, the case studies in Appendix) is insufficient for a phenomenon affecting 10-21% of outputs. The paper frames this as an "inherent trade-off," but an alternative interpretation is that per-relation independent enforcement is fundamentally flawed for tasks with high entity reuse.
- [W3] **Missing prompt-based mitigation experiments.** The introduction claims schema-informed prompting "reduces violations by 62% on average but is per-relation unreliable," citing a single comparison (Table in Appendix, 2 datasets, 1 model). This is presented as a contribution (positioning against prompting) but not experimentally evaluated with controlled prompt variations. The schema-informed prompt experiment uses a single prompt design; no ablation over prompt strategies (e.g., few-shot demonstrations of valid type pairs, chain-of-thought type checking) is provided.
- [W4] **No structured IE baseline comparison.** GoLLIE is discussed in Related Work (achieving "zero DR violations" on SciERC via Python dataclass format) but dismissed due to "severe coverage cost" (R-F1=0.0). This is not a fair comparison: GoLLIE was designed for different evaluation protocols. KnowCoder-7B's supervised R-F1=40.0 is cited but not experimentally reproduced. No code-generation IE system (GoLLIE, KnowCoder, CodeIE) is included as an experimental baseline, despite the paper claiming to address the same problem (schema compliance in LLM-based IE).
- [W5] **Zero-shot F1 is too low for meaningful extraction assessment.** RE R-F1 of 2-13% and EE entity/trigger F1 of 0-12% are near-random. The paper frames F1 as a "control variable" (Appendix discussion), but when baseline performance is near zero, any delta is within noise. The entity F1 improvement from 0% to 11-17% in EE is format compliance recovery (the parser can now read the output), not extraction improvement. The paper's main claim that "constraints compose without degrading performance" is trivially true when there is almost no performance to degrade.

### Questions for Authors
- [Q1] When will the 70B LoRA fine-tuned results (currently `XX.XX` placeholders) be available, and what is the plan if they contradict the predicted "scale-neutral" pattern? The review_context.yaml predicts DR=0.05% and R-F1 delta=+0.1pp for SciERC, but the 70B model uses AWQ INT4 quantization which could introduce logit distortions affecting semantic masking.
- [Q2] The WikiEvents grammar-only results for Qwen (RoV=76.1%) and Llama (RoV=77.0%) -- are these from actual experiments or predicted/interpolated from the Mistral grammar-only result? The experiment registry only contains `ee_mistral_wikievents_grammar_only_v3`. If predicted, this must be disclosed.
- [Q3] CF increases from grammar-only (1.1%) to full S1 (14.9%) on Mistral WikiEvents. This means the semantic layer, the paper's core contribution, *introduces* 13.8pp of CF violations. How do you reconcile this with the claim that the semantic layer is "necessary" for EE? Could a grammar-only + post-hoc filtering approach achieve better total violation rates?

### Missing Experiments
- [M1] **Few-shot evaluation.** The paper evaluates only zero-shot and fully fine-tuned regimes. Few-shot (1-5 examples) is the most common deployment scenario for LLM-based IE and would bridge the gap between the near-zero ZS F1 and the saturated FT F1, providing the most informative signal about constraint value.
- [M2] **8B FT RE constrained results for all models.** The FT composability table (Table 3 equivalent) shows Qwen and Llama on SciERC/DocRED, but Mistral is entirely absent from the FT experiments. Given that Mistral is the "boundary condition" model with the most interesting constraint behavior (56.87% CF unconstrained -> 1.27% S1), its absence from FT evaluation is a significant gap.

### Overall Assessment: 2.5/5

The paper presents a well-engineered two-layer constrained decoding framework with a clean decidability motivation and thorough ablation. However, it is not submission-ready: the 70B LoRA results contain literal XX.XX placeholders, the WikiEvents grammar-only data for two of three models may be predicted rather than actual, and the CF violation trade-off (up to 21.1%) undermines the core mitigation claim. The zero-shot evaluation regime, where the method is most needed, operates at near-zero F1 where improvements are hard to interpret meaningfully. Post-hoc filtering outperforms constrained decoding on R-F1 in most zero-shot settings. Missing few-shot experiments and structured IE baselines further limit the paper's positioning. With complete results and a more honest framing of the CF limitation as a method failure rather than an inherent trade-off, this could reach Findings quality in a future cycle.
