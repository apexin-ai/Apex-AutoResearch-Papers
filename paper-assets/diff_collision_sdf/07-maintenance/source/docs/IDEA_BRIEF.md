# Seed 2: Implicit Spatial Occupancy Field-Guided Collision-Free Robot VLA

Objective: CoRL 2026 submission.

Direction: Existing VLAs lack continuous modeling capability for 3D spatial occupancy states and cannot achieve 3D spatial collision awareness while generating language-aligned actions. OccVLA (arXiv 2509.05578, September 2025) integrates 3D occupancy prediction as an implicit supervision signal into VLA, but is entirely oriented toward autonomous driving trajectory planning — the action space, sensor configuration, and task structure are fundamentally different from robot manipulation. VLSA/AEGIS (arXiv 2512.11891, December 2025) achieves VLA collision avoidance through control barrier functions, but as a plug-in safety constraint layer — the safety mechanism is independent of VLA's spatial understanding and does not affect VLA's internal spatial representation learning. Spatial Forcing (ICLR 2026) proves implicit 3D alignment can significantly improve VLA spatial reasoning, but does not produce explicit occupancy field representations for collision reasoning. CSSDF-Net (arXiv 2603.18669, March 2026) demonstrates the effectiveness of neural implicit distance fields in collision-free motion planning, but belongs to traditional motion planning and is completely unrelated to VLA. Core gap: No work integrates implicit occupancy fields as VLA internal spatial representations for collision-free action generation in manipulation tasks. We propose deeply coupling implicit spatial occupancy fields with VLA, reconstructing continuous implicit occupancy fields from RGB-D data, integrating them into VLA action policy networks, enabling language-conditioned safe collision-free manipulation.

## Core Problem

Despite a proliferation of 3D-aware VLA methods (DepthVLA, QDepth-VLA, GeoAware-VLA, SpatialVLA, Evo-0, StemVLA, SA-VLA, ST-VLA, 3DS-VLA, Spatial Forcing, GST-VLA, 3D-CAVLA, AugVLA-3D, Evo-Depth) that improve general spatial reasoning through depth, geometry, or point cloud representations, none explicitly targets collision avoidance in cluttered manipulation. These methods optimize for task success rate, viewpoint generalization, or grasping precision — not collision metrics. Meanwhile, the only collision-safe VLAs (AEGIS/VLSA, CoFreeVLA, SafeVLA) achieve safety via post-hoc mechanisms — control barrier functions that externally correct trajectories, risk estimators that halt unsafe actions, or reward shaping via constrained MDPs — without improving the model's internal collision geometry understanding. The result: 3D-aware VLAs still collide in cluttered scenes, and collision-safe VLAs don't internalize spatial understanding. No existing work provides a differentiable collision-aware mechanism that operates within the VLA forward pass to jointly generate actions and reason about collision geometry.

## Opportunity / Why Now

The disjunction between 'spatially-aware VLAs' and 'collision-safe VLAs' creates a precise research gap at their intersection. Three observations motivate our approach: (1) Depth-as-auxiliary-supervision (QDepth-VLA) provides camera-frame 2.5D information — 'how far is each pixel from the camera' — which improves spatial reasoning but does not directly encode collision geometry, and its gradients live in image space requiring implicit perspective-to-workspace transformation. (2) Post-hoc safety methods (AEGIS CBF, CoFreeVLA risk estimation) correct or halt trajectories at inference but leave the VLA's internal representations collision-ignorant, creating a disconnect between the model's reasoning and the safety layer. (3) Workspace signed distance fields (SDFs) provide a fundamentally different and collision-native signal: 'how close is each 3D point to the nearest obstacle surface,' with gradients pointing directly toward collision-free directions in workspace frame — no coordinate transformation needed. Crucially, SDF gradients are differentiable and can flow back into action token generation during the forward pass, enabling collision-aware action generation rather than post-hoc correction. SDF is the native representation for collision checking in robotics planning (iSDF, CSSDF-Net, SAMP, ReDSDF, Neural NMPC-SDF), and recent work (SAMP, Prof. Robot) demonstrates that differentiable SDF gradients can be integrated into learned policies for collision reduction — but none of these are VLA methods. The 'why now': (a) SafeLIBERO (Dec 2025) provides the first collision-specific VLA benchmark with 32 scenarios and 1600 episodes, yet only AEGIS has been evaluated there; (b) MuJoCo 3+ natively supports SDF computation from meshes, reducing implementation barriers; (c) DFM-VLA (Mar 2026) demonstrates that iterative action refinement within VLA is feasible and effective — our SDF-gradient refinement provides a collision-specific instantiation of this paradigm.

## Proposed Approach

We propose ColliForce-VLA, whose primary contribution is a differentiable collision-aware action generation mechanism for VLAs, enabled by a learned workspace SDF that provides gradient-based collision guidance within the forward pass — not merely auxiliary supervision during training and not post-hoc trajectory correction. This is architecturally distinct from both QDepth-VLA (training-only auxiliary loss) and AEGIS (external CBF correction). Key insight: While depth auxiliary tasks (QDepth-VLA) teach the model 'how far each pixel is from the camera' with gradients in image space, our learned SDF predicts 'how close each 3D workspace point is to the nearest obstacle surface' with gradients in workspace frame — and crucially, these SDF gradients can flow back through the action generation pathway during the forward pass, directly modulating action tokens toward collision-free configurations. Architecture — three components with distinct roles: (1) SDF Decoder with Action-Gradient Coupling: A cross-attention decoder takes intermediate VLA backbone features (from π0 via openpi or OpenVLA-OFT) and predicts signed distance at sparse 3D query points (2048 points, importance-sampled near robot workspace and object surfaces). Ground-truth SDF is extracted from LIBERO/SafeLIBERO MuJoCo visual geometry meshes (OBJ/STL assets, not simplified collision convex hulls) using established mesh-to-SDF libraries (trimesh proximity queries, pysdf). Critical design choice: the SDF decoder shares intermediate representations with the action head via a bidirectional cross-attention bridge — SDF features inform action generation, and action token positions define where SDF is most needed. This creates an architectural coupling absent in QDepth-VLA's purely auxiliary decoder. (2) SDF-Gradient-Guided Action Refinement (PRIMARY NOVELTY): During the forward pass, predicted SDF values at candidate end-effector positions (derived from action tokens) are used to compute workspace-frame collision gradients via backpropagation through the SDF decoder. These gradients are projected back into the action token space through a learned linear projection, producing a collision-avoidance correction that is added to the action tokens before the final action head output. This is a single differentiable forward pass — not iterative post-hoc correction. The mechanism is end-to-end trainable: the SDF decoder learns to produce gradients that, when projected into action space, reduce collisions. Theoretical basis: ∇SDF at a point in workspace directly points away from the nearest obstacle surface (by definition of SDF), providing geometrically meaningful collision-avoidance direction. In contrast, depth gradients in camera frame encode ∂depth/∂pixel, which requires an implicit perspective-to-workspace coordinate transformation (involving camera intrinsics, extrinsics, and nonlinear projection) to yield collision-relevant workspace directions — this transformation degrades gradient quality, especially for oblique viewing angles and occluded regions. (3) SDF Auxiliary Loss (SECONDARY): Standard SDF prediction loss during training forces the backbone to internalize full 3D collision geometry including occluded regions. This provides the representational foundation for component (2) but is explicitly secondary — the key contribution is how SDF gradients modulate action generation, not the auxiliary loss itself. Training: Joint optimization of action prediction loss, SDF prediction loss, and an SDF-gradient alignment loss that encourages the gradient correction to reduce predicted collision probability. The gradient alignment loss is key: it ensures the learned SDF-to-action-space projection produces corrections that actually improve collision avoidance, not just minimize SDF prediction error. Evaluation strategy — two tiers: Tier 1 (Standard): SafeLIBERO (32 collision scenarios, 1600 episodes) measuring both collision avoidance rate (CAR) and task success rate (TSR), plus standard LIBERO suites to verify no regression. Tier 2 (Stress Tests — isolating SDF advantages over depth): We design supplementary scenarios in MuJoCo/LIBERO that specifically test SDF's claimed advantages: (a) Occluded-obstacle scenarios: obstacles placed behind the robot arm invisible to the single camera — depth cannot perceive them, SDF from 3D workspace knowledge can; (b) Dense-corridor scenarios: end-effector must navigate between closely packed objects where depth map gradients blur across multiple surfaces while SDF encodes distance to ALL nearby surfaces simultaneously; (c) Multi-angle proximity: scenarios where the camera viewing angle creates depth ambiguities (nearly parallel surfaces at similar depth) that SDF naturally resolves. These stress tests are designed so that if SDF wins convincingly on them — even with marginal gains on standard SafeLIBERO — the paper tells a compelling story about when and why SDF matters. Critical baselines: (1) QDepth-VLA (open-source GitHub) — depth-as-auxiliary-supervision, our closest methodological comparator and primary depth baseline; (2) AEGIS/VLSA — post-hoc CBF approach, current SOTA on SafeLIBERO; (3) Vanilla VLA (OpenVLA-OFT, π0) — no spatial auxiliary supervision; (4) Spatial Forcing — general 3D alignment; (5) DepthVLA — if code becomes available, otherwise secondary to QDepth-VLA. Key ablations: (A1) SDF auxiliary supervision only (no gradient-guided action generation) — isolates training-time benefit, directly comparable to 'QDepth-VLA with SDF target swap'; (A2) SDF-gradient-guided action generation only (no auxiliary loss during training, decoder trained separately then frozen) — isolates inference-time mechanism; (A3) Full ColliForce-VLA (both) — demonstrates synergy; (A4) SDF vs. depth vs. binary occupancy as auxiliary target, all with gradient-guided action generation — tests whether SDF gradients are specifically better than depth/occupancy gradients for collision avoidance; (A5) Ablation on SDF ground-truth source: visual mesh vs. collision convex hull — validates our design choice. Advantages of SDF-gradient-guided action generation over alternatives: (a) Workspace-frame gradients directly aligned with robot motion — no coordinate transformation needed; (b) Full 3D coverage including occluded regions; (c) Continuous, geometrically meaningful gradients — ∇SDF points away from obstacles by construction; binary occupancy has zero gradient almost everywhere; (d) Single forward pass — no iterative refinement or external correction loop; (e) End-to-end trainable — the entire pathway from SDF prediction to action correction is differentiable.
