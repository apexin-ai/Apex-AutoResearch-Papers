\begin{abstract}
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models report high Collision Avoidance Rate (CAR) on safety benchmarks, yet we show these metrics are contaminated by action collapse, where near-zero motor output produces zero collisions through inaction rather than avoidance. We introduce displacement-filtered CAR (dfCAR), a zero-overhead sanity check applicable to any simulation-based VLA safety evaluation, revealing that over 99\% of safe episodes in Pi0-family models are action-collapse artifacts. We further show that end-effector-only Signed Distance Field (SDF) supervision fails to deliver collision avoidance: ground-truth oracle refinement reduces CAR below unrefined baselines by redistributing collisions to unmonitored arm links, and SDF training supervision shows no statistically significant benefit beyond collision-free demonstrations alone, a null result robust across three training seeds and five evaluation seeds. These findings suggest that collision avoidance primarily arises from demonstration data rather than EE-only geometric supervision.
\end{abstract}
