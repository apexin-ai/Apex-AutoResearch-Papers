\section{EE-Only SDF Refinement is Counterproductive Regardless of Policy Capability}\label{sec:ee_only}

Could a perfect SDF signal enable effective EE-only refinement? We replace the learned SDF with ground-truth distance fields from the simulation geometry (\S\ref{sec:oracle_setup}). Table~\ref{tab:oracle} and Fig.~\ref{fig:scatter_and_oracle} (right) show that all three oracle variants achieve \emph{lower} CAR than unrefined baselines: the best oracle (76.9\%) falls 5.1 points below LoRA (82.0\%). Refinement with perfect distance information produces more collisions, not fewer.

\paragraph{Regime~1: Action-collapsed baselines (Pi0).}
The three variants apply the ground-truth SDF gradient differently (distance-adaptive, fixed, or feature-space; Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_oracle}). Per-task analysis (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_pertask_oracle}) reveals the mechanism: on the one task where the EE is the primary collision body, refinement helps (+9\,pp); on the remaining three, the gradient pushes the EE away but rotates intermediate links into obstacles. On \texttt{orange\_juice} (LoRA CAR\,=\,96\%), Oracle-adaptive reduces CAR to 82.5\%. Feature-space application yields no advantage, confirming the failure is geometric. The A3 learned SDF never triggers refinement (0 steps per episode).

\paragraph{Regime~2: Non-collapsed baseline (Pi0.5 LoRA).}
One might attribute oracle failure to the action-collapsed Pi0 baselines: if the policy is already frozen, refinement cannot improve it. We test this alternative by applying Oracle-adaptive to the non-collapsed Pi0.5 LoRA baseline (0\% action-collapse, TSR\,=\,46.0\%). Oracle refinement reduces CAR from 57.0\% to 51.9\% ($-$5.1\,pp) while maintaining 0\% action-collapse and TSR\,=\,50.6\%, confirming active manipulation throughout (Table~\ref{tab:oracle}). The two-regime result closes the confound: EE-only SDF refinement is counterproductive regardless of whether the base policy is frozen (Regime~1) or actively executing (Regime~2).
