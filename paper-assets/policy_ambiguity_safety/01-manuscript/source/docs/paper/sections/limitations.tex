\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Our ambiguity pairs are synthetically constructed, so the 34.0\,pp effect is an upper bound on the causal contribution of ambiguity; the SOPBench evaluation ($+$12.7\,pp unweighted mean) provides a cross-benchmark lower bound under naturally occurring ambiguity. We evaluate seven frontier-class models ($\geq$100B parameters or equivalent capability tier) across two customer-service domains at temperature $= 0$; a healthcare pilot ($+$58.8\,pp; Appendix~\ref{app:healthcare}) provides pilot-scale evidence for generalization beyond customer service, and a temperature $= 0.7$ pilot shows 85--97\% clause-level agreement with greedy decoding (Appendix~\ref{app:temperature}), though smaller models and extreme-temperature regimes remain untested. Failure mode coding relies on LLM annotators ($\kappa = 0.52$; human collapsed $\kappa = 0.41$); per-type judge agreement varies (Appendix~\ref{app:per_type_agreement}). Authorization scope exhibits the widest cross-model range (12--64\%) and the lowest judge agreement ($\kappa = 0.385$); coreferential has the smallest effect ($+$18.7\,pp) with below-moderate $\kappa$ ($0.479$, $n = 34$). Conclusions about these two types carry wider uncertainty than pooled estimates.
