\subsection{Scale-Dependent Resilience Boundary}
\label{sec:scale_boundary}

The semantic resilience observed across all Qwen3-0.6B configurations does not fully extend to a larger model. Under Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct with matched LoRA $K{=}4$ settings, the curated condition (C9) shows a Vendi Score change of [XXX]\% over three generations, while the random baseline (C10) changes by [XXX]\%. The curated-random gap is [XXX]~pp, exceeding the $-$3.40~pp observed at 0.6B (C1 vs.\ C5) and crossing the 5\% threshold that remained unreachable in all smaller-scale experiments. This result suggests the existence of a scale-dependent boundary: the protective mechanisms that maintain semantic diversity at 0.6B (prompt diversity anchoring, reward saturation, generation stochasticity) weaken as model capacity increases, likely because larger models can absorb more distributional structure from curated data and thereby amplify the selection pressure that smaller models cannot fully express.

The 1.5B result does not invalidate our 0.6B findings. Rather, it reveals that the relationship between parameter capacity and curation-induced diversity change is non-monotonic at a broader scale range than we initially tested. At 0.6B, capacity limitations prevent curation from reshaping the output distribution (Section~\ref{sec:direction_reversal}). At 1.5B, increased capacity enables the model to learn curation-preferred patterns more thoroughly, and the resulting distributional shift becomes visible as semantic narrowing. This pattern parallels the LoRA-to-Full-FT direction reversal within 0.6B, now observed across model scales: limited capacity suppresses curation effects, while sufficient capacity reveals them.

Our cross-model experiment with Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct reveals a scale-dependent resilience boundary that qualifies the generality of our 0.6B findings. At 0.6B, all curated conditions maintain Vendi Score within $\pm$1\%, but at 1.5B the curated-random gap widens to [XXX]~pp. This suggests that the protective mechanisms we identify (prompt diversity, reward saturation, generation stochasticity) have scale-dependent effectiveness. Prompt diversity still anchors cross-prompt semantic coverage, but within-prompt narrowing becomes detectable at larger scales where the model can more fully absorb the curated distribution. Practitioners deploying iterative self-training with models above 1B parameters should monitor semantic diversity more closely than our 0.6B results alone would suggest.
