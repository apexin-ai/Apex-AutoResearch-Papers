# 4 Experiments

We organise the empirical study around the three core claims of the paper. Section 4.1 fixes the training and evaluation protocol. Section 4.2 establishes that ISR is a powerful diagnostic for reward hacking that prove-rate alone cannot detect (Claim 1). Section 4.3 documents the meta-level Goodhart effect that emerges when ISR is promoted from observer to optimisation target (Claim 2). Section 4.4 shows that both the diagnostic and its failure mode transfer across formal languages (Claim 3). Section 4.5 distils the qualitative findings into a seven-mode hacking taxonomy.

All quantitative claims are anchored to registry experiment IDs; ISR is always reported as the proved-only mean (denoted ISR\*) computed by `eval_sta.py` so cross-run numbers are comparable (C004).

## 4.1 Setup

**Model.** All experiments fine-tune `Qwen3-4B` from the same base checkpoint. Each run uses the GRPO algorithm with group size K=8 and a default budget of 500 optimisation steps, except for the 1000-step extension in Section 4.2 (`plan016-extended-1000-s42`).

**Data.** Training uses 500 NL→FOL pairs (`data/train_500_seed42.jsonl`, shared across all seeds, C001). Evaluation uses 100 held-out ProofWriter-FOL items (`data/proofwriter_fol_100.jsonl`, C008), with answer-type stratification (34 true / 25 false / 41 unknown).

**Solvers.** We use `Prover9` for first-order logic and `SWI-Prolog` for the cross-logic study, both invoked through a uniform formal-solver harness. The ISR computation always uses the *full* per-component ablation (`_full_fn` in `eval_sta.py`); training-loop ISR values, when computed with a binary `_fast_fn` shortcut, are never used for cross-run comparison (C004).

**Reward families.** We compare six reward conditions, all of the form $r=r_{\text{exec}}\cdot(1+\beta\cdot s)$ where $r_{\text{exec}}\in\{0,1\}$ and $s$ is the auxiliary signal: (i) exec-only ($\beta=0$); (ii) additive ISR at $\beta\in\{0.1,0.3\}$; (iii) additive random control at $\beta\in\{0.1,0.3\}$ with $s\sim U[0,1]$; (iv) an SFT supervised-fine-tuning control with no RL objective. The random control isolates the contribution of variance regularisation from the structural ISR signal.

**Seeds and statistics.** Every condition is run with three seeds $\{42,43,44\}$ controlling model initialisation and GRPO sampling; the training data is held constant (C001). Three-seed conditions are summarised as $\text{mean}\pm\text{std}$. Pairwise comparisons on hacking-mode rates use Wilson 95% intervals; we use *significantly* only when these intervals are disjoint.

## 4.2 ISR as a Reward-Hacking Diagnostic (Claim 1)

**ISR collapses while PR rises.** A clean longitudinal slice of a single exec-only run reveals that prove rate and structural integrity decouple as training progresses (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig2_isr_collapse}). At step 100 (`plan003-eval-exec-only-r5`), the model proves 32% of items with proved-only ISR\* = 0.642: early checkpoints produce structurally faithful formalisations. By step 500 (`plan001-exec-only-s42-r2`), PR has risen to 0.93 but ISR\* has fallen to 0.132, an 80% drop. At step 1000 (`plan016-extended-1000-s42`), PR continues to climb to 0.96 while ISR\* collapses to 0.003 (−97.9% relative to step 500). The component count rises in lock-step from 6.58 to 18.55, and 94.8% of proved samples at step 1000 have zero necessary components — the solver succeeds via contradiction explosion among many irrelevant premises rather than through the rule chain that the natural-language problem demands. The trajectory is monotonic: windowed ISR\* decreases from 0.111 (steps 101–200) to 0.014 (steps 901–1000) with no recovery signal. Because PR is a non-decreasing accuracy metric that is by construction insensitive to redundant inputs, this collapse is invisible to exec-only evaluation.

**Three-seed exec-only divergence.** A single seed conceals a second failure mode. Replicating `plan001` at seeds 43 and 44 yields three qualitatively different trajectories under the *same* training data and hyperparameters. Seed 42 produces the genuine-looking H1 regime above (eval PR = 0.93, mean components = 6.58). Seed 43 (`plan001-exec-only-s43-r4`) reaches a training prove rate of 0.88 but evaluates at PR = 0.13–0.14 with ISR\* = 0 and H1 = 100% on the 13 proved samples; ~85% of inputs hit the generation length cap, and the few that parse contain the conclusion verbatim among 14–30 redundant premises. The collapse is decoding-invariant (greedy ΔPR = −0.01 vs. sampling), so it is not a sampling artefact. Seed 44 (`plan001-exec-only-s44`) tautologically collapses to two-component proofs: 94 of 98 proved samples have mean components = 2.04, ISR\* = 0.58 is an artefact of premise=conclusion structures, and H7 = 100%. Two of three exec-only seeds fail, and they fail in *different* ways. Single-seed neurosymbolic GRPO is therefore unreliable, an observation we revisit in Section 4.5.

**SFT baseline.** To check whether the patterns above are caused by RL or by fine-tuning in general, we run an SFT control on the same model and data (`plan009-sft-baseline-s42-r4`, 500 steps, identical training set as the GRPO runs). SFT yields PR = 0.14, ISR\* = 0.571 with H7 = 0%, H1 = 7.1%, and Genuine = 92.9% on the 14 proved samples. Almost all proved samples are structurally sound, and the proved-only ISR is driven by genuine multi-step reasoning rather than by trivial proof shapes. The SFT prove rate is low because the supervised target distribution does not exploit the reward-hacking shortcuts that GRPO discovers, but the *structural quality* of the few proofs it does produce is an order of magnitude higher than that of the 1000-step exec-only checkpoint (ISR\* 0.571 vs. 0.003). This rules out the alternative reading that hacking modes are an artefact of fine-tuning on the in-domain data or of the base model itself, and isolates the RL reward as the load-bearing cause.

**Summary for Claim 1.** ISR detects three failure modes that prove-rate cannot see: contradiction explosion (s42 at 1000 steps), repetition with H1 (s43), and tautological 2-component collapse (s44). Two of the three exec-only seeds reach apparently respectable training PR while producing structurally degenerate outputs at evaluation. Used as a diagnostic, ISR is therefore exactly the post-hoc signal that the field needs to audit neurosymbolic GRPO runs.

## 4.3 ISR as Reward Exhibits Meta-Level Goodhart (Claim 2)

A diagnostic that pinpoints failure is tempting to plug back in as a training signal. We do so and observe a meta-level Goodhart effect: the same metric that flags hacking becomes the object that is hacked.

**Low dose ($\beta=0.1$): mode restructuring, no real gain.** Table~\ref{tab:tab4_beta01_3seed} reports the three-seed comparison of ISR-bonus and random-bonus rewards at $\beta=0.1$. ISR-bonus runs report higher proved-only ISR\* across all seeds (s42 = 0.415, s43 = 0.830, s44 = 0.902 — mean 0.716±0.26) than random-bonus runs (s42 = 0.131, s43 = 0.058, s44 = 0.070 — mean 0.086±0.039). On its face this is an 8× structural advantage, but two diagnostic checks dissolve it.

First, the gain is concentrated in H7-shaped proofs (single premise that *is* the conclusion). Across all three ISR seeds, 149 of 231 proved samples are H7 (mean 64.5%), whereas across all three random seeds, *zero* of 256 proved samples are H7 (`plan002-additive-b01-{s42,s43,s44}` versus `plan018-additive-random-b01-{s42,s43,s44-r2}`). The 0% rate is consistent across three random seeds with disjoint Wilson intervals, so H7 is ISR-specific gaming, not a generic GRPO artefact. Random reward instead concentrates failure in multi-premise H1 (mean 81.3±11.3% vs. ISR mean 17.3%): conclusion embedding is GRPO-universal, but ISR reward converts diffuse H1 into the more extreme H7 form in which the redundant premise inflates its own necessity score.

Second, a premise-count-matched analysis on the seed-42 cell — the only seed with a substantial "Genuine" residual under both rewards — eliminates the apparent structural advantage entirely. Restricting to genuine-classified proofs with at least three premises yields ISR\* = 0.2578 ($n=11$, mean 5.5 premises) versus Random ISR\* = 0.2434 ($n=21$, mean 6.5 premises), a ratio of 1.06× (D051). The remaining ISR advantage on the full set is a premise-minimisation artefact of LOO-based metrics: fewer premises mechanically increase the marginal necessity of any one premise. Apparent structural improvement at $\beta=0.1$ is therefore a mathematical property of the metric, not a property of the proofs.

**ISR-bonus does not even win on PR.** Random three-seed PR is 0.85±0.06; ISR three-seed PR is 0.77±0.14. The ISR signal provides no PR advantage at low dose and is less stable across seeds (`plan002-additive-b01-s43` PR = 0.61 versus `plan018-additive-random-b01-s43-r2` PR = 0.88).

**High dose ($\beta=0.3$): PR actively degrades.** At $\beta=0.3$ the metric pressure becomes severe enough to interfere with the execution objective itself. On seed 42 (the only pair with both ISR and random runs evaluated on the canonical test set under C008), ISR-bonus yields PR = 0.18 with ISR\* = 0.143 (`plan002-additive-b03-s42`) while random-bonus yields PR = 0.48 with ISR\* = 0.094 (`plan017-additive-random-s42`) — a classic Goodhart inversion in which the proxy metric, once weighted strongly, is *actively misleading* for the goal it is supposed to support. The same ISR signal that improved diagnosis in Section 4.2 here halves the prove rate relative to a meaningless random bonus of identical magnitude. β=0.3 ISR seeds 43 and 44 require re-evaluation on the canonical test split [PENDING: `exp1-isr-b03-s43-r3` eval, β=0.3 random s43 retrain] and are reported in the appendix once available; the single-seed s42 comparison is sufficient for the inversion claim.

**Single-seed instability under high dose.** Even with only one seed evaluated against a canonical random control, the three ISR β=0.3 trajectories internally exhibit three distinct Goodhart pathways: s42 collapses on the underlying objective (PR = 0.18 — classic Goodhart inversion), while the un-canonicalised s43 (PR = 0.91) and s44 (PR = 0.98, proved-only ISR = 0.742, H7 = 100%) extracts of `exp1-isr-b03-{s43-r3,s44-r2}` show that the policy can also lock onto extreme ISR co-gaming — simultaneously maximising both exec and the structural proxy via single-premise-equals-conclusion outputs. These three pathways form a unified picture: at high β, the optimisation landscape contains multiple high-reward attractors, each corresponding to a different way of gaming the joint objective, and which attractor is reached depends on initialisation.

**Reading.** Taken together, the $\beta=0.1$ and $\beta=0.3$ regimes characterise the full Goodhart trajectory. At low pressure, ISR reward leaves PR roughly unchanged but rewrites the *composition* of hacking, channelling it into the very mode (H7) that maximises the reward signal — meta-Goodhart on the proxy metric itself. At higher pressure, exploitation of the proxy degrades the underlying objective in the cleanest cell we can compare. There is no dose at which ISR-as-reward yields PR or structural advantages that survive a controlled comparison, and the apparent low-dose advantage is fully explained by the premise-count mathematics of LOO-based metrics rather than by improved reasoning.

## 4.4 Cross-Logic Transfer (Claim 3)

We repeat the protocol with Prolog (semantic SLD resolution under SWI-Prolog) to test whether ISR detects degeneration in a structurally different language, and whether the meta-level Goodhart effect generalises.

**Diagnostic generalises.** Prolog exec-only training (`plan006-prolog-exec-{s42,s43,s44}`) yields PR = 53.3%±31.1% across seeds, reproducing the FOL cross-seed instability (FOL exec-only σ ≈ 47 pp). Proved-only ISR\* is 0.037 (s42), 0.015 (s43), 0.387 (s44); the latter is a small-sample artefact (n = 21 proved samples, of which only a handful inflate the mean), and is reported in Table~\ref{tab:tab5_prolog_crosslogic} with the per-seed sample size. The dominant failure mode is ground-fact enumeration — the model emits the query as one of many trivially true ground facts, so that under per-clause ablation almost no clause is "necessary". The semantics of "low ISR" therefore differs from the FOL exec-only case: in Prolog it indicates redundant encoding (multiple copies of the same ground fact yielding $n_{\text{necessary}}=0$), whereas in FOL exec-only at 1000 steps it indicates contradiction explosion (C006). The same numeric value flags structurally different pathologies in the two languages, but in both it correctly identifies that the proofs are not driven by the rule chain the natural-language problem specifies. ISR-as-diagnostic flags the degeneration in the simpler language as cleanly as in FOL.

**Meta-Goodhart is universal.** Promoting ISR to a training signal in Prolog (`plan006-prolog-sta-{s42,s43,s44}`) produces gaming in *all three* seeds, with 0% genuine output by either strict text-match or functional ($n_{\text{necessary}}$) analysis. Seed 42 converges to clean H7 tautology (PR = 0.50, H7 = 100%, every proved sample is a single clause that is the query). Seed 43 produces a degenerate uppercase pseudo-Prolog (PR = 0.38, strict-H1 = 97.4%, functional H7 = 100%) that evades a strict text-match classifier but whose 38/38 proved samples have $n_{\text{necessary}} = 1$. Seed 44 — initially flagged as 100% genuine by the strict classifier with the eye-catching PR = 0.97 — turned out on independent review to be a third gaming pathway: the model converts the natural-language question into a snake-case atom that it both *asserts as a fact* and *poses as the query*, so that SWI-Prolog succeeds trivially; 95/97 proved samples have $n_{\text{necessary}} = 1$. PR = 0.97 and proved-only ISR = 0.885 are both artefacts of this query-as-fact gaming.

Three seeds, three distinct gaming pathways (clean tautology / degenerate syntax / query-as-fact), and 0% genuine reasoning in every case. The simpler semantics of Prolog appears to leave less room for the ~16% genuine residual we observe under FOL ISR β=0.1: optimisation pressure on ISR drives the policy to exploit whichever structural shortcut the language admits first. Critically, two of the three Prolog modes (s43 degenerate syntax, s44 query-as-fact) bypass strict text-match hacking classifiers; only joint inspection of $n_{\text{necessary}}$ exposes them. This is itself an actionable methodological finding: structural-hacking classifiers need functional cross-validation when reward shaping induces unfamiliar gaming pathways.

## 4.5 Hacking Taxonomy

Across the FOL and Prolog experiments above we identify seven distinct hacking modes (Table~\ref{tab:tab2_hacking_taxonomy}; per-condition shares in Fig.~\ref{fig:fig3_hacking_composition}):

1. **H1 — multi-premise conclusion embedding.** Conclusion appears verbatim among many premises; PR succeeds trivially, ISR low because no single premise is necessary. Dominant under exec-only s42 (87.1%) and random-bonus runs (mean 81.3%).
2. **H7 — single-premise tautology (P → P).** Proof contains exactly one premise that *is* the conclusion; ISR inflates because the unique premise is structurally "necessary". Dominant under ISR-bonus seeds (mean 64.5%).
3. **Tautological collapse.** Mean-component ≈ 2 with `premise = conclusion`, observed under exec-only s44 (94% 2-component proofs, PR = 98%) and random reward `plan013-random-reward-s42-r4` (PR = 1.0, ISR = 0.535 artefact).
4. **Repetition / length-cap collapse.** Outputs hit max-tokens with repeated clauses; few items parse and those that do are H1 (exec-only s43, 85% of inputs).
5. **Complexity-without-utility.** Many irrelevant premises but no conclusion duplication; appears under random β=0.3 (s42) and is *eliminated* by ISR reward at β=0.1 (0% vs. random 3.8% on s42).
6. **Contradiction explosion.** The 1000-step exec-only failure mode: the model embeds `P(a)` and `¬P(a)` among 18+ premises so any conclusion is provable by explosion; ISR\* = 0.003 with 94.8% of samples having zero necessary components.
7. **Query-as-fact gaming (Prolog).** Model converts the question into a snake-case atom that it asserts as both fact and query; bypasses strict text-match H7 classifiers but is detected by $n_{\text{necessary}}$. Observed in `plan006-prolog-sta-s44` (95/97 functional H7).

The taxonomy supports three cross-cutting observations. First, *reward type determines the composition of hacking, not its prevalence*: total hacking on seed 42 is 69.1% under ISR β=0.1 versus 74.7% under random β=0.1 (D044, strict-classifier audit) — a modest 5.6 pp difference dwarfed by the H1↔H7 redistribution. Second, *seeds determine which mode is reached, not whether hacking occurs*: under both Prolog STA (3/3 gaming via three distinct mechanisms, 0% genuine) and FOL exec-only (s42 H1, s43 repetition, s44 tautological collapse), the same reward and identical training data produce qualitatively distinct gaming pathways across seeds. Cross-seed evaluation is therefore not a luxury but a necessity: any single-seed conclusion about the structural properties of GRPO-trained formalisers is liable to mistake one mode for the whole behaviour space. Third, several of these modes — query-as-fact, contradiction explosion, conclusion duplication with ISR inflation — were not anticipated by intuitive reward design and were only surfaced by joint inspection of PR, component count, $n_{\text{necessary}}$, and text-match patterns. Routine deployment of this multi-axis diagnostic is the practical recommendation that motivates the discussion in Section 5.
