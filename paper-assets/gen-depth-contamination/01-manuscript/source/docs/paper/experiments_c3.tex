\section{Consistency Check: $\Kstar$-Guided Filtering}
\label{sec:filtering}

$\Kempirical = 2$ predicts that binary curation should suffice; we validate this under extreme contamination (${\sim}$83\% synthetic), not as an independent contribution (at realistic rates $\leq$50\%, differences $<$0.3\,pp; Appendix~\ref{sec:dose_response}).
We compare six strategies on 30{,}000 documents (depths 0--5): (i)~\textbf{no-filter}, (ii)~\textbf{binary-ours} (15D LightGBM), (iii)~\textbf{binary-Drayson}~\citep{drayson2025}, (iv)~\textbf{graduated} ($w = \exp(-\alpha \hat{d})$, $\alpha \in \{0.1,\ldots,0.9\}$), (v)~\textbf{surprisal}, and (vi)~\textbf{DSIR}~\citep{xie2023dsir}, fine-tuning Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct via LoRA (rank~8) for 3 epochs across 5 seeds~\cite{shumailov2024collapse, dohmatob2024model}.

Binary-ours reduces the MMLU gap from $-$1.53\,pp (no-filter) to $-$0.77\,pp (${\sim}$50\% reduction; $p_{\text{corr}} < 0.05$ vs.\ all alternatives; Table~\ref{tab:tab_filtering_main}, \ref{tab:pairwise_significance}).
The ordering is interpretable through $\Kempirical$: binary filtering exploits the strongest boundary (0v1), while graduated strategies assign weights based on near-random classifier outputs for depths $\geq 2$, injecting noise rather than useful signal.
Graduated filtering converges toward binary: at $\alpha{=}0.9$, it matches binary-ours on HellaSwag ($p_{\text{corr}} = 1.00$) but remains worse on MMLU ($p_{\text{corr}} = 0.011$); DSIR is indistinguishable from no-filter (Figure~\ref{fig:filtering_strategies}).
At realistic contamination ratios ($\leq$50\%), strategy differences remain below 0.3\,pp (Appendix~\ref{sec:dose_response}); this extreme setting validates $\Kempirical$'s predictive power despite vanishing effect sizes at realistic ratios.
