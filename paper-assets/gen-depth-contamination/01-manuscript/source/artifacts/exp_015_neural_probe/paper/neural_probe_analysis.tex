\subsection{Neural Probing: Representation-Dependent Deepening}
\label{sec:neural_probe}

The preceding experiments establish $\Kempirical = 2$ using 15 hand-crafted statistical features.
To test whether this boundary reflects an intrinsic limit of recursive generation or information loss in the feature projection, we train a \emph{neural probe} on frozen LLM hidden states that bypass hand-crafted engineering entirely.

\paragraph{Setup.}
We extract last-layer hidden states from a frozen Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct encoder and average-pool across sequence length to obtain 1536-dimensional document embeddings.
A three-layer MLP ($1536 \to 512 \to 256 \to 6$) with ReLU activations and dropout is trained to predict generation depth (0--5) under the same 5-fold GroupKFold protocol as the OBD-LightGBM experiments.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:neural_probe} reports pairwise $\auc$ on the Qwen-continuation dataset.% TODO: pending cross-model validation
The neural probe achieves $\Kempirical^{\text{neural}} = 3$: the critical 2v3 pair yields $\auc = 0.6195 \pm 0.0094$, exceeding $\theta = 0.60$ across all five folds (range: 0.6052--0.6323), whereas the LightGBM baseline scores $0.5609$---well below threshold.% TODO: pending cross-model validation
At 1v2, the margin above $\theta$ widens from $+$0.0114 (LightGBM) to $+$0.0983 (neural probe), confirming that the LightGBM's narrow pass reflects genuine information loss in the 15D projection.% TODO: pending cross-model validation
At 3v4, the probe reaches $0.5930 \pm 0.0051$---below $\theta$ by only 0.007---indicating residual depth signal that the operational threshold does not capture.% TODO: pending cross-model validation

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The result establishes a $\Kstar$ hierarchy:
\[
  \Ktheory \;\geq\; \Kempirical^{\text{neural}} = 3 \;\geq\; \Kempirical = 2.
\]
$\Kempirical = 2$ is thus an \emph{operational lower bound} of the 15D feature set, not an information-theoretic ceiling.
The frozen 1536-dimensional representation encodes richer depth signal than the hand-crafted summary, pushing the discrimination boundary one level deeper---a phenomenon we term \emph{representation-dependent deepening}.
Yet the gain is modest: even with access to the generator's own internal representations, 3v4 falls short of $\theta$, confirming that the recursive channel destroys most depth-distinguishing structure within 2--3 steps.% TODO: pending cross-model validation

Two caveats apply.
First, these results cover a single model-dataset pair (Qwen-continuation); cross-model validation on Pythia-1.4B and OLMo-1B is pending.
Second, the probe uses the \emph{same} frozen model that generated the text; self-recognition artifacts may inflate discriminability (cf.\ the cross-model transfer analysis in Section~\ref{sec:transfer}).
