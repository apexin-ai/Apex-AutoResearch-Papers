## Core Problem

As web data becomes increasingly contaminated with multi-generation synthetic text, training corpora contain outputs of unknown recursive provenance — text generated by models that were themselves trained on synthetic outputs. Current data curation treats synthetic text as a binary category, but quality degrades nonlinearly with generational depth, with the sharpest distributional changes (tail disappearance, diversity loss) occurring in early generations. Can we estimate the generational depth of synthetic text and use this graded signal for more effective quality-aware training data filtering?

## Opportunity / Why Now

Existing contamination and machine-generated text detection operates at a binary level (synthetic vs. real), leaving a critical gap: the quality difference between first-generation and fourth-generation synthetic text is substantial but unexploited. Drayson et al. (EMNLP 2025) demonstrate that detection features evolve systematically across 100 generations and that detectability increases with depth — but stop short of building a depth classifier. DivEye (TMLR 2026) extracts 9 surprisal-based features for binary detection; Surplexity (2024) filters training data by surprise without even distinguishing human from AI text. Min-K%++ (ICLR 2025) improves membership inference but ignores depth. Schaeffer et al. (2025) identify 8 conflicting definitions of 'model collapse' and argue catastrophic collapse is unlikely under realistic conditions — but acknowledge that tail disappearance and diversity loss ARE real, practical harms that accumulate with recursive training. Verification-based approaches (Yi et al., arXiv 2025; Feng et al., ICLR 2025) use external verifiers to filter synthetic data quality, but neither estimates depth — they treat quality as a binary pass/fail signal from the verifier rather than estimating how many recursive steps produced the text. No method estimates how many recursive generation steps a text has undergone, despite strong evidence that degradation follows a predictable trajectory. This gap is newly fillable because controlled multi-generation corpora can be efficiently constructed with small open-weight models, and the feature evolution documented by Drayson et al. provides empirical grounding that depth-discriminative signals exist. Characterizing where depth discrimination breaks down (the saturation boundary) is itself a valuable contribution for the data curation community.

## Landscape (SOTA & Limitations)

The landscape spans four research threads that our work bridges:

**Thread 1 — Synthetic text detection (binary):** Min-K% and Min-K%++ (ICLR 2025) detect pre-training membership via token probability analysis. DivEye (TMLR 2026) uses 9 surprisal-based statistical features — distributional moments, first- and second-order differences of token surprisal — for interpretable binary detection, outperforming zero-shot baselines by up to 33.2%. Surplexity (2024) proposes filtering by high surprise to mitigate collapse without distinguishing human from AI text. ConStat (NeurIPS 2024) detects contamination via performance gaps on rephrased samples. All operate at the binary level.

**Thread 2 — Model collapse and recursive training dynamics:** Shumailov et al. (Nature 2024) demonstrate progressive tail disappearance and diversity loss across recursive training generations, with early model collapse (tail loss) preceding late collapse (mode conflation). The Strong Model Collapse paper (ICLR 2025) provides theoretical foundations. Schaeffer et al. (2025) challenge collapse definitions, identifying 8 distinct meanings and arguing that under realistic conditions (data accumulation, not replacement), catastrophic collapse requires 'trillions of models.' Bakshi et al. (arXiv 2026) show statistical conditions under which iterative training on contaminated sources can actually improve performance. Falahati et al. (arXiv May 2026) prove that pluralistic curation prevents collapse theoretically. The BIML recursive pollution framework (2026) distinguishes gradual pollution from catastrophic collapse, arguing models can become 'unusable disasters' well before full collapse.

**Thread 3 — Synthetic data verification and quality-aware curation:** DATAPROPHET (ICLR 2026) introduces training-free data quality metrics combining perplexity, similarity, and diversity for multimodal synthetic data selection. Feng et al. (ICLR 2025) demonstrate that verification — using external signals to select high-quality synthetic samples — prevents model collapse, providing theoretical conditions under which even imperfect verifiers suffice. Yi et al. (arXiv 2025) extend this by showing that injecting information through an external verifier reverses the collapse trajectory, though long-term performance converges to the verifier's knowledge center. Both verification approaches operate at a binary quality gate level rather than estimating the recursive provenance of the text. Drayson et al. (EMNLP 2025) demonstrate that binary machine-generated text detection prevents collapse and improves performance via importance resampling, tracking feature evolution across 100 generations of GPT-2 and SmolLM2 (124M–1.7B). They show features like diversity, Self-BLEU, and perplexity distributions shift systematically with generation depth — empirically grounding the premise that depth-discriminative signals exist — but build only a binary detector.

**The gap:** No existing work builds a practical depth-estimation algorithm. Drayson et al. characterize feature evolution across generations but do not predict depth. DivEye and Surplexity detect or filter synthetic text without generation awareness. Verification methods (Feng et al., Yi et al.) filter by quality but do not distinguish a first-generation text from a fourth-generation one. The recursive training literature (Shumailov, Bakshi, Falahati) models degradation dynamics but proposes no detection methods. We fill this gap with the first ordinal depth classifier, enabling graduated quality-aware filtering where later-generation text is downweighted proportionally rather than binary-excluded.

## Proposed Approach

**Phase 0 — Pilot saturation study (Days 1-3, hard gate):** Using a single model (Pythia-1.4B) and one seed dataset (C4 subset, 50K documents), generate a 10-depth recursive chain to empirically characterize where depth discrimination saturates. Extract DivEye-style surprisal features plus vocabulary diversity metrics at each depth and measure inter-depth discriminability (pairwise classifier accuracy, distribution overlap). This pilot determines the effective discrimination horizon K and informs the choice of depth granularity for the full study. We hypothesize K ≈ 3-5 based on Shumailov et al.'s observation that tail disappearance is sharpest in early generations. **Critical gating decision:** If K ≤ 2, the primary contribution pivots to saturation boundary characterization (see Saturation Fallback Plan below). The pilot MUST complete by Day 3 to avoid wasted compute downstream.

**Saturation Fallback Plan (activated if K ≤ 2):** If depth discrimination saturates at depth ≤2, a 3-class classifier (human/gen-1/gen-2+) is only marginally more informative than binary detection. In this case, we pivot the primary contribution to a comprehensive characterization of the saturation boundary itself: (a) **Per-feature saturation analysis:** Which features plateau first — surprisal dynamics, vocabulary diversity metrics, or tail-thinning metrics? Plotting per-feature discriminability curves across depth reveals the degradation mechanisms that become indistinguishable earliest vs. latest. (b) **Cross-model saturation comparison:** Does OLMo-1B saturate at a different depth than Pythia-1.4B or Qwen2.5-1.8B? If different architectures/training regimes produce different saturation profiles, this guides model-specific curation strategies. (c) **Decoding strategy effects on saturation:** Do temperature, top-k, and nucleus sampling shift the saturation boundary? High-temperature decoding might preserve diversity longer, extending discriminability. (d) **Scale effects on saturation:** Does the 7B model (Qwen2.5-7B) produce depth chains that saturate at a different point than 1B models? If larger models saturate later, this has direct implications for real-world contamination from frontier models. These analyses make the saturation boundary characterization itself an EMNLP-quality contribution, providing actionable guidance for when depth-aware curation is effective vs. when binary detection suffices.

**Phase 1 — Multi-generation corpus construction (Days 2-5, overlapping with Phase 0):** Construct controlled depth-labeled corpora using three small open-weight model families: Pythia-1B/1.4B (EleutherAI), OLMo-1B (AI2), and Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.8B (Alibaba). Starting from human-written seeds (C4 and Wikipedia subsets, 50K documents each — reduced from 100K for initial submission; full 100K corpus reserved for camera-ready), iteratively feed outputs back as prompts through 5 generation depths (0=human, 1-4=synthetic). Each (model, seed, depth) combination produces a depth-labeled subcorpus. Depth chains are sequential per model but parallelizable across model families.

**Phase 2 — Feature extraction and depth-discriminative analysis (Days 4-7):** Extract features at three levels, designed to extend and complement DivEye's binary features for ordinal prediction: (a) Token-level: the 9 DivEye surprisal statistics (distributional moments + first/second-order differences) plus Min-K%-style low-probability token fractions at multiple K thresholds, providing a surprisal feature set that captures both distributional shape and temporal dynamics; (b) Sequence-level: vocabulary diversity decay metrics — type-token ratio, hapax legomena ratio, n-gram novelty (proportion of n-grams not seen in a reference human corpus), and Self-BLEU — capturing the progressive homogenization documented by Drayson et al.; (c) Distributional-level: tail-thinning metrics — kurtosis and entropy of the vocabulary frequency distribution, proportion of vocabulary below frequency thresholds — capturing the specific tail disappearance mechanism identified by Shumailov et al. Conduct feature importance analysis to identify which features drive depth discrimination vs. merely distinguish human from synthetic.

**Phase 3 — Ordinal depth classifier (Days 6-8):** Train an ordinal regression classifier (CORAL/CORN framework) to predict generation depth (0 through K, where K is determined by the pilot), comparing against gradient-boosted baselines (LightGBM) and a simple MLP. Evaluate both exact-depth accuracy and coarse-grained classification (human/shallow/deep), since the pilot may reveal that fine-grained discrimination degenerates beyond a certain depth. Report calibration curves and confidence intervals. If the pilot shows saturation at depth K < 4, reframe as (K+1)-class classification and activate the Saturation Fallback Plan for supplementary analyses.

**Phase 4 — Depth-aware filtering validation (Days 7-10):** Demonstrate practical utility by comparing four filtering strategies on downstream NLP benchmarks: knowledge-intensive reasoning via MMLU subsets and HellaSwag, and text generation quality via XSum summarization: (a) No filtering (baseline); (b) Binary filtering using DivEye features (remove all detected synthetic text); (c) Depth-aware weighted filtering where documents are downweighted proportionally to estimated depth (weight = 1/(1 + α·depth)); (d) Surplexity-based filtering (filter by high surprise). These benchmarks are chosen because MMLU and HellaSwag are sensitive to data quality differences in pretraining data curation (demonstrated by DataComp-LM and FineWeb-Edu evaluations), unlike well-saturated benchmarks (SST-2, AG News) where small quality improvements may not produce statistically significant gaps. Test cross-model generalization: train depth chains on Pythia, evaluate classifier on OLMo- and Qwen-generated chains.

**Phase 4b — Cross-scale generalization probe (Days 8-10, parallel with Phase 4):** Generate a small depth chain (1K documents × 3 depths: 0, 1, 2) from Qwen2.5-7B to test whether depth features learned from 1B-scale models transfer to text generated by a 7B model. This is critical because real-world synthetic contamination primarily comes from much larger models (GPT-4, Claude, Gemini). While a full cross-scale study is beyond scope, even a small-scale probe revealing transfer (or lack thereof) provides valuable evidence about practical applicability. Cost: ~5-8 additional H100-hours.

**Phase 5 — Naturalistic validation (Days 9-10):** Apply the trained depth classifier to a sample of recent Common Crawl data (CC-2025/2026 snapshots). Since ground-truth depth labels are unavailable for wild data, validate via proxy signals, ensuring at least one proxy is feature-orthogonal to the classifier's input features to avoid tautological correlation: (a) Correlation between predicted depth and AlignScore (NLI-based factual consistency evaluation against reference corpus documents), which measures information alignment via a unified function trained on NLI, QA, and fact verification tasks — this is feature-orthogonal because AlignScore evaluates semantic accuracy, not surprisal statistics or vocabulary distributions; (b) Correlation with independently-measured vocabulary richness metrics computed by a method distinct from the classifier's features (e.g., moving average type-token ratio with different window sizes); (c) Correlation with binary AI-detection scores from existing tools (DivEye, GPTZero) as a sanity check; (d) Qualitative analysis of high-predicted-depth samples to check face validity. This validates that the classifier produces meaningful signals on naturalistic data, not just controlled corpora.

**Parallel writing plan (Days 1-14):** Writing begins from Day 1 and runs continuously alongside experiments. Days 1-3: Introduction and related work (leveraging existing landscape analysis). Days 4-6: Methods section (feature extraction, classifier design). Days 7-9: Results section (populated as experiments complete). Days 10-12: Discussion, analysis figures, ablation tables. Days 12-14: Final formatting, camera-ready figures, submission checklist, responsible NLP checklist. A co-author or the primary researcher allocates ~3 hours/day to writing during experimental phases.

**Contributions (ordered by importance):** (1) The first practical generation-depth estimation algorithm for recursively generated synthetic text, moving beyond binary detection; (2) Empirical characterization of the depth-discrimination saturation boundary — at which generation depth do texts become statistically indistinguishable, which features saturate first, and how does saturation vary by model family, scale, and decoding strategy? — providing actionable guidance for data curation practitioners; (3) Demonstration that depth-aware graduated filtering outperforms both binary filtering and no filtering on challenging downstream tasks (MMLU, HellaSwag, XSum); (4) Cross-model and cross-scale depth generalization analysis, including a 7B-scale probe; (5) Feature importance analysis revealing which degradation signals (surprisal dynamics, vocabulary decay, tail thinning) are most depth-discriminative.

[实验设计 / Appendix] — 新增以下 robustness analyses 作为 appendix 内容：(a) **heterogeneous model chain**（交替不同模型生成）；(b) **human editing robustness**（simulated span replacement、paraphrasing）；(c) **Common Crawl naturalistic validation**。原始 proposal 无此设计。Clean chains 明确定位为 theoretical upper bound，real-world depth estimates 定位为 ordinal quality proxies。 — 命中 Experimental Rigor

14. [Feature Extraction / Method] — 修正 **n-gram overlap feature 的 data leakage 问题**。原始 proposal 的 "cross-depth n-gram overlap decay rate" 需要 paired original text，inference 时不可用。拆分为：(a) analysis-only metric（用于降解序研究）；(b) inference-time computable variant（self-repetition n-gram statistics、与 reference human corpus 的统计距离）。 — 命中 Technical Soundness（消除 data leakage 这一 reject-worthy 问题）

15. [Feature Extraction / Method] — 新增 **systematic feature design** 的诚实定位：标为 "systematic and principled feature design informed by the recursive generation mechanism"，而非 overclaim 为 "novel feature contributions"。新增 token-level perplexity distribution shape features（skewness, kurtosis, entropy）。 — 命中 Technical Soundness（诚实定位避免 overclaim）

16. [Classification / Method] — 新增系统 ablation 和 baseline 对比：(a) **ordinal vs. multinomial vs. standard regression**（证明 ordinal structure 必要性）；(b) **learned representation baseline**（frozen LM embeddings + MLP vs. hand-crafted features）；(c) **LLM zero-shot/few-shot depth estimation baseline**（验证 depth estimation 需要专门方法）；(d) **LLM-as-judge quality scoring baseline**（验证 depth signal 与 LLM quality judgment 正交）；(e) **binary detector confidence as depth proxy**（Binoculars/DetectGPT score vs. true depth Spearman correlation）。原始 proposal 仅有 CORAL/CORN + 现有 filtering methods 对比。 — 命中 Baseline Completeness（堵住"为什么不直接用 X"的多个质疑）

17. [Cross-Scale Generalization / Method] — 增强 cross-scale 策略：(a) 将 probe 扩展到 **8B-14B 范围**（Llama-3-8B 或 Qwen2.5-14B）；(b) WildChat/LMSYS-Chat 上的 **formal feature trend analysis**（含 MMD domain shift quantification）；(c) 明确 **practical deployment scope 为开源模型生态（7B-14B）**；(d) frontier model limitation 扩展为 **principled discussion**（RLHF/instruction-tuning 对 depth signal 的影响、API-only access 对 feature 计算的限制）。原始 proposal 仅有 Qwen2.5-7B 一个 cross-scale probe。 — 命中 Significance（论证实际部署价值）

18. [Downstream Curation / Experimental Validation] — 大幅增强 curation 实验严格性：(a) **contamination severity 维度**（10%/30%/50% synthetic injection）；(b) **cross-model transfer**（classifier 训 on model A, curate model B）；(c) **unknown depth distribution condition**；(d) **data-quality-sensitive tasks**（held-out perplexity, factual QA，不仅限于 MMLU）；(e) **binary filtering threshold 选择协议明确化**；(f) **bootstrap CIs + paired significance tests**；(g) **实验逻辑链显式说明**。原始 proposal 对 filtering 实验的公平性和统计显著性没有详细设计。 — 命中 Technical Soundness + Experimental Rigor

19. [Depth vs. Quality Analysis / Experimental Validation] — 新增 **depth-quality 单调性直接验证**。原始 proposal 隐含假设 depth 与 quality 单调递减但未验证。新增多维度 quality proxy 曲线，验证单调性成立条件。如非单调，graduated filtering 适应为 depth-features → predicted quality → weight。 — 命中 Technical Soundness（验证核心前提假设）

20. [K* Contingency / Experimental Validation] — 新增 **显式 contingency 讨论**：K*≤3 时贡献重定位为首次量化 fundamental resolution limit + decoding strategy 对 K* 的调控效应 + 即使 coarse ordinal signal 仍有 calibration 优势。原始 proposal 的 "If…then" 结构是结构性风险。 — 命中 Soundness + Reviewer 心理管理

【主要风险】:
1. **K* 实验结果不确定性**：如果在大多数 model/decoding 配置下 K*≤2，论文的 empirical richness 和 graduated curation 收益都会受限。虽有 contingency plan（fundamental resolution limit characterization + decoding strategy modulation），但 reviewer 仍可能评为 "interesting characterization but limited practical impact"。这是无法通过 proposal 优化消除的固有风险。
2. **"Good problem + standard method" 框架**：核心分类 pipeline 是 feature engineering + off-the-shelf ordinal regression（CORAL/CORN），审稿人可能认为方法论贡献不足。信息论框架和假设检验提升了分析深度，但分类器本身没有方法论突破。对于偏重 methods 的审稿人，这可能是 borderline reject 的理由。

【实施评估】:
- 计算资源: 主实验（corpus generation + feature extraction + classifier training）可在 2-4 块 A100 上完成，估计 1-2 周。Retrain-chain validation（1 config, LoRA fine-tuning, 3-4 depth levels）约 2-3 天额外 GPU 时间。Cross-scale probe（7B-14B 模型生成 + 特征提取）约 3-5 天。总计 GPU 需求约 2-3 周集中使用。不需要大规模集群。
- 数据需求: C4 和 Wikipedia 子集（50K documents each）——公开可得。WildChat / LMSYS-Chat——公开可得。无需私有或受限数据集。
- 人工介入: 无需人工标注（ground-truth depth labels 由 generation pipeline 自动产生）。无需专家评审。唯一的人工环节是实验设计决策和论文写作。
- 训练需求: 需要模型训练，但规模有限。(a) Ordinal regression classifier（CORAL/CORN）：轻量级，训练时间可忽略。(b) Retrain-chain 的 LoRA fine-tuning：Pythia-1B 级别，每轮约 1-2 小时。(c) 不需要从头预训练任何 LLM。(d) 下游 curation 评估需要在 curated data 上训练小模型（≤1.8B），但这也是标准规模。
- 新 Benchmark: 不需要构建新的公开 benchmark。构建的 multi-depth corpus 是实验工具而非独立 benchmark 贡献（但可作为 artifact release）。
- 预估周期: Corpus generation + feature extraction: 2-3 周；Classifier training + ablation: 1 周；Downstream curation experiments: 1-2 周；Cross-scale + retrain-chain validation: 1-2 周；Analysis + writing: 2-3 周。**总计约 8-12 周**（从启动到可投稿），假设 GPU 资源持续可用。如果 GPU 间歇使用，可能延长至 12-16 周。EMNLP 2026 投稿 deadline 前有充足时间。
