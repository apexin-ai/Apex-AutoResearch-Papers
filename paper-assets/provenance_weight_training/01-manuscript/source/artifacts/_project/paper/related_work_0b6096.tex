\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Synthetic Contamination and Model Collapse.}
Training on recursively generated data leads to irreversible quality degradation---\emph{model collapse} \citep{shumailov2024model}.
\cite{alemohammad2024mad} formalize this as Model Autophagy Disorder, showing that quality or diversity inevitably degrades without fresh real data, even when original training data is retained.
More critically, \cite{dohmatob2025strong} prove under scaling-law regimes that even a vanishingly small synthetic fraction (e.g., $1/1000$) suffices to trigger collapse, ruling out data scaling as a remedy.
With synthetic content now estimated at 20--60\% of web corpora \citep{cheng2025contamination}, these results motivate contamination-aware training strategies that explicitly account for data provenance rather than treating all tokens equally.

\paragraph{Detect and Reweight.}
A natural response to contamination is to detect and remove synthetic content.
DetectGPT \citep{mitchell2023detectgpt} performs zero-shot detection via probability curvature, while Min-$k$\% Prob \citep{shi2024minkprob} uses the average log-probability of the lowest-$k$\% tokens as a reference-free signal; both produce continuous confidence scores suitable as input to reweighting frameworks.
\cite{feng2025beyond} prove that even an imperfect verifier prevents collapse, but apply hard binary filtering that discards borderline samples; \cite{guo2025escaping} similarly rely on verification-based selection.
Soft reweighting avoids the precision--recall threshold inherent in hard filtering by assigning continuous weights.

Most directly related to our work, \cite{drayson2025detection} propose $w_j = (1{-}q_j)^b$ for collapse prevention via detect-and-reweight, where $q_j$ is a detector's synthetic probability.
We adopt the same weighting formula but differ in several fundamental ways.
\textbf{(i)}~Drayson et al.\ select $b$ via validation-set grid search and fix it throughout training; different model scales require different values ($b{=}10$ for ${\leq}360$M parameters, $b{=}1$ for 1.7B), making the choice per-model and expensive.
Our $\bstar$ is derived per-batch from dual constraints ($\aeff \geq \tau$, $\mathrm{ESS} \geq \nmin$) at zero validation cost, following a minimum intervention principle that selects the smallest sufficient exponent.
\textbf{(ii)}~Their SIR resampling discretizes the weight distribution; we apply direct loss reweighting with continuous gradient contributions.
\textbf{(iii)}~They employ a ModernBERT-150M detector (AUC\,=\,0.986) on WikiText-2 (${\sim}$2M tokens); we show that a lightweight TF-IDF+LR detector (AUC\,=\,0.939) suffices on FineWeb-scale data (132M+ tokens).

\paragraph{Data Mixing and Reweighting.}
A parallel line of work optimizes data composition without targeting synthetic contamination.
At the \emph{domain} level, DoReMi \citep{xie2023doremi} learns mixture weights via Group DRO with a proxy model, DSIR \citep{xie2023dsir} performs importance resampling with $n$-gram features, \cite{ye2025mixing} discover predictable functional forms relating mixing ratios to performance, and AIOLI \citep{chen2025aioli} unifies these into an online adaptive framework.
These methods operate at the domain level and do not address instance-level contamination within a domain.
At the \emph{instance} level, SoftDedup \citep{he2024softdedup} downweights high-frequency samples via $n$-gram commonness, and \cite{sow2025dynamic} derive sample weights from per-sample loss with convergence guarantees.
Our reweighting differs from all of these in being driven by \emph{provenance}---detector scores estimating human vs.\ machine origin---rather than loss, frequency, or domain membership.

\paragraph{Theoretical Foundations.}
\cite{he2025golden} derive the optimal real-data weight under known mixture proportions, finding the golden-ratio reciprocal (${\approx}0.62$) optimal when both sources are equally sized; their result requires the contamination ratio, unavailable in web-scale settings.
\cite{wang2026survive} prove that contaminated recursive training converges at a rate governed by the effective real-data fraction $\alpha$, providing the theoretical motivation for our quality threshold: ensuring $\aeff(b) \geq \tau$ guarantees a convergence-rate lower bound.
We operationalize this without the known-proportion assumption, estimating $\aeff$ from batch-level statistics online.
For the stability constraint, we build on Kish's effective sample size and the Pareto-smoothed importance sampling framework of \cite{vehtari2024psis}.
\cite{kimura2024iw} provide a broader treatment of importance weighting under distribution shift, within which our provenance-based reweighting can be viewed as covariate-shift correction where the covariate is synthetic origin probability.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Summary.}
No existing method provides principled per-batch exponent selection without validation.
Drayson et al.\ use the same $w{=}(1{-}q)^b$ formula but require per-model grid search; Golden Ratio weighting requires known mixture proportions; DoReMi and DSIR operate at the domain level rather than instance-level contamination.
Our dual-constraint framework fills this gap by deriving the minimum intervention $\bstar$ from batch-level diagnostics alone.
