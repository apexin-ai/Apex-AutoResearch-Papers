## Core Problem

Web-scraped training corpora contain an unknown and growing fraction of synthetic text at multiple recursive generation depths, creating feedback loops that degrade model quality. Recent convergence theory (arXiv 2602.16065) proves that training outcomes depend on min(baseline_rate, real_data_fraction), and the Strong Model Collapse result (Dohmatob et al., ICLR 2025 Spotlight) shows that even 0.1% synthetic contamination causes collapse under static mixing — with larger models amplifying the effect. However, no practical pipeline estimates the real-data fraction from unlabeled web data or translates convergence insights into principled training weights. Existing detector-guided resampling (Drayson et al., EMNLP 2025) treats its weighting exponent as a hyperparameter, uses single-generator-per-experiment evaluation on a curated dataset (WikiText-2), and does not investigate whether generation depth — a key theoretical variable in collapse dynamics — is classifiable from text alone. Boosting-inspired theory-guided reweighting (Amin et al., NeurIPS 2025) prevents collapse through loss-based sample prioritization but requires a weak labeler with programmatically verifiable quality and does not estimate contamination fractions from unlabeled data — assumptions that fail on web corpora where synthetic provenance is unknown. A further complication is that the convergence bound requires convex distance metrics (Assumption A2), which excludes cross-entropy and KL divergence — the standard language model training objectives — creating a theory-practice gap. Crucially, no general inequality bridges this gap in the needed direction: Pinsker's inequality gives small KL → small TV, but convergence under A2-compatible metrics (TV, Wasserstein, MMD) does not imply convergence under cross-entropy. This necessitates empirical dual-metric validation rather than a purely theoretical bridge. We note that while Schaeffer et al. (ICML 2025 Position) argue that 'model collapse' conflates eight distinct definitions and that catastrophic scenarios often rely on unrealistic data-replacement assumptions, the web contamination setting we target — where synthetic fraction reaches 50–60% of crawled data and grows with each scrape cycle — falls squarely within the regime where collapse IS a genuine risk per Strong Model Collapse (Dohmatob et al., ICLR 2025 Spotlight), precisely because the synthetic fraction is large, persistent, and not replaced but accumulated alongside real data.

## Opportunity / Why Now

Five disconnected research threads address parts of this problem but leave a critical integration gap. (1) Convergence theory (arXiv 2602.16065, Feb 2026) provides the bound d(ℙ̂_t, ℙ_0) ≲ t^{−min{p,α}} — the first positive theoretical result on recursive training without distributional assumptions — but restricts d to convex metrics (total variation, Wasserstein-p, MMD), explicitly excluding cross-entropy/KL, and offers no detection or weighting method. (2) Detector-guided resampling (Drayson et al., EMNLP 2025) demonstrates that binary synthetic detection + importance weighting w=(1−q(x))^b prevents collapse and even improves over pure human data on WikiText-2 with GPT-2/SmolLM2 across 6 decoding strategies, but uses single-generator-per-experiment evaluation, a heuristically tuned b, and does not scale to web-like corpora with cross-generator contamination. (3) Golden-ratio weighting (arXiv 2502.18049, Feb 2025) derives the optimal real/synthetic mixing weight w⋆ = (√(k²+4k)−k)/2 by minimizing C(w,k) = [w²+(1−w)²k]/(2w−w²), with the complementary result of Garg et al. (arXiv 2509.22341, Sep 2025, revised Feb 2026) showing that the optimal real-data proportion converges to the reciprocal of the golden ratio under overparameterized interpolation learning — but both assume the synthetic fraction k is known a priori, an assumption that fails on unlabeled web crawls. (4) Strong Model Collapse (arXiv 2410.04840, Dohmatob et al., ICLR 2025 Spotlight) proves that even minimal synthetic contamination causes collapse under static mixing with known proportions, and that weighted mixing alone is insufficient — but does not consider detection-based adaptive reweighting where importance weights can drive the effective contribution of detected synthetic samples toward zero, functionally achieving their p₂→0 escape condition. (5) Boosting-inspired theory-guided reweighting (Amin et al., arXiv 2502.08924, NeurIPS 2025) proves convergence to an optimal LLM even with mostly poor-quality non-synthetic data by dynamically focusing labeling resources on challenging examples — but operates in curated training pipelines with programmatically verifiable quality (math, coding), requires a β-weak labeler, and does not address detection of synthetic provenance in unlabeled web corpora where quality cannot be automatically verified. Meanwhile, the Knowledge Collapse analysis (arXiv 2509.04796, Sep 2025) shows surface fluency is preserved across recursive depths while factual accuracy degrades, raising the empirical question of whether depth is a classifiable signal — a question no prior work has tested. Cross-generator detection generalization remains an open challenge: the best disentangled approach (arXiv 2604.13692, Apr 2026) achieves ~88% average cross-family accuracy but degrades to 68.9% on the hardest family (FLAN-T5). The critical integration gap — connecting convergence theory to weight calibration with estimated (not assumed-known) contamination fractions, validated under both theory-compatible metrics and practical cross-entropy training — remains unaddressed. The EMNLP 2026 special theme 'New Missions for NLP Research' includes a 'Data as a bottleneck and a responsibility' sub-theme that explicitly solicits work on 'contamination' and 'the consequences of synthetic data feedback loops,' providing strong venue alignment.

## Landscape (SOTA & Limitations)

Binary AI-text detection achieves 0.986 AUC in-distribution (ModernBERT on MAGE benchmark, Drayson et al., EMNLP 2025) but degrades on out-of-distribution generators (~0.943 AUC) and cross-family transfer (~88% average, 68.9% worst-case on FLAN-T5, arXiv 2604.13692). Recent feature-augmented transformer detectors (arXiv 2605.03969, May 2026) achieve up to 99.5% balanced accuracy in-domain but only 85.9% on cross-dataset transfer, confirming cross-generator generalization as a fundamental challenge. The foundational model collapse result (Nature, 2024) demonstrates tail disappearance under recursive training. The ICML 2024 accumulation result shows that mixing real + synthetic data avoids collapse. The convergence theory (arXiv 2602.16065) formalizes this as d(ℙ̂_t, ℙ_0) ≲ t^{−min{p,α}} without distributional assumptions, where d must be a convex metric — satisfied by total variation, Wasserstein-p, energy distance, and MMD, but explicitly NOT by cross-entropy, likelihood, or KL divergence. The authors identify extending the proof to cross-entropy as important future work. This metric restriction is critical: standard LM training uses cross-entropy loss, so the bound's applicability to practical training requires either extension of the theory or empirical validation that the bound's qualitative insights transfer. Note that Pinsker's inequality (TV ≤ √(KL/2)) provides only the direction small KL → small TV; it does not provide a converse, meaning convergence under A2-compatible metrics cannot be theoretically transferred to cross-entropy without additional assumptions (e.g., bounded density ratios for a reverse Pinsker bound). This asymmetry is precisely what motivates our dual-metric empirical validation rather than a purely theoretical bridge. Strong Model Collapse (arXiv 2410.04840, Dohmatob et al., ICLR 2025 Spotlight) proves that even 0.1% synthetic contamination causes collapse in regression settings within the scaling laws paradigm, with larger models amplifying collapse. Critically, their Section 5 shows that weighted single-step mixing with known proportions does not prevent collapse — the escape condition requires p₂→0 (vanishing synthetic proportion). However, their analysis considers only known-proportion weighting, not detection-based adaptive reweighting where importance weights can drive the effective contribution of detected synthetic samples toward zero. Schaeffer et al. (arXiv 2503.03150, ICML 2025 Position) argue that 'model collapse' conflates eight distinct definitions across three families — test loss behavior, distribution deformation, and scaling law changes — and that catastrophic collapse scenarios often rest on the unrealistic 'replace' paradigm where all prior data is discarded. We target collapse definitions in families (1) and (2) under the realistic 'accumulate' paradigm, where the risk is not data replacement but the growing dominance of synthetic content in accumulated web crawls — a regime where even the 'accumulate' paradigm faces degradation when synthetic fractions exceed 40–60% (per Strong Model Collapse and empirical estimates of current web synthetic content). Fu et al. (arXiv 2502.18865, ICLR 2025) introduce recursive stability and prove O(1/n) convergence for transformers in self-consuming loops with constant real-data proportion, providing architecture-dependent convergence guarantees complementary to the architecture-agnostic but metric-restricted bounds of arXiv 2602.16065. Golden-ratio weighting (arXiv 2502.18049) minimizes C(w,k) to derive optimal weights but requires known synthetic fraction k. Garg et al. (arXiv 2509.22341, Sep 2025, revised Feb 2026) extend this to overparameterized settings, showing that the optimal real-data proportion converges to the reciprocal of the golden ratio under min-ℓ₂-norm interpolation for general covariate distributions — again assuming known proportions. Amin et al. (arXiv 2502.08924, NeurIPS 2025) prove that a boosting-inspired iterative reweighting procedure converges to an optimal LLM even when non-synthetic data is mostly poor-quality, requiring only a β-weak labeler that produces a β-fraction of high-quality responses. Their approach reweights based on loss signals without a detection step, dynamically focusing labeling resources on challenging examples — complementary to but distinct from our detection-based approach. Key differences: (a) Amin et al. assume access to a weak labeler with programmatically verifiable quality (validated on math/coding tasks), whereas we target web corpora where quality is not automatically verifiable; (b) they do not estimate synthetic contamination fractions, whereas our method estimates the effective real fraction from unlabeled data via detector confidence; (c) their theoretical foundation is boosting (combining weak learners), whereas ours connects to convergence bounds under recursive contamination. The Knowledge Collapse paper (arXiv 2509.04796) reveals a three-stage degradation (preservation → knowledge collapse → instruction collapse) where Shannon entropy declines from ~4.2 to ~2.5 across depths while surface fluency is preserved. Detector-guided resampling (Drayson et al., EMNLP 2025) uses w=(1−q(x))^b with calibrated ModernBERT detection to upweight likely-human samples, preventing collapse and even improving over pure human data — but treats b as a hyperparameter, uses single-generator-per-experiment evaluation on a curated dataset (WikiText-2) across 4 LLMs (GPT-2 124M, SmolLM2 135M/360M/1.7B) from 2 model families with 6 decoding strategies, and does not consider depth or cross-generator contamination scenarios. Dynamic loss-based sample reweighting (arXiv 2502.06733, ICLR 2025) provides convergence analysis for loss-based reweighting but does not address synthetic contamination detection. Curated synthetic data with pluralistic preferences (arXiv 2605.07724, ICML 2026) proves convergence to a weighted Nash bargaining solution under multi-objective curation but focuses on diversity preservation rather than contamination mitigation. Synthetic data verification (arXiv 2510.16657) uses binary filtering without continuous weighting. The critical gap is the integration of convergence theory with practical detection and weight calibration — specifically, bridging A2-compatible convergence bounds to cross-entropy training via dual-metric validation — on realistic multi-generator web contamination with depth investigation, in a setting where neither programmatic quality verification (as in Amin et al.) nor known synthetic proportions (as in golden-ratio methods) are available.

## Proposed Approach

We propose a four-phase pipeline validated within ≤200 H100-hours that bridges the theory–practice gap through dual-metric validation — showing that convergence-informed weighting improves both theory-compatible metrics (where the bound formally applies) and cross-entropy training (where the benefit is empirically demonstrated).

**Phase 1 — Depth Classifiability Pilot (novel empirical investigation, ~12 H100-hours).** We generate multi-depth synthetic corpora by iteratively retraining Qwen3-0.6B on its own outputs at depths 1–3 on a 400M-token FineWeb subset. Limiting to 3 depths is scientifically defensible: the Knowledge Collapse paper (arXiv 2509.04796) shows the most informative degradation transitions occur in early generations. At each depth we measure corpus-level statistics (Shannon entropy, distinct-n, perplexity under an external reference model, factual retention via the KR-test) and train a ModernBERT-base (150M) classifier for 3-class ordinal depth prediction. We report per-depth-pair F1. Decision rule: if adjacent-depth F1 > 65%, depth enters the weighting function; otherwise, we report this as a negative result — itself a novel contribution — and proceed with binary detection only.

**Phase 2 — Multi-Generator Provenance Detector (~6 H100-hours).** We fine-tune ModernBERT-base on a balanced corpus of human text (C4/FineWeb) and synthetic text from two model families — Qwen3-0.6B and Llama-3.2-1B — to produce calibrated per-document synthetic probabilities q(x) via temperature scaling. We evaluate cross-generator transfer: train on Qwen3 outputs, test on Llama outputs, and vice versa. This extends beyond the single-generator-per-experiment evaluation in Drayson et al. (EMNLP 2025) to address cross-generator contamination — the realistic web scenario.

**Phase 3 — Convergence-Informed Weight Calibration (core contribution).** We derive training sample weights informed by the convergence bound of arXiv 2602.16065, explicitly addressing the theory's metric restriction and the informality of the distribution-to-sample bridge. Our approach differs fundamentally from Amin et al. (NeurIPS 2025): they reweight based on loss signals using a β-weak labeler with programmatically verifiable quality, whereas we reweight based on detection confidence to estimate the effective real fraction in unlabeled web data — a setting where quality verification is unavailable and the key challenge is identifying synthetic provenance, not curating response quality.

*Metric limitation and dual-validation strategy.* The convergence bound d(ℙ̂_t, ℙ_0) ≲ t^{−min{p,α}} requires d to be a convex metric (Assumption A2). Cross-entropy and KL divergence do not satisfy A2. No general inequality bridges A2-compatible metrics to cross-entropy in the needed direction: Pinsker's inequality gives small KL → small TV, but we need the reverse (small TV → small KL), which requires bounded density ratios — an assumption that is unverifiable for high-dimensional language distributions. We therefore adopt a dual-validation strategy: we validate the weight calibration on A2-compatible metrics (Wasserstein-1 distance and kernel MMD, computed on frozen SentenceTransformer embeddings) where the bound formally applies, and separately demonstrate whether the benefit transfers to cross-entropy training — the practical objective. This design tests the theory on its own terms while characterizing the theory-practice bridge empirically.

*Step 1 — Effective real fraction under importance weighting.* Given a training batch {x_1,...,x_N} with calibrated detector scores q(x_i) = P̂(synthetic|x_i) and importance weights w_i = (1−q(x_i))^b, we define the effective real fraction:

  α_eff(b) = Σ_i (1−q_i)^{b+1} / Σ_i (1−q_i)^b

This follows from the law of total expectation: E[w_i · 1{x_i is real} | q_i] = (1−q_i)^{b+1} when q is well-calibrated. α_eff(b) is computable from batch statistics, monotonically non-decreasing in b, with α_eff(0) = 1 − ρ̂ (the unweighted real fraction) and α_eff(b) → 1 as b → ∞.

*Step 2 — Hypothesis: connection to convergence bound.* We hypothesize that under importance-weighted training, the reweighted empirical distribution achieves effective real fraction α_eff(b), yielding convergence rate t^{−min{p, α_eff(b)}} for A2-compatible metrics. This hypothesis is plausible — importance weighting tilts the empirical distribution toward real data — but constitutes an assertion, not a theorem: the convergence bound assumes training on fixed-proportion mixtures, not importance-weighted sampling from a contaminated pool. Formally bridging this gap would require showing that importance-weighted SGD on a contaminated corpus is equivalent, in the relevant metric, to training on a clean mixture with fraction α_eff(b) — a nontrivial claim we leave as a conjecture and validate empirically. We note that even without formal proof, the qualitative insight — the rate exponent saturates at p, so pushing α_eff(b) beyond p only costs effective sample size — provides a principled stopping criterion.

*Connection to Strong Model Collapse.* Dohmatob et al. (arXiv 2410.04840) prove that collapse persists unless p₂→0 (vanishing synthetic proportion), and show that known-proportion weighted mixing is insufficient. Our detection-based approach differs fundamentally: for samples with high q(x_i), importance weights w_i = (1−q(x_i))^b → 0 as b grows, effectively achieving the p₂→0 condition for the detectable portion of contamination. The irreducible contamination from undetected synthetic text (false negatives) sets a floor: effective p₂_residual ≈ FNR × ρ, where FNR is the detector's false negative rate and ρ is the true contamination fraction. This motivates our multi-generator detector training in Phase 2 to minimize FNR.

*Step 3 — Optimal b derivation.* The effective sample size under importance weighting is ESS(b) = (Σ_i w_i)² / Σ_i w_i². Large b improves α_eff (and thus the hypothesized rate exponent) but degrades the constant factor (via reduced ESS). We solve:

  b* = inf{b ≥ 0 : α_eff(b) ≥ τ}  subject to  ESS(b*) ≥ n_min

where τ = min(p, target_rate) is the rate exponent threshold and n_min is the minimum effective sample size for stable gradient estimates. When the constraints conflict, we solve b* = argmax_b α_eff(b) s.t. ESS(b) ≥ n_min. When batch contamination is low (ρ̂ < 1−τ), we use uniform weights (b=0). This replaces the heuristic grid search over b in Drayson et al. (EMNLP 2025) with an adaptive, batch-dependent schedule.

*Step 4 — Concentration argument and calibration correction.* For finite batches, α̂_eff(b) concentrates around its expectation at a rate governed by ESS: |α̂_eff − α_eff| ≤ O(√(w_max / (ESS · Σw_i)) · log(1/δ)) with probability 1−δ. With imperfect detector calibration (expected calibration error ε), we apply a safety margin: τ_adj = τ + c·ε·(b+1) where c is estimated from the calibration curve.

If Phase 1 succeeds, we extend: w_i = (1−q(x_i))^b · α^{d̂_i}, incorporating estimated depth d̂_i as a multiplicative adjustment.

**Phase 4 — End-to-End Validation with Dual-Metric Evaluation (~145 H100-hours, with data generation explicitly budgeted).** We pretrain Qwen3-0.6B on FineWeb subsets (~2B tokens each) with controlled multi-generator contamination at 20%, 40%, and 60% synthetic fraction, comparing: (a) uniform weighting (no mitigation), (b) heuristic b via grid search à la Drayson et al. (EMNLP 2025), (c) our convergence-informed adaptive b.

*Dual-metric evaluation.* We evaluate on two metric classes: (i) *Theory-compatible metrics* — Wasserstein-1 distance and kernel MMD between model-generated text and held-out human reference text, computed in frozen SentenceTransformer embedding space. These satisfy Assumption A2, enabling direct validation of the convergence bound's prediction. (ii) *Practical metrics* — perplexity, output diversity (Self-BLEU, distinct-n), downstream accuracy (MMLU, HellaSwag). Comparing results across both metric classes reveals whether the theory's qualitative insight (optimal rate depends on effective real fraction) transfers to cross-entropy training, and characterizes the tightness of the theory-practice bridge.

Synthetic data generation budget: at 60% contamination on 2B tokens, we need ~1.2B synthetic tokens; across 3 contamination levels × 2 generators, total generation is ~4.8B tokens requiring ~40 H100-hours at ~30K tokens/s (generated once and shared across all training runs). Training: 9 core runs (3 contamination levels × 3 methods) + 2 cross-generator experiments + 2 ablations (depth-aware vs depth-agnostic) = 13 runs × ~7 H100-hours/run ≈ 91 H100-hours. Wasserstein/MMD evaluation: ~3 H100-hours (embedding extraction + distance computation post-hoc). Buffer: ~9 H100-hours for debugging and reruns.

**Total: ~163 H100-hours** (Phase 1: 12 + Phase 2: 6 + Phase 4 generation: 40 + Phase 4 training: 91 + Phase 4 eval: 3 + buffer: 9 + Phase 3 compute: 2), well within the ≤200 H100-hour hard constraint.

**Pre-registered success criteria:**

*Primary criterion (adaptive robustness):* Convergence-informed b achieves ≥90% of the perplexity improvement of the best per-condition grid-searched heuristic b, across ALL contamination levels simultaneously with a single adaptive schedule — demonstrating that convergence-informed calibration eliminates the need for expensive hyperparameter sweeps.

*Secondary criterion (theory validation):* Convergence-informed b achieves statistically significant improvement over heuristic b on at least one A2-compatible metric (Wasserstein-1 or MMD) at contamination ≥40%, validating that the convergence bound's insight translates to measurable distributional benefits.

*Tertiary criterion (absolute practical improvement):* At contamination ≥40%, convergence-informed b improves over best heuristic b by ≥3% perplexity reduction OR ≥2% downstream accuracy (MMLU/HellaSwag).
