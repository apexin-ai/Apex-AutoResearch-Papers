## Core Problem

Current inference efficiency methods optimize one sparsity dimension at a time—attention heads (Polar Sparsity), KV cache (ThinKV, R-KV, TriAttention), or MLP neurons (Deja Vu, WiSparse). While AMP (IJCNN 2025) demonstrates that distributing pruning across head+MLP jointly outperforms single-axis pruning at the same compression ratio (61.02% vs 37.63% attention-only or 56.93% MLP-only at 30% compression), and Spark Transformer (NeurIPS 2025, Google DeepMind) achieves compound sparsity across FFN (8% activation) and attention (256-token budget) simultaneously with 2.5x FLOP reduction and 1.4-1.8x wall-time speedup, no work has studied compound coordination of all three axes (head, MLP, KV) applied post-hoc to pretrained models during inference, nor whether reasoning-phase signals can guide this allocation. Spark Transformer embeds compound sparsity at training time via architectural modifications (statistical top-k), requiring training from scratch—making it inapplicable to the rapidly growing ecosystem of pretrained reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3, etc.). RAC (NeurIPS 2025) further establishes that reasoning models are fundamentally different from instruction models for compression: standard pruning calibrated on input reconstruction degrades reasoning performance far more severely, and reasoning-specific calibration (on-policy CoT traces) is needed to preserve quality—motivating phase- and reasoning-aware sparsity allocation rather than generic compression. Phase Transitions (npj AI 2026) establishes that each compression axis has a Phase Transition Point (PTP) beyond which performance collapses exponentially—structured pruning PTP at ~45%, unstructured at ~55-65%, low-rank decomposition at ~30%—and that different compression sources exploit orthogonal redundancy, enabling compound compression within the joint safe region. The Sparse Frontier (Cohere, arXiv:2504.17768) provides large-scale empirical evidence that sparsity tolerance is fundamentally phase-dependent: decoding tolerates higher sparsity than prefilling, with significant task-dependent variability—simple retrieval tasks tolerate sparsity up to 0.95 while aggregation and multi-hop tasks degrade at sparsity as low as 0.5-0.67, and optimal strategy varies by both task and phase—establishing that phase-aware allocation is not merely beneficial but necessary to avoid leaving efficiency on the table or catastrophic failure on demanding tasks. The fundamental open problem is algorithmic: given three orthogonal sparsity axes with independent PTPs and task- and phase-dependent tolerance profiles, how should a controller allocate per-axis budgets conditioned on reasoning phase and layer-local features to maximize aggregate compression while keeping each axis within its quality-safe region? Single-axis methods—even sophisticated learned ones like SeerAttention-R (ICLR 2026), which achieves up to 8.6x attention kernel speedup at 90% sparsity via self-distilled gating—push one dimension aggressively and hit the PTP wall on hard tasks; a phase-aware compound controller can distribute the same total compression budget across axes—operating well below each individual PTP—to achieve a strictly better quality-efficiency Pareto frontier.

## Opportunity / Why Now

Six converging lines of evidence motivate a compound controller approach: (1) ThinKV (ICLR 2026 Oral) demonstrated that reasoning traces decompose into distinct thought phases (Reasoning, Execution, Transition) identifiable via attention sparsity patterns with KDE-derived thresholds, and these phases exhibit markedly different KV cache requirements—establishing phase as a meaningful signal for at least one sparsity axis; (2) Polar Sparsity (NeurIPS 2025) revealed that attention head sparsity is stable and batch-invariant while MLP sparsity diminishes under batching as active neuron sets converge toward dense—critically, reasoning models typically operate at batch_size=1 with long generation chains, exactly the regime where MLP sparsity remains effective, and a compound controller should adaptively shift budget across axes as the inference regime changes; (3) RLKV (ICLR 2026 Workshop) showed only a small fraction of heads is essential for reasoning quality, and 'Doing More With Less' (arXiv April 2026) proved that carefully allocated layer-wise sparsity can even improve reasoning via test-time scaling, demonstrating that allocation strategy matters more than sparsity level; (4) Phase Transitions (npj AI 2026) showed that different compression sources exploit orthogonal redundancy, meaning their PTPs form a joint safe region where compound compression works—a phase-aware controller navigating this region can achieve better Pareto efficiency than any single axis pushed to its individual limit; (5) The Sparse Frontier (Cohere, arXiv:2504.17768) provides comprehensive empirical validation across six methods and nine tasks that sparsity tolerance is both phase-dependent (decoding vs prefilling) and task-dependent (with variability spanning from 0.5 for aggregation tasks to 0.95 for simple retrieval), with optimal strategy varying across both dimensions—directly motivating phase- and task-conditioned allocation rather than fixed budgets; (6) SeerAttention-R (ICLR 2026, Microsoft) demonstrates that learned sparse attention gating via self-distillation achieves near-lossless reasoning accuracy at 90% attention sparsity with up to 8.6x kernel speedup on H100—validating learned routing for attention at scale and providing state-of-the-art attention sparsity kernels (TileLang), but operating on a single axis without cross-axis coordination. Additionally, Flux Attention (arXiv April 2026) validated that lightweight learned routers can dynamically allocate attention sparsity per layer with only 12h training, achieving 2.8x prefill speedup and 2.0x decode speedup. EvoSparse (ACL 2026 Main) validates that attention sparsity should evolve across decoding steps and layers via cross-step importance accumulation, demonstrating the temporal dimension of sparsity adaptation on a single axis. RAC (NeurIPS 2025) provides direct empirical evidence that reasoning models require reasoning-specific compression strategies—standard pruning calibrated on input data degrades reasoning far more than instruction-following, and on-policy CoT calibration preserves up to 95% of original accuracy at 50% sparsity—strengthening the case for phase- and reasoning-aware allocation. The gap: nobody has built a compound controller that coordinates multiple axes jointly, conditioned on reasoning phase, to exploit the orthogonal structure of the joint PTP safe region. The strongest single-axis methods (SeerAttention-R for attention, DynTS/ThinKV for KV cache, WiSparse for MLP) each achieve impressive results in isolation, but their individual PTP walls on hard tasks leave significant compound compression potential untapped.

## Landscape (SOTA & Limitations)

Polar Sparsity (NeurIPS 2025) reveals that MLP contextual sparsity diminishes with batch size as active neuron sets converge toward dense, and pivots to attention head sparsity which remains stable and batch-invariant; custom Triton kernels for selective head attention achieve 2.2x end-to-end speedup; open-source code available. ThinKV (ICLR 2026 Oral) classifies reasoning phases via attention sparsity patterns (normalized attention scores thresholded at 1% of row-wise maximum) using Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to identify phase boundaries; the tri-modal sparsity distribution yields three phases—Reasoning (systematic thinking), Execution (calculation/code generation), and Transition (uncertainty/backtracking)—with distinct sparsity levels (Transition highest, Execution lowest); applies thought-adaptive hybrid quantization-eviction per phase for KV cache only, achieving 5.8x throughput with <5% KV retention. SeerAttention-R (ICLR 2026, Microsoft) learns block-sparse attention via self-distilled gating: a lightweight AttnGate module (trained with frozen backbone on 0.4B tokens, ~12h on 4 MI300x GPUs) predicts which KV blocks to attend to per head, achieving near-lossless reasoning accuracy at 90% sparsity and up to 8.6x attention kernel speedup over FlashAttention-3 on H100 via TileLang-optimized kernels; pre-trained AttnGates available for Qwen3-4B/8B/14B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B (no 7B variant available); operates on the attention axis only without MLP or KV cache coordination. Spark Transformer (NeurIPS 2025, Google DeepMind) achieves compound sparsity across FFN (8% activation) and attention (256-token budget per query) simultaneously using statistical top-k for hardware-efficient thresholding, yielding 2.5x FLOP reduction and 1.4-1.8x wall-time speedup at 2B scale; however, sparsity is embedded at training time via architectural modifications, requiring training from scratch—making it inapplicable to existing pretrained reasoning models and fundamentally different from post-hoc inference-time approaches. RaaS (ACL 2025 Findings) identifies milestone-token attention patterns in reasoning and achieves O(L) time and memory, but handles attention/KV only. R-KV (NeurIPS 2025) provides redundancy-aware KV compression achieving 6.6x throughput for reasoning models; plug-and-play with open-source code. DynTS (arXiv January 2026) introduces a learned 3-layer MLP importance predictor (trained with frozen backbone on MATH data) for dynamic thinking-token selection, achieving 3.3-5.7x KV reduction with essentially lossless reasoning accuracy; the predictor identifies decision-critical thinking tokens via supervised attention-weight accumulation, providing reasoning-aware KV eviction complementary to ThinKV's phase-based approach and R-KV's redundancy-based approach. RLKV (ICLR 2026 Workshop) uses reinforcement learning to identify reasoning-critical heads for KV compression. RAC (NeurIPS 2025) demonstrates that standard pruning degrades reasoning models far more severely than instruction models, and introduces reasoning-aware calibration via on-policy CoT trace reconstruction, preserving up to 95% of original accuracy at 50% sparsity on 7B models vs 74.4% with standard C4 calibration; directly motivates the need for reasoning-specific compression strategies. FASA (ICLR 2026) exploits frequency-chunk-level sparsity in RoPE for KV cache compression with 2.56x speedup. LazyEviction (2025) captures token importance recurrence for 50-70% KV reduction in long reasoning. AMP (IJCNN 2025) prunes attention heads and MLP neurons jointly, showing that distributing pruning across both axes outperforms single-axis pruning (61.02% vs 37.63%/56.93% at 30% compression), but operates at the weight-pruning level without phase awareness or KV cache coordination. SLiM (ICML 2025, NVIDIA) achieves compound weight compression via one-shot quantization + semi-structured sparsity + low-rank approximation with mathematical error correction, delivering 4.3x layer-wise speedup; addresses weight compression rather than inference-time dynamic sparsity. TriAttention (April 2026) exploits pre-RoPE Q/K concentration for KV compression in reasoning models, achieving 10.7x memory reduction, 6.3x throughput on MATH-500 (1,405 vs 223 tokens/s), and 2.5x throughput at matched accuracy on AIME25 with a vLLM plugin; concurrent KV-only work. LongFlow (March 2026) fuses FlashAttention, importance estimation, and token eviction into a single Triton kernel, achieving 11.8x throughput; demonstrates the kernel-fusion approach. Phase Transitions in LLM Compression (npj AI 2026) establishes Phase Transition Points (PTPs) per compression axis—structured pruning PTP at ~45%, unstructured at ~55-65%, low-rank decomposition at ~30%—and shows that axes exploit orthogonal redundancy, enabling compound compression to 10% of original size within the joint safe region; provides the theoretical framework for why coordinated per-axis allocation matters. WiSparse (Feb 2026) introduces weight-aware mixed-granularity activation sparsity with coarse-to-fine block/layer allocation, achieving 21.4% end-to-end speedup at 50% sparsity; addresses MLP sparsity allocation but not cross-axis coordination. DepthKV (April 2026) demonstrates that layer-dependent KV budget allocation consistently outperforms uniform pruning, reinforcing that allocation strategy matters. Flux Attention (April 2026) trains a lightweight layer-level router (frozen backbone, Gumbel-Softmax) to dynamically route between full and sparse attention per layer, achieving 2.8x prefill speedup and 2.0x decode speedup with 12h training on 8×A800 GPUs—validates learned routing for single-axis sparsity but does not coordinate across axes. EvoSparse (ACL 2026 Main) implements dynamic sparse attention that evolves across decoding steps via cross-step token importance accumulation and cross-layer index reuse, demonstrating that sparsity patterns should adapt temporally during generation; attention-only (single axis) without MLP or KV cache coordination; code repository not yet released (as of May 2026). The Sparse Frontier (Cohere, arXiv:2504.17768) comprehensively evaluates six sparse attention methods across nine tasks at sparsity up to 0.95, finding significant task-dependent variability in sparsity tolerance—simple retrieval tasks tolerate 0.95 sparsity while aggregation and multi-hop reasoning tasks degrade at sparsity as low as 0.5-0.67—and that decoding generally tolerates higher sparsity than prefilling, with optimal strategy being both task- and phase-specific, empirically motivating phase-aware allocation approaches. KV Pareto (Dec 2025) maps Pareto frontiers across KV quantization + weight quantization configurations, achieving 68-78% memory reduction with 1-3% accuracy loss—validates that joint optimization of complementary techniques achieves better Pareto frontiers than single-technique optimization. SpeContext (ASPLOS 2026) achieves 24.89x throughput via speculative context sparsity. Thinking Sparks! (2025) reveals functionally specialized attention heads in reasoning models. 'Doing More With Less' (April 2026) shows unstructured pruning with proper layer-wise allocation can improve test-time scaling. No published or concurrent work jointly orchestrates multiple kernel-backed sparsity dimensions post-hoc on pretrained reasoning models, conditioned on reasoning phase via a learned compound controller. Spark Transformer achieves compound sparsity but at training time; SeerAttention-R achieves sophisticated learned routing but on a single axis; PACS targets the intersection—post-hoc compound coordination across axes with phase conditioning.

## Proposed Approach

We propose PACS (Phase-Aware Compound Sparsity), a learned compound controller that coordinates two primary kernel-backed sparsity axes—attention sparsity and KV cache retention—plus a supplementary MLP neuron sparsity axis for FLOPs reduction in low-batch reasoning, all conditioned on reasoning phase. The framing is deliberately honest: attention and KV axes deliver wall-clock speedup backed by existing kernels, while MLP sparsity provides additional FLOPs and memory reduction in the batch_size=1 reasoning regime without current wall-clock kernel support. The key insight: at a fixed quality budget (e.g., <3% accuracy degradation), PACS distributes compression across orthogonal axes such that no single axis exceeds its Phase Transition Point, achieving higher aggregate compression than any single axis pushed to its individual limit. The target is not higher raw throughput than SeerAttention-R or Polar Sparsity at unconstrained quality loss, but strictly better throughput at the SAME quality constraint—or equivalently, lower quality degradation at the same throughput target. The study has four stages: (1) Phase-sparsity profiling: segment reasoning traces from DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B and Qwen3-8B into ThinKV's three phases (Reasoning, Execution, Transition) using their established methodology: normalized attention scores thresholded at 1% of row-wise maximum, KDE over the resulting sparsity distribution to identify the tri-modal structure, and phase boundaries at KDE local minima. We adopt ThinKV's 3-phase taxonomy directly rather than introducing additional phases, as their KDE analysis shows a clear tri-modal distribution in reasoning traces—adding a 4th phase would require empirical evidence of a tetra-modal structure that has not been demonstrated. For each phase, measure head activation importance (attention output norms), MLP neuron magnitude distributions, and KV token attention scores across all layers. Characterize cross-axis correlations per phase—specifically testing whether phases that are KV-sparse also show higher head sparsity and/or MLP sparsity, or whether axes vary independently (confirming the orthogonality that enables compound compression). (2) Compound controller (primary contribution): train per-layer single-FC routers that predict joint sparsity triplets {head_mask, mlp_sparsity_ratio, kv_retention_budget} from layer-local features (attention output norms, MLP activation statistics, phase embedding). Following Flux Attention's demonstrated approach of training lightweight routers with frozen backbone (Gumbel-Softmax for differentiable allocation, noting their 2.0x decode speedup validates single-axis routing feasibility), the controller targets compound coordination as its novel contribution. Train via knowledge distillation on ~2K calibration traces where phase-optimal triplets are found by global per-phase grid search: for each of 3 identified phases (Reasoning, Execution, Transition), search over 5 levels per axis (5³=125 candidates per phase, 375 total evaluations, ~22h). The controller's value is validated by comparing: (a) coordinated compound (phase-aware joint prediction) vs (b) naive compound (independent per-axis optimization applied simultaneously) vs (c) phase-agnostic compound (joint prediction without phase signal) vs (d) single-axis methods at equivalent quality degradation—including SeerAttention-R (the strongest single-axis learned attention baseline, with pre-trained AttnGates for Qwen3-8B), Polar Sparsity (head-level), ThinKV (phase-aware KV), and DynTS (learned KV predictor)—plus (e) a step-decaying sparsity baseline that linearly increases the sparsity ratio across generation steps (capturing the temporal adaptation insight from EvoSparse at a coarser granularity without requiring its full cross-step importance accumulation mechanism, whose code is not yet publicly available). EvoSparse's per-step temporal adaptation is discussed qualitatively in related work as complementary to PACS's per-phase allocation: EvoSparse adapts within a phase at step-level granularity on a single axis, while PACS coordinates across axes at phase-level granularity—these operate at different granularities and could in principle be composed. The compound controller's advantage is measured as the Pareto gap: at iso-quality, how much more compression/throughput does coordination provide? (3) Integration with existing open-source kernels: For attention sparsity, we support two backends to demonstrate PACS's modularity: (a) Polar Sparsity's open-source Triton Selective Head Attention kernels (github.com/susavlsh10/Polar-Sparsity) for head-level selection, and (b) SeerAttention-R's open-source TileLang block-sparse decoding kernels (github.com/microsoft/SeerAttention) with their pre-trained AttnGates for Qwen3-8B, which achieve up to 8.6x attention kernel speedup at 90% sparsity. Since pre-trained AttnGates are available for Qwen3-8B but not for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (the closest available variant is DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B), the dual-backend comparison (Polar Sparsity head-level vs SeerAttention-R block-sparse under compound coordination) is conducted on Qwen3-8B, while the primary model uses Polar Sparsity kernels exclusively. Training AttnGates for the primary model would require ~12h additional compute on 4 GPUs, exceeding the budget; this limitation is explicitly acknowledged. The dual-backend design validates that PACS's compound coordination provides Pareto improvement regardless of the underlying attention sparsity mechanism—whether coarse-grained head selection or fine-grained block-sparse attention—even though this comparison is limited to the validation model. For KV cache compression, use R-KV's open-source implementation (github.com/Zefan-Cai/R-KV) for attention-score-based KV eviction. These axes constitute the kernel-backed compound that delivers wall-clock throughput gains. For MLP sparsity, apply standard PyTorch top-k activation selection; MLP contribution is reported separately as FLOPs/memory reduction with explicit acknowledgment that wall-clock MLP speedup requires future kernel fusion (e.g., WiSparse-style or LongFlow-style). MLP sparsity is effective specifically in the batch_size=1 long-generation regime where Polar Sparsity confirms MLP sparsity persists; the controller adaptively reduces MLP sparsity allocation under higher batch sizes, where this axis naturally becomes ineffective. All throughput figures decompose into: kernel-backed speedup (attention+KV) and supplementary FLOPs reduction (MLP), reported transparently. (4) Comprehensive evaluation: dense inference, SeerAttention-R (learned single-axis attention, using their pre-trained gates and TileLang kernels; evaluated on Qwen3-8B only where pre-trained AttnGates are available), Polar Sparsity-style head-only (phase-agnostic, using their kernels), ThinKV-style KV-only (phase-aware), DynTS-style KV-only (learned predictor), step-decaying attention sparsity (temporal single-axis baseline), naive compound (three axes applied independently), and PACS coordinated compound. Primary model: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (7B, single GPU; 7 baselines excluding SeerAttention-R). Validation model: Qwen3-8B (8 baselines including SeerAttention-R; evaluated on top-5 benchmarks: MATH-500, MMLU, WikiText-103 perplexity, HumanEval, GPQA, plus the dual attention backend comparison). Benchmarks (primary model, full suite): perplexity (WikiText-103, C4), MMLU, HumanEval, MiniLongBench (ACL 2025 Outstanding Paper Award; 237 samples with 0.97 rank correlation to full LongBench at 4.5% cost), MT-Bench; reasoning benchmarks: MATH-500, AIME 2025, GPQA; RULER with reduced scope: 3 representative tasks × 3 context lengths (8K, 32K, 64K) × 100 examples = 900 examples per config (~1h/config). Evaluation methodology distinguishes two regimes: (i) Throughput measurement uses trace sharing—generate once with the dense model and measure wall-clock time under each sparsity configuration on the same trace, amortizing generation cost and ensuring identical workloads for fair throughput comparison. (ii) Accuracy evaluation on reasoning benchmarks (MATH-500, AIME 2025, GPQA) requires fresh end-to-end generation under each sparsity configuration, since sparsity alters attention patterns, which changes token generation and can change final answer correctness. To manage the cost of fresh generation across methods: MATH-500 uses full fresh generation for all methods; AIME 2025 (30 problems, but long traces ~1000+ tokens) uses full fresh generation for all methods given the small problem count; GPQA uses full fresh generation for all methods. Perplexity benchmarks (WikiText-103, C4) use standard per-token evaluation which is naturally method-independent. MMLU and HumanEval use standard few-shot/zero-shot evaluation with fresh generation. Key evaluation metric: Pareto frontier plots (quality degradation vs throughput/compression) demonstrating that PACS achieves points inaccessible to single-axis or uncoordinated methods. Key ablations (4 dimensions): phase-conditioned vs phase-agnostic controller (isolating phase signal value), per-axis contribution analysis (kernel-backed attention+KV vs supplementary MLP), PTP boundary analysis (does coordination effectively expand the joint safe region), and batch-size sensitivity (controller behavior as MLP sparsity diminishes). Inter-axis correlation strength is reported in the profiling results (Stage 1); the attention backend comparison is part of the main Qwen3-8B evaluation; phase classification robustness across mathematical vs coding vs general reasoning is discussed qualitatively. Target: at iso-quality (<3% degradation), PACS achieves 1.5-2.5x wall-clock throughput from coordinated attention+KV allocation (vs 1.3-1.5x for best single-axis at same quality constraint; upper bound leveraging SeerAttention-R's more efficient kernels on Qwen3-8B), plus 20-40% additional MLP FLOPs reduction in batch_s
