# R1-W1: Granularity of r_crit bound

**Weakness**: The critical mixing ratio is localized only to (0.25, 0.3], which is a coarse bound.

**Response**:

We agree that finer resolution (e.g., r = 0.27, 0.28, 0.29) would narrow the bound, and we note this as natural future work. However, we believe the current resolution is sufficient for both the paper's scientific claims and practical recommendations, for three reasons.

First, the practical recommendation is insensitive to the exact r_crit value. Practitioners need a safe threshold, not a precise critical point: setting r > 0.3 prevents spectral collapse regardless of whether r_crit is 0.26 or 0.29. The 5-percentage-point gap between our upper bound (0.3) and the nearest sub-critical point (0.25) already provides a safety margin.

Second, the qualitative boundary is robust across seeds and smoothing parameters. Both r = 0.25 seeds exhibit Type II trajectories (transient EMA-smoothed SLV violation at Gen 1--2 followed by sustained recovery), while both r = 0.3 seeds show Type III (monotonic expansion on the cross-seed mean). Recomputing the EMA for alpha in {0.3, 0.5, 0.7} yields the same interval in all cases (Section 4.4, paragraph 6).

Third, the phase diagram's contribution is the trajectory typology itself---distinguishing collapse, transitional, and stable regimes---rather than a precise critical constant. The three-type partition and the signal attenuation finding hold at any r_crit within (0.25, 0.3].
