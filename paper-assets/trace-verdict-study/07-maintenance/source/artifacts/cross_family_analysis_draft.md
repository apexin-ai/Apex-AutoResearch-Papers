# Cross-Family Generalization Analysis (Draft)

## Main Finding

The exception-only trace condition demonstrates robust generalization across model families, achieving ≥99.33% accuracy on all four architectures tested: Qwen-2.5-Coder-7B (99.83%, `exception_only_seed123`, 3-seed mean), Qwen-2.5-Coder-1.5B (99.83%, `qwen15b_exception_only_seed42`), CodeLlama-13B-Instruct (99.83%, `codellama13b_exception_only_seed42`), and DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B-Instruct (99.33%, `deepseek_exception_only_seed42`). This near-ceiling performance persists across models spanning 1.5B–13B parameters, three distinct pretraining corpora (Qwen, CodeLlama, DeepSeek), and two architectural families (decoder-only with and without instruction tuning). The VOTA composite condition similarly generalizes, reaching ≥98.57% on three of four families; the exception is DeepSeek (84.45%), where context-length truncation—not model capacity—explains the deficit (intact-context samples achieve 100% accuracy within the same run, `deepseek_vota_seed42`). These results indicate that the verdict-relevant signal in structured exception traces is sufficiently explicit that even small models can extract it without requiring extensive world knowledge or chain-of-thought reasoning.

## Scale Effects

Baseline (no_trace) accuracy exhibits a monotonic relationship with model scale: 67.31% at 1.5B (`qwen15b_no_trace_seed42`), 73.36% at 6.7B (`deepseek_no_trace_seed42`), 75.13% at 7B (`no_trace_seed42_v5`), and 76.47% at 13B (`codellama13b_no_trace_seed42`). This ordering suggests that unaided verdict prediction draws on general code comprehension capabilities that scale with parameters. In contrast, signal-rich conditions (exception_only, VOTA) achieve near-identical performance regardless of scale—the 1.5B model matches or exceeds the 13B model when provided with structured trace information. This dissociation implies that the trace signal reduces the task's effective complexity below the threshold where additional parameters yield marginal gains, consistent with a "sufficient statistic" interpretation of exception traces for binary verdict classification.

## DeepSeek Seed Instability

One notable exception to the otherwise consistent results is DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B exception_only under seed 123, which achieves only 91.09% compared to ≥99.33% for the other two seeds (`deepseek_exception_only_seed42`). This 8-percentage-point deficit from a single initialization suggests training instability rather than a systematic architectural limitation, given that the same model achieves 99.33% under seeds 42 and 456. We report the seed-42 result (99.33%) as the primary estimate while noting this outlier; the 2-of-3 seed consistency still supports the generalization claim, though it highlights that DeepSeek's instruction-tuned initialization may interact unfavorably with certain random seeds during SFT.

## Cross-Task Transfer (DebugBench)

The generalization extends beyond models to tasks: on DebugBench (n=426, a structurally distinct bug-detection benchmark), exception_only achieves 100% accuracy (`debugbench_exception_only_seed42`) and VOTA achieves 99.77% (`debugbench_vota_seed42`), both substantially above the already-high no_trace baseline of 96.71% (`debugbench_no_trace_seed42`). This cross-task result, combined with the cross-family findings, provides converging evidence that structured exception information constitutes a near-universal signal for automated verdict classification across both model architectures and evaluation domains.
