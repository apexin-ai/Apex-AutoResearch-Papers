## Paper Summary

DisPeD fine-tunes DeBERTa-v3-base on an 8-class persuasion strategy classification task (multi-task with binary jailbreak intent), then aggregates per-turn [CLS] embeddings via mean pooling for conversation-level jailbreak detection. Trained exclusively on 240 synthetic Crescendo attack/benign pairs (Qwen3-8B), the system achieves F1=1.0 on unseen Deceptive Delight (DD) attacks where a vanilla DeBERTa+GRU baseline collapses to F1=0.284. Comprehensive ablations reveal that the sole critical ingredient is fine-tuning DeBERTa on persuasion-related text; label granularity (binary vs 9-class: 1pp gap), label correctness (scrambled vs proper: 1-6pp gap), temporal architecture (mean pooling vs GRU: 0pp gap), and multi-task weighting (all lambda values: 0pp gap) are all interchangeable. A TF-IDF+LR baseline achieves F1=0.944 on DD without any neural components.

## Consensus Strengths

**S1. Exceptionally honest and thorough ablation suite.** The paper tests four independent ablation axes (label quality, label granularity, loss weighting, temporal architecture) and honestly reports that the method reduces to domain-specific fine-tuning. Both reviewers called this "rare," "commendable," and the paper's strongest intellectual contribution. The scrambled-label finding (F1=0.935-0.990 OOD despite randomized labels) is genuinely informative about how domain-adaptive fine-tuning works.

**S2. Transparent positioning against TF-IDF.** Rather than hiding the strong lexical baseline (F1=0.944 on DD), the paper reports it prominently and narrows its claims accordingly. Both reviewers noted this intellectual honesty strengthens credibility, even as it constrains the claim space.

**S3. Large, real headline delta on DD.** The +72pp gap between treatment and vanilla DeBERTa baseline on DD is dramatic and difficult to dismiss. The vanilla baseline's monotonic degradation with conversation length (K=1: 0.575 to full: 0.284) is itself an interesting observation about how DD's interleaving structure actively misleads generic embeddings.

**S4. Perturbation robustness at K=1.** The 7pp gap over TF-IDF under adversarial paraphrasing at early detection (DisPeD: -2.6pp vs TF-IDF: -9.6pp vs baseline: -14.6pp) is a concrete, deployable advantage and the strongest differentiation point from TF-IDF.

**S5. LLM-as-judge comparison.** Showing GPT-5.5 achieves at most F1=0.45 (with 30-47% unparseable rate) provides useful practical context and strengthens the case for specialized detectors over content-level reasoning approaches.

## Consensus Weaknesses

### Critical Issues

**W1. Cannot distinguish persuasion-specific learning from domain exposure (Critical — both reviewers' #1 concern).**
The scrambled-label ablation is the paper's most contentious result. Scrambled labels reduce persuasion classification accuracy from 79.6% to 64.4% but downstream detection drops only 1.3pp IID and 6.5pp OOD. This raises a fundamental confound: is the encoder learning from persuasion strategy supervision (the paper's claim), or merely from exposure to persuasion-domain text during fine-tuning? Two missing controls are needed to resolve this:
- (a) Continued pretraining (MLM objective, no classification labels) on the same persuasion-annotated text corpus
- (b) Fine-tuning on an unrelated auxiliary task (sentiment, NLI) using the same text

Without these, the paper cannot support its central claim. Both reviewers independently identified this as the single issue that, if addressed, would most change their assessment. The paper also does not cite or engage with Gururangan et al. (ACL 2020, "Don't Stop Pretraining"), the canonical work on domain-adaptive pretraining.

**W2. All evaluation data is synthetic from the same pipeline (Critical).**
Training data (480 conversations), IID test data (n=72), and OOD test data (80 FITD + 80 DD) are all generated by Qwen3-8B through the authors' pipeline. Cross-attack "generalization" may partly reflect shared generation-pipeline artifacts (vocabulary register, sentence structure, conversational formality) rather than genuine robustness to novel attack types. Without evaluation on at least one source of real multi-turn attacks (ActorAttack/SafeMTData, real Crescendo logs, Li et al. 2024 red-teaming data), ecological validity is unestablished.

### Major Issues

**W3. TF-IDF at F1=0.944 substantially narrows the practical contribution.**
The headline +72pp advantage is computed against a vanilla DeBERTa baseline that is anomalously bad on DD (F1=0.284). Against TF-IDF, the practical margin is 5.6pp on DD at full conversation length. On n=80 (40 positive DD samples), this gap is not statistically distinguishable from zero. R2 framed this more fundamentally: if a bag-of-words baseline reaches 0.944, the task may be too easy in its current formulation. DisPeD also has higher FPR than TF-IDF on WildChat hard negatives (9.5% vs 4.8%).

**W4. The "novelty paradox" — ablations undermine the technical contribution.**
The paper presents a two-stage architecture with 8-class taxonomy, GRU aggregation, and multi-task loss, then systematically shows none of these components matter. What survives is "fine-tune DeBERTa on persuasion-related text, average [CLS] embeddings, classify." This is a standard transfer learning recipe. The ablation suite is the paper's greatest intellectual contribution, but it simultaneously reduces the method's novelty to an application insight (persuasion-domain fine-tuning transfers across attack types), not a methodological advance.

**W5. Ceiling effects from small test sets prevent meaningful comparison.**
F1=1.0 with zero variance across 3 seeds on n=72 IID and n=80 OOD. By the rule of three, the 95% upper bound on error rate is 7.2%, meaning true F1 could be as low as 0.93. The pervasive ceiling prevents distinguishing treatment, binary-collapse, scrambled-label, and different-lambda variants (all 0.99-1.0). The paper cannot distinguish "the method works perfectly" from "the test set is insufficiently challenging."

**W6. FITD saturation eliminates half the cross-attack evaluation.**
On FITD, all methods (treatment, baseline, TF-IDF) achieve F1 approximately 1.0. Cross-attack advantage materializes on exactly one OOD attack type (DD). With only one discriminating evaluation dataset, the generalization claim is fragile.

### Minor Issues

**W7. Related work gaps.** Missing citations: ActorAttack/SafeMTData (Ren et al. 2024), Li et al. 2024 ("LLM Defenses Are Not Robust"), Cialdini 2001 (foundational persuasion theory), Gururangan et al. 2020 ("Don't Stop Pretraining"). Placeholder citations for BIID and SafeDream (no proper bibliographic entries). Insufficient positioning against Jaipersaud/Xiao et al. 2025 (linear probes already extract persuasion dynamics from representations).

**W8. No human evaluation of interpretability claim.** Strategy trajectory analysis (Claim 2) lacks any user study or expert evaluation confirming the trajectories are interpretable or actionable in practice. Given that the ablations show the taxonomy is unnecessary for detection, the interpretability claim is the taxonomy's sole justification and needs empirical support.

**W9. LLM-as-judge baseline uses single zero-shot prompt only.** 30-47% unparseable rate undermines the comparison. Chain-of-thought, few-shot, or conversation-level analysis prompting was not tested. Under charitable correction (all unparseable assumed correct), GPT-5.5 could reach F1 approximately 0.7-0.8, narrowing the advantage.

## Key Questions for Authors

1. **The critical missing control:** Would continued pretraining (MLM objective, no classification head) on the same persuasion text corpus bridge the OOD gap? This single experiment would determine whether persuasion supervision or domain exposure drives the gains.

2. **Synthetic data confound:** Were DD and FITD test conversations generated by the same Qwen3-8B model and annotation pipeline as training data? What prevents shared generation artifacts from inflating cross-attack transfer?

3. **Statistical significance:** Can you provide exact TP/FP/FN/TN counts and exact binomial confidence intervals for Tables 2-5? Is the 5.6pp DisPeD-TF-IDF gap on DD (n=80) statistically significant?

4. **Topic anchoring in OOD:** Were benign conversations in the OOD test sets topic-anchored to OOD attack topics? Could the DD result partly reflect topic divergence detectable by any encoder?

5. **Per-turn safety classifiers:** Why were Llama Guard and WildGuard not evaluated as baselines? Running them on concatenated first-K turns would be informative.
