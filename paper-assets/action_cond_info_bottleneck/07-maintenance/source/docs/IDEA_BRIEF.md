## Core Problem

VLA visual encoders produce token sequences where task-irrelevant tokens carry spurious correlations with environment-specific factors — correlations that collapse under visual perturbations. Existing token reduction methods operate exclusively in one of two regimes: (1) HARD PRUNING for efficiency or clean accuracy (VLA-IAP, LightVLA, GridS, DTP, Compressor-VLA, SP-VLA, VLA-Pruner, ETA-VLA, EcoVLA, FocusVLA, ADP), where post-hoc compression actively HURTS adversarial robustness by concentrating adversarial signal on retained tokens (arXiv 2601.21531, 2601.12042); or (2) FIXED MODULATION for robustness (FTM), which applies input-independent global affine parameters to all tokens uniformly. The critical missing regime is INPUT-DEPENDENT, CONTINUOUS token intervention — learned per-token importance weights that adapt to scene content and are trained to be invariant to visual perturbations via an information bottleneck objective. This gap is especially significant in COMPACT TOKEN SPACES: models like SmolVLA use only 64 visual tokens (via 9× pixel-shuffle), where hard pruning carries high risk (each removed token covers ~50×50 pixels mixing foreground and background) but continuous reweighting can suppress nuisance signal without information loss. The position paper 'Token Reduction Should Go Beyond Efficiency' (arXiv 2505.18227) argues this thesis but provides no concrete VLA instantiation. DTR (arXiv 2505.17132) demonstrates that dynamic per-token reweighting improves VLM robustness against jailbreak attacks via KV-cache optimization — validating token reweighting as a robustness lever — but operates on safety (adversarial text-image attacks) rather than physical perturbation robustness in embodied manipulation, and lacks IB grounding. We show that token-level CONTINUOUS intervention CAN improve VLA robustness — when the scorer is jointly trained with an information bottleneck objective and multi-view augmentation consistency, producing perturbation-invariant importance weights that suppress nuisance-carrying tokens without the information loss risks of hard pruning.

## Opportunity / Why Now

LIBERO-Plus (CVPR 2026) reveals catastrophic robustness gaps across VLA models, with current SOTA at SRPO 82.1% avg. SmolVLA, though untested on LIBERO-Plus, exhibits severe vulnerability to visual corruption — dropping from 43% to 0–16% under sensor artifacts (CRT, arXiv 2602.01158) — establishing that compact models are at least as fragile as larger ones. FTM (arXiv 2512.02902) demonstrates that even a simple global affine transformation on visual tokens recovers viewpoint robustness (48.5% → 87.1% on π0.5 with 4K params), proving token-level intervention is a viable robustness lever — but FTM's input-independent, fixed-parameter design cannot adapt to scene content and varies in effectiveness across perturbation types. BC-IB (ICML 2025) demonstrates that IB at the representation level improves clean accuracy but explicitly acknowledges robustness to domain shifts as an open problem. TCA (ICCV 2025) shows token condensation improves distribution shift robustness by up to 21.4% in classification, supporting the token-intervention-for-robustness thesis but operating training-free and task-agnostic. DTR (arXiv 2505.17132) demonstrates that dynamic per-token reweighting via KV-cache optimization defends VLMs against jailbreak attacks — validating per-token reweighting as a robustness mechanism but in a fundamentally different domain (adversarial safety vs. physical perturbation invariance) and without information-theoretic grounding. The UNEXPLOITED GAP has two dimensions: (A) applying IB at the TOKEN level with ADAPTIVE, INPUT-DEPENDENT intervention conditioned on ACTION prediction, using MULTI-VIEW augmentation consistency for perturbation invariance; and (B) studying the intervention spectrum from CONTINUOUS REWEIGHTING to HARD PRUNING, which is critical for compact token spaces where hard pruning is risky. No existing method provides both IB-grounded scoring and a principled choice between soft and hard token intervention — a combination that provides per-timestep, content-adaptive robustness enhancement with information-theoretic motivation.

## Landscape (SOTA & Limitations)

The VLA token intervention literature spans efficiency, accuracy-driven selection, modulation, and robustness approaches, but a critical gap exists: no method provides input-dependent, IB-grounded token intervention with both soft and hard operating modes for robustness. EFFICIENCY-FOCUSED PRUNING: VLA-IAP (Mar 2026) achieves 97.8% on LIBERO with 1.54x speedup via training-free interaction-aligned pruning; LightVLA (ICRA 2026) uses differentiable Gumbel softmax selection reducing FLOPs by 59.1%; GridS (ICML 2026) performs differentiable grid resampling; Compressor-VLA uses instruction-guided dual compression; DTP prunes distracting tokens for accuracy not perturbation robustness; SP-VLA (ICLR 2026) jointly schedules models and prunes tokens; VLA-Pruner (arXiv 2511.16449) uses dual-level importance; ETA-VLA (arXiv 2603.25766) performs temporal fusion with intra-LLM sparsification; EcoVLA (arXiv 2602.00780) applies environment-aware adaptive pruning; OneWM-VLA (arXiv 2605.07931) compresses each view to one token. ACTION-CONDITIONED PRUNING: ADP (arXiv 2509.22093) integrates action-aware trajectory gating with text-driven token selection, dynamically adjusting token retention based on manipulation dynamics — achieving 1.35× speedup on OpenVLA with ≤0.9% accuracy drop. ADP is the closest efficiency-focused method to ACIB in its action-conditioning mechanism, but differs fundamentally: ADP is training-free and targets efficiency (no IB objective, no augmentation consistency, no robustness evaluation under visual perturbations), while ACIB is trained with IB+consistency objectives targeting perturbation invariance. ACCURACY-DRIVEN TOKEN SELECTION: FocusVLA (arXiv 2603.28740) dynamically selects task-relevant visual tokens via cross-attention-based top-K selection, achieving 98.7% on LIBERO with 256 of 512 tokens — but uses attention-magnitude scores without IB objectives, augmentation consistency, or robustness evaluation. Its attention-based scores are themselves susceptible to perturbation-induced drift. TOKEN-LEVEL MODULATION: FTM (arXiv 2512.02902) applies an input-independent global affine transformation F̂=(1+γ)⊙F+β, achieving camera 87.1%, lighting 96.0%, texture 96.0%, noise 93.6% on LIBERO-V with only 4K params — remarkably effective but limited by input-independence (same transformation regardless of scene content), no token selection, and no IB grounding. FLA extends FTM with low-rank ViT updates (4.7M params, 90.8%). DYNAMIC TOKEN REWEIGHTING: DTR (arXiv 2505.17132) dynamically adjusts visual token weights via KV-cache optimization for VLM jailbreak defense — the first per-token reweighting method validated for robustness. DTR validates the mechanism (dynamic per-token weights improve robustness) but differs from ACIB in domain (adversarial safety vs. physical perturbation), objective (safety-relevant distributional shift vs. IB compression + augmentation consistency), and architecture (KV-cache optimization in VLMs vs. learned scorer in VLAs). AUGMENTATION-CONSISTENT TOKEN METHODS: SimPrune (ICLR 2025) enforces cross-branch cosine similarity matching for token-pair pruning during SSL pretraining — targets training efficiency, not deployment robustness. TCA (ICCV 2025) demonstrates training-free token condensation improves distribution shift robustness by up to 21.4% on CIFAR-100-C — supports the token-intervention-for-robustness thesis but operates training-free, at test time, without action conditioning or IB objectives. IB FOR ROBOTICS: BC-IB (ICML 2025) applies IB at the REPRESENTATION level in behavior cloning — improves clean accuracy by +5.6% but does not operate at token granularity and explicitly notes domain-shift robustness as unstudied. IBTM (OpenReview) combines IB with learnable token merging for ViT efficiency but targets FLOPs reduction without action conditioning or robustness evaluation. MULTI-VIEW IB: DRIBO (ICML 2022) uses augmentation-generated paired views with contrastive MIB objective for robust RL representations — directly inspiring our approach but operating at representation level. MIB (Federici et al., ICLR 2020) provides the theoretical foundation. VLA ROBUSTNESS: RobustVLA (arXiv 2511.01331) applies Jacobian + smoothness regularization during online RL post-training (~82.5% on observation perturbations). CRT (arXiv 2602.01158) uses a corruption restoration transformer as preprocessing — shows SmolVLA drops from 43% to 0–16% under sensor corruption, establishing baseline vulnerability. SRPO achieves 82.1 on LIBERO-Plus (current SOTA). AVA-VLA (arXiv 2511.18960) introduces active visual attention that dynamically reweights visual tokens (74.7% on LIBERO-Plus) — relevant as it also performs dynamic token reweighting, but uses POMDP-motivated attention reweighting without IB grounding, augmentation consistency, or soft/hard mode comparison. COUNTER-EVIDENCE: arXiv 2601.21531 and 2601.12042 demonstrate that POST-HOC token compression hurts adversarial robustness — but these exclusively evaluate training-free compression with attention-based importance scores. This motivates our TRAINED scorer and our emphasis on SOFT REWEIGHTING as a safer alternative to hard pruning, especially in compact token spaces. Fischer et al. (2021) show VIB's apparent adversarial robustness is partly gradient obfuscation — motivating our empirical rather than certified robustness framing. POSITION PAPER: 'Token Reduction Should Go Beyond Efficiency' (arXiv 2505.18227) argues the thesis — ACIB is the first concrete VLA instantiation with both soft and hard intervention modes.

## Proposed Approach

We propose ACIB (Action-Conditioned Information Bottleneck), a method that applies IB-grounded token intervention for VLA robustness with two operating modes: ACIB-S (soft continuous reweighting) and ACIB-H (hard top-k pruning). Both modes share a unified training procedure; only inference-time behavior differs. This dual-mode design directly addresses the central research question: IN COMPACT TOKEN SPACES, DOES HARD PRUNING OR SOFT REWEIGHTING BETTER LEVERAGE IB OBJECTIVES FOR ROBUSTNESS? ARCHITECTURE: A lightweight 2-layer MLP scorer (~50K params) is trained atop SmolVLA's frozen SigLIP vision encoder (SmolVLM-2: SigLIP + SmolLM2, 450M total params, 64 visual tokens per frame via 3×3 pixel shuffle). The scorer outputs per-token logits s_i. During training, these are relaxed via Gumbel-sigmoid to produce continuous importance weights w_i ∈ (0,1). The downstream action head receives weighted tokens V_weighted = V ⊙ w. At inference, the modes diverge: ACIB-S uses w_i = σ(s_i) directly as continuous weights (all tokens retained, nuisance-carrying tokens suppressed toward zero); ACIB-H applies hard top-k selection based on s_i (k tokens retained, rest discarded). UNIFIED TRAINING LOSS: L = L_action(f(V ⊙ w), A) + α·D_KL(q(w|V) || p_0(w)) + β·||S(V) - S(V')||². The first term maximizes action-prediction accuracy from reweighted tokens. The second term is a VIB-style token-level bottleneck: q(w|V) is the per-token Bernoulli distribution parameterized by the scorer via Gumbel-sigmoid, and p_0(w) is a Bernoulli prior with target sparsity rate k — this forces compression by pushing many weights toward zero. The third term is the CROSS-VIEW SELECTION CONSISTENCY loss: each observation X is paired with an augmented view X' generated via domain randomization (camera jitter ±15°, color/lighting perturbation, background texture swap using LIBERO's built-in randomization). Both views pass through SmolVLA's frozen SigLIP encoder. The consistency loss penalizes differences in the scorer's output logits S(·) across augmented views, forcing the scorer to be INVARIANT to augmentation-defined nuisance factors. This uses only standard KL divergence and MSE — no neural MI estimation, sidestepping bias/variance issues (Poole et al., ICML 2019). WHY DUAL-MODE MATTERS FOR THE 64-TOKEN REGIME: SmolVLA's 64 tokens via 3×3 pixel shuffle (9× spatial reduction) represent aggressive compression where each token covers ~50×50 pixels mixing foreground and background. All successful token pruning methods operate on spaces with substantial redundancy (FocusVLA 512→256, VLA-IAP on 256+, standard ViT pruning on 196-768 tokens). Whether further hard pruning from 64 tokens can improve robustness without degrading action accuracy is a GENUINELY OPEN RESEARCH QUESTION. ACIB addresses this by offering both modes: ACIB-S provides IB-grounded robustness improvement WITHOUT removing any tokens (eliminating the information loss risk entirely), while ACIB-H tests whether hard pruning adds additional benefit. The paper's experimental design treats the soft-vs-hard comparison as a core contribution: if ACIB-H at k=0.8 (retaining 51 of 64 tokens) matches or exceeds ACIB-S, this demonstrates that IB-guided pruning works even in compact spaces; if ACIB-S consistently outperforms ACIB-H, this demonstrates that continuous reweighting is the right intervention for compact token regimes — either outcome is scientifically valuable. ACIB-S MECHANISM: The VIB KL term pushes many weights toward zero, creating a SOFT bottleneck where nuisance-carrying tokens are effectively suppressed without hard removal. The consistency term ensures this suppression pattern is perturbation-invariant. Critically, ACIB-S is STRICTLY MORE GENERAL than FTM: FTM applies F̂=(1+γ)⊙F+β with input-independent γ,β (same transformation regardless of scene content, 4K params); ACIB-S applies V_out = V ⊙ σ(MLP(V)) with input-dependent, per-token, IB-trained weights (~50K params). ACIB-S learns WHICH tokens to amplify and suppress based on scene content, while FTM shifts ALL token features uniformly. ACIB-H MECHANISM: For hard mode inference, the trained scorer selects top-k tokens by logit magnitude. We sweep k ∈ {0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9} (38, 45, 51, 58 tokens retained). The IB objective's KL term provides per-sample ADAPTIVE sparsity: the prior p_0 sets a soft target, so the effective sparsity varies per-timestep — cluttered scenes suppress more background tokens, while sparse manipulation scenes retain more. DIFFERENTIATION FROM KEY METHODS: (a) vs. FTM — input-dependent per-token weights with IB grounding vs. input-independent global affine; ACIB is content-adaptive, FTM is scene-agnostic; composable as FTM+ACIB; (b) vs. DTR — physical perturbation invariance in VLA manipulation vs. adversarial safety defense in VLMs; IB scorer vs. KV-cache optimization; trained consistency vs. inference-time optimization; (c) vs. FocusVLA — IB-grounded perturbation-invariant scoring vs. attention-magnitude selection without IB or robustness evaluation; soft+hard modes vs. hard-only; (d) vs. ADP — IB-trained robustness scorer vs. training-free efficiency-focused action-conditioned gating; (e) vs. BC-IB — token-level vs. representation-level IB, robustness-targeted vs. accuracy-targeted; (f) vs. AVA-VLA — IB-grounded + augmentation-consistent vs. POMDP-motivated attention reweighting; soft/hard mode comparison vs. single reweighting mode; (g) vs. post-hoc pruning — trained perturbation-invariant scorer with soft mode option vs. unstable attention-based hard pruning. THEORETICAL FRAMING: We frame ACIB with information-theoretic motivation rather than provable robustness certificates. Fischer et al. (2021) demonstrated that VIB's apparent adversarial robustness is partly gradient obfuscation. We target empirical perturbation invariance through joint IB compression and augmentation consistency, extending DRIBO's multi-view IB principle from representation-level RL to token-level VLA intervention. SMOLVLA LIBERO-PLUS BASELINE CONTINGENCY: Since no published work evaluates SmolVLA on LIBERO-Plus, establishing the baseline is our first experiment. Three contingency plans: (1) If SmolVLA baseline is weak (<30% avg): the robustness gap is large, ACIB's contribution is substantial robustness recovery — we compare against FTM-SmolVLA and CRT-SmolVLA. (2) If SmolVLA baseline is moderate (30-60%): ACIB's contribution is meaningful improvement on top of a known-fragile model. (3) If SmolVLA baseline is surprisingly robust (>60% avg, possibly due to 9× compression acting as implicit regularization): the contribution narrative shifts to 'ACIB's soft reweighting provides principled, IB-grounded robustness enhancement with interpretable per-token importance maps' — the soft mode ensures non-negative contribution regardless of baseline strength, since it can only improve action-relevant signal-to-noise ratio. FTM BASELINE CONTINGENCY: If FTM adapted to SmolVLA recovers most of the robustness gap (>75% avg on LIBERO-Plus), our contribution shifts to demonstrating that adaptive, input-dependent ACIB-S provides COMPLEMENTARY gains — we evaluate FTM+ACIB-S (global affine modulation followed by IB-guided soft reweighting) and show the combination exceeds either alone. PROGRESSIVE SUBMISSION STRATEGY: Given the 15-day timeline (abstract May 26, paper May 29), we define a minimal viable submission (MVS) and ideal submission. MVS (non-negotiable): SmolVLA LIBERO-Plus baseline; ACIB-S (soft mode) on 4 perturbation dimensions (camera, lighting, texture, noise); ACIB-H at k=0.8; ablations isolating VIB and consistency terms; FTM-SmolVLA baseline; soft-vs-hard comparison analysis. Ideal submission (if time permits): full k sweep for ACIB-H, FTM+ACIB-S combination, additional baselines (VLA-IAP, BC-IB, ADP), per-dimension analysis. The FTM+ACIB-S combination and extended k sweep are deferred to camera-ready if time runs short.
