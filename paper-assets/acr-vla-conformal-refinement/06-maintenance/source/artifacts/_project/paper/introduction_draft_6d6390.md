# Introduction (Narrative B Draft — Rev 2)

## Paragraph 1: VLA Deployment Problem

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a promising paradigm for generalist robot manipulation, with flow-matching architectures such as SmolVLA and OpenVLA achieving strong performance on standard benchmarks. However, when these models are deployed as frozen policies in environments that deviate from training conditions, success rates degrade substantially. Distribution shift in object configurations, lighting, or spatial layouts exposes the brittleness of policies trained via behavior cloning, where compounding errors accumulate over multi-step execution horizons. Retraining or fine-tuning for every deployment scenario is prohibitively expensive, motivating lightweight post-hoc correction strategies that operate at inference time without modifying the underlying policy.

## Paragraph 2: Conformal Prediction and Uncertainty

Conformal prediction (CP) offers a distribution-free framework for constructing calibrated prediction sets, providing statistical coverage guarantees without distributional assumptions. Applied to VLA inference, CP enables principled uncertainty quantification over action predictions: by drawing K candidate action chunks from the policy's flow-matching process and scoring each against a calibration set, one can identify which candidates fall within or outside the learned distribution's typical behavior. This multi-sample scoring naturally enables sample selection, where the candidate with the lowest nonconformity score is executed. Sample selection provides consistent, if modest, improvement over single-sample execution by filtering out low-quality action proposals. Yet selection faces an inherent ceiling: when all K candidates are poor (as occurs under strong distribution shift), choosing the best among them cannot produce an action better than the best candidate itself. This ceiling motivates a natural extension: can conformal uncertainty signals guide a learned correction module to synthesize actions that improve upon any individual candidate? If the nonconformity scores carry directional information about where and how much a candidate deviates from the desired behavior, a lightweight correction head might exploit this signal to produce refined actions that no single sample achieves alone.

## Paragraph 3: Gap — Correction Conditioning and Magnitude

Two critical design choices for learned action correction remain unexplored. The first is the choice of conditioning features: what signal should guide the correction direction? Candidates include raw observation features (which encode the current visual and proprioceptive state), per-degree-of-freedom (per-DOF) conformal nonconformity scores (which provide calibrated uncertainty estimates for each action dimension independently), and scalar uncertainty summaries (which compress the full uncertainty profile into a single value). The second is magnitude control: how large should corrections be, and what happens when they are unbounded? Without systematic study of these axes, it is unclear whether learned corrections will reliably help or inadvertently harm policy performance. Despite growing interest in inference-time VLA refinement, no existing method systematically investigates the interaction between conditioning features and correction magnitude.

## Paragraph 4: Our Method and Core Findings

We propose a sample-then-correct pipeline for inference-time VLA improvement. The pipeline first draws K action candidates from the frozen VLA's flow-matching decoder, selects the most promising candidate via conformal nonconformity scoring, then applies a bounded additive correction through a lightweight Conformal Residual Module (CRM, approximately 330K parameters, approximately 3ms inference overhead). We conduct a systematic comparison of three conditioning strategies for the CRM: observation-only features (obs_only), per-DOF conformal nonconformity scores (perdof), and scalar uncertainty summaries. Our experiments on the LIBERO Object benchmark reveal that the interaction between conditioning features and correction magnitude determines whether correction helps or catastrophically fails. Observation-conditioned corrections maintain stable performance across all tested magnitudes (mean success rate approximately 17% across four seeds and norm bounds ranging from 0.03 to 1.42, compared to 10-12% for the VLA-only baseline and 14% for conformal sample selection alone). Per-DOF conditioned corrections, despite their theoretical appeal as calibrated dimensional uncertainty estimates, exhibit progressive collapse as magnitude increases: from 14% at low norms (matching sample selection) to 0% when corrections are uncapped. On LIBERO Spatial, obs_only and perdof perform comparably at norm 0.1 (35%), suggesting the magnitude sensitivity manifests primarily in higher-dimensional manipulation tasks where action distribution shift is more pronounced. This divergence identifies magnitude sensitivity as a previously unrecognized failure mode for learned inference-time corrections on VLA policies.

## Paragraph 5: Contributions

Our contributions are as follows:

1. **A sample-then-correct pipeline for inference-time VLA improvement.** We introduce a two-stage framework that combines conformal prediction-based candidate selection with bounded additive corrections, enabling consistent non-degradation with task-dependent improvement on frozen VLA policies without retraining or architectural modification. *(Section 3: Method; Table 1: pooled results across seeds)*

2. **Conditioning-magnitude interaction as a previously unidentified failure mode.** Through systematic comparison of three conditioning strategies across correction magnitudes, we show that the same correction architecture succeeds or fails depending on the conditioning feature's interaction with correction norm. Observation-derived corrections remain stable across all magnitudes (mean approximately 17% across seeds and norms 0.03 to 1.42), whereas per-DOF conformal nonconformity scores exhibit progressive collapse (14% to 0%) beyond a narrow safe region. This identifies bounded magnitude control as necessary for safe inference-time VLA correction. *(Section 4.2: Magnitude Curve Analysis; Figure 2; Section 4.3: Uncapped comparison)*

3. **Honest negative result: per-DOF conformal features do not generalize as correction signals.** Despite their theoretical appeal as calibrated per-dimension uncertainty estimates, conformal nonconformity scores fail to provide useful directional guidance for corrections when magnitude is not tightly constrained. This negative finding informs future work on conformal prediction applications in robot learning. *(Section 4.4: Discussion; Spatial benchmark shows no method separates from baseline at 34-35%)*
