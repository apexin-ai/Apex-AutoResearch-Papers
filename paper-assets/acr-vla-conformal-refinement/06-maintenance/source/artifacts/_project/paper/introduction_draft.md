# Introduction (Narrative B Draft)

## Paragraph 1: VLA Deployment Problem

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a promising paradigm for generalist robot manipulation, with flow-matching architectures such as SmolVLA and OpenVLA achieving strong performance on standard benchmarks. However, when these models are deployed as frozen policies in environments that deviate from training conditions, success rates degrade substantially. Distribution shift in object configurations, lighting, or spatial layouts exposes the brittleness of policies trained via behavior cloning, where compounding errors accumulate over multi-step execution horizons. Retraining or fine-tuning for every deployment scenario is prohibitively expensive, motivating lightweight post-hoc correction strategies that operate at inference time without modifying the underlying policy. This direction parallels the success of test-time compute scaling in large language models, where inference-time interventions (repeated sampling, verification, refinement) yield gains that complement training-time capabilities. For VLA policies, the analogous question is whether inference-time mechanisms can recover performance lost to distribution shift, without the cost of additional training data or environment interaction.

## Paragraph 2: Conformal Prediction and Uncertainty

Conformal prediction (CP) offers a distribution-free framework for constructing calibrated prediction sets, providing statistical coverage guarantees without distributional assumptions. Applied to VLA inference, CP enables principled uncertainty quantification over action predictions: by drawing K candidate action chunks from the policy's flow-matching process and scoring each against a calibration set, one can identify which candidates fall within or outside the learned distribution's typical behavior. This multi-sample scoring naturally enables sample selection, where the candidate with the lowest nonconformity score is executed. Sample selection provides consistent, if modest, improvement over single-sample execution by filtering out low-quality action proposals. Yet selection faces an inherent ceiling: when all K candidates are poor (as occurs under strong distribution shift), choosing the best among them cannot produce an action better than the best candidate itself. This ceiling motivates a natural extension: can conformal uncertainty signals guide a learned correction module to synthesize actions that improve upon any individual candidate? If the nonconformity scores carry directional information about where and how much a candidate deviates from the desired behavior, a lightweight correction head might exploit this signal to produce refined actions that no single sample achieves alone.

## Paragraph 3: Gap — Correction Conditioning and Magnitude

Two critical design choices for learned action correction remain unexplored in the literature. The first is the choice of conditioning features: what signal should guide the correction direction? Candidates include raw observation features (which encode the current visual and proprioceptive state), per-degree-of-freedom (per-DOF) conformal nonconformity scores (which provide calibrated uncertainty estimates for each action dimension independently), and scalar uncertainty summaries (which compress the full uncertainty profile into a single value). The second is magnitude control: how large should corrections be, and what happens when they are unbounded? Without systematic study of these axes, it is unclear whether learned corrections will reliably help or inadvertently harm policy performance. Existing work leaves these questions open. A2C2 demonstrates that asynchronous correction heads can refine action chunks from frozen VLAs using recent observations, but does not explore alternative conditioning features or study magnitude sensitivity. ReconVLA applies conformal quantile regression for uncertainty-based out-of-distribution detection and sample selection, but performs no learned correction. SC-VLA trains residual corrections via reinforcement learning with task-specific reward signals, requiring per-task online training that precludes plug-and-play deployment. None of these methods systematically investigates the interaction between conditioning features and correction magnitude.

## Paragraph 4: Our Method and Core Findings

We propose a sample-then-correct pipeline for inference-time VLA improvement. The pipeline first draws K action candidates from the frozen VLA's flow-matching decoder, selects the most promising candidate via conformal nonconformity scoring, then applies a bounded additive correction through a lightweight Correction Residual Module (CRM, approximately 330K parameters, approximately 3ms inference overhead). We conduct a systematic comparison of three conditioning strategies for the CRM: observation-only features (obs_only), per-DOF conformal nonconformity scores (perdof), and scalar uncertainty summaries. Our experiments on the LIBERO Object benchmark reveal that the interaction between conditioning features and correction magnitude determines whether correction helps or catastrophically fails. Observation-conditioned corrections maintain stable performance across all tested magnitudes (mean success rate approximately 17% across four seeds and norm bounds ranging from 0.03 to 1.42, compared to 10-12% for the VLA-only baseline and 14% for conformal sample selection alone). Per-DOF conditioned corrections, despite their theoretical appeal as calibrated dimensional uncertainty estimates, exhibit progressive collapse as magnitude increases: from 14% at low norms (matching sample selection) to 0% when corrections are uncapped. This divergence identifies magnitude sensitivity as a previously unrecognized failure mode for learned inference-time corrections on VLA policies.

## Paragraph 5: Contributions

Our contributions are as follows:

1. **A sample-then-correct pipeline for inference-time VLA improvement.** We introduce a two-stage framework that combines conformal prediction-based candidate selection with bounded additive corrections, enabling consistent non-degradation with task-dependent improvement on frozen VLA policies without retraining or architectural modification. *(Section 3: Method; Table 1: pooled results across seeds)*

2. **Mechanism insight: observation features provide robust correction signals while per-DOF conformal features collapse under magnitude scaling.** Through systematic comparison of conditioning strategies, we demonstrate that observation-derived corrections maintain stable performance across magnitudes (mean approximately 17% across seeds and norms for norm 0.03-1.42), whereas per-degree-of-freedom nonconformity scores exhibit progressive collapse (14% to 0%) beyond a narrow safe region. This establishes magnitude sensitivity as the critical factor determining correction viability. *(Section 4.2: Magnitude Curve Analysis; Figure 2: magnitude-performance curves; Table 2: cross-magnitude comparison)*

3. **Magnitude sensitivity as a previously unidentified failure mode for learned corrections.** We show that the same correction architecture succeeds or fails depending solely on the conditioning feature's interaction with correction norm, revealing that bounded magnitude control is necessary for safe inference-time intervention on VLA policies. *(Section 4.3: Uncapped comparison; obs_only 17% vs perdof 0% at default norm)*

4. **Honest negative result: per-DOF conformal features do not generalize as correction signals.** Despite their theoretical appeal as calibrated uncertainty estimates per action dimension, conformal nonconformity scores fail to provide useful directional guidance for corrections when magnitude is not tightly constrained. This negative finding informs future work on conformal prediction applications in robot learning. *(Section 4.4: Discussion; Spatial benchmark shows no method separates from baseline at 34-35%)*
