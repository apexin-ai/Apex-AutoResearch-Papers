# Related Work

## VLA Robustness and Inference-Time Adaptation

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have rapidly advanced general-purpose robot control by leveraging large-scale vision-language pretraining [Brohan et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024]. Flow-matching architectures such as pi0 [Black et al., 2024] and SmolVLA [Shukor et al., 2025] achieve strong performance on standard benchmarks but remain brittle under distribution shift, as systematically documented by LIBERO-Plus [Fei et al., 2025] and LIBERO-PRO [Xu et al., 2025].

Several lines of work address VLA robustness at *training time*. RobustVLA [Zhang et al., 2025] applies Jacobian and smoothness regularization via RL post-training. SRPO [Fei et al., 2025] uses self-referential policy optimization to reach 82.1% on LIBERO-Plus. DiG-Flow [Zhang et al., 2025] introduces representation-level discrepancy guidance. ACoT-VLA [AgibotTech, 2026] adds chain-of-thought reasoning to VLA action prediction. VLA-GSE [Jiang et al., 2026] employs parameter-efficient fine-tuning with specialized experts. AFIL [2026] leverages failure trajectories as negative guidance during training. These methods require modifying the VLA training pipeline; our work is complementary, operating purely at inference time on a frozen model.

At *inference time*, the landscape is sparser. ReconVLA [Chen et al., 2026] applies conformal prediction to produce calibrated uncertainty estimates and selects the best action from K candidates — a pure sample-selection strategy. VLA-Pilot [2025] uses a multimodal LLM verifier with evolutionary diffusion optimization for zero-shot policy steering, but incurs 2.41s per step. SC-VLA [Li et al., 2026] performs online action refinement via sparse future state prediction and residual RL, requiring environment interaction. TT-VLA [Liu et al., 2026] adapts VLAs through test-time reinforcement learning with progress-based rewards. A2C2 [Sendai et al., 2025] proposes an asynchronous action chunk correction head — the closest architecture to our CRM, but trained jointly with the VLA rather than as a post-hoc module. SAFE [Gu et al., 2025] focuses on failure detection using internal VLA features and conformal prediction to determine *when* to intervene. Our work differs from all of the above by (1) operating on a fully frozen VLA with no environment interaction, (2) systematically comparing correction conditioning features, and (3) identifying correction magnitude as a critical factor.

SnapFlow [Luan et al., 2026] takes the opposite computational trade-off — distilling multi-step flow matching into a single inference step — while we invest additional compute (K=10 samples + CRM correction) to improve robustness.

## Conformal Prediction in Robotics

Conformal prediction (CP) provides distribution-free coverage guarantees without distributional assumptions [Shafer & Vovk, 2008; Angelopoulos & Bates, 2023]. Extensions to non-exchangeable settings [Barber et al., 2023] and online adaptation via Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI) [Gibbs & Candès, 2021] enable deployment under the distribution shifts inherent in robot operation.

In multivariate action spaces, Conformalized Gaussian Scoring (CGS) [Braun et al., 2025] provides Mahalanobis-based nonconformity scores respecting inter-dimensional correlations, which we adopt for sample selection. Conformalized Quantile Regression (CQR) [Romano et al., 2019] offers per-dimension heteroscedastic coverage. Recent work explores alternatives: Fan and Sesia [2025] develop coordinate-wise standardization for tighter marginal intervals, and Klein et al. [2025] use optimal transport ordering for principled multivariate quantiles.

Several works apply CP to robot planning and control. Zhao et al. [2024] use adaptive CP for uncertainty detection in high-DOF teleoperation at RSS 2024. Wang and Ning [2025] integrate CP into trajectory optimization loops with coverage guarantees. Zhou et al. [2025] combine ACP with control barrier functions for formally safe RL at ICRA 2025. Gonzales et al. [2026] pair conformal risk control with CBFs for human-robot interaction safety. Lekeufack et al. [2024] develop conformal decision theory for calibrated autonomous decisions. In the CP-for-robotics literature, CLAPS [2025] extends CP to Lie group configuration spaces, and LCP [2025] learns context-aware nonconformity functions for robot planning.

A distinct thread uses CP for *downstream decision-making* rather than coverage: Jin and Candès [2023] use conformal p-values for selection with FDR control, and Huang et al. [2023] deploy CP in learned feature spaces. SafeDiffCon [2025] uses CP uncertainty quantiles to guide diffusion-based control at ICML 2025. Yuan et al. [2026] calibrate VLM+policy composition for strategy selection (act/ask/learn) using CP. Our work connects to this thread: we use conformal scores for sample selection, then investigate whether conformal statistics can additionally serve as conditioning features for learned correction — finding, surprisingly, that they cannot.

The emerging area of VLA-specific uncertainty quantification provides further context. Zollo and Zemel [2025] present the first systematic calibration study for VLAs, introducing prompt ensembles and action-wise Platt scaling. Tang et al. [2026] identify the "averaging trap" in VLA uncertainty where mean aggregation dilutes critical signals. Xing et al. [2026] propose single-pass uncertainty via velocity divergence in flow matching models. Valle et al. [2025] establish evaluation metrics for VLA uncertainty. These works focus on *measuring* uncertainty; we focus on *acting on it* through learned correction.

## Residual Correction and Learned Action Refinement

Residual policy learning [Silver et al., 2018] establishes the framework of learning a correction on top of a base controller, with bounded corrections shown to preserve stability [Johannink et al., 2019]. This paradigm has been extended to VLA settings: PLD [Xiao et al., 2026] applies residual RL with scheduled magnitude bounds on LIBERO at ICLR 2026, and DAWN [Ma et al., 2026] analyzes scale mismatch between base policy and residual in value estimation. DRC [Pan et al., 2025] introduces temporal decay of correction magnitude over episodes. TRANSIC [Jiang et al., 2024] demonstrates that ungated residual corrections lead to model collapse, motivating gated correction mechanisms — closely related to our finding that unbounded correction magnitude (norm=1.42) yields catastrophic 0% success rate.

A separate line of supervised (non-RL) correction includes DAgger [Ross et al., 2011], which reduces compounding error via iterative expert corrections, and its variants: CR-DAgger [Cai et al., 2025] learns compliant residual corrections from human interventions, and RaC [2025] scales recovery and correction from human intervention data. Reactive Diffusion Policy [Xue et al., 2025] uses a two-level slow-fast architecture with supervised fast correction. DART [Laskey et al., 2017] injects optimized noise for compounding error robustness without online interaction. Our CRM follows the supervised correction paradigm — trained offline via L2 regression on calibration data — but uniquely conditions on VLA observation features and constrains correction magnitude, avoiding the RL training loops required by PLD, SC-VLA, and DAWN.

The question of *what to condition corrections on* is underexplored. Most residual methods condition on the full state or observation. Dodeja and Schmeckpeper [2025] show that base policy uncertainty can focus residual RL exploration on low-confidence regions. TRACE [Fang et al., 2026] defines scalar nonconformity scores via transport alignment in flow matching, avoiding per-dimension decomposition. Our systematic ablation across conditioning features — observation embeddings, per-DOF conformal statistics, and scalar conformal scores — reveals that richer statistical features do not translate to better correction, challenging the intuition that more informative conditioning is always beneficial.
