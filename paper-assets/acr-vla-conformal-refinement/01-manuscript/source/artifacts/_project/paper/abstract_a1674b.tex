Flow-matching vision-language-action models (VLAs) achieve high success rates on standard manipulation benchmarks but degrade sharply under distribution shift---dropping over 65 percentage points on LIBERO-Plus perturbations.
Existing inference-time remedies either select among multiple action samples, which cannot exceed the quality of the best candidate, or learn corrections through reinforcement learning or slow optimization loops.
%
We propose ACR-VLA, an inference-time two-stage pipeline that wraps a frozen flow-matching VLA without retraining.
At each control step, ACR-VLA draws $K{=}10$ action samples, selects one via Mahalanobis-based conformal scoring, and applies a bounded residual correction through a lightweight Conformal Residual Module (CRM, ${\sim}$330K parameters, ${\sim}$3\,ms overhead).
%
On LIBERO-Object, observation-conditioned CRM achieves 19.0\% success versus 12.0\% for selection alone (+7.0\,pp, 58\% relative gain).
Per-degree-of-freedom conformal features do not improve over scalar baselines (11.0\% vs.\ 9.0\%), an honest negative we attribute to noisy covariance estimation at small sample budgets.
Magnitude control proves critical: unbounded correction leads to 0\% success.
On LIBERO-Spatial, correction and selection perform comparably (best CRM within 1\,pp of the selection baseline), indicating the benefit is task-dependent.
%
Sample-then-correct outperforms sample-then-select for object manipulation, but whether correction helps depends on the structure of the distribution shift.
