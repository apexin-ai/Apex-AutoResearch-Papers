# Conclusion

We presented ACR-VLA, a sample-then-correct pipeline for inference-time improvement of frozen Vision-Language-Action policies, combining conformal action selection with a lightweight Conformal Refinement Module. Our central finding is that the interaction between conditioning features and correction magnitude determines whether learned corrections help or catastrophically fail. Observation-conditioned corrections maintain 16-18% across the full norm range (4-seed mean 17.5% at the optimal ε=0.2), while per-DOF conformal conditioning exhibits progressive collapse (14% to 0%) as magnitude increases. The failure of theoretically richer conformal features to improve correction highlights a fundamental gap between offline prediction accuracy and closed-loop task success. On LIBERO object manipulation, obs_only CRM improves in pooled evaluation over argmin selection (+4pp, 17.5% vs 13.5%) and the VLA-only baseline (+6.7pp).

Several limitations scope these findings. We evaluate on a single VLA backbone (SmolVLA with flow-matching decoder) and a single simulation benchmark (LIBERO). The effect size over argmin selection is modest, with pooled confidence intervals that include zero. The spatial benchmark shows no method separation (34-35%), indicating task-dependent correction benefits.

Future work should verify whether the conditioning-magnitude interaction generalizes to other VLA architectures (OpenVLA, Pi0) and real-world settings. Two natural extensions are adaptive norm selection, choosing the correction bound per step rather than fixing it globally, and multi-step temporal correction that exploits inter-step dependencies. These findings establish magnitude sensitivity as a key design consideration for safe inference-time VLA correction.
