Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies trained on large-scale demonstration datasets degrade under distribution shift, yet retraining remains prohibitively expensive. Inference-time correction offers an attractive alternative, but the interaction between conditioning features and correction magnitude has never been systematically studied for robotic action generation. Without this understanding, learned corrections risk degrading rather than improving downstream task performance.

We propose a sample-then-correct pipeline that combines conformal selection from K=10 candidate actions with a bounded Correction Residual Module (CRM, approximately 330K parameters trained offline). Within this framework, we systematically compare three conditioning strategies (observation features, per-degree-of-freedom (DOF) conformal scores, and scalar conformal scores) across a range of correction magnitudes on the LIBERO manipulation benchmark.

Our experiments reveal a stark divergence in robustness. Observation-conditioned corrections remain stable across the full magnitude range, with a mean success rate of 17.5% (4 seeds) compared to 10.8% for the frozen VLA baseline. In contrast, per-DOF conformal features progressively collapse from 14% at low magnitudes to 0% when corrections are uncapped, while observation-conditioned corrections maintain 16.5% under identical conditions. This divergent behavior identifies magnitude sensitivity as a previously unrecognized failure mode for inference-time VLA correction and establishes that conditioning feature choice, not architecture or training procedure, determines whether learned corrections improve or destroy policy performance.
