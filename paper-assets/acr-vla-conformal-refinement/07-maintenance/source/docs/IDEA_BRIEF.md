## Core Problem

How to improve VLA task success under realistic distribution shifts by combining online-adaptive multivariate conformal prediction with uncertainty-guided action refinement, going beyond static calibration, scalar uncertainty scores, and mere failure detection?

## Opportunity / Why Now

ReconVLA (arXiv:2604.16677) applies Conformal Quantile Regression (CQR) to pi0's flow-matching samples (K=10, continuous 7D actions) for uncertainty-guided action selection, improving LIBERO success from 56% to 73%. However, ReconVLA uses static offline calibration that provides no coverage guarantees under distribution shift, a scalar Euclidean nonconformity score that discards cross-dimensional covariance structure, and a pure selection mechanism (argmin uncertainty among K samples) that cannot synthesize actions better than any individual sample. SAFE (NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2506.09937) provides failure detection via functional conformal prediction on VLA hidden states with <1% overhead but does not improve actions. RobustVLA (ICLR 2026, arXiv:2510.00037) achieves +14% robustness via adversarial flow-matching fine-tuning but lacks deployment-time uncertainty quantification. DiG-Flow (arXiv:2512.01715) achieves 98.3% on standard LIBERO via representation-level geometric regularization on flow-matching VLAs but operates at training time and does not provide calibrated uncertainty. TT-VLA (arXiv:2601.06748) enables test-time adaptation via reinforcement learning with dense VLAC progress signals, modifying VLA parameters on-the-fly — but requires a pre-trained progress estimator, introduces RL instability risks during manipulation, and does not provide calibrated uncertainty quantification. Conformal Prediction in the Loop (NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2510.16376) feeds CP information back into trajectory optimization for collision avoidance, demonstrating the value of closing the CP-decision loop, but uses planning-based optimization (not a learned refinement module) in a domain (vehicles/quadrotors) distinct from VLA manipulation. UPS (arXiv:2602.22474) combines conformal prediction with residual learning for robot policy correction, but uses CP only for strategy gating (act/ask/learn) — the residual policy receives observation and base action as inputs without conformal features, and CP operates at the semantic VLM-verifier level rather than on continuous action uncertainty. The key unoccupied niche: no method combines (1) online-adaptive multivariate conformal calibration that captures cross-DOF covariance structure with (2) a learned parametric refinement module that uses structured per-DOF conformal statistics as input features to synthesize improved VLA actions. Adaptive Conformal Inference (Gibbs & Candès, NeurIPS 2021) provides the online alpha-update rule, and Conformalized Gaussian Scoring (Braun et al., arXiv:2507.20941) provides multivariate Mahalanobis-based nonconformity scores that avoid Bonferroni conservatism — but neither has been applied to VLA action correction. Flow-matching VLA heads (pi0, SmolVLA) produce exchangeable action samples that provide the raw material for conformal prediction at each step, and the pi0 architecture's KV-cached observation encoder enables efficient batched multi-sample generation.

## Landscape (SOTA & Limitations)

The VLA uncertainty and robustness space has become highly active in 2025-2026, with methods addressing different facets of the problem:

(1) **Uncertainty quantification**: ReconVLA applies CQR to flow-matching samples for action selection; SAFE trains a lightweight failure detector on VLA hidden states; 'Shifting Uncertainty to Critical Moments' (arXiv:2603.18342) proposes per-DOF instability indicators and DoF-adaptive calibration for token-based VLAs; 'Ask Before You Act' proposes token-level uncertainty for intervention; UA-Flow (arXiv:2602.10326) extends flow matching with heteroscedastic per-sample uncertainty estimation; 'Divergence is Uncertainty' (arXiv:2605.00941, May 2026) proves that the posterior covariance of flow matching equals the velocity field divergence in closed form — providing per-dimension aleatoric uncertainty from a single forward pass without multi-sample generation. Crucially, DiU captures aleatoric uncertainty (inherent velocity field curvature/task noise) that persists even in-distribution, whereas CGS's empirical multi-sample covariance captures epistemic uncertainty (inter-sample disagreement reflecting model ignorance), which increases under distribution shift and is therefore more informative for detecting and correcting OOD failures (see Section 1 positioning).

(2) **Robustness improvement**: RobustVLA uses adversarial flow-matching optimization (+14% on pi0); DiG-Flow achieves robustness via representation-level geometric regularization (98.3% avg on standard LIBERO); VLS (arXiv:2602.03973) provides VLM-guided steering (+31% on CALVIN); ACG (arXiv:2510.22201) offers training-free coherence guidance for flow-matching VLAs (+30.8% real-world); AsyncVLA (arXiv:2511.14148) achieves 97.4% on LIBERO via flow-matching self-correction; SRPO (arXiv:2511.15605) achieves 99.2% on standard LIBERO and 82.1% average on LIBERO-Plus via self-referential policy optimization; CRT (arXiv:2602.01158) provides a plug-and-play Corruption Restoration Transformer that restores clean observations from corrupted visual inputs, maintaining near-baseline success rates under severe sensor noise — orthogonal to action-level correction.

(3) **Test-time adaptation and action refinement**: TT-VLA (arXiv:2601.06748) performs test-time RL with VLAC dense progress rewards, achieving 3.83% absolute improvement on Nora — adapts VLA parameters directly but requires a pre-trained progress estimator; PDF (arXiv:2604.18107) proposes verifier-free test-time perturbation learning with delayed feedback for token-based VLAs, achieving +7.4% on LIBERO via uncertainty-based data augmentation and action voting — targets discrete action logits rather than continuous flow-matching actions; A2C2 (arXiv:2509.23224) trains a lightweight correction head for VLA action chunks; PLD (ICLR 2026, arXiv:2511.00091) uses residual RL to generate self-improving VLA data (99% on LIBERO); SC-VLA (arXiv:2602.21633) refines actions via sparse world imagination; FPC-VLA (arXiv:2509.04018) uses a supervisor for failure prediction and correction at keyframes; VLA-in-the-Loop (OpenReview 2026) uses a world model as an event-triggered corrector for grasping; 'Accelerating Residual RL with Uncertainty Estimation' (arXiv:2506.17564, RA-L 2026) uses base policy uncertainty to guide residual RL exploration.

(4) **Conformal prediction for robotics and planning**: Conformal Prediction in the Loop (NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2510.16376) feeds CP information back into shrinking-horizon trajectory optimization with provable online improvement for collision avoidance; Conformalized Teleoperation (RSS 2024, arXiv:2406.07767) applies ACI to assistive controllers; Learnable Conformal Prediction (arXiv:2509.21955) uses learned context-aware nonconformity scores for robotic planning with 52% tighter intervals; UPS (arXiv:2602.22474) combines conformal prediction calibration with residual learning — CP calibrates a VLM verifier for strategy selection (act/ask/learn gating), while a separate residual policy learns from collected interventions without receiving CP features as input.

(5) **Multivariate conformal prediction advances**: Conformalized Gaussian Scoring (arXiv:2507.20941) uses Mahalanobis-based scores for joint multivariate coverage avoiding Bonferroni; Kernel CP Score (arXiv:2604.21595) captures residual geometry via MMD; Interpretable Multivariate CP (arXiv:2512.15383) provides coordinate-wise standardization with transductive guarantees.

(6) **Inference acceleration**: SnapFlow (arXiv:2604.05656) achieves 1-step flow matching via progressive self-distillation with 9.6× denoising speedup (274ms→83ms end-to-end) while matching 10-step teacher performance (98.75% on LIBERO) — directly applicable to reducing ACR-VLA's multi-sample inference cost.

(7) **Benchmarks**: Nominal LIBERO is saturated (97-99% for top methods like PLD, SRPO, DiG-Flow, AsyncVLA, SnapFlow); LIBERO-Plus reveals catastrophic robustness gaps across 7 perturbation dimensions with 5 difficulty levels each (top methods: SRPO 82.1%, Cosmos-Policy 82.2% via world-action-model video pretraining (arXiv:2603.22078), VLA-GSE 81.2% with only 2.51% parameter updates, OpenVLA-OFT+ 79.6%, AVA-VLA 74.7% average).

The critical gap: no existing method combines online-adaptive multivariate conformal calibration with a learned action refinement module that receives structured per-DOF conformal statistics as input features. TT-VLA adapts VLA parameters but without calibrated uncertainty structure. CP in the Loop closes the CP-decision loop via planning optimization, not learned parametric refinement. UPS uses CP for strategy gating, not as features for correction. Residual policies (A2C2, PLD, SC-VLA) learn corrections but without calibrated uncertainty features as conditioning. ReconVLA uses CP but only for sample selection with scalar scores. PDF targets token-based VLAs with discrete action logits. ACR-VLA uniquely occupies the intersection: learned parametric refinement conditioned on multivariate conformal structure with online adaptation, complementary to both training-time robustness (RobustVLA, DiG-Flow, SRPO) and parameter-level adaptation (TT-VLA).

## Proposed Approach

ACR-VLA introduces three interlocking technical contributions layered atop any flow-matching VLA (pi0 or SmolVLA), preceded by a motivating experiment that directly validates the core hypothesis.

**0. Motivating Experiment: Per-DOF Features vs. Scalar Uncertainty (Section 3).** Before committing to the full pipeline, we construct a controlled synthetic experiment validating that per-DOF conformal features carry more actionable information than scalar summaries. Setup: a 3D point-reaching task where a simulated 'policy' generates actions with known anisotropic uncertainty — σ_x=0.5, σ_y=0.1, σ_z=0.1 (e.g., simulating camera-induced x-axis ambiguity). We introduce an x-direction perturbation (target shift of +0.3 in x). We train three small correction MLPs (2-layer, dim 64): (A) conditioned on scalar average uncertainty (σ_avg=0.23), (B) conditioned on per-DOF uncertainties [σ_x, σ_y, σ_z], (C) conditioned on observation features only (no uncertainty). Prediction: Network B learns to apply large corrections in x (high uncertainty = low confidence = more correction needed) while preserving y,z; Network A applies uniform corrections degrading y,z performance; Network C learns corrections without uncertainty guidance. We measure per-axis MSE across 1000 test episodes with varying perturbation directions. This experiment takes <0.5 GPU-hours on any GPU, requires no VLA infrastructure, and directly tests whether per-DOF uncertainty features enable dimension-selective correction. A positive result (B achieving >10% lower MSE than A on non-perturbed axes while matching on perturbed axis) validates the core bet; a negative result would prompt reconsidering the approach before investing in full-scale experiments.

**1. Conformalized Gaussian Scoring (CGS) for Multivariate Action Conformal Prediction.** At each control step, draw K=10 flow-matching samples (matching ReconVLA's sample count for fair comparison). For each of the H=5 receding-horizon steps, compute the empirical 7×7 covariance from K=10 samples and apply Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage: Σ_shrunk = (1−α_LW)Σ_emp + α_LW·I, where α_LW is optimized analytically (Ledoit & Wolf, JMVA 2004). With p/n=0.7, unshrunk sample covariance suffers ~30-40% relative error in eigenvalue estimation per the Marchenko-Pastur law; Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage reduces this to <5% by regularizing toward the identity, producing a well-conditioned covariance for downstream Mahalanobis computation. Use the Mahalanobis distance s_h = (a − μ_h)^T Σ_shrunk^{-1} (a − μ_h) as the nonconformity score following Braun et al. (arXiv:2507.20941). This single joint score captures cross-DOF correlations (e.g., x-y coupling during grasps) and produces ellipsoidal conformal regions — strictly tighter than Bonferroni-corrected per-DOF intervals that ignore inter-DOF dependence. Joint H-step coverage uses Bonferroni correction only across H=5 time steps (α/5), not across 7 DOFs, yielding substantially less conservative regions than the original α/35 correction.

**Epistemic vs. Aleatoric Uncertainty — Positioning against Divergence is Uncertainty (DiU).** DiU (arXiv:2605.00941) proves that per-DOF aleatoric uncertainty can be obtained from a single forward pass via the velocity field divergence — a remarkable result that eliminates the need for multi-sample generation. However, aleatoric uncertainty captures inherent task noise (velocity field curvature) that persists even in-distribution: a grasp with intrinsic rotational ambiguity shows high aleatoric uncertainty regardless of whether the scene is familiar or novel. In contrast, CGS's empirical covariance from K=10 samples captures epistemic uncertainty — disagreement among plausible denoising trajectories — which increases specifically under distribution shift when the model encounters unfamiliar inputs. For ACR-VLA's goal of correcting OOD failures, epistemic uncertainty is the more informative signal: it reveals where the model is confused (and thus where correction is needed), not where the task is inherently ambiguous. We include a DiU-based ablation (A7) that replaces CGS's multi-sample epistemic features with DiU's single-pass aleatoric features as CRM input, empirically validating this epistemic-vs-aleatoric distinction. If DiU features prove complementary rather than substitutive (epistemic + aleatoric > either alone), we incorporate both, yielding a richer 52D uncertainty feature set at reduced inference cost (fewer samples needed for stable epistemic estimation when aleatoric structure is independently available).

The CRM receives structured uncertainty features extracted from each per-step shrunk covariance: per-DOF standard deviations (7×H=35 features encoding which dimensions are uncertain), the ACI-calibrated conformal radius q_h (H=5 features encoding overall per-step uncertainty), and per-step ellipsoid log-volume (H=5 features encoding prediction spread). Total: 45 structured uncertainty features that reveal both per-DOF and cross-DOF uncertainty structure — fundamentally richer than ReconVLA's scalar Euclidean distance.

**2. Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI) via Chunk Overlap Consistency.** Replace ReconVLA's static offline CQR calibration with ACI's online update rule (Gibbs & Candès, NeurIPS 2021/JMLR 2024): α_{t+1} = α_t + γ(α − err_t). This provides long-run average coverage guarantees under arbitrary distribution shift — directly resolving the coverage-under-shift paradox that invalidates static conformal methods.

**ACI Coverage Signal — Chunk Overlap Consistency (COC):** The original self-coverage signal via inverse kinematics has a critical failure mode: during grasping and contact-rich manipulation, contact dynamics cause significant state-action mismatch (the achieved end-effector delta ≠ commanded action due to contact forces, controller tracking error, and object interaction dynamics). We replace this with Chunk Overlap Consistency, which avoids inverse kinematics entirely by exploiting the receding-horizon action chunk structure. At step t, ACR-VLA predicts an action chunk [a_t^1, ..., a_t^H] with corresponding conformal ellipsoids [C_t^1, ..., C_t^H]. After executing a_t^1 and observing s_{t+1}, we draw K=10 new samples from the VLA and compute the new mean action μ_{t+1}^1 for the next step. The coverage signal is: err_t = 𝟙[μ_{t+1}^1 ∉ C_t^2], i.e., whether the next-step prediction's first action falls outside the previous step's second-action conformal region. Intuition: if our conformal regions are well-calibrated, consecutive predictions should be mutually consistent — the region predicted for 'what we'll do next' should contain what we actually predict when we get there. This signal is: (a) self-referential (no external supervision or trained reward model), (b) robust to contact dynamics (comparing prediction-to-prediction, not prediction-to-execution), (c) directly measures calibration quality (over- or under-coverage manifests as systematic in/out patterns), (d) available at every control step (not just episode boundaries). Within episodes, use exponentially-weighted conformal prediction (Barber et al., Annals of Statistics 2023) with decay parameter ρ to downweight stale calibration examples. Episode-level aggregation: the fraction of per-step COC hits within an episode provides the err_t for cross-episode ACI updates.

Validation plan: on a held-out LIBERO calibration set, measure (a) the empirical coverage rate of COC vs. the target α (should converge under ACI), and (b) rank correlation between episode-level COC coverage fraction and task success. Expected outcome: episodes where COC signals frequent coverage violations (conformal regions are poorly calibrated) should correlate with lower task success. If Spearman ρ < 0.3 OR if ACI with COC does not achieve tighter conformal regions (>10% lower average ellipsoid volume) than static calibration at matched coverage rate, fall back to episode-level task success as ACI signal (available in simulation).

**3. Conformal Refinement Module (CRM).** A lightweight MLP (2 hidden layers, dim 256, ~131K parameters) that takes as input: (a) the sample mean action from K=10 flow-matching samples (7×H=35D), (b) the 45D structured CGS uncertainty features described above (per-DOF standard deviations, ACI-calibrated radii, ellipsoid volumes), and (c) the VLA's frozen observation encoder features. The CRM outputs a refined action that can synthesize improvements beyond any individual sample.

Key motivation for per-DOF conformal features over scalar uncertainty: Consider camera perturbation causing high VLA uncertainty in x-y translation but low uncertainty in z-translation and rotation. A scalar uncertainty score (like ReconVLA's Euclidean distance) averages over all DOFs, signaling 'medium uncertainty' everywhere. The per-DOF conformal structure reveals 'very uncertain in x-y, confident in z and rotation,' enabling the CRM to apply corrections only to the uncertain dimensions while preserving the VLA's confident predictions elsewhere. This information-theoretic argument — that per-DOF conformal features have strictly higher mutual information with the optimal correction vector than scalar summaries — is validated by the motivating experiment (Section 3).

**Training: CRM trained with action loss only (simplified objective).** Following reviewer guidance, we decouple training from calibration. CRM is trained with a single objective:
  L = MSE(a_refined, a_expert)
Training uses standard LIBERO demonstration trajectories augmented with synthetic perturbations to simulate distribution shifts. ACI operates purely at inference time for calibration — it adjusts the conformal threshold α online via the COC signal, which in turn changes the conformal radius q_h fed as input features to the CRM. This design eliminates multi-objective tension (the previous calibration-aware loss created competing gradients between coverage maintenance and action accuracy with only 131K parameters). The CRM simply learns: 'given these per-DOF uncertainty features and this mean action, produce the best correction.' ACI independently ensures the uncertainty features remain well-calibrated under shift. They compose at inference without training-time entanglement, simplifying hyperparameter tuning (no λ_cal, β to sweep) and making the narrative cleaner: CRM is a conformal-feature-conditioned correction module; ACI is an online calibration mechanism; they are orthogonal.

**Synthetic augmentation and coverage analysis for LIBERO-Plus perturbation dimensions:**
| LIBERO-Plus Dimension | Synthetic Augmentation | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Camera viewpoints | Camera pose jitter (±15° rotation, ±0.1m translation) | COVERED |
| Sensor noise | Gaussian noise injection on observations (σ=0.02-0.1) | COVERED |
| Robot initial states | Random initial joint offsets (±0.05 rad per joint) | COVERED |
| Light conditions | Brightness/contrast/hue augmentation | PARTIALLY COVERED |
| Background textures | Random color/texture overlay on non-object regions | PARTIALLY COVERED |
| Object layout | NOT directly augmentable — requires scene-level rearrangement | NOT COVERED |
| Language instructions | NOT augmentable via observation-level perturbation | NOT COVERED |

For covered dimensions (camera, noise, robot state), the CRM learns dimension-specific correction patterns during training. For partially covered dimensions (light, background), augmentation provides approximate exposure. For uncovered dimensions (layout, language), the CRM's correction capacity is limited to what the per-DOF uncertainty features reveal at inference time — ACI adapts the conformal thresholds online, but the CRM cannot learn novel correction strategies. We expect strongest gains on camera/noise/robot-state perturbations and modest gains on layout/language perturbations. If LIBERO-Plus L1-L2 difficulty levels become accessible for training (per the benchmark's protocol), we propose using L1-L2 for CRM training augmentation and L3-L5 for evaluation, creating a controlled in-distribution/out-of-distribution split.

Even under conservative estimates, total inference fits within the 500ms re-plan budget with >200ms margin. **SnapFlow acceleration path:** SnapFlow (arXiv:2604.05656) demonstrates 1-step flow matching via self-distillation achieves 98.75% on LIBERO matching the 10-step teacher. With K=10 × 1 step, denoising drops from 150-200ms to ~15-20ms (conservative), yielding total inference of ~87-92ms — approaching real-time 10Hz control without receding horizon. We plan to evaluate ACR-VLA with both 10-step and SnapFlow-distilled 1-step generation, testing whether single-step samples provide sufficient diversity for covariance estimation. If single-step samples preserve inter-sample variance structure (as expected from SnapFlow's consistency training preserving the marginal distribution), this provides a practical path to <100ms inference.

**Execution Protocol:** At each control step: draw K=10 samples → compute per-step Ledoit-Wolf-shrunk CGS covariance and ACI-calibrated conformal ellipsoids → CRM produces refined action → execute first action of chunk → observe new state → draw K=10 new samples → compute COC signal → update ACI. Three execution modes gated by aggregate ellipsoid volume: (1) normal (full-speed execution), (2) cautious (H=2, reduced speed), (3) halt (flag for intervention).

**Evaluation Protocol:** Primary experiments: (I) ACR-VLA on pi0/SmolVLA base evaluated on LIBERO-Plus L3-L5; (II) **ACR-VLA stacked on SRPO-finetuned VLA** — the strongest impact argument, since SRPO (82.1%) and Cosmos-Policy (82.2%) represent the current LIBERO-Plus SOTA. If ACR-VLA + SRPO > SRPO alone by ≥2%, this demonstrates orthogonal value of inference-time conformal correction beyond what training-time optimization achieves. This is our primary stacking experiment because SRPO keeps the flow-matching architecture, enabling direct application of CGS; Cosmos-Policy uses a world-action-model architecture that may not expose exchangeable flow-matching samples. (III) ACR-VLA + TT-VLA stacking (ACR-VLA keeps VLA frozen, TT-VLA adapts parameters — they operate on orthogonal axes). Secondary metrics: (a) per-perturbation-dimension breakdown (hypothesis: largest gains on camera, noise, robot-state; modest on layout, language); (b) ACI online coverage validity (COC coverage converges to target α); (c) mean ellipsoid volume (tighter is better at fixed coverage); (d) inference latency per control step (both optimistic and conservative). Baselines: pi0/SmolVLA base, ReconVLA (static CQR + selection), RobustVLA (adversarial fine-tuning), SAFE (detection only), ACG (coherence guidance), DiG-Flow (complementary; also test ACR-VLA on top of DiG-Flow), SRPO (current LIBERO-Plus co-SOTA at 82.1%), Cosmos-Policy (LIBERO-Plus co-SOTA at 82.2% via WAM).
