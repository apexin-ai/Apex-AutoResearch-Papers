\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
We evaluate on four English NER benchmarks spanning a difficulty gradient, each serving a distinct role.
\textbf{SciERC} \cite{luan2018scierc} (551 test instances, 6 entity types) is the primary medium-difficulty test bed where models achieve ${\approx}$65\% F1.
\textbf{Few-NERD} \cite{ding2021fewnerd} (INTRA coarse; 37{,}648 instances, 8 entity types, ${\approx}$75\% F1) provides a low-degeneracy contrast where LP selection succeeds (\S\ref{sec:fewnerd}).
\textbf{CoNLL-2003} \cite{tjongkimsang2003conll} (3{,}453 test sentences, 4 entity types, ${\approx}$91\% F1) serves as a ceiling-effect control where limited oracle headroom constrains selection gains.
\textbf{WNUT-17} \cite{derczynski2017wnut} (1{,}287 social media instances, 51\% zero F1) probes the signal-collapse boundary where model capability is insufficient.

\paragraph{Models and sampling.}
\textbf{Qwen3-8B} and \textbf{LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct} are LoRA fine-tuned using LLaMA Factory \cite{zheng2024llamafactory} (training details in Appendix~\ref{app:training}; Few-NERD uses 3 epochs vs.\ 5 for others due to earlier convergence; Appendix~\ref{app:epoch_confound}).
We draw $N{=}8$ samples at $T{=}1.0$ (primary) with vLLM constrained decoding \cite{willard2023guided}, with $N{=}16$ for scaling experiments (Appendix~\ref{app:n_scaling}).

\subsection{The Correlation-Selection Gap}
\label{sec:selection_gap}

Four of five signals (SJ, FK, EM, VC) assign identical scores to all candidates from the same input by construction (\S\ref{sec:formulation}); they can estimate quality across instances but cannot rank candidates within an instance.
The empirical question is whether LP, the sole per-sample signal, can exploit its candidate-discriminative nature for selection.
LP selection effectiveness is primarily determined by LP-quality alignment rather than degeneracy level (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
Cross-instance $\rho$ (Table~\ref{tab:quality_estimation}) is attenuated by varying instance difficulty; within-instance $\rho$ directly measures whether LP discriminates candidates for a fixed input, making it the relevant predictor of selection success.

Instance-level signals assign identical QE scores to all candidates; MBR conversion does not improve over greedy on the primary test bed (Table~\ref{tab:selection}; Figure~\ref{fig:selection_gap}).
Instance-level signals predict \textit{which inputs} the model handles reliably, not \textit{which output} is best.

\paragraph{LP: the sole per-sample signal also fails.}
On SciERC ($n{=}529$, 2-seed sign tests\footnote{MDE${\approx}$1.5~pp at 80\% power ($n{=}529$); Few-NERD ($n{=}32{,}565$; MDE${\approx}$0.22~pp) provides the definitive test.}), the oracle (0.775) reveals 13.0~pp of headroom, but LP achieves $+0.2$~pp ($p{=}0.29$), below the MDE (${\approx}$1.5~pp); selection effects below this threshold cannot be reliably detected.
The pattern holds across CoNLL and WNUT-17 (Table~\ref{tab:cross_selection}); under stringent TOST anchors, some configurations show significant \textit{degradation} (Appendix~\ref{app:tost}).
Few-NERD provides the definitive contrast: LP achieves $+1.39$~pp ($p{<}0.001$) at low degeneracy (\S\ref{sec:fewnerd}), while structural signals still degrade selection.

\paragraph{Qualitative factors governing selection effectiveness.}
Three factors shape LP selection gains: (1)~\textit{instance degeneracy} (SciERC 17.8\%, CoNLL 61.7\%; Table~\ref{tab:fewnerd}); (2)~\textit{oracle headroom} (CoNLL ${\approx}4$\,pp vs.\ SciERC 13.0\,pp); (3)~\textit{LP-quality alignment} (Few-NERD $\rho{\approx}0.5$--$0.7$ vs.\ SciERC $\rho{\approx}0.26$).
The pattern matches observations: Few-NERD (low degeneracy, strong alignment) yields $+1.39$\,pp; SciERC (weak alignment) $+0.04{\pm}0.48$\,pp (3-seed; Table~\ref{tab:multiseed}); CoNLL (high degeneracy) none; WNUT-17 shows reversed LP ($\rho{=}{-}0.446$) yet QE remains effective (VC $\rho{=}0.724$, $N{=}16$; Table~\ref{tab:wnut}).
The factors do not combine into a predictive model ($R^2{<}0$, 23 configurations) but each is supported by controlled ablations (\S\ref{sec:fewnerd}, \S\ref{sec:discussion}).

\label{sec:robustness_main}

Consistency signals are robust across $T{\in}\{0.7, 1.0\}$ with sublinear $N$-scaling gains (Table~\ref{tab:robustness}); signal rankings are stable across seeds ($W{=}1.0$; Table~\ref{tab:ranking-stability}). NER calibration is reasonable (ECE 0.069--0.096; Appendix~\ref{app:calibration}).

\paragraph{Scale robustness.}
With Qwen3-4B (Table~\ref{tab:scale_ablation}), QE is comparable (mean $|\Delta\rho|{=}0.042$), yet selection remains below greedy despite 13.0~pp headroom. Task difficulty is a stronger predictor than model scale within the 3B--8B range (Appendix~\ref{app:llama3b}).
Degeneracy scales with capability: 8B shows 17.8\% vs.\ 4B's 10.4\% on SciERC (Table~\ref{tab:degeneracy}).

\paragraph{Controlled ablations.}
LoRA rank${=}8$ (vs.\ 32) lowers degeneracy to 7.2\% but LP remains below greedy ($\rho{\approx}0.24$), confirming low degeneracy and adequate alignment are independently necessary ($T{=}1.2$; \S\ref{sec:discussion}).
Supervised classifiers, a DeBERTa verifier, and hidden state probes all fail to close the gap (Appendix~\ref{app:supervised_combination}, \ref{app:hidden_probe}); the bottleneck extends to model-internal representations.

\label{sec:scaling}

LP $\rho$ drops without fine-tuning (Table~\ref{tab:scale_comparison}) and LP selection fails on all datasets. Structural signals generalize (Few-NERD VC $\rho{=}0.416$). Degeneracy increases (Few-NERD: $46.2$\% vs.\ $13.0$\%; CoNLL: $67.7$\% vs.\ $61.7$\%); this comparison simultaneously varies model family, regime, and quantization, so individual effects cannot be disentangled.
LP-weighted construction yields $+1.53$\,pp on SciERC 72B ZS ($p{=}0.008$, CI [$+0.25$, $+2.82$]), recovering $10.2$\% of headroom. Construction fails on Few-NERD ($-0.20$\,pp) and CoNLL ($-0.37$\,pp) where degeneracy exceeds $46$\%---consistent with FT-regime degeneracy gating.

\paragraph{Few-shot demonstrations improve QE but not construction.}
Adding 3-shot demonstrations to 72B improves greedy on SciERC and CoNLL ($+3.3$--$6.2$\,pp) but not Few-NERD ($-0.9$\,pp), and strengthens structural QE (CoNLL SJ $\rho$ $.304{\to}.442$, LP $-.002{\to}.106$). Yet construction is ineffective across all three datasets (SciERC $-0.08$\,pp, CoNLL $+0.03$\,pp, Few-NERD $+0.02$\,pp; all n.s.). On SciERC, this failure despite low degeneracy shows the bottleneck is regime-dependent (the mechanism is not simple error correlation; Appendix~\ref{app:fs_error}); on Few-NERD, high FS degeneracy ($45.2$\%) places it in the expected failure regime.

\paragraph{Degeneracy is model-family-dependent, not scale-dependent.}
Qwen2.5 exhibits systematically higher degeneracy than Qwen3 on Few-NERD (7B--72B: $31.7$--$46.2$\% vs.\ Qwen3-8B: $13.0$\%; Table~\ref{tab:scale_comparison}), confirming degeneracy is shaped by architecture rather than parameter count. Construction fails for all Qwen2.5 configurations on Few-NERD (both n.s.); 3$\to$5 epochs triples degeneracy within the same model (\S\ref{sec:fewnerd}).

Oracle F1 follows $F_1(N) = a - b \cdot N^{-c}$ ($R^2 > 0.999$; Figure~\ref{fig:oracle_scaling}): FewNERD ($c{\approx}0.61$) scales comparably to reasoning tasks \cite{brown2024monkeys}, while SciERC converges more slowly ($c{\approx}0.35$); a single stochastic sample underperforms greedy ($N{\geq}4$ needed to match).
The ceiling grows---but confidence-based aggregation recovers ${<}1$\,pp, establishing a persistent aggregation gap.

\subsection{Prescriptive Results}
\label{sec:prescriptive}

Since instance-level selection fails, we present approaches that extract value from multi-sample generation without sample ranking.

\paragraph{Entity-level construction.}
We aggregate entities across samples, retaining those exceeding $\theta{=}2/N$, with LP-weighted or uniform scoring ($\theta$ stable in $[0.15, 0.35]$; Appendix~\ref{app:theta_sweep}). In zero-shot, LP-weighting is necessary (SciERC $+1.5$\,pp; \S\ref{sec:scaling}).

LP-weighted construction improves on Few-NERD ($+1.40{\pm}0.51$\,pp, $p{<}0.001$; survives Holm--Bonferroni at $k{=}57$) and SciERC ($+1.02{\pm}0.49$\,pp, $p{=}0.004$, $p_{\text{adj}}{=}0.20$; Table~\ref{tab:entity_construction}), recovering 8--11\% of oracle headroom. CoNLL shows no gain ($-0.39$\,pp), consistent with high degeneracy ($N$-scaling in Appendix~\ref{app:n_scaling}).

\paragraph{LP-alignment gating.}
Construction and LP selection are complementary, governed by $\rho_{\text{adj}}$: Few-NERD ($\rho_{\text{adj}}{=}0.516$) favors both, while SciERC ($\rho_{\text{adj}}{=}0.167$) favors construction only. A three-way routing protocol ($\rho_{\text{adj}} \geq 0.3$: LP; $<0.3$ with positive construction: construction; else: greedy) is stable across $\tau{\in}[0.2, 0.4]$.

\paragraph{Trained entity-level verifier.}
Entity verification features achieve AUC${=}0.923{\pm}0.002$ (LightGBM, OOF evaluation) but yield only $+0.30$\,pp overall via construction ($+1.28$\,pp SciERC, $+0.02$\,pp Few-NERD).
Cross-dataset transfer is negative (Few-NERD: $-0.76$, $-1.02$\,pp; Appendix~\ref{app:verifier_transfer}), demonstrating dataset-specific feature mappings. This motivates distillation approaches using each dataset's own SC consensus as supervision.

\paragraph{Selective prediction.}
QE signals support selective prediction: at 80\% coverage, filtering low-confidence instances yields $+5$--$8$\,pp F1 (AUPRC 0.70--0.92; Table~\ref{tab:cross_selection}).
