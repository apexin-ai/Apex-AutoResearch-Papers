## Core Problem

Standard VLA fine-tuning via behavioral cloning treats all demonstration timesteps equally, forcing the model to learn from demonstrator noise (hesitations, corrections, suboptimal motions) as valid behavior. Even high-quality teleoperation data contains substantial noise — our analysis of LIBERO's human-teleoperated demonstrations identifies 3,007 no-op instances across 4,500 LIBERO-90 trajectories (defined as timesteps where end-effector velocity norm < 0.01 m/s and gripper state is unchanged from the previous timestep), representing pauses, hesitations, and corrective movements that inflate action distribution entropy at ambiguous states. This noise degrades policy quality along three dimensions: (1) **Clean performance**: noisy timesteps dilute the learning signal, requiring more data to converge — analogous to label noise in supervised learning, where down-weighting mislabeled examples improves generalization; (2) **Data efficiency**: because all timesteps are weighted equally, the effective information content per demonstration is reduced — extracting more signal per demonstration is especially valuable in robotics where data collection is expensive; (3) **Robustness**: when test-time perturbations (camera shifts, lighting changes, layout variations) push observations to nearby but non-identical states, a policy with already-uncertain action distributions is pushed further into high-entropy territory, increasing the probability of catastrophic action samples. LIBERO-Plus documents this collapse: 95% → <30% under modest perturbation across seven dimensions. We note that training-data quality is one contributing factor alongside positional bias, vision-action mapping rigidity, and limited training distribution — LIBERO-Plus shows that positional bias and visual invariance are primary drivers of robustness failure. However, TAD addresses an orthogonal axis: it improves the action-quality signal within existing demonstrations rather than expanding the training distribution. These two interventions are complementary — data diversity augmentation addresses visual invariance while TAD addresses action distribution quality — and we hypothesize their benefits compose. ARFM (arXiv Sep 2025) provides direct evidence that advantage-weighted VLA training improves perturbation robustness by 11.4% on LIBERO with batch-level softmax weighting; per-timestep IQL advantages should provide at least comparable gains with finer granularity. This is an empirical hypothesis to be validated, not a theoretical guarantee.

## Opportunity / Why Now

V-GPS (CoRL 2024) proved offline Cal-QL value functions trained on demo-only data produce reliable quality scores for test-time re-ranking but never applied them at training time. ARFM (arXiv Sep 2025) applies offline RL advantage weighting to VLA flow-matching loss but uses a global adaptive scaling factor via softmax weighting across a batch (not per-timestep advantage weighting) and is architecture-specific to flow-matching VLAs (π0 family). CO-RFT (arXiv Aug 2025) applies TD learning with action chunking for direct offline RL policy optimization but replaces BC entirely rather than curating it. ReinboT (ICML 2025) predicts dense returns as an auxiliary multitask objective but does not use them to weight training samples. RINSE (arXiv Apr 2026) scores demonstrations via kinematic smoothness (SAL/TED) but uses no RL signal and operates at trajectory level. SCIZOR (ICRA 2026) performs timestep-level progress filtering via temporal distance classification but assumes linear task progression and explicitly fails on non-monotonic tasks (e.g., stirring, waiting). DataMIL (arXiv May 2025) uses datamodels to estimate per-trajectory influence scores and selects top-k% trajectories for co-training, but operates at trajectory/sub-trajectory level and does not perform per-timestep weighting of the BC loss — it is a data selection method (binary include/exclude) rather than a soft weighting method. No existing work combines (1) per-timestep offline RL advantage estimation that captures relative action quality at each state with (2) VLA BC fine-tuning as soft sample weights via advantage-weighted regression, at (3) training time. The key differentiator from progress-based curation (SCIZOR, RINSE) is that IQL advantages capture which actions are best at states where multiple actions produce non-zero progress — progress scoring cannot distinguish 'adequate but suboptimal' from 'optimal' actions. The key differentiator from policy-driven selection (DataMIL, CUPID) is per-timestep granularity and soft weighting rather than binary trajectory-level selection. The key differentiator from ARFM is per-timestep weighting (not batch-level softmax) applicable to any VLA architecture (not flow-matching-specific). The enabler is a validated dense progress reward on frozen VLA features, following recent evidence that frozen VLA representations encode state transition functions with temporally smooth structure (Molinari et al., Emergent World Representations in OpenVLA, 2025) and that lightweight progress predictors on frozen vision features produce effective dense rewards (Rewarding DINO, 2026; ReWiND, CoRL 2025).

## Landscape (SOTA & Limitations)

Demonstration curation: SCIZOR (ICRA 2026) trains a temporal distance classifier on sub-trajectories and flags timesteps where predicted progress lags elapsed time; achieves 15.4% average improvement across RoboMimic, OXE, and Sirius-Fleet (no LIBERO evaluation), but explicitly assumes linear task progression and breaks on non-monotonic tasks (stirring, waiting). RINSE (arXiv Apr 2026) scores trajectory-level quality via smoothness metrics SAL/TED (16% higher success with 1/6 data on RoboMimic, 5.6% on LIBERO-10); architecture-agnostic but uses no RL signal and cannot distinguish semantically poor-but-smooth motions. DataMIL (arXiv May 2025, Dass et al.) uses datamodels to estimate trajectory-level influence scores via metagradient estimators, selecting top-k% of prior data for co-training; achieves 10% average improvement on MetaWorld and consistent gains on LIBERO-10 with Octo, but operates at trajectory/sub-trajectory granularity (not per-timestep), requires several times the cost of training a full model for datamodel estimation, and performs binary selection rather than soft weighting. CUPID (CoRL 2025) and QoQ (Mar 2026) use influence functions at trajectory level, requiring evaluation rollouts. DemInf (RSS 2025) uses MI estimators for 5-10% gains. Offline RL for VLA: ARFM (arXiv Sep 2025) does advantage-weighted flow-matching loss for π0 with global batch-level softmax scaling and theoretical bias-variance analysis (4.5% multi-task uplift on clean LIBERO achieving 92.1% average, 11.4% perturbation robustness improvement under action noise from 43.3% to 48.2%); architecture-specific to flow models, uses return-to-go advantage via leave-one-out estimation, not per-timestep IQL advantage. Does not evaluate on LIBERO-Plus multi-modal perturbations. CO-RFT (arXiv Aug 2025) extends TD learning to handle VLA action chunking for direct offline RL policy optimization (57% real-world improvement), replacing BC entirely. ReinboT (ICML 2025) integrates dense return prediction via expectile regression as an auxiliary VLA objective on CALVIN (not LIBERO). Value/reward guidance: V-GPS (CoRL 2024) trains Cal-QL offline on demos with hindsight reward for test-time re-ranking across 5 generalist policies. VACO (ICLR 2025) does value-aligned sample weighting for BC via bi-level optimization on D4RL. Dense progress rewards: VLAC (arXiv Sep 2025) is a VLA-native critic built on InternVL that outputs pairwise progress delta and done signal — powerful but heavyweight (requires full InternVL model, withdrawn from ICLR 2026). Rewarding DINO (arXiv Mar 2026) trains lightweight dense reward functions from frozen vision features (DINOv2), showing pre-trained vision encoders contain sufficient information for reward estimation. ReWiND (CoRL 2025) uses frozen DINOv2 + language encoders with a trained progress predictor for dense reward from demonstrations. Robometer (RSS 2026) combines frame-level progress supervision with trajectory-comparison preferences at scale. Progress-aware VLAs: SPR (arXiv Mar 2026) integrates spatial progress monitoring into VLA for error recovery, achieving SOTA on LIBERO-Plus; ProgressVLA (Microsoft, Mar 2026) uses progress-guided diffusion policy for sub-goal termination. Robustness: RobustVLA (ICLR 2026) achieves 12.6% gain via Jacobian regularization and smoothness regularization against observation and action perturbations but does not address training data quality — it is a post-training method orthogonal to training-time data curation. LIBERO-Plus (CVPR 2026) identifies positional bias and vision-action mapping rigidity as primary failure modes; training with diverse augmented data (20K+ trajectories with varied objects, backgrounds, lighting, cameras, language) improves robustness from 68.1% to 79.6% — this is data diversity augmentation (expanding training distribution), not data quality filtering (improving signal within existing data), and the two approaches are complementary. VLA feature analysis: Probing studies (Lu et al., 2025; Molinari et al., 2025) demonstrate OpenVLA's middle layers encode symbolic states (>0.90 probing accuracy) and state transition functions with smooth temporal structure. AFBC (2021) showed advantages oscillate near zero in demo-only data under sparse reward — solvable via dense progress reward. SimpleVLA-RL (ICLR 2026) uses online GRPO with binary rewards but requires environment interaction.

## Proposed Approach

Temporal Advantage Distillation (TAD) is a two-component method: (1) offline advantage scoring via IQL with validated dense progress reward on frozen VLA features, and (2) advantage-weighted regression (AWR) during VLA LoRA fine-tuning. The method proceeds in three phases.

**Phase 0 — Feature Validation & Diagnostic (4 H100-h):** Extract frozen intermediate features from OpenVLA-OFT's vision-language backbone at multiple layer depths (middle layers 15, 22, and final layer ~4096-dim each) for all LIBERO demonstration timesteps. For each layer, plot cos_sim(φ_l(s_t), φ_l(s_goal)) vs. normalized timestep t/T for 50+ trajectories across all four LIBERO suites. **Decision gate 1 (feature selection):** If any layer exhibits monotonically increasing trend with Spearman ρ > 0.8 across >80% of trajectories, use raw cosine-similarity progress reward r(t) = cos_sim(φ(s_t), φ(s_goal)) − cos_sim(φ(s_{t-1}), φ(s_goal)) at that layer. If cosine similarity is noisy/non-monotonic (likely for action-prediction-optimized features), train a lightweight progress predictor: a 3-layer MLP (~500K params) on frozen features to predict normalized temporal progress t/T from state features φ(s_t) and goal features φ(s_goal), following the Rewarding DINO / ReWiND approach of learning dense reward functions from frozen vision representations. Use predicted progress delta as the dense reward signal. **Fallback:** If neither approach yields IQL advantage IQR > 0.1 (normalized), use pre-trained VIP features (proven to produce monotonic embedding distances to goal) as the feature backbone. **Decision gate 2 (advantage separation):** Compute IQL advantages on the selected features; if IQR > 0.1, proceed with per-timestep weighting; if advantages cluster despite dense reward, fall back to trajectory-level advantage ranking.

**Phase 1 — Value Function Training (12 H100-h):** Train IQL with expectile τ ∈ {0.7, 0.8, 0.9} on the validated features. Select τ by held-out temporal-difference error. Compute per-timestep advantages A(s_t, a_t) for every demonstration timestep. The dense progress reward ensures meaningful variation: timesteps with genuine task progress receive positive advantages; hesitations (near-zero progress, which constitute ~670 no-ops per 1000 trajectories in LIBERO-90, identified by velocity norm < 0.01 m/s and unchanged gripper state) and corrections (negative progress) receive low/negative advantages. This is the key differentiator from SCIZOR: IQL advantages reflect relative action quality at each state (Q(s,a) − V(s)), not just absolute progress. At states where multiple actions produce positive progress, IQL identifies which actions are best — SCIZOR's progress score treats all progress-producing actions equally. It also differs from DataMIL's trajectory-level binary selection: TAD assigns continuous per-timestep weights, preserving all demonstrations while modulating their contribution.

**Phase 2 — Advantage-Weighted VLA Fine-tuning (~50 H100-h):** Fine-tune OpenVLA-OFT via LoRA (rank 32) on LIBERO with advantage-weighted regression loss: L = Σ_t exp(A(s_t, a_t) / β) · L_BC(s_t, a_t), where β is a temperature controlling weighting sharpness. AWR's exponential weighting naturally up-weights high-advantage timesteps and down-weights noisy ones — more principled than SCIZOR's hard deletion thresholding (which the SCIZOR authors acknowledge lacks theoretical grounding). Unlike ARFM's batch-level softmax weighting, TAD applies per-timestep advantage directly, and unlike ARFM's flow-matching-specific formulation, TAD applies to any VLA architecture that uses BC loss.

**Evaluation:** LIBERO (4 task suites) for clean success rate; LIBERO-Plus (7 perturbation dimensions, difficulty levels L1-L5) for robustness. Note: LIBERO demos are human-teleoperated (SpaceMouse at 20Hz), containing natural teleoperation noise — no synthetic noise injection is needed, ensuring ecological validity. Baselines: (1) standard BC (OpenVLA-OFT default); (2) SCIZOR (timestep-level progress filtering — the most comparable non-RL method, re-implemented on LIBERO since original paper lacks LIBERO results); (3) RINSE (trajectory-level smoothness filtering); (4) ARFM-BC (batch-level advantage-weighted loss adapted from flow-matching to BC for fair comparison). **Complementarity experiment:** (5) TAD + data diversity augmentation (using LIBERO-Plus's augmented training data with TAD weighting applied on top) to test whether TAD's action-quality improvements compose with distribution-diversity improvements. This is a key evaluation: if TAD + diversity > diversity alone, it demonstrates that action quality filtering and distribution expansion address orthogonal failure modes. **Data efficiency analysis:** (6) Compare TAD trained on 100% of demonstrations vs. BC trained on 100%, 75%, and 50% — if TAD-100% matches or exceeds BC-100% while effectively down-weighting noisy timesteps, this validates the claim that TAD extracts more learning signal per demonstration, reducing the effective data requirement. **Expected effect size:** ARFM achieves 11.4% perturbation robustness improvement on LIBERO with batch-level advantage weighting and 4.5% clean improvement. TAD's per-timestep IQL advantages provide finer-grained quality signal; we conservatively expect comparable clean improvement (3-5%) and perturbation robustness improvement (8-12% on action-sensitive perturbation dimensions such as layout and initial state). The complementarity experiment with data diversity augmentation is expected to yield the largest overall robustness gain. Key diagnostics: (a) measure action distribution entropy at LIBERO-Plus perturbed states — the entropy margin hypothesis predicts TAD exhibits lower entropy than BC, tested as an empirical claim; (b) visualize advantage distributions to confirm meaningful separation between progress/hesitation/correction timesteps; (c) plot per-perturbation-dimension robustness to identify which failure modes TAD addresses (expected: strongest on layout/initial-state perturbations where action quality matters most, weaker on camera/lighting perturbations where visual invariance dominates — the complementarity experiment tests whether combining with diversity augmentation fills this gap).

**Ablations (within budget):** (1) Soft AWR weighting vs. hard advantage filtering (top-k% timesteps) to quantify AWR's advantage over SCIZOR-style thresholding; (2) β temperature sensitivity (2 values: β=1.0 and β=5.0, bracketing the expected optimal range); (3) Trained progress predictor vs. raw cosine similarity vs. VIP features to validate Phase 0 feature selection; (4) Per-timestep IQL advantage vs. trajectory-level mean advantage to justify the per-timestep design over simpler trajectory-level approaches.
