A. FOR EVERY SUBMISSION

A1. Did you describe the limitations of your work?
Answer: Yes
Section: Limitations (after Section 5, unnumbered)
Justification: The Limitations section covers: (1) single training runs per
configuration — cannot fully disentangle architectural from seed-specific
effects, though within- vs. cross-architecture Jaccard gap (3x) and Pythia
70M seed calibration (2.7-4.3x) provide proxy evidence; (2) coverage —
three primary architectures (+ two extended in appendix) from one model zoo, four main benchmarks + two
supplementary, two scale points only; (3) pre-training only — no fine-tuning
evaluation, discrete accuracy only; (4) statistical power — n=5 attention
ratios limits minimum achievable p to 0.017, LAMBADA Bonferroni-adjusted
p=0.170 does not reach conventional significance.

A2. Did you discuss any potential risks of your work?
Answer: Yes
Section: Ethics Statement (after Limitations)
Justification: This work analyzes existing publicly available pretrained
models and introduces no new risks. The identified per-example behavioral
differences may inform practitioners about limitations of aggregate benchmark
comparisons. No new data collection, human subjects, or model training is
involved.

A3. Do the abstract and introduction summarize the paper's main claims?
Answer: Yes
Justification: Three scoped claims: (1) task-specific aggregate convergence
(LAMBADA at 1.3B, rho=-1.0, p=0.017, with honest reporting that
Bonferroni-adjusted p=0.170); (2) scale-dependent convergence (complete at
1.3B, partial at 340M, two scale points only); (3) per-example predictive
multiplicity — J ≤ 0.14 across five architectures at 340M, alpha
spectrum 0.14-0.30 task-dependent. All claims explicitly scoped to the
architectures and benchmarks studied.

ADDITIONAL NOTES FOR FORM FIELDS
=================================

Fairness / Bias Implications (if asked):
Per-example predictive multiplicity has direct fairness implications.
Different architectures with equivalent aggregate performance affect largely
non-overlapping sets of examples (J ≤ 0.14, ~86% non-overlap). This means
the choice of architecture — often made based on aggregate benchmarks alone
— determines WHICH specific examples are correctly or incorrectly predicted.
The ambiguity metric alpha ranges from 0.14 (reasoning tasks) to 0.30
(retrieval tasks), quantifying the fraction of examples whose predictions
depend on architecture choice. This is an instance of predictive multiplicity
(Marx et al. 2020) arising from architectural variation, complementing
underspecification (D'Amour et al. 2022) which focuses on same-pipeline
variation. Practitioners should be aware that aggregate-equivalent models
are not per-example equivalent.

Reproducibility:
All models are publicly available from the Wang et al. (2025) model zoo on
HuggingFace (m-a-p organization). Evaluation uses the standard
lm-evaluation-harness with default settings. Analysis code and per-example
outputs will be released upon publication. No training is required to
reproduce results — only inference on public checkpoints.
