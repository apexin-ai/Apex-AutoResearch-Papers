## Seed Topic

Target: EMNLP 2026, ARR submission. Track: Machine Learning for NLP.

Direction: Ye et al. (arXiv 2602.01763, Feb 2026) prove the first formal exponential expressiveness separation between hybrid linear-full attention architectures: sequential function composition requiring L+1 full-attention layers cannot be compensated by even 2^(3L²) additional linear attention layers, a result applying to all recurrence-based variants including Mamba, DeltaNet, and RWKV. Meanwhile, Waleffe et al. (arXiv 2406.07887) show empirically that at 8B scale a hybrid with only 7% full-attention layers recovers performance lost by pure Mamba on copying, in-context learning (5-shot MMLU), and long-context reasoning — but neither they nor Wang et al. (arXiv 2507.06457), who trained 72 hybrid models finding 3:1 to 6:1 linear-to-full ratios work well, provide any principled explanation for which tasks degrade, why, or how to select layers. We propose a theory-guided empirical framework that maps Ye et al.'s composition depth hierarchy to specific NLP task families, tests the predicted stepwise (not gradual) degradation signature, and produces actionable, task-aware guidelines for hybrid architecture design — going beyond the fixed-ratio approaches of OLMo Hybrid (arXiv 2604.03444) and Mamba-3 (arXiv 2603.15569) by making layer allocation depend on the target task's composition depth.

  — Establishes the empirical problem at scale: at 8B parameters, pure Mamba/Mamba-2 degrades on copying, 5-shot MMLU (in-context learning), and long-context reasoning, while a hybrid with only 7% full-attention layers recovers full Transformer performance across all 12 standard tasks (+2.65 points average). The gap is about composition depth, not model size, and applies to all linear attention variants expressible as recurrences (Mamba, DeltaNet, RWKV, GLA). Predicts that task degradation should be stepwise with composition depth, not gradual.
- "A Systematic Analysis of Hybrid Linear Attention" (arXiv 2025, 2507.06457)
  — Trained and open-sourced 72 hybrid models (340M and 1.3B parameters) across 6 linear attention variants and 5 hybridization ratios, finding 3:1 to 6:1 linear-to-full ratios achieve Transformer-level recall. Key insight: superior standalone linear attention does not predict superior hybrid performance. Limitation: layer selection and ratio choice remain entirely heuristic — no principled connection to task structure or formal expressiveness theory.
- "MaBERT: A Padding Safe Interleaved Transformer Mamba Hybrid Encoder" (arXiv 2026, 2603.03001)
  — Latest encoder hybrid: interleaves Transformer and Mamba layers with a fixed MMTMMTMMTMMT pattern for 2.4x speedup on extended-context tasks. Confirms the gap between theory and practice: the interleaving pattern is selected by grid search, not by theory, and no task-dependent layer assignment is attempted.
- "OLMo Hybrid: From Theory to Practice and Back" (arXiv 2026, 2604.03444)
  — Closest existing work bridging theory and practice: replaces sliding-window attention with GatedDeltaNet in a fixed 3:1 ratio, achieving 2x data efficiency vs. OLMo 3 7B. Authored by William Merrill (formal languages background). However, uses a single fixed ratio rather than a task-dependent framework and does not test Ye et al.'s composition depth predictions.

Known Gaps:
- No work maps the formal composition depth hierarchy (Ye et al. 2026) to specific NLP tasks — we do not know which tasks require depth-2 vs depth-3 vs depth-L composition. The formal theory community (Ye et al., STOC/FOCS venues) and the NLP empirical community (Waleffe et al., Wang et al.) publish in non-overlapping venues with different methodologies.
- No principled method exists to determine which layers in a hybrid need full attention given a target task distribution — all existing approaches (Wang et al.'s ratios, MaBERT's fixed interleaving, OLMo Hybrid's 3:1, Samba's Mamba-SWA-MLP pattern) are heuristic or use a single universal ratio.
- The theory predicts stepwise degradation (sharp drops when full-attention layers fall below composition depth requirements), but no empirical study has tested this specific prediction — existing benchmarks report aggregate accuracy, not degradation curves as a function of attention layer count at controlled composition depths.

In-scope: Developing a predictive framework that (1) classifies NLP tasks by their composition depth requirements using structural analysis of the computation each task demands, (2) tests the specific prediction from Ye et al. that performance degrades in a stepwise fashion as composition depth exceeds full-attention layer count (not gradually with overall difficulty), (3) produces a task-to-architecture mapping that predicts which layers need full attention given a task distribution, validated by held-out task prediction. Concrete approaches include: annotating existing benchmarks with composition depth estimates derived from task decomposition analysis, constructing synthetic tasks with controlled composition depth, measuring per-layer attention entropy and information flow in trained hybrids, testing whether composition depth predicts degradation patterns better than surface-level task difficulty metrics, and validating cross-variant generalization (Mamba-2, GatedDeltaNet, RWKV-6).

NOT in-scope: Proposing a new hybrid architecture or training recipe — the contribution is the diagnostic and predictive framework, not another model. Not another empirical benchmark-sweep without theoretical grounding (Wang et al. 2025 already did this with 72 models). Not extending Ye et al.'s theoretical results — we take them as given and test their empirical implications. Not treating all "hard" tasks as equivalent — the theory makes specific predictions about which tasks fail and why, distinguishable from general difficulty.

## Core Problem

Hybrid linear-full attention models are deployed with universal attention ratios (3:1 to 6:1), yet three mutually incompatible theoretical frameworks predict different degradation behaviors as attention is reduced: (1) Ye et al.'s composition depth hierarchy predicts task-specific stepwise degradation at thresholds that increase with compositional complexity; (2) Csordas et al.'s depth-efficiency findings predict task-agnostic degradation because pure transformers do not use depth for sequential composition — but their analysis covers only pure transformers where all layers have equivalent computational capacity, leaving open whether hybrids with provably constrained linear layers behave differently; (3) the functional segregation view, supported by two independent lines of evidence — Michalak & Abreu's retrieval-exclusive attention finding (arXiv 2510.19861) and Borobia et al.'s component ablation revealing linear attention as the primary language-modeling backbone (arXiv 2603.22473) — predicts function-specific degradation (retrieval collapses while language modeling persists) independent of composition depth. No empirical study has tested these three predictions against each other in a controlled setting. Crucially, the overlap between H_comp and H_func is deeper than it appears: if retrieval tasks are inherently low composition depth and reasoning tasks are inherently high depth, both hypotheses predict the same ordering of degradation. Discriminating them requires tasks that cross these categories — high-depth non-retrieval tasks and low-depth retrieval tasks — where the two hypotheses make opposite predictions. This disambiguation is essential because the practical implications diverge sharply: H_comp implies attention should be allocated by task composition depth, H_func implies allocation by retrieval demand, and H_agnostic implies universal ratios suffice.

## Opportunity / Why Now

Three conditions create a unique empirical opportunity: (a) Ye et al.'s expressiveness hierarchy (arXiv 2602.01763, Feb 2026) provides the first formal composition-depth separation for hybrids, with the authors explicitly acknowledging that 'the implications of our results in the real world demand verification of practice' — this is an open invitation for the empirical test we propose; (b) Csordas, Manning & Potts (arXiv 2505.13898, May 2025) show that pure-transformer models do not use depth for composition — but their analysis covers only full-attention architectures (Llama 3.1, Qwen 3, OLMo 2) where every layer can in principle compute arbitrary functions, making the result about redundancy rather than fundamental capacity; in hybrids, linear attention layers provably CANNOT compute certain compositions (Ye et al.'s Theorem 1.1 shows that even 2^{3L²} linear layers cannot substitute for L-1 full attention layers), so the few attention layers are structurally load-bearing rather than redundant, making the depth-efficiency question qualitatively different; (c) functional segregation evidence comes from two independent groups: Michalak & Abreu (arXiv 2510.19861, NeurIPS 2025) demonstrate that retrieval depends exclusively on attention (0% accuracy when ablated in RecurrentGemma and Jamba), while Borobia, Seguí-Mas & Tormo-Carbó (arXiv 2603.22473, Mar 2026) independently show that the linear attention/SSM component is the primary language-modeling backbone (>35,000× perplexity degradation when removed vs ~82× for attention in Qwen3.5 and Falcon-H1). Separately, the Priming paper (arXiv 2605.08301, May 2026) validates that the expressiveness ordering of SSM variants (GKA > GDN > Mamba-2) predicts downstream performance — demonstrating that expressiveness theory can cross the theory-practice bridge for variant ranking, while leaving open whether it also crosses for composition depth thresholds, which is precisely our question. Wang et al.'s 72 open hybrid models (arXiv 2507.06457) across 5 attention ratios and 6 linear attention variants provide the experimental infrastructure for a controlled comparison without requiring any pretraining.

The strongest argument for why this test matters NOW is the unexplained industry convergence: OLMo Hybrid 7B, Qwen3.5, Kimi Linear, and Mamba-3 all independently converged on 3:1–5:1 attention ratios through empirical tuning, not theoretical guidance. Is this convergence principled (reflecting a fundamental property of language tasks) or coincidental (reflecting shared training data distributions and benchmark suites)? Only a controlled test against explicit hypotheses can answer this, and the answer determines whether the field should invest in task-adaptive architectures or trust universal ratios.

## Landscape (SOTA & Limitations)

Current SOTA hybrids converge on universal attention ratios: OLMo Hybrid 7B (3:1 GatedDeltaNet, arXiv 2604.03444), Qwen3.5 (3:1 GDN, 8–19× faster decoding), Kimi Linear (3:1 KDA, arXiv 2510.26692), Mamba-3 (5:1, ICLR 2026, arXiv 2603.15569). On layer selection: KL-guided selection (arXiv 2512.20569, ICLR 2026) outperforms heuristic interleaving but is data-dependent and teacher-specific; Distill-then-Replace (arXiv 2601.11667) does greedy task-specific replacement without modeling why certain layers matter; Priming (arXiv 2605.08301, May 2026) shows that the expressiveness ordering of SSM layer types (GKA > GDN > Mamba-2) predicts downstream performance at 32B scale, establishing that variant-level expressiveness theory transfers to practice — but the composition-depth dimension of expressiveness theory (Ye et al.) remains untested. On dynamic routing: Flux Attention (arXiv 2604.07394) does per-input context-aware routing; Neural Attention Search Linear (arXiv 2602.03681) does token-level adaptive search; Mixture of Layers (arXiv 2605.09516, May 2026) pairs shared softmax with routed GDN blocks. On theory: Ye et al. (arXiv 2602.01763) prove that even 2^{3L²} linear attention layers cannot replace L-1 full attention layers for L-sequential function composition, explicitly stating their results 'demand verification of practice'; Cooper et al. (arXiv 2603.08859) prove complementary expressiveness-efficiency tradeoffs on selective copying and associative recall; OLMo Hybrid proves hybrids can express tasks (code execution) beyond either component alone. On interpretability: Csordas, Manning & Potts (arXiv 2505.13898) find no evidence of compositional depth usage across Llama 3.1, Qwen 3, and OLMo 2 — but crucially, these are all pure transformers where every layer has full computational capacity; Borobia, Seguí-Mas & Tormo-Carbó (arXiv 2603.22473) show via component ablation that in hybrids, the linear attention/SSM component is the primary language-modeling backbone (>35,000× perplexity degradation when removed vs ~82× for attention), while Michalak & Abreu (arXiv 2510.19861, NeurIPS 2025) independently show that retrieval depends exclusively on attention (0% accuracy when ablated) in RecurrentGemma and Jamba. On recall benchmarks: MQAR (Arora et al., ICLR 2024) established that multi-query associative recall is a single-layer (depth-1) task that discriminates linear from full attention; GAR (Ni et al., AAAI 2025, arXiv 2412.12841) generalizes this to compositional relational reasoning with controlled depth. On compression: Nature npj AI 2026 identifies universal phase transitions at 30–45% structured sparsity that are NOT task-dependent. The key gap: all empirical layer-allocation methods optimize against specific training signals without connecting to formal expressiveness theory, and all theoretical results are worst-case without empirical validation — no work tests whether expressiveness separations predict practical degradation patterns, and no work distinguishes whether degradation is governed by composition depth, functional role, or neither.

## Proposed Approach

We design a controlled three-way hypothesis test that discriminates between Ye et al.'s composition-depth-dependent prediction (H_comp), Csordas et al.'s task-agnostic prediction (H_agnostic), and the functional-segregation prediction (H_func). The key methodological innovation is a 2×2 task matrix that crosses composition depth (low vs high) with functional role (retrieval vs non-retrieval), producing four task categories where H_comp and H_func make divergent predictions. The study proceeds in four phases.

**Phase 1: Synthetic Validation Pilot (~5 A100-hours).** We implement Ye et al.'s exact L-sequential function composition tasks at depths L=1–5, directly matching their formal construction (lookup tables over finite alphabets encoded as in-context key-value mappings). We evaluate all 72 pretrained models from Wang et al. (340M and 1.3B, across 5 attention ratios: pure linear, 24:1, 12:1, 6:1, 3:1) on these synthetic tasks using 8-shot prompting to mitigate the out-of-distribution concern — pre-trained LLMs weren't trained on these tasks, so we rely on in-context learning of the lookup tables, which tests architectural expressiveness rather than training distribution. This is the cleanest test of H_comp. We apply changepoint detection (PELT algorithm with BIC penalty) with pre-specified thresholds: 'stepwise' requires at least one detected changepoint with a performance drop ≥15% within a single attention-ratio step; 'gradual' requires no such changepoint. If synthetic tasks show no stepwise pattern, Phase 2–4 pivot to characterizing WHY worst-case separations don't manifest.

**Phase 2: Discriminating 2×2 Task Matrix (~30 A100-hours).** This is the core methodological contribution. We evaluate on four task categories that cross composition depth with functional role, specifically designed so that H_comp and H_func make OPPOSITE predictions about degradation ordering:

(a) *Low-depth retrieval* (MQAR, needle-in-haystack, multi-key value lookup): These are depth-1 tasks requiring parallel random access from arbitrary positions. H_comp predicts these are EASY (low composition depth, minimal attention needed) while H_func predicts these FAIL FIRST (require attention for retrieval regardless of depth).

(b) *High-depth non-retrieval* (nested center-embedded syntactic agreement at 2–4 levels of embedding, inspired by Wilcox et al. 2024 and Lakretz et al. 2021; iterative arithmetic chains where each step uses only the previous result, not retrieved from context; controlled variable reassignment traces): These are depth-3+ tasks where composition is required but no specific tokens need to be retrieved from arbitrary context positions — the computation can in principle be tracked through sequential state updates. H_comp predicts these FAIL FIRST (high composition depth, need attention layers proportional to depth) while H_func predicts these SURVIVE (non-retrieval tasks handled by linear attention backbone).

(c) *High-depth retrieval* (MuSiQue 2–4 hop with distractors; code execution traces requiring variable lookup across scopes): Both H_comp and H_func predict these degrade — the control condition confirming both mechanisms operate.

(d) *Low-depth non-retrieval* (next-token perplexity on natural text, single-step arithmetic): Both predict these survive — the baseline control.

The critical discriminating comparison is (a) vs (b): H_comp predicts (b) degrades before (a); H_func predicts (a) degrades before (b). H_agnostic predicts both degrade at the same rate regardless of category. For each task, we measure accuracy/perplexity vs attention-ratio curves across all 72 models and test degradation ordering using paired permutation tests (Bonferroni-corrected).

**Phase 3: Component Ablation Validation (~20 A100-hours).** Following Borobia et al.'s (arXiv 2603.22473) methodology, we selectively disable attention vs linear-attention layers in a subset of models (3:1 and 6:1 ratios, both 340M and 1.3B) and measure differential impact across all four task categories. This provides a mechanistic complement to Phase 2's ratio-sweep: if H_func holds, attention ablation should selectively destroy category (a) and (c) performance while preserving (b) and (d); if H_comp holds, ablation impact should track composition depth rather than functional role. We also replicate and extend Michalak & Abreu's finding that as few as 15% of attention heads suffice for retrieval, testing whether this sparsification threshold varies with composition depth (H_comp predicts yes; H_func predicts no).

**Phase 4: Practical Implications Test (~15 A100-hours).** We compare theory-guided attention allocation against empirical methods. If H_comp is supported: allocate attention by task composition depth. If H_func is supported: allocate by retrieval demand (measurable via attention-head activation patterns). If H_agnostic is supported: universal ratios are justified. We test each allocation strategy against KL-guided layer selection (arXiv 2512.20569) and Distill-then-Replace's greedy search (arXiv 2601.11667) on held-out task configurations. This tests practical value: if theory-guided allocation outperforms empirical optimization, the field gains principled design tools; if not, the contribution is characterizing when theory fails to transfer.

**Pre-specified analysis plan.** We commit to formal registration on OSF (Open Science Framework) with timestamped predictions for each hypothesis before running Phase 2–4 experiments. The registration will specify: (i) the exact prediction table for each hypothesis across the 2×2 task matrix, (ii) statistical tests and significance thresholds (α=0.01 for primary comparisons, Bonferroni-corrected), (iii) the decision rule for which hypothesis is supported. We use the OSF Prereg template (Bowman et al., 2020) adapted for ML experiments, following the precedent set by the NeurIPS 2020/2021 pre-registration workshops (Bertinetto et al.). Phase 1 serves as the go/no-go gate decided BEFORE registration.

**Negative-result planning.** All three outcomes produce publishable insights: H_comp validated would be the first empirical confirmation of composition-depth theory in practice; H_agnostic would explain why universal 3:1 ratios work and provide evidence that expressiveness theory has limited practical value for hybrid design; H_func would provide a simpler, actionable alternative to composition-depth analysis — allocate attention by retrieval demand rather than reasoning depth. A mixed outcome (e.g., H_comp holds for synthetic tasks but H_func holds for natural tasks) would be the most informative result, revealing the boundary conditions of theoretical transfer.

### Summary

原始 entrainment 假设（attention 系统性降解 recurrent 层能力）在 3 架构 × 5 attention ratios × 2 scales × 6 benchmarks 上全面 FAIL——无 benchmark 在所有架构上显示一致的 attention 负效应方向。但发现强 secondary finding：Equalizer Effect——attention 使不同 recurrent 架构在 LAMBADA 上趋同（1.3B cross-arch std 从 0.024 降至 0.005），且效应 scale-dependent（1.3B 完全收敛 ρ=-1.0，340M 部分收敛有 rebound）。Per-example 分析揭示 attention 在不同架构上作用于几乎完全不同的 sample 子集（Jaccard ~0.09），产生方向对称的效应（GDN net=-153 vs HGRN2 net=+153）。
