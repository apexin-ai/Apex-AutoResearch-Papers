(1{-}\\alpha) \\cdot \\mathcal{D}_\\text{real}, \\quad \\alpha \\in [0, 1].\n    \\label{eq:mixing}\n\\end{equation}\nAt $\\alpha{=}1.0$, this reduces to the full-replacement paradigm of \\citet{shumailov2024curse}.\n\n\\paragraph{Metric families.}\n\\emph{Canary metrics}: token entropy ($H(p_\\theta | \\text{context})$ on held-out samples) and expected calibration error (ECE).\n\\emph{Downstream metrics}: distinct-$n$ ($n \\in \\{1, 2, 3\\}$)~\\cite{li2016diversity} and perplexity.\nEach metric produces a time series of $T{+}1$ observations.\n\n\\paragraph{Lead-lag testing.}\nGiven canary series $X$ and downstream series $Y$, the central question is whether changes in $X$ temporally precede changes in $Y$, requiring directionality and confounder control beyond simple correlation.\nWe employ five methods spanning permissive to conservative: naive cross-correlation, differenced cross-correlation, threshold onset detection, permutation Granger causality~\\cite{shojaie2022granger}, and Toda--Yamamoto Granger causality~\\cite{toda1995statistical}.\n\n\\section{MMCT Algorithm}\n\\label{app:mmct}\n\nAlgorithm~\\ref{alg:mmct} describes the Multi-Method Consensus Test (MMCT) introduced in \\S\\ref{sec:mmct}.\n\n\\begin{algorithm}[h]\n\\caption{Multi-Method Consensus Test (MMCT)}\n\\label{alg:mmct}\n\\begin{algorithmic}[1]\n\\Require Time series pair $(X, Y)$; methods $\\{m_1, \\ldots, m_K\\}$ ($K{=}5$); null-control data $\\mathcal{N}$ (from $\\alpha{=}0.0$); tolerance $\\epsilon{=}0.01$\n\\Ensure $p$-value, consensus level $\\hat{K}$\n\\For{each method $m_k$}\n    \\State Compute empirical FPR: $\\widehat{\\mathrm{FPR}}_k \\gets$ fraction of null-control pairs where $m_k$ rejects $H_0$\n    \\State Compute weight: $w_k \\gets 1 / \\max(\\widehat{\\mathrm{FPR}}_k,\\; \\epsilon)$\n    \\State Apply $m_k$ to $(X, Y)$: $d_k \\gets \\mathbf{1}[m_k \\text{ rejects } H_0]$\n\\EndFor\n\\State Weighted consensus: $W(X {\\to} Y) \\gets \\sum_{k} w_k \\, d_k \\;/\\; \\sum_{k} w_k$\n\\State Build empirical null distribution of $W$ from all null-control pairs in $\\mathcal{N}$\n\\State Compute $p$-value from empirical null: $p \\gets \\Pr_{\\mathcal{N}}(W \\geq W_{\\mathrm{obs}})$\n\\State Consensus level: $\\hat{K} \\gets \\sum_{k} d_k$\n\\State \\Return $p$, $\\hat{K}$\n\\end{algorithmic}\n\\end{algorithm}\n\n\\paragraph{Design rationale.}\nFPR-inverse weighting penalizes methods with high false positive rates on null-control data: naive cross-correlation (FPR${=}91.7\\%$) receives weight $w{=}1.09$, while PermG (FPR${=}11.1\\%$, the lowest among all five methods) receives $w{=}9.0$.\nThe empirical null distribution avoids parametric assumptions that are unreliable for short ($T{=}5$--$20$), non-stationary series typical of iterative training.\nMMCT is a consensus testing protocol, not a novel statistical method; its value lies in systematic method comparison and the identification of method-dependent conclusions.\n\n\n\\section{Methodological Comparison Table}\n\\label{app:method_comparison}\n\nTable~\\ref{tab:method-comparison} summarizes the methodological coverage of existing canary and monitoring analyses across the diagnostic dimensions identified in \\S\\ref{sec:method}.\nNo prior work combines all six dimensions.\n\n\\begin{table}[h]\n\\centering\n\\small\n\\caption{Methodological comparison of canary and monitoring analyses. \\checkmark\\ = present, $\\times$ = absent, $\\circ$ = partial (${\\leq}2$ levels or limited replication). GPT-2-medium (0/8, both seeds), SmolLM3-3B (3/8, canary metric splitting), and one Pythia-1.4B seed (2/8) do not confirm the pattern.\n\n\\paragraph{Domain-dependent directionality.}\nThe unidirectional forward causation observed on WikiText (PermG reverse${=}$0/8 across all conditions) does not hold universally.\nOn C4 at $\\alpha{=}0.50$ (s42 fp32), PermG reverse reaches 7/8, indicating near-symmetric bidirectional coupling; however, this is seed-dependent (s43: 1/8) and noted as preliminary.\nThe practical implication is that directional asymmetry should be verified per domain.\n\n\\paragraph{Token entropy versus perplexity.}\nThe distinction lies in the evaluation domain: token entropy is $H(p_\\theta | \\text{context})$ on held-out real data, while perplexity is $\\exp(H_\\text{cross})$ on model-generated text.\nFirst-differencing removes their shared downward trend: the canary$\\to$diversity channel survives because entropy leads diversity by one generation, whereas the canary$\\to$perplexity channel vanishes because both co-move synchronously.\n\n\\paragraph{NLP-specific constraints.}\nOur application of Granger-type methods faces constraints absent in econometrics: (i)~observation length ($T{=}5$--$20$ vs.\\ $T{=}100$--$1000$); (ii)~non-stationarity by design rather than incidental; (iii)~small multiplicity for FDR correction (8--32 pairs per regime).\nTransfer entropy (0/192 FDR-significant results) confirms that method sensitivity extends beyond the five-method panel.\n\n\\paragraph{Prevention paradox.}\nEffective intervention truncates the collapse trajectory that established Granger precedence.\nDifferenced cross-correlation retains significance under intervention (31/32 pairs), while threshold onset fails entirely (0/8), reinforcing the multi-method rationale.\n\n\\paragraph{Grouped testing rationale.}\nWe test 8 canary$\\to$downstream pairs jointly (2 canaries $\\times$ 4 downstream metrics) rather than individual per-metric tests because: (i)~joint testing with BH-FDR correction controls family-wise error across the canary panel, avoiding the inflated false-positive rate that would result from reporting the single most significant pair; (ii)~the count of significant pairs ($k/8$) provides a natural effect-size measure that distinguishes isolated from systematic detection; (iii)~per-metric tests cannot capture the cross-metric consistency that separates genuine temporal precedence from metric-specific artifacts (e.g., MAUVE floor saturation affects 2/8 pairs universally, identifiable only in grouped results).\n\n\\paragraph{Canary metric choice.}\nSmolLM3-3B reveals that canary metric selection constitutes a sensitivity dimension beyond the five statistical methods: token entropy exhibits non-monotonic oscillations (0/4 significant pairs), while ECE maintains monotonic growth (3/4 pairs).\nNo single canary metric is universally optimal.\n\n\\paragraph{Exploratory CSD analysis.}\nCanary metrics show rising autocorrelation (Kendall $\\tau{=}0.79$--$0.82$) while downstream metrics show none ($\\tau{=}0.03$--$0.30$), but this pattern does not survive multiple testing correction (2/10 post-BH) and remains exploratory.\nWe note that \\citet{truong2026phase} show model collapse is a first-order transition, ruling out CSD as a structural detection mechanism;
