## Core Problem

Online monitoring tools for model collapse during training now exist—SIGMA tracks spectral contraction of embedding Gram matrices, DCES couples intra-cluster dispersion monitoring with reactive data rebalancing—but they operate descriptively or reactively, detecting collapse as it manifests rather than predicting it ahead of time. Empirical work has begun to hint at temporal ordering among metrics: Du et al. (arXiv 2510.05133) observe informally that calibration degradation (ECE) precedes accuracy loss in Pythia models trained on synthetic data, but this observation is based on a single metric pair across only 1–3 iterations without formal statistical testing. Theoretical frameworks now characterize the dynamics governing collapse-vs.-improvement trajectories as functions of mixture weights and sample sizes (Bakshi & Chakraborty 2026; Wang et al. 2026; Fu et al. ICLR 2025), yet the key parameters of these frameworks (effective synthetic contamination ratio, true-data fraction decay) are difficult to observe directly during training. Meanwhile, Truong & Truong (arXiv 2512.12381) prove that entropy collapse in feedback-amplified systems constitutes a first-order phase transition where standard early-warning indicators (autocorrelation, variance) remain finite—raising the fundamental question of whether any observable signals can provide advance warning. The open question is: which families of cheaply computable observable metrics provide the earliest predictive warning of collapse, under what contamination regimes do predictive signals exist (vs. abrupt first-order transitions), how many training steps ahead can they forecast downstream degradation, and can predictive (rather than reactive) intervention timing improve both collapse prevention and compute efficiency?

## Opportunity / Why Now

SIGMA (arXiv 2601.03385) provides mathematically grounded spectral monitoring but proposes no corrective interventions and does not quantify predictive lead time relative to downstream metrics. DCES (Electronics 2026) couples monitoring with interventions but uses a single signal (intra-cluster dispersion) and adjusts reactively when drift is already detected, not predictively before it manifests. Du et al. (arXiv 2510.05133) provide the closest empirical precedent: using the same Pythia model family (410M–12B) with synthetic data ratios of 0–50%, they observe that ECE increases 'dramatically' while accuracy 'remains stable until Iteration 1,' with a threshold rule (>50% ECE increase predicts accuracy degradation at t+1 with 91% precision). However, this is a single informal observation about one metric pair (ECE → accuracy) across only 1–3 training iterations, without formal lead-lag statistical testing, without comparison to alternative metric families, and without extending beyond 3 iterations where cumulative degradation dynamics differ qualitatively. Neither SIGMA, DCES, nor Du et al. systematically compares text-surface metrics (n-gram entropy, self-BLEU, tail-token coverage, calibration error) against representation-space metrics (spectral bounds, embedding anisotropy) for predictive power using formal time-series methodology. On the theoretical side, Truong & Truong (arXiv 2512.12381) prove that entropy collapse in Class 1 feedback-amplified systems (which includes iterative self-training) is a first-order phase transition at the critical threshold α_c(β) = 1/(1−β), where autocorrelation and variance remain finite, providing no advance warning. This poses a fundamental theoretical challenge to any early-warning monitoring approach—but applies specifically to systems where feedback amplification α exceeds the critical threshold, and specifically to autocorrelation and variance as warning indicators. Whether the gradual-contamination regimes typical of practical synthetic data pipelines (where the effective α may remain below α_c) admit detectable precursors via other signal families is an open empirical question that our work directly tests. The theoretical dynamics framework of Bakshi & Chakraborty (arXiv 2602.10531) precisely characterizes the recurrence relation governing risk evolution—showing that collapse depends on mixture weight trajectories (αt) and sample size scaling (nt)—while Fu et al. (arXiv 2502.18865, ICLR 2025) prove that even a constant-sized proportion of real data ensures convergence in self-consuming loops. But neither provides methods for practitioners to observe where they sit on this trajectory during training. The surplexity framework (arXiv 2410.12341) introduces novel collapse-characterization metrics from next-token distributions but does not study their temporal dynamics during iterative training. Kalinowski (arXiv 2604.26984) demonstrates that topology-based monitoring can predict representational collapse ~1.4–5.6 epochs early in fine-tuning, but targets representational collapse geometry rather than synthetic-data distribution degradation, and requires maintaining discrete Morse complexes with architecture-specific hyperparameter tuning—making it impractical as a cheap, general-purpose canary. With iterative training runs costing thousands of GPU-hours, knowing which cheap metrics reliably predict collapse K steps early—and under which contamination regimes such prediction is theoretically possible—could save significant compute. This directly addresses EMNLP 2026's special theme on the consequences of synthetic data feedback loops within the 'New Missions for NLP Research' track.

## Landscape (SOTA & Limitations)

Post-hoc collapse analysis is well-established: declining n-gram entropy, self-BLEU increase, and tail distribution erosion are documented signatures (Nature 2024; EMNLP 2025 Oral on recursive training). The transition to online monitoring has begun from multiple directions. SIGMA (arXiv 2601.03385, Jan 2026) derives deterministic and stochastic bounds on embedding Gram matrix spectra to track representation-space contraction during recursive training, demonstrating on OPT-125M that its size-corrected Track II metric shows earlier sensitivity than conservative Track I bounds—but does not quantify predictive lead time relative to downstream metrics, test NLP-native text-surface signals, or propose corrective interventions. DCES (Electronics 2026, Feb 2026) implements a closed-loop framework coupling intra-cluster dispersion monitoring with dynamic center-edge sampling rebalancing during iterative self-training—the first system to close the monitor-intervene loop—but operates reactively (adjusting when dispersion changes are detected) rather than predictively, and relies on a single monitoring signal without comparing alternative metric families for detection speed. Du et al. (arXiv 2510.05133, Oct 2025) conduct the most directly relevant empirical study: using Pythia 410M–12B with synthetic data ratios of 0–50% across 1–3 training iterations on five diverse tasks, they observe that calibration degradation (ECE increase) precedes accuracy loss, establishing a threshold rule where >50% ECE increase predicts significant accuracy degradation at t+1 with 91% precision and 87% recall. This is the closest empirical predecessor to our lead-lag analysis. However, their observation is limited in three key ways: (a) they examine a single metric pair (ECE → accuracy) without comparing alternative canary families; (b) they span only 1–3 iterations, insufficient to observe the cumulative degradation dynamics that emerge over longer iterative chains; (c) they use a simple threshold rule rather than formal time-series statistical testing (no Granger causality or cross-correlation analysis). Our work generalizes Du et al.'s insight into a systematic, statistically rigorous framework: comparing four full metric families across 10 generations with panel Granger causality, ranking metrics by predictive lead time, and testing whether calibration is indeed the best canary or whether spectral or text-surface metrics provide earlier warning. On the theoretical side, Bakshi & Chakraborty (arXiv 2602.10531, Feb 2026) derive the exact recurrence relation for risk evolution under iterative training on synthetic-contaminated data, proving that collapse, improvement, or consistency depends on the trajectory of mixture weights (αt) and sample sizes (nt)—specifically, if αt is bounded away from zero and nt grows sufficiently, the true distribution is recoverable. Fu et al. (arXiv 2502.18865, ICLR 2025) independently prove that even a constant-sized proportion of real data ensures convergence in self-consuming training loops, establishing the concept of 'recursive stability' as a function of both model architecture and real-to-synthetic data balance—directly supporting our 50% accumulation condition as a theoretically grounded convergence regime. Wang et al. (arXiv 2602.16065, Feb 2026) extend these results to general universal approximators without distributional assumptions. These theoretical frameworks characterize *when* collapse occurs but do not address *how to detect it early from observables*—the key practical gap our work fills. Truong & Truong (arXiv 2512.12381, Dec 2025) prove that entropy collapse in feedback-amplified adaptive systems constitutes a first-order (discontinuous) phase transition at the critical threshold α_c(β) = 1/(1−β), where α is feedback amplification strength and β is bounded novelty regeneration rate. Crucially, they show that autocorrelation and variance—the standard early-warning indicators from critical transitions theory—remain finite at the transition, providing no advance warning. This impossibility applies to their Class 1 systems (convex feedback, κ > 0), which includes multiplicative-weight mechanisms and iterative self-training. However, three important boundary conditions limit the scope of this impossibility result for our setting: (a) the impossibility applies specifically when α approaches or exceeds α_c, not to gradual degradation in regimes where α remains below threshold—our 50% accumulation condition likely maintains α below α_c due to continuous real-data injection; (b) it applies to autocorrelation and variance as indicators, not to all possible observable signals—our canary families (spectral concentration, n-gram entropy rates, calibration error, cluster dispersion) measure fundamentally different quantities than the two signals proven uninformative; (c) their Class 2 systems (linear feedback, κ = 0) remain amenable to critical-slowing-down monitoring, and practical synthetic data pipelines with data accumulation may exhibit intermediate dynamics between the two universality classes. Our experimental design directly tests the boundary between regimes where predictive signals exist and where they do not—itself a novel contribution. Kalinowski (arXiv 2604.26984, April 2026) proposes topology-based online monitoring via a Collapse Index (CI) combining Betti number changes, critical cell churn, cycle fragility, and boundary footprint. CI predicts representational collapse ~1.4–5.6 epochs early in LLM fine-tuning, making it the closest prior work to early-warning monitoring. However, CI targets representational collapse geometry (dimensional collapse via Betti numbers) rather than the distributional degradation specific to synthetic data feedback loops (tail erosion, entropy decay, mode narrowing), and requires maintaining discrete Morse complexes with architecture-specific hyperparameter tuning—making it impractical as a cheap, general-purpose canary. The surplexity framework (arXiv 2410.12341) introduces collapse-characterization metrics from next-token probability distributions but does not study their temporal dynamics across iterative training generations. The 'Position: Model Collapse Does Not Mean What You Think' (arXiv 2503.03150, ICML 2025) argues that prominent collapse scenarios rely on unrealistic assumptions but acknowledges that tail data losses and diversity erosion are genuine concerns—motivating a monitoring-as-guardrail approach. The critical gap remains the transition from concurrent/reactive monitoring to predictive early warning: no work systematically evaluates which metric combinations predict collapse K steps ahead using formal lead-lag analysis, ranks metric families by earliest reliable warning time, tests under which contamination regimes predictive signals exist (addressing Truong & Truong's impossibility boundary), or demonstrates that predictive intervention timing outperforms reactive approaches. Our work bridges the theoretical dynamics frameworks (which characterize collapse conditions in terms of unobservable parameters), the informal empirical observation of metric temporal ordering (Du et al.'s ECE-leads-accuracy finding), the phase-transition impossibility results (Truong & Truong's first-order boundary), and practical monitoring (which provides observable early-warning signals to practitioners).

## Proposed Approach

We conduct a systematic lead-lag analysis comparing four families of cheaply computable collapse canary metrics for their ability to predict downstream degradation ahead of time, validate empirical findings against both convergence theory and phase-transition impossibility results, and demonstrate that predictive intervention timing outperforms reactive monitoring.

Canary metric families and topology exclusion rationale. We define four families of lightweight canary metrics chosen for minimal computational overhead beyond normal training: (1) Text-surface: token-level entropy, bigram/trigram frequency decay rate, self-BLEU over generated samples, tail-token coverage (fraction of the original distribution's low-frequency tokens still generated above threshold), and surplexity (surprise-based metric from next-token distributions, following arXiv 2410.12341). These require only sampling from the current model. (2) Calibration: expected calibration error (ECE) and overconfidence gap, computed on a held-out validation set. Du et al. (arXiv 2510.05133) informally observed ECE leading accuracy loss in Pythia models; we include calibration as a separate family to rigorously test this finding with formal statistical methodology across extended iterative chains (10 generations vs. their 1–3). (3) Spectral: SIGMA's Track I (deterministic Gram envelope) and Track II (size-corrected covariance statistic) computed on embeddings from a frozen sentence encoder, plus eigenvalue concentration (effective rank) of the representation covariance matrix. These require a single forward pass through a frozen encoder. (4) Clustering-based: intra-cluster dispersion via k-means on the embedding space, following DCES's monitoring signal. We deliberately exclude topology-based metrics (Kalinowski's Collapse Index) from our canary families for three reasons: (a) CI targets representational collapse geometry in fine-tuning (dimensional collapse via Betti numbers) rather than the distributional degradation specific to synthetic data loops (tail erosion, entropy decay); (b) CI requires maintaining discrete Morse complexes and k-NN graphs with architecture-specific hyperparameter tuning—not 'cheap' in our practitioner-deployability sense; (c) CI's 6-component weighted formula with task-specific weight calibration conflicts with our goal of identifying simple, broadly applicable canaries. We note CI as the closest prior work to early-warning monitoring and discuss its complementary scope.

Experimental design. We run controlled iterative retraining chains using Pythia-410M (primary) and Pythia-160M (scaling analysis). Primary dataset: WikiText-103. At each generation, the model is fine-tuned on a mixture of real and synthetic data at two ratios (50% realistic, 100% stress test) for 10 generations per chain, with 5 independent replications per condition (2 ratios × 2 models × 5 reps × 10 gens = 200 generation runs). We additionally run a 0% control (1 replication per model, 20 generation runs) as a sanity check confirming no degradation from the fine-tuning procedure itself; this does not enter the panel analysis. For cross-domain generalizability, we run a second-domain validation on a web-crawl subset (C4, subsampled to match WikiText-103 size) at 50% ratio with Pythia-410M (3 reps × 10 gens = 30 generation runs), testing whether metric family rankings transfer across text domains. All canary metrics are computed every 500 training steps within each generation (yielding ~100+ checkpoint-level observations per chain) alongside downstream metrics (perplexity on held-out real data, MAUVE score, distinct-n, accuracy on a held-out task subset). The 50% ratio with data accumulation (each generation adds to the training pool rather than replacing) directly addresses the ICML 2025 position paper's critique of unrealistic full-replacement assumptions, and aligns with the theoretical conditions under which both Bakshi & Chakraborty (arXiv 2602.10531) and Fu et al. (arXiv 2502.18865) predict convergence rather than collapse (bounded αt > 0 with constant real-data proportion).

Stationarity and lead-lag analysis. Training metrics during iterative collapse (e.g., monotonically declining entropy, rising self-BLEU) are likely non-stationary—a critical methodological concern for Granger causality testing, which assumes stationary series. We address this with a three-stage protocol. Stage 1 — Unit root pre-testing: we apply both ADF (Augmented Dickey-Fuller) and KPSS tests to each canary and downstream metric time series within each chain, classifying series as I(0) or I(1). We expect many metrics to be I(1) during collapse phases (trending) and I(0) during stable phases. Stage 2 — Primary analysis via Toda-Yamamoto augmented-lag panel Granger causality: following the Emirmahmutoglu & Kose (2011) extension of Toda & Yamamoto (1995) to heterogeneous panels, we estimate panel VARs in levels with lag order p augmented by d_max additional lags (where d_max is the maximum integration order from Stage 1), testing causality only on the first p lags. This approach is valid regardless of integration order and avoids the information loss and potential spurious regression pitfalls of pre-differencing, while accommodating cross-sectional heterogeneity across replications. We apply bootstrap correction for cross-sectional dependence across chains. Stage 3 — Robustness checks: (a) for series confirmed I(0) by both ADF and KPSS, we additionally report standard Dumitrescu-Hurlin panel Granger causality as a benchmark; (b) we apply Pedroni panel cointegration tests to determine whether canary and downstream metrics share common stochastic trends—if cointegrated, we supplement with panel VECM-based Granger causality to capture long-run equilibrium adjustment dynamics; (c) we report individual time-series Granger causality tests on each chain combined via Fisher's method; (d) we compute cross-correlation functions as a non-parametric complement. All unit root and cointegration test results are reported transparently in the appendix. We apply Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction for multiple metric comparisons across the four canary families. We rank metrics by earliest reliable warning time, test whether cheap text-surface or calibration metrics match or exceed spectral metrics in predictive power, and assess whether metric predictiveness varies with synthetic data ratio, model scale, or text domain.

Theory validation and phase-transition boundary testing. We validate empirical lead-lag signatures against two complementary theoretical frameworks. First, convergence theory: at 50% ratio with data accumulation (αt bounded, nt growing), both Bakshi & Chakraborty's recurrence relation and Fu et al.'s recursive stability theory predict convergence, so canary metrics should show late or no warning; at 100% ratio (αt → 0 after generation 0), theory predicts collapse, so canary metrics should show early warning. Second, phase-transition theory: Truong & Truong prove that when feedback amplification α exceeds the critical threshold α_c(β) = 1/(1−β), entropy collapse is first-order with no advance warning from autocorrelation or variance. Our 100% ratio condition—where the system receives maximal feedback amplification—may approach or exceed this threshold, and we explicitly test whether our canary families (which measure different quantities than autocorrelation and variance) detect precursors even in this regime. If our 100% condition indeed shows abrupt collapse with no lead-lag signal from any canary family, this would be consistent with Truong & Truong's prediction and constitute an empirical validation of their theoretical boundary—a finding that refines the scope of 'which canaries work where' rather than a failure of our method. Conversely, our 50% accumulation condition, where effective feedback amplification is attenuated by continuous real-data injection, likely operates below the critical threshold in a regime of gradual degradation where precursors may be detectable. Concordance between theoretical regime boundaries and empirical canary activation patterns strengthens both the theoretical frameworks and our monitoring tool.

Composite Collapse Risk Score (CRS). We combine the top-ranked predictive metrics into a composite score using logistic regression on the binary outcome 'collapse onset within K steps,' with calibrated thresholds validated via leave-one-chain-out cross-validation.

Intervention experiments. We compare three strategies on Pythia-410M at 50% synthetic ratio (5 reps × 10 gens each, 150 additional runs): (a) no intervention baseline, (b) CRS-triggered predictive intervention (switch to 0% synthetic when CRS exceeds calibrated threshold), (c) DCES-style reactive intervention (adjust ratio when intra-cluster dispersion drops below threshold). With 5 replications per condition (increased from 3 to ensure adequate statistical power), we conduct pairwise comparisons using non-parametric tests (Wilcoxon rank-sum). We measure both collapse prevention effectiveness (final perplexity, MAUVE, tail-token coverage) and compute efficiency (total GPU-hours to reach target quality).

Timeline and pre-writing strategy. With 14 days to the ARR deadline, we adopt a parallel writing strategy: related work and methodology sections are drafted during days 1–4 (before experiments complete, since these are fully specified); experiments run days 1–10; results analysis and remaining sections days 8–14. This tight but feasible schedule assumes the auto-research execution mode.

[方法论定位 / §Why Standard Correlation Analysis Fails] — 原始 proposal 将 Toda-Yamamoto Granger causality 定位为"causal analysis"。修改为 **"robust temporal precedence analysis with causal-inference-grade statistical controls"**，明确区分两个层次：不是论证因果方向（受控实验中已知），而是识别哪些可观测指标具有对下游退化的时序领先性。增加专段论述为什么 naive Pearson/Spearman 在非平稳训练序列上产生伪回归（Phillips 1986），以及 panel 结构中 replications 作为 cross-sectional units 的局限性正面讨论。 — 命中 Soundness（消除"受控实验中为何需要因果推断工具"的最强攻击面）+ Novelty（伪回归问题的揭示本身是方法论贡献）

4. [方法论 ablation / 新增章节] — 原始 proposal 无方法论对比。新增 **4-way methodological ablation**：(i) naive Pearson/Spearman lag-correlation；(ii) 差分后 cross-correlation（消除共趋势）；(iii) threshold-based lead time；(iv) Toda-Yamamoto panel temporal precedence。预测在 α=50-75% 过渡区间，naive correlation 显示伪显著关联而 robust 方法正确识别缺少真实领先性。显式报告 **"多少比例的 naive 显著关联在 robust 检验下被推翻"** 的量化指标。 — 命中 Novelty（"ML 训练曲线中伪回归的普遍性"有独立引用价值）+ Soundness（方法选择从 argued 变为 empirically demonstrated）

5. [合成数据生成协议 / 实验设计] — 原始 proposal 完全未指定生成过程。新增 **三种生成模式的完整协议**：(a) Self-Consuming (SC)：每代用上一代 checkpoint 生成，nucleus sampling p=0.95，对应 Truong & Truong Class 1 反馈放大系统；(b) Fixed-Source (FS)：Pythia-6.9B 一次性生成固定池，对应 Bakshi & Chakraborty 平稳污染框架；(c) Cross-Family (CF)：OPT-6.7B 生成，模拟 web 数据中混入未知来源 LLM 输出。每种模式显式映射到对应理论框架的假设条件。 — 命中 Soundness（实验可复现性从无到有）+ Significance（CF mode 直接回答生态效度质疑）

6. [污染梯度 / 实验设计] — 原始仅 50% 和 100% 两个比例。扩展为 **α ∈ {0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 90%, 100%} 六个水平**，0% 为纯真实数据 baseline。这使得 predictability phase diagram（相图）有实质数据支撑，可定位 α_c 在相邻测试点之间，区分一阶/连续相变。 — 命中 Soundness（从两个端点到完整 dose-response curve）+ Novelty（phase diagram 需要足够密的采样点）

7. [模型规模 / 实验设计] — 原始仅 Pythia-160M 和 410M。扩展为 **四级规模**：160M/410M（full factorial）、1.4B（核心条件完整 temporal precedence 分析，3 reps）、2.8B（3 核心条件 × 3 reps 的 spot-check）。增加 Kendall's τ 跨规模排名一致性检验。但诚实将 scale 定位为 **limitation + 最高优先级 future work**，不暗示已验证 scale invariance 到 7B+。 — 命中 Soundness（reviewer 必问的 scale 问题有初步回应）+ 避免 over-claim

8. [Canary Metric 族 / 方法] — 原始为四族已有指标。修改为 **五族**，新增：(a) **Family 5: Critical Slowing Down (CSD) Indicators**（lag-1 autocorrelation、rolling variance、recovery rate），基于动力系统相变理论（Scheffer et al. 2009），在 phase diagram 中提供独立于 predictability 分析的第二条证据线（dual-evidence / converging evidence 结构）；(b) 在 Spectral 族中新增 **attention homogenization index**（跨 head/layer 的 attention entropy 均值），作为 transformer-specific 新指标。 — 命中 Novelty（CSD 是理论驱动的指标设计，attention homogenization 非标准；不再是纯"已有指标拼盘"）+ Soundness（converging evidence 大幅提升 regime boundary 声明可信度）

9. [Lead Time 操作化定义 / 新增章节] — 原始无定义。新增完整的 **operationalized lead time**：signal onset（canary 偏离 α=0% baseline > 2σ 且持续 ≥ 2 window）到 degradation onset（downstream 偏离 > 2σ 且持续 ≥ 2 window）的步数差。补充 **out-of-sample VAR 预测**（K ∈ {500, 1000, 2000, 5000}，canary-augmented vs. AR-only 的 RMSE/MAE）和 **minimum actionable lead time** 标准（2,000 步 ≈ 1 generation）。 — 命中 Soundness（从模糊的"ranking by lead time"到精确可复现的度量）

10. [CRS 设计 / 方法] — 原始仅提及"combining the most predictive signals"。重新设计为 **理论驱动的 composite score**：z-score 归一化 + L1 正则化权重学习（ROC 优化）。关键新增：(a) **CRS-equal 零校准 baseline**（等权，无需 collapse 先验数据），与 CRS-learned 对比——如果 CRS-equal 足够好则解决 cold-start 问题；(b) **WikiText→C4 zero-shot transfer** 测试跨域泛化；(c) **α-agnostic 监控模式**：当 α 未知时（现实最常见场景），CRS 提供 behavior-based 告警，结合 phase diagram 反向推断污染程度；(d) 开源 HuggingFace callback 工具包承诺。 — 命中 Significance（从 ad hoc 组合到 principled + deployable artifact）+ Novelty（zero-calibration 部署 + α-agnostic 模式是对 practitioner 的直接价值）

11. [Intervention 实验 / 方法] — 原始仅为 placeholder。新版设计为 **聚焦的 3×1 核心比较**（CRS-predictive vs. DCES-reactive vs. no-intervention，action 统一为 data replacement），在 α=75% 和 100% × SC × Pythia-410M × 3 reps 执行（9 runs）。替代 action（mixture adjustment、rollback）放入 supplementary。评估指标：最终质量（perplexity/MMLU/GSM8K）、recovery efficiency（AUC）、intervention 频率、compute overhead。在 contribution list 中升级为 **三大核心贡献之一**。 — 命中 Significance（完成 detect→decide→act→measure 闭环）+ Soundness（从不可执行的 placeholder 到可复现的 protocol）

【主要风险】:

1. **Novelty ceiling → Findings 分流风险**：尽管 framing 和贡献数量显著改善，核心范式仍是"跨学科方法迁移（计量经济学→NLP）+ 系统性实证研究"。如果审稿人不认可 temporal precedence framework、phase diagram 概念、和伪回归演示的独立贡献价值，可能将论文归类为"thorough empirical study"而推荐 Findings。**能否突破这一 ceiling 高度依赖方法论 ablation 的实验结果**——如果伪回归 prevalence 的量化数据确实 surprising（如 >30% 的 naive 显著关联被推翻），reviewer 会更倾向于认可方法论贡献。

2. **Scale gap 的 significance 折扣**：160M-2.8B 的实验范围在 2026 审稿语境下偏小。即使诚实标注为 limitation，reviewer 仍可能在 significance 评分中扣分，认为对 7B+ production 模型的 practitioner 参考价值有限。这一风险无法在当前预算内完全消除，只能通过 scaling trend 的初步证据和 future work 的明确指向来缓解。

【实施评估】:
- **计算资源**: 约 500-600 H100-GPU-hours。核心实验 ~130-150 training run chains（3 generation modes × 6 α levels × 4 model scales × 2-5 replications）+ 9 intervention runs + 3 extended 20-generation runs + downstream evaluation overhead。可在标准云 GPU 集群（如 AWS p5、GCP a3）上分批执行，单次最大并行需求约 8-16 H100。
- **数据需求**: WikiText-103、C4、CNN/DailyMail、MMLU、GSM8K（全部公开可得）。Pythia 系列和 OPT-6.7B checkpoints（HuggingFace 公开）。无需私有数据。
- **人工介入**: 无需人工标注。Linguistic collapse phenotyping 使用自动化文本分析（n-gram 统计、parse tree 分析）配合有限的人工定性检查（约 2-3 人天用于 case study 挑选和描述）。实验设计、代码实现、结果分析和论文写作为标准研究者工作量。
- **训练需求**: 需要模型训练。每个 training run 是对 Pythia 模型（160M-2.8B）在 WikiText-103 混合数据上的 10,000 步微调/继续预训练 × 10-20 generations。不涉及从头预训练。单个 run（Pythia-410M, 10 gen）约 2-3 H100-hours；Pythia-2.8B 约 8-12 H100-hours。
- **新 Benchmark**: 不需要构建新 benchmark。所有评估使用现有公开 benchmark。核心产出是 CRS 监控工具包（开源 HuggingFace callback）和 predictability phase diagram（实验结果的可视化框架），不是新的评测数据集。
- **预估周期**: 从启动到可投稿约 **5-6 个月**。拆分为：实验基础设施搭建 2-3 周；核心实验执行（含 SC/FS/CF 全矩阵）6-8 周；intervention 实验 2-3 周；分析与 ablation 3-4 周；论文写作与内部 review 4-5 周。关键路径在核心实验执行（受 GPU 并行度限制）和 2025-2026 文献系统调研（需在写作前完成）。
