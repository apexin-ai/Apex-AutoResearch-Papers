=== Key Diagnostic ===

1. DETRENDING ELIMINATES SYNC-DIP SIGNIFICANCE
   - Original: 4/6 pairs significant (p<0.001 for mauve pairs)
   - After first-difference: 0/6 significant (p_min=0.112)
   - Conclusion: Original TE signal was driven by shared monotonic downward
     trend (canary↓ + downstream↓), NOT genuine nonlinear information transfer.
   - The m=2 ordinal patterns (only "up"/"down") are dominated by the shared
     trend direction, inflating apparent TE.

2. m=3 SHOWS NO SIGNIFICANCE
   - All 12 pairs non-significant with m=3 (6 patterns vs 2).
   - Caveat: with T=10-20 data points and m=3, effective series length is
     T-2=8-18, yielding very few ordinal patterns. Low statistical power may
     contribute, but the direction matches the detrending result.

3. POSITIVE CONTROLS MOSTLY UNCHANGED
   - Controls remain non-significant across all conditions (0/6),
     except T15_a050_s42 distinct_1 detrended (p=0.000) — isolated anomaly.
   - This single detrended-significant control pair does not support a
     "reversed significance" interpretation.

4. HIGH SURROGATES CONFIRM ORIGINAL
   - 2000 surrogates produce identical significance pattern to 500.
   - Original p-values were already well-resolved.

=== Implications for Paper ===

The Symbolic TE finding (4/6 sync-dip pairs significant) is NOT robust to
detrending. It should NOT be presented as evidence of nonlinear causal coupling
in the sync-dip regime.

Options:
  a) Remove TE claim entirely from the paper.
  b) Report TE result WITH the detrending caveat as a transparency/honesty point:
     "Symbolic TE detects shared-trend coupling but not trend-independent
     information transfer, consistent with the linear shared-cause interpretation."
  c) Use the negative detrending result as SUPPORTING evidence that the sync-dip
     phenomenon is driven by shared linear dynamics (shared trend), not nonlinear
     causal mechanisms — which actually strengthens the PermG-based interpretation.

Recommendation: Option (c) — reframe TE as a diagnostic that confirms the
shared-trend nature of the coupling, rather than claiming nonlinear causation.
