# FPR Sensitivity Analysis: PermG at 10% FPR

We evaluate the robustness of the MMCT consensus framework under the conservative assumption that PermG's true FPR could be as high as 10% (the upper bound of a cluster-adjusted confidence interval with only 3 seed-clusters). Even under this assumption, PermG remains the lowest-FPR method among the five in our framework: the next-best method (Toda-Yamamoto) has a pooled FPR of 16.7%, followed by Threshold Onset at 25.0%, Differenced Cross-Correlation at 37.5%, and Naive Cross-Correlation at 91.7%. Crucially, our primary results rely on equal-weight K≥3 majority voting, where each method contributes exactly one vote regardless of its FPR—this mechanism is completely unaffected by any FPR assumption. For the exploratory FPR-inverse weighting analysis, PermG's weight drops from effectively infinite (at observed 0%) to 10.0 (at hypothetical 10%), but this still exceeds all alternatives (TY: 6.0, Threshold: 4.0, DiffXCorr: 2.67), preserving the full rank ordering. The insensitivity of both the voting mechanism and the method ranking to this worst-case FPR assumption provides strong evidence that the MMCT framework's conclusions are robust to uncertainty in PermG's Type I error rate.
