## Core Problem

Agent self-correction training methods — including ETO's trajectory-level DPO, DMPO's multi-turn DPO with length normalization, Agent-R's MCTS-guided SFT, IPR's step-level MC-reward contrastive training, CSO's critical-step DPO, GiGPO's anchor-state grouping, and HCAPO's hindsight credit assignment — identify WHAT step to correct but not WHAT TYPE of error occurred. They apply undifferentiated recovery mechanisms and, critically, uniform temporal credit assignment regardless of whether the agent failed due to a single-step action slip (correctable in 1 step) or a multi-step planning breakdown (requiring 4-8 steps of replanning). AP2O (AAAI 2026) demonstrates progressive error-type curriculum for code generation, but its frequency-based ordering and single discount factor miss a fundamental property of interactive agent errors: different error types have structurally different correction horizons — the number of steps needed to recover. This correction-horizon mismatch means DMPO's length normalization (single γ for all steps) systematically under-credits long planning corrections and over-credits short action fixes. GiGPO's anchor state grouping achieves fine-grained credit assignment via state-based trajectory grouping but applies uniform treatment regardless of error type. Can a framework that (1) orders training by error-type difficulty grounded in correction horizon, (2) adapts the temporal discount factor to match each error type's correction span, and (3) ensures correction pairs actually address the diagnosed error type, yield better recovery rates and cross-environment transfer than type-agnostic or flat-conditioned approaches?

(8) The gap is specific: interactive agent errors have a natural correction-horizon gradient — action errors (correction span ~1 step), verification errors (~2-4 steps), planning errors (~4-8 steps) — that should inform both curriculum ordering AND temporal credit assignment in the loss function. No prior work adapts the discount factor to error-type-specific correction horizons.

## Landscape (SOTA & Limitations)

General self-correction: SCoRe (ICLR 2025 Oral) establishes that SFT for self-correction fails due to distribution mismatch and behavior collapse, advocating online RL. ETO (ACL 2024) proposes trajectory-level DPO on failure-success pairs across ALFWorld/WebShop/ScienceWorld with ~9-20% gains — trajectory-level, no error-type awareness; we use its public codebase for environment infrastructure. DMPO (EMNLP 2024, arXiv 2406.14868) extends DPO to multi-turn agents via SAOM constraints and length normalization with discount weighting φ(t,T) = γ^t·(1-γ^(T-t))/(1-γ^T), evaluated on ALFWorld/WebShop/ScienceWorld with Llama-2-7B. DMPO's single γ treats all correction types identically; we extend its normalization with error-type-specific discount factors. IPR (EMNLP 2024, arXiv 2406.11176) performs step-level contrastive training using MC rollout rewards and expert demonstrations on WebShop/ALFWorld/InterCodeSQL with Llama-2-13B. Agent-R (arXiv 2501.11425, ByteDance) identifies first-error steps via MCTS and trains recovery via iterative SFT on Llama-3.1-8B, +5.59% avg — no error-type conditioning. CSO (arXiv 2602.03412, submitted to ACL 2026) performs step-level DPO at verified critical steps, achieving 37% improvement over SFT on GAIA-Text-103 — step-level but not error-type-conditioned. STEP-LEVEL RL METHODS: GiGPO (NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2505.10978) introduces two-level credit assignment with anchor state grouping — episode-level macro advantages paired with step-level micro advantages from grouping actions at identical states — achieving >12% on ALFWorld and >9% on WebShop over GRPO with Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B/7B. GiGPO identifies credit via state-based grouping across trajectories but applies uniform treatment regardless of error type; we show error-type-specific discount factors provide an orthogonal and complementary signal. SALT (EACL 2026 Findings, arXiv 2510.20022) constructs trajectory graphs for step-level advantage assignment in GRPO, achieving +3.4pp on ALFWorld and +4.7pp on AppWorld across Qwen2.5-Instruct 1.5B/7B/32B — step-level advantage without error-type awareness. HCAPO (arXiv 2603.08754, March 2026) leverages the LLM itself as a post-hoc critic to refine step-level Q-values through hindsight reasoning with a multi-scale advantage mechanism, achieving +13.8% on ALFWorld and +7.7% on WebShop with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct — concurrent work addressing credit assignment quality but without error-type conditioning. StepPO (arXiv 2604.18401, April 2026) proposes step-level PPO with step-level GAE, evaluated on HotpotQA — no error-type conditioning. T2PO (ICML 2026 Spotlight, arXiv 2605.02178) introduces uncertainty-guided token- and turn-level exploration control for stable multi-turn agentic RL on WebShop/ALFWorld — addresses training stability without error-type awareness. Turn-PPO (EACL 2026 Findings, arXiv 2512.17008) applies turn-level advantage estimation. STeCa (ACL 2025 Findings) uses step-level trajectory calibration with DTW-based reward refinement. These methods confirm step-level credit assignment is critical but none differentiate error types or adapt temporal discounting to correction horizons. ERROR-TYPE APPROACHES: AgentDebug (Sept 2025) classifies errors and provides type-specific prompting feedback, +26% — prompting only. E2CL (EMNLP 2024 Findings) identifies 8 error types in embodied agents but trains generic SFT. CMFR (REALM 2025) uses multi-stage chain prompting for failure recovery. AP2O (AAAI 2026, arXiv 2510.02393) applies progressive error-type curriculum (error sliding window + adaptive replay) to code generation — frequency-ordered compiler/runtime errors, single discount, single-turn. Curry-DPO (EMNLP 2024 Findings) uses curriculum DPO with easy-to-hard ordering, +7.5% on MT-Bench. POSITIONING: (a) ETO/DMPO/Agent-R/CSO/IPR/SALT/StepPO/T2PO/GiGPO/HCAPO do step-level optimization but ignore error types; (b) AgentDebug/CMFR/E2CL diagnose error types but only use prompting or generic SFT; (c) AP2O uses progressive error-type curriculum but for single-turn code with frequency ordering and single γ; (d) no work adapts temporal discount factors to error-type-specific correction horizons — we fill this intersection with Error-Type-Adaptive Discount Normalization (ETADN), progressive curriculum, and type-consistent rejection sampling.

## Proposed Approach

Phase 1 — Trajectory Collection, Error Diagnosis & Correction-Horizon Measurement (Days 1-5): Generate ~800 trajectories on ALFWorld, ~400 on WebShop, and ~200 on ScienceWorld using Qwen3-8B (leveraging ETO's public codebase; Qwen3-8B validated for agentic fine-tuning by Klear-AgentForce). Apply LLM-based zero-shot error classification following AgentDebug's taxonomy to classify each failure step into 3 categories: (1) action errors (wrong action/object — single-step correction), (2) verification errors (failure to check state, premature termination — state re-checking), (3) planning errors (wrong subgoal decomposition — multi-step replanning). Critically, MEASURE the empirical correction span for each error type: for each classified failure, count the minimum number of steps needed for a valid correction (using expert or best-of-N corrections as reference). This yields empirical correction-horizon distributions per error type, which calibrate ETADN's discount factors. Use AgentErrorBench's annotated trajectories for classifier accuracy validation. Expected: action errors ~1.2 mean span, verification ~3.1, planning ~5.8 (validated against AgentDebug's findings). Phase 2 — Type-Consistent Self-Correction Pair Generation (Days 5-9, writing begins Day 8): For each classified failure, prompt Qwen3-8B to generate correction continuations (10 attempts per failure as default; Qwen3-32B for planning errors where Qwen3-8B success rate is <30%). KEY INNOVATION — Type-consistent rejection sampling: filter corrections to retain only those that actually address the diagnosed error type. For action errors, accept corrections changing ≤2 actions; for verification errors, accept corrections containing explicit state-checking actions; for planning errors, accept corrections demonstrating subgoal re-decomposition. This ensures the training signal is TYPE-FAITHFUL — the model learns type-specific recovery strategies rather than generic workarounds. Target yield: 900-1400 valid DPO pairs (300-500 per benchmark, minimum 100 per error type per benchmark). Contingency: if yield <800, extend to 15 attempts and expand trajectory collection by 50%. Apply DMPO's length normalization base formula; partition pairs into three error-type pools. Begin paper writing in parallel (introduction, related work, method description) during Phase 2 downtime while corrections generate. Phase 3 — Progressive Step-Level DPO with Error-Type-Adaptive Discount Normalization (Days 9-12): CORE METHODOLOGICAL CONTRIBUTION. QLoRA-finetune Qwen3-8B with step-level DPO using three-stage progressive curriculum AND Error-Type-Adaptive Discount Normalization (ETADN). ETADN extends DMPO's length normalization φ(t,T) = γ^t·(1-γ^(T-t))/(1-γ^T) by replacing the single γ with error-type-specific discount factors γ_e calibrated to empirically measured correction horizons from Phase 1: γ_action ≈ 0.85 (short correction horizon, 1-2 steps — credit concentrates on immediate correction), γ_verify ≈ 0.92 (medium horizon, 2-4 steps — credit spans state re-checking sequence), γ_plan ≈ 0.97 (long horizon, 4-8 steps — credit distributes across replanning trajectory). THEORETICAL MOTIVATION: the discount factor controls the temporal span of credit assignment. Action errors need focused, local credit (the one corrected step matters most); planning errors need distributed credit (the entire recovery trajectory contributes). A single γ either under-credits planning corrections (too low) or over-credits action corrections (too high). ETADN resolves this mismatch. Three-stage curriculum: Stage 1 — Action Errors (~2 epochs): Train on action-error DPO pairs with γ_action. Builds foundational single-step correction ability. Stage 2 — Verification Errors (~2 epochs): Train on verification-error pairs with γ_verify + adaptive replay of 20% action-error pairs (retaining γ_action for replayed pairs). Stage 3 — Planning Errors (~2 epochs): Train on planning-error pairs with γ_plan + adaptive replay of 15% action + 15% verification pairs (each retaining their γ_e). Adaptive replay (adapted from AP2O): after each stage, evaluate on held-out set (50 pairs per error type). If performance on earlier types drops >5% relative, increase replay ratio by 10%. Phase 4 — Evaluation + Ablations in Parallel (Days 12-14): CORE EXPERIMENTAL CONTRIBUTION. Three leave-one-out transfer experiments: (a) Train on ALFWorld+WebShop → zero-shot ScienceWorld, (b) Train on ScienceWorld+WebShop → zero-shot ALFWorld, (c) Train on ALFWorld+ScienceWorld → zero-shot WebShop. Transfer hypothesis: if error-type recovery strategies are environment-agnostic skills (planning errors require replanning regardless of domain), progressive error-type training with ETADN should transfer, establishing the contribution as a general inductive bias. Direct evaluation on all three benchmarks (ALFWorld 134 episodes, WebShop 200 tasks, ScienceWorld 200 tasks) against eight baselines: (1) base Qwen3-8B, (2) Reflexion-style retry, (3) AgentDebug-style prompting, (4) ETO trajectory-level DPO, (5) DMPO multi-turn DPO with single γ, (6) GiGPO anchor-state grouping (results from paper or reproduced with public verl-agent codebase), (7) flat step-level DPO (no error types, no curriculum), (8) error-type-conditioned step-level DPO without curriculum or ETADN (single γ, all types simultaneously). Report: task completion rate, recovery success rate per error type, per-stage learning curves, and transfer gap (zero-shot vs. in-domain). Run three ablations in parallel on separate GPUs during evaluation: (a) ETADN vs. single γ — tests correction-horizon discounting hypothesis: does error-type-adaptive γ outperform the best single γ? (b) Progressive vs. flat training — tests curriculum value: does action→verification→planning ordering improve over all types simultaneously? (c) Type-consistent rejection vs. random acceptance — tests pair quality: does ensuring corrections address the diagnosed type improve training? Phase 5 — Writing Finalization (Days 14-15): Complete paper writing. Additional analysis sections: ETADN sensitivity (±0.05 on each γ), classifier noise robustness (10/20/30% label noise injection), per-error-type recovery curves across stages, correction-horizon distribution visualization across benchmarks. Position GiGPO and HCAPO: both achieve strong step-level credit assignment but neither conditions on error types — our ETADN provides an orthogonal signal that could complement their methods.

【综合理由】: 三位审稿人在所有核心维度上高度一致（std=2.2%，远低于 3% 阈值）。Novelty 弱是三人一致的首要限制因素——在 step-level credit assignment 赛道已有 GiGPO、HCAPO、StepPO、AP2O、EGCA 等密集覆盖的背景下，error-type-conditioned discounting 被一致认为是 "obvious next step" 而非 surprising contribution。三人均独立指出 AP2O (AAAI 2026) 的高度重叠性，使得本 proposal 的独立贡献进一步缩窄。Technical Soundness 中等但有可攻击点（classifier 依赖、γ 校准方法论缺失），不足以弥补 novelty 短板。Feasibility 强但这是必要非充分条件。三人中最严格的 R2（中位 17%）与最宽松的 R1（中位 22%）差距仅 5pp，且 R1 的 22% 已处于其区间上端（其下界也仅 17%），说明三人对该 proposal 的客观定位高度趋同。取三人中位数 18% 并微调至 19%，反映 cross-benchmark transfer 评估设计的微弱加分。区间 [15%-23%] 覆盖三人区间的重叠带并略向保守端收窄。该 proposal 落在"形式完善但内核薄弱"锚点（15-20%）的上沿，符合三位审稿人的共同判断。
