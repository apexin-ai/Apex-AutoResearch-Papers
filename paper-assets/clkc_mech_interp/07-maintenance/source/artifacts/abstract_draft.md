# Abstract Draft

> ~200 words | Ready for LaTeX integration

When multilingual large language models (LLMs) encounter conflicting information across languages, the language of presentation influences which answer prevails, yet the internal encoding of this conflict signal has not been studied beyond monolingual settings. We probe the residual stream representations of two architecturally distinct LLMs (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen3-8B) and find that knowledge conflict signals are linearly detectable both within and across languages, with balanced accuracy reaching 75--77\% and all cross-lingual transfer directions exceeding chance. Cross-lingual transfer exhibits a directional asymmetry: probes trained on Chinese representations transfer to English approximately 7 percentage points more accurately than the reverse. This asymmetry, however, is not conflict-specific---probes trained on a non-conflict semantic task (XNLI) show the same pattern at comparable magnitude---indicating that conflict signals inherit the directional biases of the model's general cross-lingual representations. Representation steering reveals a further dissociation: the detectable signal peaks at late layers (~88\% depth), but causal influence on conflict resolution is confined to a mid-layer (~41\% depth; Fisher $p = 0.002$) in Llama, with all late-layer interventions producing no behavioral change. These findings demonstrate that cross-lingual conflict detection operates within shared multilingual representation space, and that linear detectability does not imply causal relevance at the same computational stage.
