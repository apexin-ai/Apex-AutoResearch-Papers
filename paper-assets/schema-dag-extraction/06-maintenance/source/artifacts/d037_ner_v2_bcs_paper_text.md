To verify that the batch-level BCS–accuracy relationship is not an artifact of prompt design, we repeated the NER confusability-controlled experiment (Exp. 2) with a simplified prompt that omits character offsets (v2: text+type only). The positive BCS–F1 correlation persists under the v2 prompt (count_12: r=0.58, p=0.002, 95% bootstrap CI [0.19, 0.81]; count_6: r=0.27, p=0.18), consistent with the v1 result (count_12: r=0.50, p=0.008). As discussed in §4.2, this positive direction reflects a type-frequency confound at the batch level—high-BCS batches happen to contain high-frequency entity types that models extract more reliably—rather than evidence against the confusability hypothesis. The pairwise SCS→confusion analysis (§4.1), which is immune to this aggregation artifact, remains the primary evidence for the confusability channel.
