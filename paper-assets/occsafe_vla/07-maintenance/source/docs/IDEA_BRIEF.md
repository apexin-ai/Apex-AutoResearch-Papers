# Seed 2: Implicit Spatial Occupancy Field-Guided Collision-Free Robot VLA

Objective: CoRL 2026 submission.

Direction: Existing VLAs lack continuous modeling capability for 3D spatial occupancy states and cannot achieve 3D spatial collision awareness while generating language-aligned actions. OccVLA (arXiv 2509.05578, September 2025) integrates 3D occupancy prediction as an implicit supervision signal into VLA, but is entirely oriented toward autonomous driving trajectory planning — the action space, sensor configuration, and task structure are fundamentally different from robot manipulation. VLSA/AEGIS (arXiv 2512.11891, December 2025) achieves VLA collision avoidance through control barrier functions, but as a plug-in safety constraint layer — the safety mechanism is independent of VLA's spatial understanding and does not affect VLA's internal spatial representation learning. Spatial Forcing (ICLR 2026) proves implicit 3D alignment can significantly improve VLA spatial reasoning, but does not produce explicit occupancy field representations for collision reasoning. CSSDF-Net (arXiv 2603.18669, March 2026) demonstrates the effectiveness of neural implicit distance fields in collision-free motion planning, but belongs to traditional motion planning and is completely unrelated to VLA. Core gap: No work integrates implicit occupancy fields as VLA internal spatial representations for collision-free action generation in manipulation tasks. We propose deeply coupling implicit spatial occupancy fields with VLA, reconstructing continuous implicit occupancy fields from RGB-D data, integrating them into VLA action policy networks, enabling language-conditioned safe collision-free manipulation.

## Core Problem

The spatial-VLA and safety-VLA research communities have developed in complete isolation, and their implicit assumptions about each other remain untested. At least fifteen recent methods (DepthVLA, QDepth-VLA, SpatialVLA, Spatial Forcing, GeoAware-VLA, GST-VLA, StemVLA, 3D-CAVLA, CorridorVLA, Evo-0, etc.) improve VLA spatial reasoning via auxiliary 3D supervision or representation alignment — but evaluate only task success rate, never collision safety. Simultaneously, safety-specific methods (AEGIS/VLSA, SafeVLA, CoFreeVLA, Safe-Night VLA) build explicit collision avoidance mechanisms — often using control barrier functions — without leveraging any spatial representation advances. Whether improved spatial understanding actually reduces collisions — and more critically, how to optimally compose spatial representations WITH safety mechanisms — is the fundamental unanswered architectural question for safe VLA design. The community currently selects spatial and safety components independently, with no evidence for how they interact. This matters because the interaction may be non-trivial: spatial representations that encode free-space structure could either (a) make safety filters redundant by steering actions away from collisions preemptively, (b) provide richer scene understanding that IMPROVES safety filter effectiveness, or (c) have no impact on safety because the gradients from spatial auxiliary tasks don't reach action-generation pathways in collision-relevant ways. Meanwhile, the classical robotics community has demonstrated that occupancy-informed barrier functions work — OGM-CBF constructs CBFs from occupancy grid maps for navigation, and Learning to Nudge learns composable barrier functions for manipulation in dense clutter — but these insights have not been transferred to VLA representation spaces, where learned continuous occupancy features could enable richer semantic and contextual collision reasoning than discrete grid-based or object-centric approaches.

## Opportunity / Why Now

The disconnect between spatial and safety research creates an opportunity for both an empirical first and a principled new architecture. The implicit assumption in spatial-VLA work — that better 3D understanding yields safer behavior — has never been validated. Conversely, safety-VLA methods ignore spatial representation advances that could make their filters more effective. We seize this opportunity with a two-pronged contribution: (1) the first systematic factorial study crossing spatial representation type with safety mechanism type, evaluated on both task success AND collision metrics, and (2) OccSafe-VLA, a new architecture that uses the factorial study's findings to compose spatial and safety components in a principled manner — specifically, volumetric occupancy-auxiliary supervision paired with an occupancy-conditioned barrier function (OCC-CBF) that uses learned free-space features rather than Euclidean distance for more expressive collision reasoning. Critically, OCC-CBF is motivated by convergent evidence from classical robotics: OGM-CBF (arXiv 2405.10703) demonstrates that occupancy-derived representations produce more effective barrier functions than raw distance for navigation, and Learning to Nudge (arXiv 2601.02686) shows that learned barrier functions can be composable and scalable for manipulation in dense clutter. OCC-CBF transfers these principles to VLA representation space — using continuous neural occupancy features instead of discrete grids (OGM-CBF) or object-centric functions (Learning to Nudge) — enabling semantic and contextual collision reasoning integrated with the VLA's own learned world model. VLANeXt (arXiv 2602.18532) provides 'recipes for building strong VLA models' along foundational/perception/action dimensions — but its 12 findings address task performance exclusively, with no investigation of 3D spatial representations, safety mechanisms, or collision metrics. Our work fills the orthogonal dimension: recipes for safe VLA design. The VLA safety survey (arXiv 2604.23775, Apr 2026) categorizes defenses by timing but does not investigate the spatial representation dimension. Neither VLA-Risk nor HazardArena benchmarks evaluate spatial representation impact on safety. CorridorVLA (arXiv 2604.21241, Apr 2026) implicitly blurs the spatial-safety boundary by using spatial constraints for action corridors but does not frame this as a safety mechanism — further evidence that the community needs principled understanding of how these dimensions compose.

## Proposed Approach

We present OccSafe-VLA, a VLA architecture that principally composes volumetric occupancy supervision with an occupancy-conditioned safety mechanism, motivated and validated by the first systematic factorial study of spatial-safety interactions in VLAs. The work has three integrated components.

**Component 1: Factorial Study (analysis framework).** We design a controlled factorial experiment isolating the contributions of spatial representation and safety mechanism to collision avoidance. **Factor 1 — Spatial Representation** (applied to OpenVLA-OFT as controlled base): (i) None (vanilla baseline), (ii) General-purpose 3D: unified condition combining depth-auxiliary prediction (DepthVLA-style, Depth Anything V2 pseudo-labels) with implicit 3D embedding alignment (Spatial Forcing-style, frozen 3D foundation model) — we merge these into a single condition because both represent the community's dominant approach of repurposing general-purpose 3D supervision for VLA enhancement, and the key scientific question is whether general-purpose vs. collision-specific spatial representations differ in safety impact, not which general-purpose variant is best. (iii) Volumetric occupancy-auxiliary: auxiliary 3D occupancy prediction head trained from multi-view depth, predicting voxelized free/occupied space — encoding collision-relevant free-space structure that depth-to-surface maps miss. **Factor 2 — Safety Mechanism**: (i) None (spatial representation alone), (ii) External CBF filter (AEGIS-style, faithfully reproduced). This yields a 3×2 = 6 condition matrix. **Factor 3 — Scene Complexity**: SafeLIBERO Level I and II, plus novel dense-clutter extensions with systematically varied safe correction space. Evaluation metrics: task success rate (TSR), collision avoidance rate (CAR), correction budget (volume of CBF-QP feasible set per timestep), execution steps.

**Note on factorial design**: The 3×2 design sharpens the central research question. Merging depth-auxiliary and implicit alignment into 'general-purpose 3D' is principled because both encode surface/geometric information without explicit free-space reasoning — the distinction that matters for safety. If the factorial reveals that general-purpose 3D already provides significant safety gains, this is a strong finding (spatial understanding transfers to safety for free). If only occupancy-auxiliary provides safety gains, this motivates targeted spatial representation design. Either outcome yields actionable design principles. The full 4×2 factorial separating DepthVLA-style from Spatial Forcing-style is reserved for the extended ICRA 2027 version.

**Component 2: OccSafe-VLA (method contribution).** Based on the factorial study's analysis, we propose OccSafe-VLA, which composes spatial representation and safety mechanism in a principled, integrated manner rather than treating them as orthogonal. The key innovation is the Occupancy-Conditioned Barrier Function (OCC-CBF), building on convergent evidence from classical robotics that occupancy-informed barriers outperform Euclidean-distance barriers (OGM-CBF for navigation, Learning to Nudge's DCBF for manipulation): (a) During training, the occupancy-auxiliary head learns dense free-space features z_occ that encode volumetric spatial structure including occluded regions and multi-object relationships. (b) Instead of AEGIS's Euclidean-distance barrier function h(x) = min_v ||x_robot - v||, we define h_occ(x) = f_θ(z_occ, x_robot) — a learned barrier function conditioned on occupancy features. Unlike OGM-CBF's discrete grid + SDF formulation, OCC-CBF operates on continuous learned features, enabling semantic collision reasoning (e.g., distinguishing fragile vs. robust obstacles). Unlike Learning to Nudge's object-centric DCBF, OCC-CBF conditions on volumetric occupancy features that capture continuous spatial structure including occluded regions without requiring explicit object decomposition. (c) f_θ is a lightweight MLP with spectral normalization (ensuring Lipschitz continuity for CBF guarantees) trained using collision labels from the factorial study (same data, no extra annotation cost) to predict collision risk from occupancy features. (d) The OCC-CBF QP correction operates in the occupancy-informed feature space, enabling reasoning about occluded obstacles, approach directions, and complex multi-object configurations that Euclidean-distance barriers miss. (e) Adaptive activation via the correction budget metric: in sparse scenes where the occupancy representation alone sufficiently reduces collisions (detected by high correction budget), the OCC-CBF correction is minimal; in dense scenes where collision risk escalates, OCC-CBF applies stronger corrections. This adaptive behavior avoids the task-performance penalty of always-on safety filtering. OccSafe-VLA is the first architecture to use spatial representation features to INFORM the safety mechanism rather than treating spatial and safety components as independent modules.

**Component 3: Generalization validation.** To address single-architecture generalizability limits, we run a 2×2 sanity check on SmolVLA (0.45B parameters, architecturally distinct from OpenVLA's 7B) with the two extreme conditions: {none, occupancy-auxiliary} × {none, OCC-CBF}. This tests whether the key spatial-safety interaction finding transfers across VLA architectures of different scales and designs. We leverage vla-eval's 47× throughput improvement for efficient evaluation across conditions.

**Mechanistic analysis:** Linear probes for free-space encoding on intermediate representations across all spatial conditions. Attention pattern analysis testing whether spatial auxiliary tasks shift action distributions toward collision-averse trajectories. Gradient flow analysis verifying that occupancy auxiliary gradients reach action-generation pathways.

**Design principles (headline output):** The factorial study + OccSafe-VLA comparison yields actionable principles: (1) quantified spatial-safety interaction effects (e.g., 'occupancy-auxiliary alone provides X% of explicit CBF's collision benefit in sparse scenes but only Y% in dense clutter'), (2) when to invest in spatial representations vs. safety filters vs. their principled composition, (3) whether general-purpose or collision-specific spatial representations are more safety-effective, (4) the correction budget threshold below which explicit safety filtering becomes necessary.

**Scope for AAAI 2027**: OpenVLA-OFT base with SmolVLA sanity check. Three spatial representations × two safety mechanisms + OccSafe-VLA + SmolVLA 2×2. Reserve full 4×2 factorial (separating DepthVLA-style and Spatial Forcing-style conditions), GR00T N1, and GST-VLA Gaussian representation conditions for extended ICRA 2027 version.
