## 1. Introduction

Temporal abstraction---the ability to reason and plan at multiple timescales---is a long-standing aspiration in reinforcement learning \citep{sutton1999options, precup2000temporal}. In world-model RL, where an agent learns a predictive dynamics model and trains its policy entirely within imagined rollouts \citep{hafner2023dreamerv3}, temporal abstraction is especially appealing: by conditioning the world model on a duration token $d_t$, one can in principle generate imagined trajectories at variable temporal granularity, improving planning efficiency for slow dynamics without sacrificing precision for fast ones. Recent work on Time-Aware World Models \citep[TAWM;][]{nhu2025tawm} demonstrates that such $\Delta t$-conditioning improves performance and data efficiency when combined with model-predictive control in TD-MPC2 \citep{hansen2024tdmpc2}. These results suggest that variable-rate world models are a viable path toward temporally abstract model-based agents.

However, the story changes when variable-rate predictions are used not for online planning but for \emph{imagination-based actor-critic training}---the paradigm underlying DreamerV3 \citep{hafner2023dreamerv3} and its predecessors \citep{hafner2020dreamerv1, hafner2021dreamerv2}. In this setting, the actor and critic are trained entirely on synthetic rollouts generated by the world model. The critic's value estimates are never grounded against real environment returns during training; instead, they bootstrap from the model's own predictions. We find that introducing a trained duration embedding ($d_t$-embedding) into this loop creates a pathological feedback cycle: the critic learns $d_t$-conditioned value estimates that exploit the world model's imprecise multi-step predictions, producing systematic overestimation that the actor then exploits, further degrading policy quality. We term this failure mode \emph{Conditioning-Induced Value Overestimation} (CIVO).

In this paper, we present a systematic diagnostic analysis of CIVO in DreamerV3. Through a factorial experiment design that independently varies $d_t$-embedding training, temporal subsampling, imagination horizon, and duration skip on DeepMind Control Suite tasks, we isolate the trained $d_t$-embedding as the necessary condition for pathological value overestimation. We introduce the Normalized Value Excess (NVE)---the ratio of the critic's imagined value to the realized evaluation return, normalized by the baseline's ratio at the same training step---as a diagnostic metric, and show that a threshold of NVE $> 1.5\times$ achieves perfect binary separation between trained and untrained $d_t$-embedding configurations across all tested seeds. Gradient decomposition reveals the mechanistic pathway: by the time of collapse, 93\% of the gradient flowing through $d_t$-embedding originates from observation decoders, with reward contributing 0\%. This motivates Full Selective Gradient Stopping (Full-SGS)---detaching the observation decoder gradient from $d_t$-embedding---which eliminates CIVO as a proof-of-concept intervention, reducing NVE from $4.12\times$ to $1.34\times$ and recovering $3.4\times$ higher evaluation return than the matched CIVO condition.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Phenomenon identification.} We establish that all variable-rate imagination configurations in DreamerV3 degrade performance relative to single-step baselines, with severity monotonically increasing in $d_t$ (Section~4.1). This failure is absent from MPC-based approaches \citep{nhu2025tawm}, revealing a paradigm-dependent asymmetry in the viability of temporal conditioning.

    \item \textbf{Causal diagnosis.} Through controlled ablations across two environments (Walker Walk, Cheetah Run) and multiple seeds, we identify the trained $d_t$-embedding as the necessary condition for CIVO. Removing it eliminates pathological overestimation; freezing it prevents the feedback loop. The NVE $> 1.5\times$ threshold provides a reliable diagnostic marker with perfect separating power across conditions (Section~4.2--4.4).

    \item \textbf{Mechanistic explanation.} Gradient decomposition traces CIVO to observation decoder gradient dominance over $d_t$-embedding: a positive feedback loop where reconstruction loss reshapes temporal conditioning, which corrupts critic estimates, which degrades the actor, which increases reconstruction loss. This reveals the causal pathway and explains why reward-only gradient interventions are ineffective (Section~4.5).

    \item \textbf{Proof-of-concept intervention.} Full-SGS, which blocks the identified gradient pathway, eliminates CIVO in matched experiments. We present this as mechanistic validation rather than a mature solution, and characterize residual failure modes (Pathway B instability) that trained $d_t$-embeddings introduce through channels beyond value overestimation (Section~4.6--4.7).
\end{enumerate}

Our findings carry a practical implication for the field: before building variable-$d_t$ world-model agents that train via imagination, the CIVO failure mode must be addressed. The specific vulnerability we identify---that temporal conditioning amplifies rather than compensates for model imprecision in imagination-based training---suggests that architectural solutions (e.g., hierarchical temporal abstractions \citep{gumbsch2024thick, mattes2024hieros} that avoid flat $d_t$-conditioning) may be more promising than parametric fixes.
