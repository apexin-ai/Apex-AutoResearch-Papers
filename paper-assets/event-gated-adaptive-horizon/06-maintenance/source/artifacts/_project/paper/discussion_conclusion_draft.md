## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Mechanism Interpretation

The failure mode we diagnose—Conditioning-Induced Value Overestimation (CIVO)—operates through a gradient-level positive feedback loop that is qualitatively distinct from previously characterized forms of model exploitation. The mechanism proceeds as follows: the observation decoder, which constitutes the largest loss component in RSSM training, dominates gradient updates to the shared duration embedding $\mathbf{e}_{dt}$. As $\mathbf{e}_{dt}$ specializes to minimize observation reconstruction at $dt>1$ steps, it encodes temporal information that the critic treats as precise conditioning—despite the RSSM's degraded prediction fidelity at multi-step horizons. The critic then overestimates imagined values for longer-skip trajectories, the actor exploits these overestimates, and the resulting policy distribution shift further increases observation decoder loss, amplifying the gradient signal.

Our gradient decomposition traces this loop quantitatively: observation decoder gradients grow from 26\% to 93\% of total gradient norm flowing through $\mathbf{e}_{dt}$ between step 55K (pre-collapse) and 105K (post-collapse), while reward head contribution drops to 0\%. This temporal evolution reveals a critical asymmetry: by the time collapse occurs, the reward head has lost all influence on $\mathbf{e}_{dt}$ training, meaning the embedding is shaped entirely by pixel-reconstruction pressure rather than task-relevant dynamics. The reward-only SGS experiment (exp\_016e) confirms this interpretation causally—blocking reward gradients from $\mathbf{e}_{dt}$ is a no-op (NVE worsens to $10.42\times$), because the reward pathway carries no signal at the critical phase.

This feedback loop constitutes a novel form of model exploitation. Unlike ensemble-based overestimation \citep{fujimoto2018td3, lee2021sunrise}, which arises from the max operator over noisy value estimates, CIVO arises from \emph{structured} misinformation: the temporal embedding carries a coherent but misleading signal that systematically biases value predictions upward for multi-step transitions. Pessimistic value estimation cannot address this because the overestimation is not a stochastic artifact—it is a deterministic consequence of the embedding's training objective being misaligned with the fidelity of the underlying dynamics model.

### 5.2 Relationship to Prior Work

\paragraph{Delusional bias.} \citet{lu2018nondelusional} identify delusional bias as value estimates predicated on unrealizable policies. CIVO shares the structural property of internally consistent but externally unachievable value estimates, but differs in origin: Lu et al.'s delusion arises from policy-class constraints in value iteration, while CIVO arises from the world model providing precise temporal conditioning atop imprecise predictions. The critic is not "deluded" about the policy—it is deluded about the fidelity of the world model at $dt>1$.

\paragraph{Update-to-data ratio effects.} \citet{hussing2024dissecting} show that high update-to-data ratios drive value divergence through optimizer momentum. Our CIVO mechanism is orthogonal: all experiments use DreamerV3's default update schedule, and the overestimation is driven by the content of gradient updates (observation decoder dominance), not their frequency. The two mechanisms could compound in practice, but they require different interventions.

\paragraph{TAWM and the planning-imagination asymmetry.} TAWM \citep{nhu2025tawm} demonstrates that $\Delta t$ conditioning improves performance in TD-MPC2, a planning-based architecture. The critical difference is that TD-MPC2 uses online model-predictive control at test time: the model generates short-horizon predictions that are evaluated against realized outcomes within a planning loop, providing an implicit correction for prediction errors. In imagination-based actor-critic training, no such correction exists—the actor-critic trains entirely on imagined rollouts, and any systematic bias in multi-step predictions propagates unchecked into the value function. This paradigm-dependent asymmetry explains why the same architectural choice (temporal conditioning) is beneficial in one setting and pathological in another.

### 5.3 Limitations

We identify five specific limitations of this work:

\paragraph{Sample size.} The trained $dt$-embedding condition has $n=4$ runs across two environments; the no-$dt$-embedding control has $n=2$. The Full-SGS intervention (exp\_018v2) is $n=1$. While the effect size is large (Cohen's $d = 1.02$, CI excludes zero), the stochastic component of collapse (2/3 seeds collapse in Walker, 1/1 in Cheetah) cannot be precisely characterized with these sample sizes. We cannot estimate whether the true collapse probability is 0.67 or 0.85 without additional seeds.

\paragraph{Environment diversity.} Primary experiments use Walker Walk ($n=7$ configurations) with one Cheetah Run replication ($n=1$). While the Cheetah result confirms environment-generality of the mechanism, we have not tested environments with discontinuous dynamics (contact-rich manipulation), high-dimensional observations (visual RL), or significantly different reward structures. The ManiSkill3 extension remains pending.

\paragraph{Full-SGS intervention cost.} Full Selective Gradient Stopping eliminates 79\% of excess NVE but also prevents $\mathbf{e}_{dt}$ from receiving any useful information from observation reconstruction. The resulting agent achieves 449@105K versus vanilla DreamerV3's 916@105K—a 51\% gap that reflects the inability of the embedding to learn temporal relationships from the dominant gradient source. This is a proof-of-concept demonstrating causal sufficiency, not a practical solution.

\paragraph{NVE diagnostic threshold.} The $1.5\times$ NVE threshold achieves 87.5\% classification accuracy (7/8), with one false positive: exp\_008e\_v3\_s2 exhibits NVE $3.78\times$ through Pathway B (entropy-divergence driven), not CIVO. The threshold cannot distinguish between the two collapse pathways without supplementary diagnostics (entropy trend, value variance). A composite diagnostic incorporating these signals would improve specificity.

\paragraph{Pathway B characterization.} The secondary instability mode (Pathway B) is observed in one seed under $d_t=1$ imagination. Its mechanism—value variance explosion ($7.4 \rightarrow 18.3$) with entropy increase—is qualitatively distinct from CIVO but insufficiently characterized. We cannot determine whether Pathway B requires trained $dt$-embedding or merely correlates with it beyond the single same-seed control (exp\_020).

### 5.4 Broader Implications

\paragraph{Warning for temporal abstraction in MBRL.} Our results demonstrate that temporal conditioning cannot be naively incorporated into imagination-based training pipelines. The dominant gradient source (observation reconstruction) will reshape any shared temporal embedding toward pixel prediction, regardless of the embedding's intended role in policy optimization. This finding applies to any architecture where (a)~temporal information is encoded in a shared representation, (b)~the world model trains on reconstruction losses, and (c)~the policy trains on imagined rollouts. This combination describes DreamerV3, DreamerV4, and any future RSSM- or transformer-based world-model agent that introduces variable-rate imagination.

\paragraph{Gradient pathway analysis as diagnostic tool.} The per-loss gradient decomposition methodology we employ provides a general-purpose diagnostic for identifying which training objective dominates the shaping of shared representations. This approach can be applied to any multi-loss architecture (multi-task learning, auxiliary losses in RL) to detect gradient dominance pathologies before they manifest as performance degradation.

\paragraph{Design recommendations.} Based on our findings, we offer three concrete recommendations for variable-$dt$ world-model agent design: (1)~if temporal embeddings are trained, they should receive gradients only from objectives whose fidelity is sufficient to support the conditioning (e.g., reward prediction at validated accuracy levels); (2)~imagination-based policy training should either operate at $dt=1$ (verified safe in 2/3 seeds) or use explicit value regularization against multi-step prediction uncertainty; (3)~gradient pathway monitoring should be a standard diagnostic during development of multi-loss world-model architectures.

### 5.5 Future Work

\paragraph{Selective gradient attenuation.} Rather than fully blocking observation decoder gradients from $\mathbf{e}_{dt}$ (Full-SGS), a decay coefficient $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ could allow partial information flow: $\nabla_{\text{obs}} \cdot \alpha$ where $\alpha$ anneals as RSSM fidelity degrades at $dt>1$. This requires an online fidelity estimator—possibly the ratio of realized to predicted observation error at each $dt$ value. Experiment: sweep $\alpha \in \{0.01, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5\}$ under the exp\_008d configuration, measuring whether any $\alpha > 0$ preserves useful temporal information without triggering CIVO.

\paragraph{Architectural separation.} A dual-encoder architecture with separate embeddings for $dt$ information used in observation decoding versus policy conditioning would structurally prevent the feedback loop. The observation encoder's $dt$ representation can specialize for reconstruction fidelity, while the policy encoder's $dt$ representation trains exclusively from reward and value gradients. Experiment: replace single $\mathbf{e}_{dt}$ with $\mathbf{e}_{dt}^{\text{obs}}$ and $\mathbf{e}_{dt}^{\text{policy}}$; measure whether CIVO is eliminated without the performance cost of Full-SGS.

\paragraph{Continuous temporal conditioning.} Our experiments use discrete $dt \in \{1, 2, 4\}$. Continuous $\Delta t$ conditioning (as in TAWM) may produce different gradient dynamics—the discrete embedding table concentrates gradients on a small number of parameters, potentially amplifying the dominance effect. Experiment: replace discrete embedding with continuous sinusoidal encoding; test whether gradient dominance still emerges.

\paragraph{Transformer world models.} DreamerV4's transformer architecture changes the gradient flow structure: attention-based prediction may distribute temporal information differently than RSSM's recurrence. Whether CIVO transfers to transformer-based imagination-trained agents is an open question with immediate practical relevance.

\paragraph{Complete Pathway B characterization.} The secondary instability mode requires systematic investigation: (a)~more seeds under $dt=1$ imagination to establish base rate; (b)~gradient decomposition at the onset of Pathway B collapse; (c)~testing whether Full-SGS prevents Pathway B (current evidence: unclear, as 018v2 uses $dt=2$ imagination). If Pathway B is independent of observation decoder gradients, additional interventions targeting value variance (e.g., critic ensembles, gradient clipping) would be needed.

## 6. Conclusion

We identify and diagnose Conditioning-Induced Value Overestimation (CIVO), a failure mode that emerges when trained temporal embeddings interact with imagination-based actor-critic training in world-model RL. Through systematic ablation across 20 experiments, we establish that trained duration embeddings are a necessary condition for catastrophic value overestimation (NVE $5.61\times$ mean, $n=4$; effect $\Delta = +4.71$ versus no-embedding controls), and trace the causal mechanism to observation decoder gradient dominance that reshapes the embedding toward reconstruction fidelity rather than task-relevant temporal representation.

Our contributions are: (1)~identification and characterization of CIVO as a previously undocumented failure mode specific to the intersection of temporal conditioning and imagination-based training; (2)~a gradient pathway decomposition revealing that observation reconstruction—not reward prediction—drives the pathological feedback loop (93\% gradient share at collapse); (3)~demonstration that Full Selective Gradient Stopping eliminates the failure mode ($n=1$, proof-of-concept), confirming causal sufficiency of the identified pathway; and (4)~a cross-environment NVE diagnostic that classifies collapse with 87.5\% accuracy using a simple threshold.

The central lesson for the field: temporal abstraction in world-model RL requires architectural safeguards against gradient-pathway coupling between reconstruction objectives and policy-facing representations. Adding $dt$ conditioning to imagination-based agents without such safeguards will, based on our evidence, produce catastrophic overestimation in the majority of training runs.
