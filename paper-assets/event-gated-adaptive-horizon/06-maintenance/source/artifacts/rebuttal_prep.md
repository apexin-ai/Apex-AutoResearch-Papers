# Anticipated Reviewer Q&A — ICML 2026

## Q1: Frozen dt_emb prevention rate is only 71% (5/7) — is this reliable enough as a diagnostic?

**Anticipated question**: "Your frozen dt_emb only prevents NVE>1.5× in 5 of 7 seeds (71%). How can you claim it's a reliable diagnostic tool when it fails 29% of the time?"

**Response**: The 71% figure counts *any* NVE>1.5× exceedance, including transient spikes that self-correct. The operationally relevant metric is *sustained* CIVO: 0/7 frozen seeds exhibit sustained CIVO (defined as NVE≥1.5× persisting over a ≥35K-step window). The 2/7 transient exceedances self-corrected within training — one Walker seed peaked at NVE 1.71 before returning to 0.56, another at 1.88→0.177. By contrast, all trained dt_emb seeds in the original validation exceeded NVE>1.5× (2/2 Walker trained seeds: 008d NVE 2.09×, 009b NVE 1.62×), and additional seeds (s3/s4/s5) confirm CIVO triggering across seeds. This yields sensitivity=1.0 and specificity=1.0 for the sustained-CIVO criterion in the original 4-experiment validation (2 trained + 2 removed). The transient exceedances actually *strengthen* the diagnostic: they demonstrate the system's self-corrective capacity when the trained-dt_emb amplification loop is absent.

**Evidence**: Frozen locomotion — Walker s0 NVE 0.19, s1 NVE 1.71→0.56 (transient), s2 NVE 1.88→0.177 (transient); Cheetah s0 NVE 1.35, s1 NVE 0.78 (no CIVO). Trained locomotion — 8/8 Walker runs (6 unique seeds) all trigger CIVO onset (NVE 1.53–6.57×), including independent same-seed reproduction (exp_068 s1 NVE 6.57×); Cheetah 2/2 onset (exp_010b s1 NVE 8.27×, exp_078 s0 NVE 4.10×). Total: 10/10 locomotion onset. Fisher exact test p<0.0001 for trained (10/10) vs control (0/8 frozen+removed).

## Q2: Manipulation 0/5 CIVO — how do you know dt_emb-dependent CIVO doesn't exist there?

**Anticipated question**: "You found 0/5 dt_emb-dependent CIVO in manipulation tasks. How can you distinguish 'CIVO doesn't exist here' from 'your protocol failed to detect it'? PegInsertion showed ve_ratio bursts — isn't that CIVO?"

**Response**: The PegInsertion ve_ratio bursts are environment-driven transients, not dt_emb-dependent CIVO. Our evidence is a paired seed ordering reversal: frozen s1 peaked at 11.32 while trained s1 peaked at only 3.08 — the opposite of what dt_emb-dependent CIVO predicts. In s0 the ordering was as expected (trained 10.05 > frozen 1.82), but the reversal in s1 falsifies dt_emb causality (D146). Additionally, PickCube showed NVE self-correction to 0.2 regardless of dt_emb configuration. We interpret this as a genuine domain boundary: manipulation tasks have shorter effective horizons and dense reward shaping, reducing the temporal-gap prediction errors that drive CIVO in locomotion. We report this as a scope limitation, not a negative result.

**Evidence**: D146 ordering reversal — exp_055 trained s1 peak 3.08, exp_056 frozen s1 peak 11.32 (frozen > trained). exp_050 trained s0 peak 10.05, exp_053 frozen s0 回落到 1.82. PickCube: NVE self-corrects to 0.2 in all configs (trained/frozen/dual-critic). 0/5 manipulation seeds show trained > frozen ordering consistently.

## Q3: All results are from DreamerV3 — how does this generalize to other MBRL algorithms?

**Anticipated question**: "Your findings are limited to a single algorithm (DreamerV3) with a specific architecture (RSSM). Why should the community care if CIVO doesn't generalize beyond this one system?"

**Response**: We deliberately scope claims to DreamerV3 and make the scope explicit. However, the *mechanism* — temporal conditioning amplifying world-model prediction errors into value overestimation via imagination-based critic bootstrapping — applies to any MBRL system that (a) uses learned temporal conditioning, (b) trains critics on imagined trajectories, and (c) has latent dynamics with temporal-gap fidelity degradation. The strongest generalization evidence is the MPC/imagination boundary: TAWM (Nhu et al., ICML 2025) successfully uses dt-conditioning in TD-MPC2 (MPC setting) because MPC re-plans from real states each step, breaking the bootstrapping feedback loop. This architectural contrast localizes CIVO to imagination-based actor-critic — not just DreamerV3, but the entire class. The gradient-causality dissociation finding (gradient magnitude ≠ causal importance) is architecture-agnostic and relevant to any multi-head MBRL system.

**Evidence**: TAWM succeeds with dt-conditioning in MPC (re-planning from real states), fails in imagination (critic bootstrapping). Gradient decomposition: reward head 38–49% gradient share but causally irrelevant; obs decoder 16–22% but causally critical — this dissociation is a general methodological insight for multi-loss MBRL debugging.

**Anticipated question**: "Walker and Cheetah are both simple locomotion tasks from DeepMind Control Suite. Two environments is far too few to make general claims about CIVO."

Additionally, 3 ManiSkill manipulation tasks (PickCube, PegInsertion, StackCube) establish the domain boundary where CIVO does not trigger. Total: 5 environments, 158 experiments, 80 successful.

Severity continuum confirmed across dual-critic (Walker 2/2 prevented vs Cheetah partial), TCN (Walker 2/4 vs Cheetah 0/3), and frozen (both 100%). ## Q5: Why not compare interventions head-to-head (e.g., TCN vs frozen vs dual-critic)?

**Anticipated question**: "You test seven interventions but never compare them against each other on the same seeds. Without controlled head-to-head comparison, how can you rank the Intervention Hierarchy levels?"

**Response**: The Intervention Hierarchy is ranked by success rate across all tested seeds, not by paired comparison — this is deliberate. The success rates are so disparate that head-to-head pairing adds no discriminative power: gradient-level SGS 1/11 (9%), output-level RAVE 0/2 (0%), representation-level TCN 4/11 (36%), architecture-level dual-critic 3/8 (38%), vs embedding removal 5/5 (100%). The hierarchy's contribution is taxonomic: it organizes methods into four mechanistic levels and demonstrates that each level fails for a *distinct* reason — gradient stopping fails because forward-pass representational damage is irreducible, RAVE fails because the EMA anchor is polluted by early overestimation, TCN fails because effective dose is environment-dependent with no universal Goldilocks zone, and architecture-level interventions (dual-critic) partially succeed in moderate-CIVO environments but fail in severe ones. A head-to-head comparison on matched seeds would confirm what aggregate rates already show, at the cost of doubling GPU budget with no new mechanistic insight.

**Evidence**: Intervention Hierarchy table — L1 Gradient: Full SGS 0/3, Obs-decoder SGS 1/6, Reward SGS 0/2 (1/11). L2 Output: RAVE 0/2. L3 Representation: TCN 4/11 (σ=0.1 1/1 borderline NVE=1.02×, σ=0.2 2/6 Walker only 45–91% eval retention, σ=0.5 0/3, σ=1.0 1/1 pyrrhic). ## Q6: NVE 1.5× threshold — is this arbitrary? How sensitive are claims to this cutoff?

**Anticipated question**: "Your entire diagnostic framework relies on the 1.5× NVE threshold. How was it chosen, and do your claims hold under different thresholds?"

**Response**: The 1.5× threshold was not hand-tuned — it emerges from ROC analysis. Across 56 labeled data points (12 configuration groups), Youden-optimal threshold is 1.53×, independently converging on our pre-specified 1.5×. Leave-One-Config-Out (LOCO-CV) cross-validation across K=12 configuration groups (n=56) yields mean AUROC 0.988±0.003, mean optimal threshold θ=1.61±0.23, and cross-validated accuracy 53/56=94.6% — identical to in-sample accuracy, confirming zero threshold overfitting. In 10/12 folds the training-optimal threshold falls within ±0.05 of 1.5×. Zero false negatives: all 24 CIVO-positive experiments are correctly detected across all folds. Claims are stable across a range: at 1.3× the only change is 2 additional borderline transients reclassified (no sustained CIVO misses); at 2.0× we lose early detection of moderate Walker CIVO but severe cases remain captured. The threshold's utility is as a *diagnostic alarm*, not a mechanistic boundary — sustained NVE>1.5× across consecutive checkpoints indicates dt_emb-driven CIVO with zero false positives in our dataset. We acknowledge in-sample calibration and validation as a limitation and recommend held-out validation on other architectures.

**Evidence**: In-sample AUROC 0.990 (n=56, F1=0.941). **LOCO-CV (K=12 configs, n=56)**: mean train AUROC 0.988±0.003, mean optimal θ=1.61±0.23, cross-validated accuracy 53/56=94.6% (identical to in-sample), zero false negatives (24/24 CIVO detected). 10/12 folds select θ≈1.5±0.05; the 3 persistent FPs (exp_008e_v3 mitigation transient, exp_047/057 frozen transients) are mechanistically non-CIVO and self-correct. Youden-optimal 1.53× vs pre-specified 1.5×. Trained dt_emb: 8/8 Walker runs + 2/2 Cheetah = 10/10 onset. Frozen: 0/7 sustained CIVO (2/7 transient, self-corrected). Removed: 0/9 exceed 1.5×.

## Q7: Sample sizes are small (n=2–4 per condition) — are conclusions statistically reliable?

**Anticipated question**: "Most conditions have only 2–4 seeds. With such small samples, your results could be driven by lucky/unlucky initializations. Why not run 10+ seeds?"

**Response**: We report 158 experiments across 80 successful conditions — the aggregate evidence base is substantial even if individual conditions have few seeds. Key claims rely on *perfect separation*, not effect sizes: trained dt_emb triggers CIVO in 10/10 locomotion runs (8/8 Walker across 6 unique seeds including same-seed reproduction, 2/2 Cheetah), frozen/removed prevents in 0/8 control runs — Fisher exact test p<0.0001. For the intervention hierarchy, the signal is overwhelming: removal succeeds 5/5 while all other interventions combined succeed 8/32. Where seed variance matters (e.g., TCN Walker σ=0.2: 2/4 seeds succeed), we report it transparently as evidence of unreliability, not as a positive result. We follow Agarwal et al. (2021) in reporting IQM with stratified bootstrap CIs rather than claiming significance from small n. The compute budget (~40 GPU-days total) was allocated to maximize mechanistic coverage (7 methods × 3 levels × 5 environments) over per-condition replication.

**Evidence**: Aggregate: 158 experiments, 80 success, 39 negative. Per-condition: trained CIVO 10/10 locomotion runs (Walker 8/8 + Cheetah 2/2), frozen 0/7 CIVO, removal 0/9 CIVO. Fisher p<0.0001 for trained (10/10) vs control (0/8 frozen+removed). TCN seed variance (45–91% eval retention at σ=0.2 Walker) reported as instability evidence. Dual-critic 2×2 factorial fully populated.

## Q8: CIVO onset is universal but outcomes diverge — does this weaken the CIVO threat narrative?

**Anticipated question**: "You show 10/10 locomotion runs trigger CIVO onset (NVE>1.5×), but only some lead to collapse while others self-correct. If CIVO self-corrects in some seeds, is it really a problem worth diagnosing?"

**Response**: The onset/outcome divergence actually *strengthens* the contribution. CIVO onset (NVE exceeding the 1.5× threshold under step-matched baseline normalization) is deterministic: 10/10 locomotion runs trigger it (8/8 Walker across 6 unique seeds, 2/2 Cheetah), while 0/8 control runs (frozen+removed) do, establishing dt_emb training as the causal factor (Fisher exact p<0.0001). The downstream *outcome* — whether onset progresses to irreversible collapse or self-corrects — is stochastic and seed-dependent, but overwhelmingly destructive: the majority of locomotion runs suffer irreversible policy collapse despite NVE sometimes self-correcting. Seed 5 exemplifies this dissociation: its NVE drops from 4.72× to 1.22× (critic error self-corrects), yet eval remains at 95.8 (10.5% of baseline) — the policy damage inflicted during the CIVO episode is permanent. This is the expected behavior of a feedback loop operating near a bifurcation point: the critic anomaly may resolve, but the policy has already been driven into a degenerate attractor from which it cannot escape. The practical implication is that CIVO onset is a *necessary precursor* to collapse and almost always sufficient — practitioners cannot rely on NVE self-correction to save the run. The frozen dt_emb diagnostic eliminates onset entirely (0/5 seeds), removing both collapse risk and the false hope of self-correction. This makes the diagnostic more valuable, not less: it prevents a near-deterministic catastrophic failure mode.

**Evidence**: Walker trained runs — 8/8 onset (6 unique seeds, NVE 1.53–6.57×), outcomes: s1 collapse (eval 83@105K; independent repro exp_068 NVE 6.57×), s2 self-correct (eval 268@105K — unique Walker recovery), s3 late-onset collapse then slow recovery over 300K (exp_045 eval 868), s4 late-spike collapse (NVE 4.44@105K endpoint), s5 collapse† (NVE 4.72→1.22×, eval 95.8 = 10.5% baseline — NVE self-corrected but policy did not recover). Cheetah: 2/2 onset (exp_010b s1 NVE 8.27×, exp_078 s0 NVE 4.10× — onset_self_correcting, eval 26.1→119.5). Total locomotion: 10/10 onset. Frozen: 0/7 onset (0/2 sustained). Control (removed): 0/9 onset.

## Q9: Why only test on DreamerV3? Do other MBRL algorithms also exhibit CIVO?

**Anticipated question**: "You only test CIVO in DreamerV3 with the RSSM architecture. This is a single algorithm — how do we know this isn't an artifact of DreamerV3's specific implementation rather than a general MBRL phenomenon? Have you tested TD-MPC2, IRIS, or other imagination-based methods?"

**Response**: We deliberately position this work as the first systematic identification and analysis of CIVO *within DreamerV3*, not as a universal MBRL claim. However, the mechanism we identify — temporal conditioning amplifying world-model prediction errors into value overestimation via imagination-based critic bootstrapping — is not DreamerV3-specific. It requires three architectural conditions: (a) learned temporal conditioning on dynamics, (b) imagination-based critic training (value bootstrap over model-generated trajectories), and (c) temporal-gap fidelity degradation in the latent dynamics model. Any MBRL system satisfying all three is theoretically susceptible. The strongest generalization evidence comes from the MPC/imagination boundary: TAWM (Nhu et al., ICML 2025) successfully uses dt-conditioning in TD-MPC2's MPC setting because MPC re-plans from real states at each step, breaking the bootstrapping feedback loop that accumulates errors. This architectural contrast localizes CIVO to imagination-based actor-critic as a class — not DreamerV3 alone. Testing TD-MPC2 in imagination mode, IRIS, and TWM is explicit future work; our contribution is establishing the causal mechanism and diagnostic framework that enables such testing.

**Evidence**: CIVO mechanism requires (a) + (b) + (c) jointly (§Discussion). TAWM succeeds with dt-conditioning in MPC (re-planning from real states) but would face CIVO risk in imagination mode — this is the MPC-immunity prediction (Appendix §MPC Immune). The RSSM develops a 2.78× reward prediction gap under trained conditioning (Table: RSSM fidelity). Gradient-causality dissociation (gradient magnitude ≠ causal importance) is architecture-agnostic — applicable to any multi-head MBRL system. The three-condition framework provides a concrete testable prediction for other architectures.

## Q11: How does CIVO relate to overestimation bias in model-free methods (TD3/SAC)?

**Anticipated question**: "Value overestimation is a well-studied problem in model-free RL — TD3 and SAC already address it with clipped double Q-learning. How is CIVO different from standard overestimation bias? Why don't existing model-free solutions (twin critics with min operation) work?"

**Response**: CIVO is mechanistically distinct from model-free overestimation bias. In TD3/SAC, overestimation arises from function approximation error in the critic — the max operator over noisy Q-estimates introduces a positive bias. The twin-critic min operation effectively counters this because the two critics have independent approximation errors. In CIVO, overestimation arises from *world-model prediction errors compounding over imagined trajectories*, amplified by temporal conditioning. The causal chain is: trained dt_emb → biased temporal conditioning on world model → compounding prediction error over imagination horizon → systematically inflated value targets → actor exploitation of inflated values → further world-model distortion (feedback loop). The critical difference: in CIVO, both critics are trained on the *same* RSSM-generated imagination trajectories, so they share the same systematic bias. Empirically, dual critics in DreamerV3 converge to ρ > 0.999 correlation across all 7 tested configurations, with pessimism at only 3–4% of the independent-critic bound (§Architecture-Level Ablation). The min operation is thus ineffective against CIVO because it addresses *independent* estimation noise, not *shared* systematic bias from a corrupted generative model. This is precisely why CIVO requires model-level or embedding-level intervention rather than critic-level fixes.

**Evidence**: Dual-critic convergence — ρ > 0.999 across n=7 configurations, pessimism 3–4% of independent-critic bound (§Architecture-Level Ablation). Wide dual-critic provides regularization through uncharacterized mechanisms (3/4 Walker prevention) but fails on Cheetah (0/1). RAVE (value-level capping, analogous to model-free solutions) also fails: 0/2 seeds, EMA anchor tracks biased values during CIVO onset. RSSM reward prediction gap: 2.78× under trained conditioning — this is the upstream bias that twin critics cannot correct. Embedding removal (5/5 prevention) confirms the fix must target the bias source, not the critic.

## Q12: Why doesn't CIVO appear in ManiSkill manipulation tasks?

**Anticipated question**: "You claim CIVO is absent in manipulation (0/5 seeds), but you don't convincingly explain why. Is it reward structure? Observation modality? Dynamics? Without understanding the boundary conditions, the result is merely an observation, not a mechanistic finding."

**Response**: We acknowledge that identifying the precise mediator of the locomotion/manipulation boundary is an open question, and we state this explicitly in §Discussion. What we *can* rule out is reward sparsity: all five environments (DMC and ManiSkill) use dense shaped rewards, so reward density cannot explain the boundary. We identify three candidate mediators that differ systematically between the domains. First, action-space periodicity: locomotion requires sustained periodic coordination (gait cycles), where temporal conditioning errors accumulate coherently; manipulation involves episodic, contact-gated dynamics where the relevant horizon resets at each contact transition. Second, observation structure: DMC locomotion uses low-dimensional proprioceptive states where the RSSM can develop spurious temporal shortcuts; ManiSkill uses richer state representations including object poses. Third, continuous vs. contact-gated dynamics: locomotion dynamics evolve continuously (smooth state transitions amenable to temporal extrapolation), while manipulation involves discrete contact mode switches that limit temporal prediction utility. The value of the negative result is diagnostic: the frozen-embedding diagnostic correctly identifies ManiSkill value spikes as non-CIVO (environment-driven transients), validating NVE as a domain-aware diagnostic that does not produce false positives in manipulation tasks.

**Evidence**: All 5 environments use dense shaped rewards — reward sparsity ruled out (§Discussion). PegInsertion D146 ordering reversal falsifies dt_emb causality: frozen s1 peak 11.32 > trained s1 peak 3.08. PickCube NVE self-corrects to 0.2 regardless of dt_emb config (trained/frozen/dual-critic). StackCube frozen control spike 4.30× ≈ trained spike 5.05× — no trained > frozen ordering. Candidate mediators identified: action-space periodicity, observation structure, continuous vs. contact-gated dynamics (§Discussion). 0/5 manipulation false positives validates NVE diagnostic specificity across domains.

## Q13: All SGS variants fail — what does this tell us about the mechanism?

**Anticipated question**: "You test three SGS variants and achieve only 1/11 CIVO prevention (9% success rate). If gradient stopping doesn't work at any level, does this mean dt_emb isn't actually the cause? Or is your intervention methodology flawed?"

**Response**: The SGS failure is itself a mechanistic finding, not a methodological shortcoming. Gradient stopping addresses *backward-pass* gradient flow to the dt_emb, but CIVO operates through *forward-pass* representational conditioning. In the forward pass, the trained dt_emb conditions every RSSM transition step — this conditioning biases latent state representations regardless of whether gradients flow back through the embedding. SGS confirms that the vulnerability is irreducible at the gradient level: even blocking all non-KL gradients (Full-pathway SGS, 0/3 seeds) cannot prevent the representational damage that a trained, exposed dt_emb inflicts on world-model predictions. The gradient decomposition analysis reveals a deeper insight: gradient magnitude does not predict causal importance. The reward head contributes 38–49% of the total gradient to dt_emb but is causally irrelevant — blocking it alone (Reward-only SGS, 0/2) and including it (Full-SGS) yield the same failure. The observation decoder contributes only 16–22% but carries the critical signal: obs-decoder-only SGS achieves the sole 1/6 success. This dissociation between gradient share and causal importance is a general methodological contribution applicable to any multi-head architecture — it warns against using gradient magnitude as a proxy for causal influence in multi-loss systems.

**Evidence**: SGS results — Reward-only SGS 0/2, Full-pathway SGS 0/3, Obs-decoder SGS 1/6 → total 1/11 (9%) (Table: Intervention Hierarchy). Gradient decomposition at exp_008d_55K: reward 49.0%, KL 34.7%, observation 16.3%, continuation 0.0%. At exp_009e_112K: reward 38.1%, KL 40.4%, observation 21.5%. Reward-detach condition (exp_044): CIVO onset not prevented (peak NVE 4.45×), delayed self-correction (eval 44→538 over 224K, 59% baseline) — lower asymptote than spontaneous recovery (exp_045: 868, 95%). Full-SGS exp_018: s1 partial mitigation (eval 448, NVE 1.66×), s2 collapse. The forward-pass conditioning mechanism explains why gradient-level intervention is fundamentally insufficient.
