144	\subsection{Online CIVO Detection}
145	\label{sec:detector}
146	
147	\paragraph{Motivation.}
148	The Normalized Value Excess ($\nve$) quantifies CIVO severity by comparing the value-to-evaluation ratio against a reference baseline that does not employ temporal abstraction. This comparison is informative for post-hoc diagnosis but unusable during deployment: no practitioner has access to a converged baseline run while training a new agent. A metric that requires a completed control experiment cannot serve as an early warning.
149	
150	We therefore ask whether the \emph{dynamics} of value overestimation, rather than its absolute magnitude relative to an external reference, contain sufficient signal to detect CIVO onset in real time. By tracking the trajectory of the value-to-evaluation ratio $r_t = \bar{V}_t / \max(\bar{E}_t, \varepsilon)$ (with $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$ for numerical stability) across checkpoints, a lightweight rule-based detector can flag dangerous overestimation using only quantities available within a single run.
151	
152	The causal structure of CIVO motivates acceleration as the key detection signal. CIVO operates as a positive feedback loop: the trained embedding biases reward predictions, which inflate value estimates, which in turn distort actor gradients toward trajectories that reinforce the bias (\S\ref{sec:civo_mechanism}). This self-amplifying cycle produces superlinear growth in $r_t$. In healthy training, a natural negative feedback counteracts value growth: improved value estimates yield a better actor, which raises evaluation returns, stabilizing the ratio. CIVO breaks this negative feedback, so sustained positive acceleration in $r_t$ serves as a causal signature that distinguishes pathological divergence from normal value learning.
153	
154	\paragraph{Algorithm.}
155	The detector monitors $r_t$ and fires an alert only when four conditions hold simultaneously at checkpoint $t$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:civo_detector}). The conjunction makes the detector conservative: any single noisy checkpoint is unlikely to satisfy magnitude, velocity, acceleration, and decline together. The computational cost is negligible (four scalar comparisons per checkpoint, no additional model inference).
156	
157	\begin{algorithm}[t]
158	\caption{Online CIVO Detector}
159	\label{alg:civo_detector}
160	\begin{algorithmic}[1]
161	\REQUIRE Stream of $(\bar{V}_t, \bar{E}_t)$ at each checkpoint $t$
162	\STATE $\tau_\text{mag} \leftarrow 0.49$, \quad $\tau_\text{vel} \leftarrow 0.1$, \quad $\varepsilon \leftarrow 10^{-6}$
163	\STATE Initialize $r_\text{prev} \leftarrow 0$, $\Delta r_\text{prev} \leftarrow 0$, $\mathcal{H} \leftarrow []$
164	\FOR{each checkpoint $t$}
165	  \STATE $r_t \leftarrow \bar{V}_t \,/\, \max(\bar{E}_t,\, \varepsilon)$
166	  \STATE $\Delta r_t \leftarrow r_t - r_\text{prev}$
167	  \STATE $c_\text{mag} \leftarrow (r_t \geq \tau_\text{mag})$
168	  \STATE $c_\text{vel} \leftarrow (\Delta r_t > \tau_\text{vel})$
169	  \STATE $c_\text{acc} \leftarrow (\Delta r_\text{prev} > \tau_\text{vel}) \wedge (\Delta r_t > \Delta r_\text{prev})$
170	  \STATE $c_\text{dec} \leftarrow \bigl|\{i : \mathcal{H}[i] < \mathcal{H}[i{-}1],\; i \in \text{last 3}\}\bigr| \geq 2$
171	  \IF{$c_\text{mag} \wedge c_\text{vel} \wedge c_\text{acc} \wedge c_\text{dec}$}
172	    \STATE \textbf{Alert:} CIVO onset at step $t$
173	  \ENDIF
174	  \STATE $r_\text{prev} \leftarrow r_t$, \quad $\Delta r_\text{prev} \leftarrow \Delta r_t$
175	  \STATE Append $\bar{E}_t$ to $\mathcal{H}$
176	\ENDFOR
177	\end{algorithmic}
178	\end{algorithm}
179	
180	The four conditions capture complementary signals: (1)~\emph{Magnitude} ($r_t \geq 0.49$) confirms that the value-to-evaluation ratio has reached a level indicative of overestimation; (2)~\emph{Velocity} ($\Delta r_t > 0.1$) rules out slow drift or plateau behavior; (3)~\emph{Acceleration} ($\Delta r_t > \Delta r_{t-1}$, both $> 0.1$) filters oscillatory paths where the ratio rises and falls without sustained divergence; (4)~\emph{Evaluation decline} ($\geq 2$ of 3 recent checkpoints decline) confirms that overestimation coincides with actual performance degradation. A 2-of-3 majority threshold balances noise robustness against single-checkpoint evaluation variance with sensitivity to sustained decline.\footnote{A full comparison of window sizes (3/3, 2/3, 2/5) and their trade-offs appears in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_sensitivity}.}
181	
182	Sensitivity analysis over $\tau_\text{mag} \in [0.30, 0.70]$ confirms that $\tau_\text{mag} = 0.49$ maximizes Youden's $J = 0.714$; raising the threshold to $0.50$ loses one true positive (seed~3 triggers at $r_t = 0.497$) with no reduction in false positives, since both false alarms have $r_t > 0.70$ at trigger time. The velocity threshold $\tau_\text{vel} = 0.1$ is stable across $[0.05, 0.18]$ (TPR remains $3/3$); we retain $0.1$ to avoid overfitting to $n{=}10$ runs.
183	
184	\paragraph{Validation.}
185	We apply the detector retrospectively to eleven runs: trained duration embedding with sustained overestimation (CIVO-positive, $n{=}3$), untrained or absent embedding (CIVO-negative, $n{=}3$), vanilla baselines ($n{=}4$), and one cross-simulator case (PickCube-v1 in ManiSkill3, trained embedding). Ground truth labels derive from manual inspection: a run is CIVO-positive if it exhibits sustained $\nve \geq 1.5\times$ with subsequent evaluation collapse. Table~\ref{tab:detector-validation} reports all results.

216	\paragraph{Results.}
217	The detector identifies all three CIVO-positive runs (TPR = 3/3). Two of three are detected \emph{predictively}: 008d seed~0 triggers at 45K, one checkpoint before evaluation crosses the 20\% decline threshold; seed~3 triggers at 45K, a full 50K steps before decline begins. The third case (010b, Cheetah Run) involves single-step collapse (evaluation drops 80\% in one interval), and the detector fires reactively at the next available checkpoint. No checkpoint-based monitor can provide advance warning for instantaneous collapse.
218	
219	Two false positives occur among eight negative cases (FPR = 2/8, precision = 3/5 = 0.60, F$_1$ = 0.75). Run 009c (no duration embedding) develops genuine late-stage value overestimation unrelated to CIVO; the detector correctly identifies value-performance dissociation, and the alert is arguably useful despite the label mismatch. Run 009a (vanilla baseline) triggers during a transient three-checkpoint eval dip at 85K; the model subsequently recovers to 966, constituting a pure false alarm. In practice, the cost of a false alert (manual checkpoint inspection, minutes) is orders of magnitude lower than the cost of undetected collapse (wasted GPU-hours, corrupted model); moderate FPR is therefore acceptable in this application.
220	
221	All three true positives triggered before 65K steps (range 45--65K) while both false positives occurred after 85K (range 85--105K), suggesting that early-training alerts carry higher diagnostic confidence. In practice, alerts before 70K steps can be treated as high-confidence CIVO indicators; later alerts warrant manual inspection.
222	
223	\paragraph{Cross-simulator specificity (PickCube).}
224	Exp\_019b applies the CIVO-trigger configuration (trained $\dt$-embedding, subsample ON, $H{=}8$) to PickCube-v1 in ManiSkill3, a manipulation task with sparse rewards and qualitatively different dynamics from DMC locomotion. The V/E ratio exhibits an oscillatory pattern rather than monotonic divergence: it spikes to $r_t = 5.66$ at 52.5K (eval crashes from 24.9 to 3.7), then deflates to 1.38 by 72.5K as value estimates self-correct, eventually reaching 0.065$\times$ NVE at 202.5K. This spike-then-deflation trajectory prevents the acceleration condition from co-occurring with evaluation decline at any single checkpoint. When V/E accelerates during early training (22.5--32.5K, consecutive velocities $+0.50$, $+1.85$), evaluation has not yet declined (C4 fails). When evaluation does decline (42.5--52.5K), the preceding V/E velocity is negative ($-0.81$), so the acceleration condition (C3) fails. Across all 14 checkpoints evaluated through 132.5K, no checkpoint satisfies all four conditions simultaneously, and the detector correctly remains silent (TN). This confirms that the acceleration filter distinguishes transient overestimation spikes from the sustained monotonic divergence characteristic of CIVO, extending the detector's specificity validation to a second simulator and a sparse-reward manipulation domain.
225	
226	\paragraph{Limitations.}
227	Four constraints bound the current detector. First, the three-checkpoint window cannot distinguish transient dips from sustained collapse (the source of the 009a false alarm). Second, the acceleration condition assumes monotonically increasing overestimation during onset; oscillatory trajectories that eventually collapse could evade detection until acceleration stabilizes. Third, the detector identifies a \emph{pattern} (rising value-evaluation ratio with declining performance) rather than a \emph{mechanism}: run 009c shows that non-CIVO pathologies produce the same signature. Fourth, threshold values ($\tau_\text{mag} = 0.49$, $\tau_\text{vel} = 0.1$) were calibrated on eleven runs across three environments; generalization to different reward scales requires further validation.
