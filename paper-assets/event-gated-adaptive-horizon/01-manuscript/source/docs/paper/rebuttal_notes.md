# Rebuttal Pre-drafted Responses

## Q1: Limited Environment Generalization

We thank the reviewer for raising the question of environment coverage. Our study is designed as a diagnostic investigation: the primary goal is to establish a causal chain from temporal embedding co-adaptation to value overestimation and policy collapse, not to maximize benchmark breadth. To this end, we demonstrate that the same causal mechanism reproduces across environments with substantially different reward scales (Walker, eval range 443--916 vs. Cheetah, eval 520--625) and qualitatively distinct dynamics (a 6-joint biped vs. a 2-joint runner). Notably, the absolute strength of the CIVO effect varies considerably across these environments (Walker NVE ranges from 1.5x to 4x while Cheetah NVE ranges from 8x to 109x), yet the underlying causal sequence remains invariant: trained temporal embeddings combined with multi-rate exposure produce value overestimation that precipitates collapse. We further include PickCube-v1 as a third evaluation environment on a second simulator (ManiSkill, distinct from DeepMind Control Suite) to verify detector specificity, extending coverage beyond locomotion tasks. We acknowledge in the Limitations section that broader architecture and environment generalization remains an open direction for future work. Furthermore, the protective mechanism (trained embedding without multi-rate exposure) transfers across both environments: all 5 seeds remain below the 1.5x diagnostic threshold (Walker 3 seeds: peak NVE 0.83--0.93x; Cheetah 2 seeds: peak NVE 1.08--1.29x at 35K, resolving to 0.48--0.49x by 105K), confirming that data exposure rather than embedding capacity or environment-specific dynamics is the operative trigger. Additional evidence: DreamerV3 vanilla baseline exhibits substantial seed variance (eval 443--916), yet CIVO consistently emerges across all tested trigger seeds, demonstrating that the phenomenon is robust to initialization randomness.

## Q2: Threshold Circularity / Overfitting

We appreciate the reviewer's concern regarding potential circularity in threshold selection. The NVE threshold of 1.5x was not post-hoc selected — Youden-optimal threshold from ROC analysis is 1.53×, independently converging on our pre-specified 1.5×. We now report Leave-One-Config-Out (LOCO-CV) cross-validation across K=12 configuration groups (n=56 experiments): mean train AUROC=0.988±0.003, mean optimal θ=1.61±0.23, cross-validated accuracy 53/56=94.6% (identical to in-sample), and zero false negatives (24/24 CIVO-positive detected in all folds). In 10/12 folds the training-optimal threshold falls within ±0.05 of 1.5×. The 3 persistent false positives (exp_008e_v3 mitigation transient, exp_047/057 frozen embedding transients) are mechanistically non-CIVO and self-correct without intervention. This confirms the threshold is not overfit to any single configuration group.

## Q3: Architecture Generalization

We appreciate this important question. Our claim is precisely scoped to "imagination-based actor-critic architectures with learned temporal embeddings," and we deliberately avoid generalizing to all reinforcement learning methods. The TAWM comparison directly isolates the role of feedback architecture: TAWM uses the same RSSM world model but replaces imagination-based bootstrapping with re-planning, and CIVO does not manifest. This result demonstrates that the failure originates in the feedback loop of imagined rollouts rather than in RSSM per se. At the mechanistic level, CIVO requires three structural ingredients: (1) temporal embeddings that co-adapt with the value function through shared gradient flow, (2) multi-step imagined rollouts used for critic training, and (3) a reward head that inherits latent biases from a shared representation. These conditions are defined functionally and are not tied to any specific network topology. We identify transformer-based world models as an explicit future direction in Section 7, and we believe the diagnostic contribution of this paper, identifying and characterizing a previously undocumented failure mode, holds independent value from exhaustive architectural enumeration.

## Q4: Post-CIVO Outcome Coverage

This concern has been addressed in the revised manuscript. The post-CIVO outcome table (Appendix) now reports all CIVO-positive runs: 8/8 Walker runs (6 unique seeds, NVE 1.53–6.57×) and 2/2 Cheetah runs (NVE 4.10–8.27×), totaling 10/10 locomotion onset. The majority outcome is collapse; Walker seed 2 is the sole self-correction case (eval 268 = 29.3% baseline). Cheetah exp_078 (seed 0, NVE 4.10×) shows onset with self-correction (eval 26.1→119.5), demonstrating environment-dependent outcome divergence.

## Q5: SGS Sample Size (n=2)

Our claim is deliberately conservative: SGS is "necessary but not sufficient for reliable CIVO elimination." This qualitative finding, that gradient pathway participation is required but insufficient, holds regardless of the exact recovery rate. The 50% observation establishes a Clopper-Pearson 95% CI of [6.8%, 93.2%], consistent with our conservative framing. A third data point from Cheetah Run (exp 021) shows partial recovery from transient CIVO onset (NVE 9.94x at 45K, recovering to eval 221.5), confirming the cross-environment pattern of SGS enabling recovery rather than prevention.
