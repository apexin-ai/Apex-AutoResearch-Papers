## Core Problem

When LLM evaluation results vary across reproductions, practitioners cannot determine whether the culprit is prompt phrasing, random seed, GPU hardware, numerical precision, temperature, or few-shot ordering—let alone how these factors interact—because every prior study isolates at most two sources. Mesting (2026) recently decomposed methodological sources (prompt variant, temperature, judge model) via Generalizability Theory but treated infrastructure as fixed background; no existing work crosses infrastructure factors (hardware type, numerical precision) with methodological factors (prompt, temperature, seed, data ordering) in a unified variance decomposition that produces actionable reproducibility budgets.

## Opportunity / Why Now

Multiple 2025–2026 papers each quantify individual variance sources in isolation: Brittlebench (2026) attributes up to 50% of performance variance to prompt perturbations and shows single perturbations alter model rankings in 63% of cases; Bui et al. (2025) document extreme seed sensitivity (RTES variance = 18.22) in fine-tuning; Yuan et al. (2025) demonstrate up to 9% accuracy variation and 9,000-token response-length shifts from GPU count, type, and precision changes alone via their LayerCast study; Mesting (2026) applies G-theory to five evaluation facets on AILuminate, MMLU, and Chatbot Arena, finding judge model contributes 43.8% and prompt×judge interaction 12.4% of total evaluation error—but uses only API-based models and excludes hardware, precision, and CUDA-level seed from the facet structure. The recent discovery that even greedy decoding (T=0) produces nondeterministic outputs due to floating-point non-associativity in BF16/FP16—formalized as 'background temperature' T_bg by Messina & Scotta (2026) and empirically measured at 80/1,000 unique completions for Qwen3-8B under default vLLM settings (Thinking Machines Lab, 2025)—makes infrastructure variance decomposition urgent. No study jointly crosses hardware × precision × prompt × seed × temperature × few-shot ordering to produce ranked variance attributions with interaction terms and D-study reproducibility budgets.

## Landscape (SOTA & Limitations)

Dodge et al. (2020) established seed sensitivity for BERT fine-tuning; Bethard (2022) found >50% of ACL papers have risky seed usage. Brittlebench (arXiv:2603.13285, 2026) introduced variance decomposition for prompt sensitivity specifically on standard NLP benchmarks. Yuan et al. (arXiv:2506.09501, 2025) showed hardware/precision effects reaching 9% accuracy variation on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B and proposed LayerCast (FP16 storage + FP32 compute) as mitigation, but did not conduct factorial attribution across multiple variance sources. Messina & Scotta (arXiv:2604.22411, 2026) formalized hidden nondeterminism as an 'effective background temperature' T_bg persisting even at nominal T=0. Thinking Machines Lab (2025) showed that batch-invariant GEMM/RMSNorm/attention kernels eliminate this nondeterminism with 1.6–2.1× latency overhead, demonstrating the infrastructure-level nature of the problem. Mesting (arXiv:2604.11581, 2026) is the closest methodological precedent: a G-theory decomposition of five facets (item, prompt variant, temperature, judge model, replication) on AILuminate (50,760 API calls), MMLU (200-item pool), and Chatbot Arena (4,676 matches), finding judge model = 43.8%, item×judge = 16.7%, within-category item = 20.8%, prompt×judge = 12.4% of total variance. Critically, Mesting uses API-based frontier models where infrastructure is opaque; the 'replication' facet captures only API-level stochasticity, not hardware-induced variance. Hardware, numerical precision, CUDA seeds, batch configuration, and data ordering are absent from Mesting's facet structure. 'Same Prompt, Different Outcomes' (arXiv:2602.14349, 2026) evaluated 2 strategies × 6 models × 4 temperatures with 10 repeats but excluded hardware/precision. Lauziere et al. (arXiv:2603.26830, 2026) applied regression to prompt components explaining 72–77% of variance but only for arithmetic tasks. Kunievsky & Evans (arXiv:2506.16584, 2025) decomposed variance into intent, articulation, and model uncertainty facets but without infrastructure factors. Hua et al. (EMNLP 2025, 'Flaw or Artifact?') demonstrated that log-likelihood-based evaluation inflates apparent prompt sensitivity—a confounder our generation-based evaluation protocol controls for. Exhaustive search of arXiv (Mar–May 2026) and OpenReview ICLR/NeurIPS/COLM 2026 submissions confirmed no concurrent study crossing infrastructure with methodological factors in a G-theory framework.

## Proposed Approach

We conduct two complementary fully-crossed Generalizability-Theory (G-theory) studies on inference plus a fully-crossed fine-tuning validation, using a Docker-containerized evaluation pipeline with pinned vLLM (v0.8.x), PyTorch, and CUDA versions. The inference framework uses DEFAULT vLLM settings (without batch-invariant kernels) to reflect realistic practitioner conditions, meaning CUDA-level nondeterminism is a measurable variance source rather than a controlled-away nuisance.

**Feasibility-Driven Design.** The central design constraint is that FP32 inference of 7–9B models requires 28–37 GB VRAM, which exceeds the RTX 4090's 24 GB. Rather than attempting an infeasible full crossing, we partition the study into two fully-crossed G-studies sharing overlapping cells:

**G-Study 1 (Precision Study):** 4 models (Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Gemma-2-9B-IT, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3) × 2 datacenter GPUs (NVIDIA A100-80GB, H100-SXM-80GB) × 3 numerical precisions (FP32, BF16, FP16) × 3 temperature configurations (T=0 greedy; T=0.5/top-p=0.9; T=1.0/top-p=0.8) × 6 semantics-preserving prompt paraphrases (generated following the Brittlebench perturbation protocol, random facet) × 6 CUDA RNG seeds (random facet) × 4 few-shot example orderings (random facet) = 10,368 fully-crossed conditions. Answers: How much does numerical precision contribute to total variance? Does precision interact with hardware type or prompt choice?

**G-Study 2 (Hardware Study):** Same 4 models × 3 GPUs (A100-80GB, H100-SXM-80GB, RTX 4090-24GB) × 2 precisions (BF16, FP16) × same 3 temperatures × 6 prompts × 6 seeds × 4 orderings = 10,368 fully-crossed conditions. Answers: How much does hardware class (datacenter vs. consumer) contribute? Does it interact with model architecture?

The two G-studies share 6,912 overlapping conditions (A100/H100 × BF16/FP16 cells), yielding 13,824 unique conditions total. The complete design matrix, including the explicit feasibility table showing why FP32 × RTX 4090 is structurally absent (Qwen3-8B: 32 GB > 24 GB; Gemma-2-9B: 37 GB > 24 GB), is released as supplementary CSV files.

**Benchmarks and Item Sampling.** Each condition is evaluated on stratified samples from five benchmarks spanning diverse task types: MMLU (200 items, stratified by subject), GSM8K (200 items), HumanEval (all 164 items), HellaSwag (200 items), ARC-Challenge (200 items)—totaling 964 items per condition and approximately 13.3 million inference calls. Items constitute an additional random facet in the G-theory model. We use generation-based evaluation with exact-match scoring plus a fixed external LLM judge to avoid the log-likelihood scoring artifacts identified by Hua et al. (2025).

**Scale Extension:** Qwen3-14B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-14B on A100/H100 at BF16/FP16 only (2 models × 2 hardware × 2 precisions × 432 within-cell conditions = 3,456 additional conditions) test whether variance attribution patterns change with model scale and architecture (dense vs. reasoning-distilled).

**Fine-Tuning G-Study:** RoBERTa-large (355M; fits all GPUs at all precisions, <2 GB weights) on four GLUE/SuperGLUE tasks (SST-2, MNLI, RTE, COPA), fully crossing: random seed (6 levels) × learning rate (3 levels: 1e-5, 3e-5, 5e-5) × data ordering (4 shuffles) × hardware (3 GPUs) × precision (2: BF16, FP16) = 432 balanced runs per task × 4 tasks = 1,728 total fine-tuning runs. This validates whether the variance attribution hierarchy differs between inference and fine-tuning regimes.

**Statistical Analysis.** Variance components are estimated via REML using lme4 (R) with crossed random effects. Fixed facets: model (4), hardware (2–3), precision (2–3), temperature (3). Random facets: prompt (6), seed (6), few-shot ordering (4), item (164–200). We report: (a) ranked variance attribution tables with bootstrap 95% CIs for each G-study and each benchmark; (b) all pairwise interaction variance components (prompt×seed, prompt×hardware, seed×precision, etc.); (c) D-study projections via the GeneralizIT Python package showing the number of prompts, seeds, and orderings needed to achieve generalizability coefficients G ≥ {0.90, 0.95, 0.99} for reliable model comparison; (d) a combined mixed-effects analysis of all 13,824+3,456 conditions treating missing FP32 × RTX 4090 cells as structurally absent. The D-study output constitutes quantitative 'reproducibility budgets'—e.g., 'to achieve G ≥ 0.95 for model ranking, use ≥K prompt variants and ≥J seeds, while hardware choice contributes <X% and can be reported rather than controlled.'

**Reproducibility Infrastructure.** We release: (a) Docker containers with pinned CUDA, PyTorch, vLLM, and driver versions for each GPU target; (b) complete design matrices as CSV files with feasibility annotations; (c) all 6 prompt paraphrases and 4 few-shot orderings per benchmark; (d) an open-source variance-audit CLI toolkit (Python, building on GeneralizIT) enabling researchers to generate G-study reproducibility report cards for arbitrary model–benchmark pairs with minimal configuration; (e) a public dashboard of variance profiles across all tested conditions.

### Summary

MVP Phase A: 108 条件 × 50 MMLU items = 5,400 次推理（Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, RTX PRO 6000 96GB, FP32/BF16/FP16 × 3 prompts × 3 seeds × 2 temps × 2 orderings）。G-theory 方差分解成功产出可解释的方差分量：item_id 主导（58.4%），precision×item（7.2%）、temperature×item（6.9%）、prompt×item（5.7%）均显著（CI 不含零）。直接的 precision×methodology 交互极小（<0.02%），separability 在聚合层面基本成立。D-study 显示 G=0.357，item 采样是提升可靠性的最大杠杆。float32 因 vLLM 引擎差异（V0 vs V1）与 BF16/FP16 存在混淆因子，需在全研究阶段统一引擎设置。

### Recommendation

GO — 技术可行性（pipeline + G-theory）、统计可行性（REML/ANOVA 产出可解释分量）、科学价值（item 交互效应是被忽视的关键发现）均已验证。全研究需解决：(1) float32 引擎混淆——统一用 V0 或全部用 V1 支持的精度；(2) 加入多硬件交叉（Phase B）；(3) 扩展 items（50→200+）提升 G 系数；(4) Framing 调整——从单纯测试 separability 转向 "item selection is the overlooked dominant variance source in LLM evaluation"
