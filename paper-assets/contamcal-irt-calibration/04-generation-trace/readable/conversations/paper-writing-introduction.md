2	\label{sec:method}
3	
4	ContamCal operates in two stages: Stage~1 estimates per-item contamination exposure from multiple detection signals via training-free fusion; Stage~2 embeds these scores in a contamination-augmented IRT model to produce calibrated ability estimates with credible intervals.
5	
6	\subsection{Problem Formulation}
7	\label{sec:formulation}
8	
9	Let $y_{ijk} \in \{0,1\}$ denote the binary response of model~$j$ ($j=1,\ldots,J$) to item~$i$ ($i=1,\ldots,I_k$) on benchmark~$k$. Standard IRT assumes $P(y_{ijk}=1) = f(\theta_j, \boldsymbol{\xi}_i)$ where $\theta_j$ is latent ability and $\boldsymbol{\xi}_i$ collects item parameters. When training corpora contain benchmark items, the observed ability $\hat{\theta}_j$ confounds genuine capability with memorization.
10	
11	Our goal is twofold: (1)~estimate $\theta_j^{\text{clean}}$, the ability each model would exhibit had it never encountered test items during training, and (2)~diagnose contamination effects at domain granularity within each benchmark. We draw on item compromise IRT \citep{eckerly2017,sinharay2017} and explanatory IRT \citep{deboeck2004}, adapting these frameworks through three structural modifications for LLM evaluation.

1	\section{Introduction}
2	\label{sec:intro}
3	
4	Large language model (LLM) evaluation increasingly relies on standardized benchmarks to compare models and track progress, yet mounting evidence reveals that these benchmarks are systematically compromised by training data contamination \citep{chang2025benchmarking}. \citet{yang2026rl} demonstrate that rephrased benchmark items appearing in training corpora inflate MMLU accuracy by 8--15 percentage points while evading standard $n$-gram decontamination, and that reinforcement learning (RL) post-training with GRPO further amplifies contamination-driven gains while simultaneously concealing the detection signals that would reveal them. Dose-response analyses confirm a monotonic relationship between contamination exposure and score inflation, with effects persisting across model scales \citep{dong2026dose}. Even minor perturbations to benchmark item surface forms---which should not affect genuinely capable models---produce substantial accuracy drops, questioning the validity of reported scores \citep{wang2025fragility}. Within a single benchmark like MMLU, contamination may strongly inflate scores on mathematics items while leaving humanities items unaffected or producing negative effects. Aggregate corrections that average over such heterogeneity will systematically underestimate the problem in affected domains and overestimate it elsewhere, providing neither accurate calibration nor actionable diagnostic information. Second, the proliferation of open-weight models trained on trillion-token internet corpora means that contamination is now pervasive and cannot be precisely measured at the instance level \\citep{oren2024proving}. Contamination-free benchmarks \\citep{white2024livebench} offer a partial solution but sacrifice the comparability and institutional knowledge accumulated around established benchmarks. The field needs post-hoc statistical tools that quantify contamination effects despite imperfect detection, and that provide domain-level diagnosis rather than aggregate point corrections.\n\nDetection methods such as Min-K\\%++ \\citep{zhang_2024_minkpp}, ConStat \\citep{dekoninck_2024_constat}, and Kernel Divergence Signatures \\citep{choi_2025_kds} can flag contaminated items but stop at detection. They do not quantify how much scores are inflated, in which domains the inflation occurs, or whether effects are uniform across test items. Score adjustment methods like DCR \\citep{li_2025_dcr} provide point corrections without uncertainty quantification or domain-level granularity. IRT-based evaluation frameworks \\citep{polo_2024_tinybenchmarks,habba_2025_growing_pains} use psychometric ability estimation to improve evaluation efficiency and handle new models, but they ignore contamination entirely \\citep{lalor_2024_irt_nlp}. No existing method provides domain-level contamination \\textit{diagnosis} with calibrated uncertainty. This gap prevents practitioners from understanding \\textit{where} their benchmark scores are distorted, \\textit{by how much}, and whether the distortion is uniform or heterogeneous across cognitive domains within a single benchmark.\n\nWe propose ContamCal, a two-stage diagnostic framework that bridges contamination detection and IRT-based evaluation. Stage~1 fuses multiple detection signals into continuous per-item exposure scores via training-free Reciprocal Rank Fusion \\citep{cormack_2009_rrf}, avoiding the meta-circularity of learned aggregators that would require the very contamination labels we lack. The problem is not new, but its characterization remains superficial: existing work treats contamination as a binary property of models or benchmarks and proposes aggregate corrections that assume uniform effects across all test items. This framing masks critical structure. Within a single benchmark like MMLU, contamination may strongly inflate scores on mathematics items while leaving humanities items unaffected or producing negative effects. Aggregate corrections that average over such heterogeneity will systematically underestimate the problem in affected domains and overestimate it elsewhere.
