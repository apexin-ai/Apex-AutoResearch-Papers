\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\vspace{-0.3em}

We conducted a controlled factorial study comparing spectral optimizers (Muon, SOAP, Shampoo) against AdamW across three architecture families (CNN, transformer, hybrid) and three vision datasets, totaling over 300 training runs.
Spectral optimizers consistently outperform AdamW by $+5.9$ to $+24.3$\,pp, with the Newton-Schulz family (Muon) exhibiting the strongest robustness; stable rank divergence correlates positively with the spectral advantage on CNNs ($\rho{=}{+}0.58$, $p = 3.6 \times 10^{-6}$), and normalization-swap experiments provide controlled evidence that normalization type mediates this effect.
For practitioners, spectral optimizers are beneficial across all tested paradigms, reaching near-ceiling accuracy within 20 epochs and converging 2--15$\times$ faster in wall-clock time (configuration-dependent).
Our study is limited to CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, and ImageNet-100 at $224{\times}224$ resolution; full 90-epoch ImageNet-1K validation and characterizing the architecture-dependent transformer correlation pattern remain key open directions.
