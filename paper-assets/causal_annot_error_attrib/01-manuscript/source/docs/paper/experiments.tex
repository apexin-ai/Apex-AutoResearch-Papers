We validate through Monte Carlo simulation ($K{=}3$), cross-$K$ robustness checks ($K{=}5,7$), and empirical validation with real LLM confusion matrices from three natural language processing (NLP) tasks ($K{=}2,3,10$).

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

For each topology, we sample 100{,}000 confusion matrices from a Dirichlet$(1,1,1)$ prior, compute analytical probability limits, and validate against Monte Carlo simulation ($N{=}10{,}000$). ASV experiments use $C(\delta) = (1{-}\delta)I + \delta C_0$.

\subsection{Bias Taxonomy}
\label{sec:taxonomy_results}

\label{fig:dirichlet_overlay}

\paragraph{Theory-MC agreement.}
Analytical probability limits match Monte Carlo estimates with mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of 1.6\%, averaged across 161 scenarios (23 LLM configurations $\times$ 7 topologies; per-topology range: 0.3\%--3.7\%), validating the closed-form derivations and justifying probability limits as practical bias diagnostics.

\paragraph{Exposure DAG: sign reversal under multi-class misclassification.}
Under realistic accuracy (diagonal $\geq 0.7$), the violation rate is 13.7\% with 2.1\% sign reversal; at diagonal $\geq 0.9$, violation persists at 11.9\% (Appendix~\ref{sec:iv_dirichlet_subspace}). Under the full Dirichlet prior, 85\% of matrices violate the binary ``toward-null'' guarantee and 83.4\% cause sign reversal; $\det(C) < 0.014$ predicts sign reversal with 91\% accuracy (Appendix~\ref{sec:sign_determinants}). The $K{=}2$ control confirms classical attenuation~\cite{brenner1993}. This establishes the multi-class failure as structurally distinct.

\paragraph{Structural guarantee for non-exposure topologies.}
Five topologies exhibit zero sign-flip rate (Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}): confounding, mediation, and front-door show 100\% amplification~\cite{brenner1993, dosemeci1990} but never reverse the sign; collider is protective ($|B(C)|$ non-increasing; Conjecture~\ref{conj:monotone}); M-bias shows weakest sensitivity (53.2\% violation). All patterns persist at $K{=}5,7$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:cross_k_robustness}).

\paragraph{IV: Wald estimator instability.}
The IV topology is the sole non-exposure exception: 52.6\% of random matrices cause sign reversal, stable across $K{=}3,5,7$. Diagonal dominance ($C_{kk} > 0.5$) eliminates sign reversal but preserves amplification (Appendix~\ref{sec:iv_dirichlet_subspace}); however, all 23 empirical LLM configurations show zero IV sign reversal.

Figure~\ref{fig:dirichlet_overlay} overlays all 23 empirical LLM configurations onto the theoretical violation-rate curves; real-world accuracy ($\min_k C_{kk} \geq 0.6$) places all models in the low-risk region for IV but leaves exposure vulnerable.

\paragraph{ASV results.}
The ASV envelope is $\varepsilon$-approximately monotonic ($\varepsilon < 0.02$; Conjecture~\ref{conj:monotone}); IV is maximally fragile, M-bias is immune ($\text{ASV} = \infty$; Figure~\ref{fig:asv_ranking}). Sign-flip ASV: 83.4\% of exposure matrices yield $\delta^*_{\text{sf}} < 1$; IV ranges 1.1--6.7 (Table~\ref{tab:asv_summary}). Significance ASV exhibits a phase transition at $N^* \approx 6{,}000$--$10{,}000$; bootstrap CIs (Appendix~\ref{sec:bootstrap_asv},~\ref{sec:c_robustness}) confirm stable topology ranking, with the safe/fragile partition recovering from $n_{\text{gold}} \geq 100$ (82\% stability; Appendix~\ref{sec:asv_diagnostics},~\ref{sec:asv_case_study}).

\subsection{Empirical Validation}
\label{sec:empirical}

We validate using real LLM confusion matrices from HumAID~\cite{alam2021humaid} ($K{=}10$), VAST ($K{=}3$), and CivilComments ($K{=}2$): four LLMs, 23 configurations, 161 bias scenarios. HumAID ($K{=}10$) matches predictions: IV (12.4--42.1\%) and exposure (9.0--17.4\%) dominate; remaining topologies $<$15\%. $F_1$ does not exhibit significant rank correlation with bias ($\rho = -0.10$, $p = 0.45$; Figure~\ref{fig:f1_vs_bias}), but per-topology patterns diverge ($\rho \leq -0.53$ for five topologies vs.\ $0.87$ for M-bias; Appendix~\ref{sec:within_topo_f1}). Of exposure coefficients, 14/23 (61\%) exhibit sign reversal, all secondary; zero across 138 non-exposure scenarios.

\subsection{Correction Guidance}
\label{sec:correction}

We evaluate five correction methods (naive plug-in, maximum likelihood adjustment (MLA), design-based supervised learning (DSL)~\cite{egami_hinck_stewart_wei2023}, Monte Carlo simulation extrapolation (MC-SIMEX)~\cite{kuchenhoff2006simex}, PPI++~\cite{angelopoulos2023}) across all topologies (210 configurations; Table~\ref{tab:correction}, Appendix~\ref{app:supplementary}).

\paragraph{FWL invariance.}
Under linear regression, FWL invariance renders matrix-based methods (naive plug-in, MLA) provably ineffective for covariate topologies (0\% bias reduction; Appendix~\ref{app:fwl_proof}): the treatment coefficient depends only on the projection orthogonal to the covariate space, so relabeling $A^* \to A$ leaves the coefficient unchanged. Under logistic regression this invariance breaks, but the method ranking persists ($|\Delta| < 0.02$; Appendix~\ref{sec:fwl_nonlinear}).

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig4_binarization.pdf}
\caption{Binarization collapse $\Delta$: additional bias from collapsing $K{=}3$ to binary. Topology determines severity ($>$145$\times$ range); exposure and IV exhibit sign flips.}
\label{fig:binarization}

\paragraph{Method ranking and decision procedure.}
PPI++ achieves the highest root mean squared error (RMSE) reduction (47.0\% at $K{=}3$) but requires gold labels (Appendix~\ref{sec:ppi_gold_ratio}) and harms M-bias ($-$88\%); MC-SIMEX is gold-free and $K$-robust (27--70\%), though it harms front-door ($-$62.4\%); rankings are stable across $K$ ($\rho \geq 0.80$; Appendix~\ref{sec:correction_k_sensitivity}). Algorithm~\ref{alg:correction} routes through risk assessment, method selection, and FWL screening. End-to-end demos (Appendix~\ref{sec:e2e_demo},~\ref{sec:real_cm_demo},~\ref{app:case_study}) validate: PPI++ reduces exposure bias from 42.9\% to 0.2\%, while topology-agnostic IV correction amplifies bias ($-$139\%). Collapsing $K{=}3$ to binary is not a safe fallback: the additional bias $\Delta$ varies $>$145$\times$ by topology (Figure~\ref{fig:binarization}; Appendix~\ref{app:binarization}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Correction method selection for LLM-annotated causal inference.}
\label{alg:correction}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE DAG topology $\mathcal{T}$, gold-label availability $G \in \{\textsc{yes}, \textsc{no}\}$
\ENSURE Recommended method $M$, risk level $R$
\STATE \textbf{Step 1: Risk assessment} \COMMENT{If $\mathcal{T}$ uncertain, repeat per candidate}
\STATE $R \gets$ \textsc{Low} if M-bias; \textsc{Med} if Conf/Med/Coll/FD; \textsc{High} if Exp/IV
\STATE \textbf{Step 2: Method selection}
\IF{$G = \textsc{yes}$}
    \STATE $M \gets \text{PPI++}$ (47\% RMSE red.) except M-bias $\to$ \textsc{None} ($-$88\%)
\ELSE
    \STATE $M \gets \text{DSL}$ if Conf/Med; \text{MC-SIMEX} if IV/Coll/M-bias; $\emptyset$ otherwise
\ENDIF
\STATE \textbf{Step 3: FWL screening}
\IF{$\mathcal{T} \in \{\text{Conf., Med., Coll., M-bias}\}$}
    \STATE Skip Naive plug-in and MLA \COMMENT{0\% bias red.\ on $T$}
\ENDIF
\RETURN $(M, R)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
