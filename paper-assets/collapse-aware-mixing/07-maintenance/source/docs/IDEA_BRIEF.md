## Core Problem

Current approaches to mixing synthetic and real data in iterative LLM pretraining either use static ratios (golden-ratio weighting, fixed proportions) or apply generic online optimization (Aioli via loss-based feedback, GRAPE via minimax DRO) without monitoring for collapse-specific dynamics. Static mixing ratios — including the golden-ratio weighting (arXiv:2502.18049) — optimize over the restricted class of fixed weights: the golden ratio 1/φ minimizes asymptotic steady-state error as a constant weight across all generations under iterative dynamics where estimation error compounds via the recurrence Err̄(θ̂_t) = (1−w)²Err̄(θ̂_{t−1}) + [(1−w)²k + w²]tr(Σ₀⁻¹). However, a generation-dependent weight schedule w_t that allocates less real data early (when synthetic quality is high and the model is close to the true distribution) and more late (when drift has accumulated) can improve over any fixed weight — the classic static-vs-dynamic optimization gap. Bakshi & Chakraborty (arXiv:2602.10531) formalize the generation-specific structure via the recurrence R_t = (n_0/n_t)R_0 + ((n_t-1)/n_t)(1-α_t)² R_{t-1}, where the contamination propagation factor (1-α_t)² depends on generation-specific synthetic quality — their analysis of both fixed and decaying weight schedules motivates generation-dependent mixing, though they do not prove that static ratios are suboptimal. Training-objective modifications like truncated cross-entropy (ForTIFAI) and token-level editing (ToEdit) increase collapse tolerance but do not control data composition dynamically. While DCES monitors cluster dispersion for adaptive sampling in classification self-training, no existing method for autoregressive LLM pretraining uses online spectral collapse indicators to dynamically adjust the synthetic-to-real data ratio across model generations. Recent dynamic mixing methods optimize general domain proportions using loss or gradient signals that cannot distinguish between benign loss variation and collapse-induced representational narrowing. SIGMA (arXiv:2601.03385) demonstrates that spectral analysis of embedding Gram matrices can detect collapse onset in LLMs, but uses it only for monitoring, not for closed-loop control. The Topology Collapse Index (arXiv:2604.26984) mentions 'data mix adjustments' as a potential application but implements only monitoring without any feedback mechanism. DiSF (arXiv:2504.20644) uses eigenvalue uniformity for offline data file selection, and SSO (arXiv:2601.08393) enforces spectral constraints on weight matrices during optimization — both adjacent uses of spectral metrics that do not address online mixing ratio control. The ERBP framework (arXiv:2512.14879) proves that avoiding collapse in self-referential learning requires maintaining sufficient entropy-reservoir coupling — specifically, the condition λ·s_min > κ (where λ is the coupling coefficient, s_min is the entropy lower bound of the reservoir distribution, and κ is the optimization imperfection bound) — but provides no mechanism to dynamically adjust this coupling during training, and explicitly identifies the multi-round dynamics analysis as 'a significant theoretical undertaking and a key direction for future research.' Crucially, theoretical work (Barzilai & Shamir, ICML 2025; Bakshi & Chakraborty, 2026; ERBP, 2025) proves that avoiding collapse requires structural assumptions beyond MLE consistency — simply accumulating data is insufficient without monitoring whether those conditions hold and adjusting accordingly. Dynamic regret theory (Zinkevich, ICML 2003; Besbes et al., Operations Research 2015) establishes that any fixed policy incurs regret O(√(T·P_T)) where P_T is the path length (cumulative non-stationarity) — in iterative training where synthetic quality degrades across generations, P_T grows, making the improvement of an adaptive policy over any fixed policy increasingly significant. This motivates a controller that uses spectral collapse indicators to dynamically adjust the entropy-reservoir coupling (i.e., real data fraction) in a generation-aware manner, exploiting the static-vs-dynamic optimization gap that even the optimal fixed weight (golden ratio) cannot close.

## Opportunity / Why Now

Recent evidence shows model collapse is condition-dependent: rephrased synthetic data may not trigger collapse at foreseeable scales (Kang et al., EMNLP 2025), while model-generated content under replace workflows does degrade quality (Kazdan et al., ICML 2025). Schaeffer et al. (ICML 2025) further narrow the genuine threat to specific data workflows. Two recent theoretical advances create a precise opportunity: (1) The ERBP framework (arXiv:2512.14879) proves that self-referential training dynamics can be stabilized by an entropy reservoir with coupling satisfying λ·s_min > κ (where s_min is the reservoir's entropy lower bound and κ bounds optimization imperfection), providing closed-form rates and a sufficient condition for maintaining a non-trivial entropy floor — but does not prescribe how to dynamically set λ during training, and explicitly acknowledges that characterizing multi-round dynamics is an open problem. For the KL divergence generator (natural for language models), ERBP shows L_F = 1 analytically, simplifying the stability bound but leaving two empirical quantities: κ (optimization imperfection) and s_min (reservoir entropy). (2) SIGMA (arXiv:2601.03385) shows the log-determinant of the embedding Gram matrix provides scalable, early-warning spectral collapse detection in LLMs — Track II detects collapse substantially earlier than conservative Track I metrics, with over an order of magnitude more sensitivity — but uses this only for post-hoc monitoring, not active control. Together, these create a clear gap: a controller that monitors spectral collapse indicators and dynamically adjusts the entropy-reservoir coupling (real data fraction) to maintain the ERBP stability condition. The potential advantage of adaptive over fixed-weight control follows from the static-vs-dynamic optimization gap: the golden ratio (1/φ) is the optimal constant weight under the iterative error recurrence (arXiv:2502.18049), but it cannot respond to the actual per-generation synthetic quality. Early generations, where the model is still close to the true distribution, produce high-quality synthetic data requiring less real-data correction, while later generations accumulate drift requiring more. A generation-dependent weight schedule can exploit this structure. The expected magnitude of improvement depends on the degree of non-stationarity. Shumailov et al. (Nature 2024) report perplexity degradation of 20-28 points over 9 generations under pure replacement, indicating substantial non-stationarity. Dynamic mixing methods in the general domain setting (DoReMi, Aioli) show 0.3-12 perplexity point improvements and up to 6.5 percentage point downstream gains depending on distribution shift magnitude. However, when the static baseline is already near-optimal (as the golden ratio is, being derived under iterative dynamics), the adaptive advantage may be smaller — potentially <1 perplexity point in low-non-stationarity regimes. Our pilot (Phase 0) measures inter-generation KL divergence to quantify the actual non-stationarity in our setting, providing a concrete estimate of the expected improvement margin before committing to the full experiment. We define explicit success thresholds (see approach). Three recent dynamic mixing methods are conceptually related but use generic signals: Aioli (ICLR 2025, arXiv:2411.05735) uses loss-based online adaptation; GRAPE (NeurIPS 2025) uses minimax DRO for multi-target robustness. AC-ODM (arXiv:2505.23878) and TiKMiX (arXiv:2508.17677) proposed actor-critic RL and gradient-based Group Influence respectively, but both were withdrawn from ICLR 2026 review — we cite their technical approaches for completeness but note their unvalidated status. None monitors spectral representational health or connects to information-geometric stability conditions. Adjacent uses of spectral metrics for active training control exist — SSO (arXiv:2601.08393) enforces spectral constraints on weight matrices during LLM pretraining optimization, DiSF (arXiv:2504.20644) uses eigenvalue uniformity for offline data file selection, and the Topology Collapse Index (arXiv:2604.26984) mentions 'data mix adjustments' as a potential application but implements only monitoring — yet no published work integrates spectral collapse indicators into a closed-loop controller for dynamically adjusting the synthetic-to-real data ratio during iterative pretraining. A PI controller with spectral collapse-risk estimation fills this specific gap: classical control with well-understood stability properties (vs. RL without convergence guarantees), cheap inference-time spectral features (vs. gradient computation), and theoretically grounded collapse-specific signals connected to ERBP's sufficient stability condition (vs. generic loss). Feasible on Pythia-1B across 5 generations within ~190 H100-hours, targeting EMNLP 2026.

## Landscape (SOTA & Limitations)

**Static mixing approaches.** Golden ratio weighting (arXiv:2502.18049, Feb 2025) proves that weighting real data by 1/φ minimizes asymptotic steady-state error as a constant weight under iterative dynamics where estimation error compounds via the recurrence Err̄(θ̂_t) = (1−w)²Err̄(θ̂_{t−1}) + [(1−w)²k + w²]tr(Σ₀⁻¹) — applicable to Gaussian, GLM, and nonparametric settings. Crucially, this result optimizes over the restricted class of fixed weights: the paper models iterative training with compounding errors but does not analyze generation-dependent weight schedules w_t, leaving open whether adaptive weights can improve over the golden ratio. Preventing Model Collapse Under Overparametrization (arXiv:2509.22341) derives optimal static mixing ratios without adaptive adjustment. The accumulate strategy (Gerstgrasser et al., ICLR 2025) prevents collapse by retaining all prior data but does not optimize the mixing ratio. Bakshi & Chakraborty (arXiv:2602.10531) derive the recurrence R_t = (n_0/n_t)R_0 + ((n_t-1)/n_t)(1-α_t)²R_{t-1} and analyze both fixed (constant α) and decaying (α_t → 0) weight schedules, characterizing convergence conditions for each. Their analysis shows the contamination propagation factor (1-α_t)² depends on generation-specific synthetic quality, motivating generation-dependent weights — though they do not prove that static ratios are fundamentally suboptimal. The paper discusses 'naturally adaptive' weights in Appendix E as supplementary analysis and notes that estimating mixture weights in practice 'remains a challenge.'

**Dynamic mixing approaches — generic.** Aioli (ICLR 2025, arXiv:2411.05735) dynamically adjusts domain proportions via loss-based signals, achieving 0.27-12.0 perplexity point improvements over static baselines depending on distribution shift magnitude — but targets general domain optimization rather than collapse dynamics. GRAPE (arXiv:2505.20380, NeurIPS 2025) uses minimax DRO for group-robust multi-target adaptive pretraining. DoReMi (NeurIPS 2023) achieves +6.5 percentage points average few-shot accuracy over The Pile's default domain weights (a known-suboptimal static baseline) and 2.6× convergence speedup. DoGraph (arXiv:2604.07963, Apr 2026) addresses domain weight optimization. AC-ODM (arXiv:2505.23878, May 2025) proposed actor-critic RL (DDPG) for dynamic domain weight adjustment, claiming 71% faster convergence — but was withdrawn from ICLR 2026. TiKMiX (arXiv:2508.17677, Aug 2025) proposed gradient-based Group Influence with 2% average gains at 20% of REGMIX's compute — also withdrawn from ICLR 2026. We cite AC-ODM and TiKMiX for their technical approaches (actor-critic and gradient-based control respectively) but note their unvalidated peer-review status; Aioli and GRAPE are the established accepted-paper baselines. Data Mixing Agent (arXiv:2507.15640, Jul 2025) learns RL-based domain reweighting for continual pretraining — targets catastrophic forgetting, not collapse. The data mixing survey (Chen et al., arXiv:2604.16380, Mar 2026) taxonomizes approaches, identifying limited transferability as a key open challenge. Next-Generation LLM Training (arXiv:2603.14712, Mar 2026) proposes a unified data-model interaction training system with dynamic data selection and reweighting, but does not use spectral collapse indicators or address synthetic-vs-real mixing.

**PID control in machine learning.** An et al. (CVPR 2018) established PID control for deep network optimization, framing PID-inspired approaches as 'motivated by the outstanding performance of PID controllers in automatic control' — providing design intuition from control theory with empirical validation, rather than closed-loop stability proofs for nonlinear DNN dynamics (up to 50% training acceleration). PIDAO (Chen et al., Nature Communications 2024) extends this with formal convergence analysis for convex objectives. Li et al. (arXiv:1908.10920, 2019) frame deep learning as optimal control for 'principled guidance for architecture and algorithm design.' All apply PID to optimizer dynamics (parameter updates), not to data composition. Recent work on data-driven adaptive PID (arXiv:2510.04591, Oct 2025) demonstrates physics-informed approaches to stability-guaranteed PID tuning. Beyond Quadratic Costs (arXiv:2505.00319, 2025) derives stabilizing controllers using Bregman divergence cost functions via Riccati-like identities.

**Collapse-specific methods — surface metrics.** DCES (Electronics, Feb 2026) monitors intra-cluster dispersion for adaptive sampling in classification. ForTIFAI (arXiv:2509.08972, Sep 2025) introduces TCE loss achieving 2.3× greater collapse tolerance, modifying training objectives rather than data composition. ToEdit (arXiv:2412.14689, ICML 2025) proposes token-level editing with theoretical guarantees — operates on data synthesis rather than mixing ratio control, making it complementary. Surplexity (arXiv:2410.12341, Oct 2024) provides perplexity-based surprise metrics for offline data filtering.

**Collapse-specific methods — spectral, topological, and information-geometric.** SIGMA (arXiv:2601.03385, Jan 2026) introduces spectral analysis of the embedding Gram matrix for scalable LLM collapse monitoring. Their log-determinant metric tracks representation space contraction, with Track II detecting collapse substantially earlier than Track I — demonstrating over an order of magnitude greater sensitivity. Critically, SIGMA validates spectral metrics as early-warning collapse indicators in LLMs, but uses them only for monitoring, not closed-loop control. RankMe (Garrido et al., ICML 2023, arXiv:2210.02885) directly validates that spectral effective rank (exponential of Shannon entropy of normalized singular values) serves as a label-free, computationally lightweight proxy for representation quality and dimensional collapse in self-supervised learning — correlating with downstream performance without requiring labels. Monitoring Neural Training with Topology (arXiv:2604.26984, Apr 2026) proposes a composite Collapse Index based on Modular Morse Homology — providing topology-aware online monitoring and mentioning 'data mix adjustments' as a potential application, but implementing only monitoring without any feedback mechanism. Escaping Mode Collapse in LLM Generation via Geometric Regulation (arXiv:2605.00435, May 2026) proposes RMR, operating at inference time rather than training-time data mixing, making it complementary. DiSF (arXiv:2504.20644, Apr 2025) combats dimensional collapse in LLM pretraining data by selecting files with uniform eigenvalues (1.5× training efficiency), demonstrating that effective rank captures data diversity directly — an offline data selection method using spectral metrics, complementary to but distinct from online mixing ratio control. SSO (arXiv:2601.08393, Jan 2026) enforces strict module-wise spectral constraints on weight matrices during LLM pretraining optimization, consistently outperforming AdamW and Muon — an active use of spectral metrics for training control, but applied to weight-space optimization rather than data composition. Spectral Lens (arXiv:2605.05683, May 2026) shows activation covariance spectra forecast downstream token efficiency — validating spectral features as leading indicators of training dynamics. ERBP (arXiv:2512.14879, Dec 2025) provides an information-geometric unification using the Bregman projection framework — proving that entropy-reservoir coupling satisfying λ·s_min > κ (with L_F = 1 for KL divergence) guarantees a non-trivial entropy floor. ERBP explicitly states that 'analyzing the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the self-referential fixed-point equation' is 'a significant theoretical undertaking and a key direction for future research,' and that 'characterising how optimiser noise compounds over many rounds is an important practical question.' Shi et al. (arXiv:2509.16499, NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight) demonstrate entropy-driven collapse in diffusion models and propose entropy-based data selection.

**Theoretical foundations.** Dynamic regret theory establishes that any fixed policy incurs regret O(√(T·P_T)) where P_T is the path length quantifying cumulative non-stationarity (Zinkevich, ICML 2003; Besbes et al., Operations Research 2015). In iterative synthetic-real training, P_T grows across generations as synthetic quality shifts — bounding the cost of any fixed weight relative to the best generation-dependent schedule. Barzilai & Shamir (ICML 2025) prove non-asymptotic bounds showing collapse avoidance requires structural assumptions beyond MLE consistency. Strong Model Collapse (ICLR 2025) shows even 1/1000 synthetic fraction can prevent scaling improvements.

**Collapse condition characterization.** Schaeffer et al. (ICML 2025) identify 8 definitions and argue prominent scenarios rely on unrealistic assumptions. Kang et al. (EMNLP 2025) find rephrased synthetic data does NOT collapse at foreseeable scales while model-generated content does. Kazdan et al. (ICML 2025) characterize replace/accumulate/fixed-compute workflows.

No published work integrates spectral collapse indicators into a closed-loop controller for dynamically adjusting the synthetic-to-real data ratio during iterative autoregressive LLM pretraining. Adjacent uses of spectral metrics exist for active training control (SSO for weight-matrix spectral constraints, DiSF for offline data file selection) and for collapse monitoring with aspirational mentions of interventions (Topology Collapse Index), but none implements closed-loop feedback between spectral representational health signals and data composition in the iterative synthetic-real setting.

## Proposed Approach

**Core method: Entropy-reservoir PI controller with spectral collapse-risk estimation.** We implement a PI controller that monitors online collapse risk via spectral analysis of the model's output embeddings, adjusting the synthetic-to-real data sampling ratio to maintain the ERBP sufficient stability condition across 5 generations of iterative Pythia-1B pretraining (2B tokens per generation). The method has three novel components: (1) a spectral collapse indicator based on the embedding Gram matrix integrated into a closed-loop controller (extending SIGMA's monitoring to active control), (2) a theoretical contribution connecting PI control design to ERBP's entropy-reservoir framework (with explicit tiered framing — see below), and (3) empirical validation that spectral indicators provide higher-resolution collapse signals than perplexity in autoregressive LLMs.

**Theoretical motivation: Why adaptive control can improve over the optimal fixed weight.** The golden-ratio weighting (1/φ) is the optimal constant weight under iterative dynamics where estimation error compounds via the recurrence Err̄(θ̂_t) = (1−w)²Err̄(θ̂_{t−1}) + [(1−w)²k + w²]tr(Σ₀⁻¹) (arXiv:2502.18049). This result optimizes over the restricted class of fixed weights — the paper models iterative training with compounding errors but constrains the policy to use the same w at every generation. A generation-dependent weight schedule w_t that responds to actual per-generation synthetic quality can improve over any fixed weight: early generations, where the model is close to the true distribution, produce high-quality synthetic data requiring less real-data correction, while later generations accumulate drift requiring more. This is the standard static-vs-dynamic optimization gap, bounded by dynamic regret theory: Zinkevich (ICML 2003) and Besbes et al. (Operations Research 2015) establish that any fixed policy incurs regret O(√(T·P_T)) where P_T is the path length — the cost of being static grows with the cumulative non-stationarity of synthetic quality across generations. Bakshi & Chakraborty (arXiv:2602.10531) provide additional structural motivation: their recurrence R_t = (n_0/n_t)R_0 + ((n_t-1)/n_t)(1-α_t)² R_{t-1} shows the contamination propagation factor (1-α_t)² depends on generation-specific synthetic quality, and their analysis of both fixed and decaying weight schedules characterizes convergence conditions for each regime — motivating generation-dependent weights without proving that static ratios are fundamentally suboptimal.

**Expected improvement magnitude and success thresholds.** The adaptive advantage over fixed-weight mixing depends on the degree of non-stationarity in the synthetic distribution. When the static baseline is already near-optimal (as the golden ratio is, being derived under iterative dynamics), the improvement margin may be smaller than comparisons to naive baselines suggest. Aioli's improvement over static baselines averages 0.27 perplexity points (up to 12 in extreme distribution shift), and DoReMi's 6.5pp downstream gain is over The Pile's default domain weights — a known-suboptimal static baseline. We define explicit success thresholds: (a) **Primary success**: >1 perplexity point improvement over golden-ratio static mixing at generation 5, or >1.5% downstream accuracy improvement on HellaSwag/ARC-Challenge. This would demonstrate that collapse-aware adaptive control provides meaningful gains over the best fixed weight. (b) **Secondary success**: If the mixing ratio improvement is smaller but statistically significant (<1 perplexity point), we frame the contribution as demonstrating the value of spectral indicators for collapse monitoring — showing that spectral effective rank provides higher-resolution representational health signals than perplexity-based metrics, with marginal but consistent control benefit. This secondary framing has independent value: a validated spectral collapse indicator that can be integrated into any data mixing framework. (c) **Negative result (still publishable)**: If the adaptive controller does not improve over golden-ratio mixing, this constitutes evidence that the golden ratio is near-optimal not just as a fixed weight but across generation-dependent policies in our setting — a non-trivial empirical finding that strengthens the golden-ratio result.

**Phase 0: Pilot experiment (reduced scale, ~5 H100-hours).** Before the main experiment, we run a diagnostic pilot: train Pythia-1B for 2 generations with 100% synthetic data (pure replacement, 500M tokens per generation) tracking both perplexity and spectral/diversity metrics at every checkpoint (~50M token intervals). This pilot serves three purposes: (a) validates the 'spectral collapse precedes perplexity degradation' hypothesis for autoregressive LLMs; (b) provides collapse trajectory exemplars for probe calibration and estimates of the ERBP constants κ and s_min; (c) **quantifies the inter-generation distribution shift** by computing KL divergence between G_0→G_1 and G_1→G_2 synthetic distributions, providing an empirical estimate of P_T that predicts the adaptive controller's improvement margin.

**Estimating s_min and κ from the pilot.** The ERBP stability condition λ·s_min > κ requires estimating two empirical quantities: (1) κ (optimization imperfection bound) = √(2ε_max), estimated from the maximum per-step training loss deviation during stable G_0 training. (2) s_min (entropy lower bound of the reservoir distribution) — for the real training data serving as entropy reservoir, s_min represents the minimum entropy rate of P_data. We estimate this by computing the empirical token-level entropy of the real training corpus (a plug-in estimator that converges for large corpora), cross-validated against the model's best-achieved cross-entropy (which provides an upper bound on H(P_data)). For English text, s_min is bracketed between ~0.6 bits/char (Shannon's lower bound from human prediction experiments) and the best model's cross-entropy rate. We report s_min as this bracketed range and verify the ERBP condition holds for our chosen operating point. The key insight is that s_min depends only on the fixed real data distribution — it does not change across generations — so a single estimate suffices for the entire experiment.

**Secondary value proposition (if hypothesis partially holds).** Even if spectral and perplexity metrics degrade concurrently rather than sequentially, the spectral effective rank provides a continuous, fine-grained representation health signal versus perplexity's scalar aggregate over token-level losses. Perplexity can remain stable while the embedding space contracts along specific dimensions (dimensional collapse) — the model produces acceptable average log-likelihood but has lost representational diversity. RankMe (Garrido et al., ICML 2023) directly validates this: spectral effective rank correlates with downstream performance in self-supervised learning without labels, demonstrating it captures representational quality that loss-based metrics miss. The spectral indicator enables proportional control responses to representational narrowing before it accumulates to impact perplexity.

**Spectral collapse-risk estimator (novel indicator).** We train a lightweight linear probe mapping a multi-dimensional feature vector to a scalar collapse-risk score. The key novel feature is the spectral effective rank of the output embedding Gram matrix, inspired by SIGMA (arXiv:2601.03385) and validated by RankMe (ICML 2023) as a representation quality proxy, but applied here in a closed-loop control context rather than for monitoring or offline selection (as in DiSF's eigenvalue-based file selection). Features (computed at evaluation checkpoints every ~200M tokens on 50K generated samples):
- **(Novel in control context) Spectral effective rank**: Compute the Gram matrix G = E·Eᵀ of generated-text embeddings (using the model's final-layer hidden states), then compute effective rank as exp(H(σ)) where H(σ) is the entropy of the normalized singular value distribution. This captures dimensional collapse directly, as validated by SIGMA, DiSF (arXiv:2504.20644), RankMe, and Spectral Lens (arXiv:2605.05683).
- **(Novel) Token-level prediction entropy variance**: For each generated token, compute the entropy of the next-token probability distribution; report the variance across all tokens. High variance indicates selective confidence (healthy); low variance indicates uniform overconfidence (pathological collapse) or uniform uncertainty.
- Unigram/bigram/trigram entropy (standard surface-level features)
- Vocabulary coverage rate and tail-token frequency mass
- Perplexity divergence and perplexity acceleration (second derivative)

**Probe calibration (resolving the bootstrapping problem).** Phase 1 (G_0 training): At checkpoints during G_0, record feature vectors as the healthy baseline distribution. Phase 2 (pilot data): The 100%-synthetic pilot provides a complete collapse trajectory. The probe is trained to predict collapse risk = normalized distance along the trajectory from healthy baseline (risk=0) toward pilot collapse endpoint (risk=1).

**Theoretical contribution: PI stability via ERBP connection (tiered framing).** We present the theoretical contribution in a tiered structure that honestly reflects what is achievable:

*Tier 1 (standalone, days 1-2): Discrete-time PI stability analysis.* We derive conditions on PI gains (Kp, Ki) under which the controlled mixing ratio converges to a stable equilibrium, using standard discrete-time control theory. This requires only standard control theory and is achievable in 2 days.
