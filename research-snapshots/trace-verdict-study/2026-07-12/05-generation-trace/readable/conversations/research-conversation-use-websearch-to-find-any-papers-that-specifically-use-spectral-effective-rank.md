## Overview of Dimensional Collapse

Dimensional collapse is a problem where embeddings shrink into a lower-dimensional subspace, like a 3D sculpture compressed into a 2D drawing. Dimensional collapse, where a few large eigenvalues dominate the eigenspace, poses a significant obstacle for self-supervised learning (SSL).

## Prevention Methods in Self-Supervised Learning

Several approaches have been developed to prevent dimensional collapse during pretraining:

1. **Orthogonal Regularization**: A mitigation approach employing orthogonal regularization (OR) across the encoder, targeting both convolutional and linear layers during pretraining, has been proposed to prevent the dimensional collapse of weight matrices, hidden features, and representations.

2. **Regularization on Representations**: Existing methods to prevent dimensional collapse include modifying representations in downstream tasks, whitening representations directly (removing the projector), and incorporating regularizers on representations during pretraining.

3. **Projector Design**: The projector in contrastive learning is essential to prevent dimensional collapse in the representation space.

## Dimensional Collapse in LLM Pretraining

To prevent collapse and enhance diversity, DiSF selects files by minimizing the Frobenius norm of the features' covariance matrix, achieving more uniform eigenvalues. This approach addresses dimensional collapse in large language model pretraining data selection.

The research indicates that dimensional collapse prevention requires adaptive controls at multiple levels during the pretraining process, with methods ranging from data selection strategies to architectural modifications and regularization techniques.
