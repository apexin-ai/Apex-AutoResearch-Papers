## Paper Summary
Title: "Adaptive Provenance Reweighting for Contamination-Robust Language Model Pretraining"

The paper proposes adaptive b*, a per-batch estimator for the reweighting exponent in detect-and-reweight methods for contamination-robust LM pretraining. Instead of grid-searching b on a validation set (as in Drayson et al. 2025), it derives b* by maximizing an effective tail index α_eff(b) subject to an ESS (effective sample size) constraint ESS(b) ≥ n_min.

Key claims:
1. Theoretical framework: α_eff(b) with ESS constraints is a principled method for selecting b
2. Practical result: Adaptive b* matches or exceeds grid-search best across conditions
3. Problem setting: Works with unknown contamination proportion using only detector scores

## Method Details (Section 3)
- Weighted loss: L = (1/Σw_j) Σ w_i ℓ(x_i; θ), w_i = (1-q_i)^b
- α_eff(b) estimated from Pareto tail behavior of weight distribution {w_i = (1-q_i)^b}
- b* = argmax_{b≥0} α_eff(b) s.t. ESS(b) ≥ n_min
- ESS(b) = (Σw_i)² / Σw_i²
- n_min = 2 throughout
- Loss reweighting (direct gradient weighting) rather than SIR resampling
- Algorithm 1 is a 4-step procedure per batch

## Experimental Setup (Section 4)
- Data: FineWeb subsets, 200M tokens, contamination ρ ∈ {0.4, 0.6}
- Synthetic generated by Qwen3-0.6B (same-gen) or Llama-3.2-1B (cross-gen)
- Domain-skewed variant: 70% code, 20% tech, 10% general
- Detectors: TF-IDF+LR (AUC=0.939) and ModernBERT-150M (AUC=0.986)
- Models: Qwen3-0.6B and Llama-3.2-3B, effective batch size 16
- 6,103 steps, 200M tokens
- Baselines: Uniform, Grid-best (over {0.1,0.2,0.3,0.5,1.0,2.0,5.0} × 3 seeds), Detect-and-remove (q>0.5), Random reweight

72	\subsection{Problem Setup}
73	
74	Let $\mathcal{D} = \{x_1, \ldots, x_N\}$ be a pretraining corpus containing a mixture of human-written and machine-generated text at an unknown contamination proportion $\rho$. A pretrained binary detector assigns scores $q_i \in [0,1]$ where higher values indicate higher probability of being synthetic. We train a language model by minimizing the weighted loss:
75	\begin{equation}
76	    \mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{\sum_j w_j} \sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{B}|} w_i \, \ell(x_i; \theta), \quad w_i = (1 - q_i)^b,
77	\end{equation}
78	where $\mathcal{B}$ is a training batch and $\ell$ is the per-token cross-entropy loss. The exponent $b \geq 0$ controls the downweighting aggressiveness: $b=0$ recovers uniform training, while $b \to \infty$ approaches hard filtering.
79	
80	\subsection{Effective Tail Index $\alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}(b)$}
81	
82	Motivated by the convergence bound $d(\hat{\mathbb{P}}_t, \mathbb{P}_0) \lesssim t^{-\min\{p,\alpha\}}$ of~\citet{convergence2025}, we define an effective tail index that captures how well a given $b$ suppresses contaminated gradient contributions. For a batch $\mathcal{B}$ with empirical $q$-score distribution, we estimate $\alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}(b)$ from the Pareto tail behavior of the weight distribution $\{w_i = (1-q_i)^b\}_{i \in \mathcal{B}}$. Intuitively, higher $\alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}$ means faster convergence to the clean distribution.
83	
84	\subsection{Adaptive $b^*$ with ESS Constraint}
85	
86	We select $b^*$ per batch by solving:
87	\begin{equation}
88	    b^* = \arg\max_{b \geq 0} \; \alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}(b) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathrm{ESS}(b) \geq n_{\min},
89	\end{equation}
90	where the effective sample size is:
91	\begin{equation}
92	    \mathrm{ESS}(b) = \frac{\left(\sum_{i \in \mathcal{B}} w_i\right)^2}{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{B}} w_i^2}.
93	\end{equation}
94
