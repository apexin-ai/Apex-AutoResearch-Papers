Based on the search results, I found comprehensive information about ACL Rolling Review reviewer guidelines for 2026. Here are the key details:

## Overview
Every submission is assigned to an area chair (AC), whose job is to oversee a team of reviewers evaluating the paper, and to write a meta-review summarizing the conclusions.

## Reviewer Standards and Expectations

ARR reviewers are instructed to write a review as if the paper were being considered for the ACL conference. Thus low scores may indicate the submission is not a good fit for ACL, even if it may be a good fit for other/more specialized venues.

## Author Anonymity Preservation

Reviewers for *ACL conferences and TACL should be instructed not to actively try to discover the identity of the authors and not to let any suspicion or knowledge of author identity affect their judgment of the paper. This should not prevent them from searching for related work, but they should take special care and postpone such searches until after they have read the paper completely and formed an initial impression of it.

## Responsibility Incentives

Starting in May 2025, ARR adopts a new policy on the 'highly irresponsible' (meta-)reviewers. The program chairs and ARR EiCs will consider egregious cases including extremely terse reviews, unprofessional tone, and LLM-generated reviews. The 'highly irresponsible' reviewers or chairs may have their work desk-rejected from the current cycle, and ineligible for resubmission or commitment during the following ARR cycle. For review cycles associated with ACL conferences, this means that the papers of such reviewers cannot be committed to the upcoming conference.

## Awards for Excellent Reviewing

Starting from May 2025, ACL introduces an awards policy for reviewers & chairs. In short, the area chairs or senior area chairs may nominate great reviewers, and senior chairs may also nominate area chairs who perform great service. The nominees will then receive more recognition during the ACL conferences (public lists on conference webistes, special stickers for conference badges, more visibility in the program).

For more detailed guidelines, you can visit the official ARR Reviewer Guidelines page.

# ARR Review Dimensions and Scoring Scales

Based on the ARR Reviewer Guidelines, the review system includes **three primary scoring dimensions**:

## The Three Scores

1. **Soundness** - Evaluates technical correctness and methodological rigor. As stated, "The _soundness_ scores must be justified by the text of the review."

2. **Excitement** - Captures personal interest and relevance. The guidelines note this is "orthogonal to the assessment of the soundness of the paper" and "reflect[s] our personal taste."

3. **Overall Assessment** - A composite recommendation reflecting "soundness, excitement, and also other factors like novelty and impact."

## Evaluation Criteria

Beyond scoring, reviewers assess papers across multiple dimensions:

- **Expertise alignment** - Whether the reviewer is qualified for the specific paper
- **Desk-rejection factors** - Appropriateness, anonymity compliance, formatting adherence
- **Reproducibility** - Sufficient detail to reproduce experiments
- **Common methodology problems** (M1-M5) - Including LLM validation and data quality
- **Common results issues** (R1-R5) - Statistical rigor and appropriate claim scoping
- **General issues** (G1-G5) - Research clarity and related work coverage

The guidelines emphasize that "all papers recommended for Findings and main conferences are expected to be sufficiently sound and reproducible."
