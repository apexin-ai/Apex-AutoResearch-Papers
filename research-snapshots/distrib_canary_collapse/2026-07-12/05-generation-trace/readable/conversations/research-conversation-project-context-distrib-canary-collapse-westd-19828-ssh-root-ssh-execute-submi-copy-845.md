**Paragraph 1: Granger precedence ≠ true causality**
- Granger causality tests temporal predictive improvement, not true causality \cite{granger1969investigating, shojaie2022granger}
- Bivariate Granger cannot exclude confounders: an unobserved process could drive both canary and downstream metrics
- Our framework inherits this limitation—"Granger precedence" throughout this paper means predictive improvement, not causal mechanism
- Connect to why we scope claims to "token entropy Granger-precedes diversity collapse" rather than "causes" it

**Paragraph 2: Calibration scope and generalization**
- All FPR/FNR calibration results are specific to our setup: Pythia-410M, WikiText-103, T=10 generations, 5 contamination levels
- Generalization to other architectures, datasets, or training protocols requires independent validation

**Paragraph 3: FPR/FNR dimension asymmetry**
- FPR is validated on method dimension only (single seed at α=0.0)
- FNR is validated on joint method×seed dimension (4 seeds at α=1.0)
- This asymmetry means FPR estimates may be optimistic if seed-level variance in false positive rates is non-negligible
- Full joint calibration (multi-seed null-control experiments) remains future work
- Note: this is already mentioned in §Calibration but Discussion should explicitly flag it as a limitation

**Paragraph 4: Short time-series constraint**
- T=10 generations (11 data points) is typical for iterative training studies but severely limits statistical power
- Toda-Yamamoto's poor performance (FNR=33.3%) is attributable to insufficient residual degrees of freedom at T=10, not to a fundamental flaw in the method
- This is consistent with \citet{boettiger2012limits}'s finding that early-warning signals have limited detection power in short time series
- Longer generation chains would improve power but are computationally expensive; the trade-off between statistical power and computational cost is inherent to iterative training research

**Paragraph 5: Limitations**
- Single architecture family (Pythia): no guarantee that canary behavior generalizes across architectures
- Single dataset (WikiText-103 subset): domain-specific effects may influence canary-downstream dynamics
- No intervention experiment: we observe but do not manipulate canary metrics, so we cannot test whether acting on canary signals prevents collapse
- Replace-only paradigm: accumulate-mode training \cite{dey2024breaking, kazdan2025collapse} may exhibit different canary dynamics
- Diversity scoping: our canary claims apply to distinct-n metrics specifically, not to model collapse broadly (perplexity channel is spurious) \cite{schaeffer2025position}

**Paragraph 6: Future work**
- Multivariate VAR models to test for confounders and multi-directional Granger causality
- Cross-architecture validation (GPT-2-medium in progress; larger models needed)
- Online monitoring: integrating canary metrics into training pipelines for real-time collapse detection
- Accumulate-mode experiments to test whether canary dynamics differ from replace-mode
