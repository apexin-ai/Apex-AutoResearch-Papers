# Paper Summary: TrACE for Adaptive Compute in LLM Agents

## Abstract
The paper introduces a method for improving LLM agent reliability by allocating computational resources adaptively across decision steps, rather than uniformly. The research demonstrates significant efficiency gains on reasoning and navigation benchmarks.

## Main Contribution
The authors present **TrACE** (Trajectorical Adaptive Compute via agrEement), which:

- Measures "inter-rollout action agreement" to determine decision difficulty at each agent step
- Requires "no learned components, no external verifier, and no human labels"
- Allocates additional LLM sampling calls only when agreement is low (high uncertainty)
- Achieves comparable accuracy to fixed-budget baselines while using 33-65% fewer calls depending on the task

## Rollout-Level Signals
**Yes, directly.** The method explicitly uses rollout-level signals: it "samples a small set of candidate next actions and measures how consistently the model commits to the same action." High consistency indicates an easy step requiring fewer rollouts; low consistency triggers additional sampling up to a configured limit.

The core insight validates that "the model's own output consistency encodes difficulty information that can be exploited without training."

## Key Research Areas

**Variance-Informed Rollout Allocation (VIP)**: VIP is a variance-informed predictive allocation strategy that allocates rollout budgets to prompts to minimize expected gradient variance. It uses a lightweight Gaussian process model to predict per-prompt success probabilities from recent rollouts, translating these into variance estimates fed into a convex optimization problem to determine optimal rollout allocations under compute budget constraints.

## Rollout-Guided Reinforcement Learning (RGRL)

Rollout-Guided Reinforcement Learning integrates rollout-based simulation with policy optimization. A rollout refers to simulating the future trajectory of a policy from a given state or prompt, yielding a sampled sequence of states, actions, and rewards.

RGRL methods employ rollouts to evaluate or improve policies by comparing simulated outcomes under different actions, and to allocate sampling budgets adaptively, directing computation to ambiguous or high-variance states for efficient learning.
